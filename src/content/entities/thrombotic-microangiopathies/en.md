## Definition and epidemiology

**What are thrombotic microangiopathies?** Thrombocytopenia and MAHA result when microthrombi block small vessels.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c001--> Hemolytic–uremic syndrome (HUS) and thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) are the classic members.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c002--> Typical HUS usually starts with diarrhea from E. coli O157:H7, a toxin-producing bacterium.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c003--> Factor H and other complement regulators fail in atypical HUS, which is not linked to diarrhea.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c004--> TTP stems from absent or inactivated ADAMTS13 (gene defect, autoantibodies).<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c005--> Ultra-large von Willebrand factor multimers then bind platelets.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c006-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the vessels and the blood smear show?** HUS kidney lesions closely mimic malignant hypertension.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c007--> Fibrin-rich thrombi occlude vessels; arterioles show fibrinoid necrosis.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c008--> On electron microscopy, plasma proteins pushed under the damaged glomerular endothelium form a clear layer that crowds the capillary lumen.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c009--> In TTP, brain, heart and kidneys carry the heaviest load of PAS-positive hyaline microthrombi, though small vessels everywhere are affected.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c010--> TTP lesions lack inflammation, unlike vasculitis.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c011--> Platelet-rich thrombi, more numerous and in many organs, set TTP apart from similar-looking HUS.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c012--> In TTP, red cells are cut into schistocytes as they squeeze past thrombi, and the blood smear shows them.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c013-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to a TMA?** The hemolysis is nonimmune (Coombs-negative).<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c014--> Red cell breakdown raises serum unconjugated bilirubin and LDH.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c015--> In TTP, PT, APTT and fibrinogen stay normal.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c016--> Bloody diarrhea followed by fast-worsening kidney failure suggests typical HUS.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c017--> To find the cause, look for a coexisting disease or a drug history.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c018-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical HUS | Mostly children; kidney failure dominates after bloody diarrhea.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c019--> | Diarrhea history |
| Atypical HUS | Worse prognosis; complement defects, no diarrhea, more often adults.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c020--> | Complement regulator studies |
| TTP | Platelet-rich thrombi in many organs; kidney often spared; neurologic signs and fever.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c021--> | ADAMTS13 activity and antibody |
| [[disseminated-intravascular-coagulation\|DIC]] | Normal clotting screen favors TTP.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c022--> | PT, APTT, fibrinogen |
| [[malignant-hypertension]] and other secondary TMA | Malignant hypertension, some drugs and autoimmune disease can also cause a picture like HUS or TTP.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c023--> | Drug and disease history |

## High-yield summary

- **MAHA** with **thrombocytopenia** marks both HUS and TTP.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c024-->
- **Typical HUS** follows bloody diarrhea from toxin-producing E. coli and hits the kidney.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c025-->
- **Atypical HUS** involves complement regulators such as **factor H** and carries a worse prognosis.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c026-->
- **TTP** is **ADAMTS13** deficiency with platelet-rich thrombi.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c027-->
- **Schistocytes** with a **normal clotting screen** favor TTP over DIC.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c028-->
- **Plasmapheresis** or plasma infusion treats TTP.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c029-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is TTP a kidney disease?** No. The kidney is often spared.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c030-->

**Does TTP activate coagulation like DIC?** No. PT, APTT and fibrinogen stay normal.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c031-->

**Is the hemolysis autoimmune?** No. It is Coombs-negative.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c032-->

**Are typical and atypical HUS the same?** No. Atypical HUS lacks preceding diarrhea and often recurs.<!--c:thrombotic-microangiopathies-c033-->
