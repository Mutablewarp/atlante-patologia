## Definition and epidemiology

**What is emphysema, and who gets it?** Fibrosis is absent in this chronic lung disease, which destroys the walls of airspaces beyond the terminal bronchioles and so enlarges them.<!--c:emphysema-c001--> How advanced the disease is counts for more than its anatomic type.<!--c:emphysema-c002--> Several patterns can coexist in one lung, and advanced disease is harder to classify.<!--c:emphysema-c003--> Most patients first seek care at 60 or older.<!--c:emphysema-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes emphysema?** Cigarette smoking is the major cause.<!--c:emphysema-c005--> Nonsmokers rarely develop more than mild emphysema.<!--c:emphysema-c006--> About 1% of COPD patients have inherited α1-antitrypsin (α1-AT) deficiency.<!--c:emphysema-c007--> It is far more common among young people with severe emphysema.<!--c:emphysema-c008--> Inherited PiZ α1-AT deficiency lies behind most emphysema diagnosed in patients younger than 40.<!--c:emphysema-c009--> Emphysema appears at about 45 to 50 years in PiZZ homozygotes who never smoke.<!--c:emphysema-c010--> Smoking brings emphysema forward by 5 to 10 years in these people.<!--c:emphysema-c011--> Most PiZZ homozygotes who never smoke stay free of emphysema.<!--c:emphysema-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does smoke destroy the alveolar walls?** The lung normally balances making elastin against breaking it down.<!--c:emphysema-c013--> More elastin breakdown or less protection from it causes emphysema.<!--c:emphysema-c014--> Lavage of smokers' lungs recovers extra neutrophils, rich in elastase and other proteases.<!--c:emphysema-c015--> α1-AT loses its activity in smokers because smoke oxidizes its methionine residues.<!--c:emphysema-c016--> Poor elastic recoil, caused by elastase wearing away the elastin of distal airways unchecked, adds to airflow obstruction.<!--c:emphysema-c017--> Other cellular proteases may contribute, unconfirmed.<!--c:emphysema-c018-->

Elastase is among the serine proteases that circulating α1-AT inhibits.<!--c:emphysema-c019--> Liver-made α1-AT supplies 90% of the proteinase-blocking power of blood.<!--c:emphysema-c020--> α1-AT restrains neutrophil elastase in the lung, sparing elastin and the rest of the alveolar wall.<!--c:emphysema-c021--> Each person carries two codominant SERPINA1 (Pi) alleles, and together they set how much α1-AT is made and of which kind.<!--c:emphysema-c022--> PiMM is the usual genotype, built from the most frequent allele, PiM.<!--c:emphysema-c023--> Hepatocytes break down some misfolded mutant proteins in proteasomes.<!--c:emphysema-c024--> Mutants that polymerize build up inside hepatocytes.<!--c:emphysema-c025--> PiZ is the most harmful allele because the liver secretes its protein poorly.<!--c:emphysema-c026--> A PiZZ homozygote has just 15% to 20% of the normal plasma level of α1-AT.<!--c:emphysema-c027--> PiZZ people risk cirrhosis and emphysema.<!--c:emphysema-c028--> Smoking is thought to cause emphysema by upsetting the protease–antiprotease balance, a view backed by the emphysema seen in α1-AT deficiency.<!--c:emphysema-c029-->

## Gross morphology

**How is it classified?** Types are defined by where in the acinus the lesions lie.<!--c:emphysema-c030-->

- **Centrilobular (centriacinar):** the damage stays at the respiratory bronchiole, the proximal part of the acinus.<!--c:emphysema-c031--> It is the most common type.<!--c:emphysema-c032--> Symptoms are usual, and smoking typically accompanies it.<!--c:emphysema-c033--> Between the enlarged spaces, distal ducts and alveoli stay normal at first.<!--c:emphysema-c034--> Later they can be destroyed too, leaving large irregular spaces.<!--c:emphysema-c035--> The worst damage lies in the upper lobes, extending into the top of the lower lobes.<!--c:emphysema-c036-->
- **Panacinar (panlobular):** septa are lost from the center of each acinus outward, and the whole acinus is affected evenly.<!--c:emphysema-c037--> Late panacinar emphysema leaves only a fine scaffold, called cotton-candy lung.<!--c:emphysema-c038--> α1-AT deficiency is the typical setting for it.<!--c:emphysema-c039--> When a smoker has both types, the panacinar one tends to lie lower in the lung.<!--c:emphysema-c040-->
- **Paraseptal (distal acinar, localized):** only a single site or a handful of sites are damaged, and normal lung surrounds them.<!--c:emphysema-c041--> It can arise anywhere but usually lies beneath the pleura, at the top of an upper lobe.<!--c:emphysema-c042-->
- **Bullae:** large destroyed areas that a localized focus can grow into.<!--c:emphysema-c043--> They range from 2 cm to a whole hemithorax.<!--c:emphysema-c044-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the slides show?** In the centrilobular type, the bronchioles leading into the emphysematous spaces are narrowed and inflamed.<!--c:emphysema-c045--> Next to normal lung at the same magnification, α1-AT-deficient lung looks hollowed out, with sparse alveolar walls and wide, uneven spaces.<!--c:emphysema-c046--> In localized emphysema, lost alveolar tissue leaves enlarged subpleural airspaces.<!--c:emphysema-c047-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What is examined, and how?**

- **How it is studied:** whole-mount lung slices show where the enlarged airspaces lie in the lobes.<!--c:emphysema-c048-->
- **Histology:** comparing diseased lung with normal lung shows how many alveolar walls are lost.<!--c:emphysema-c049-->
- **Special stains, IHC, electron microscopy:** none described here.
- **Molecular:** the PiZ allele of SERPINA1 lies behind most emphysema diagnosed in patients younger than 40.<!--c:emphysema-c050-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does a patient with emphysema look like?** Dyspnea on effort lasts for years; the slight cough brings up no sputum.<!--c:emphysema-c051--> Breathing draws on accessory muscles, and patients have lost weight.<!--c:emphysema-c052--> Weight loss probably comes from the effort of breathing rather than from eating too little.<!--c:emphysema-c053--> Patients breathe rapidly, and expiration takes longer than normal.<!--c:emphysema-c054--> Fast breathing and high minute ventilation let pink puffers keep arterial hemoglobin saturation near normal.<!--c:emphysema-c055--> Cor pulmonale is less likely in emphysema than in chronic bronchitis, and recurrent infections are no more common.<!--c:emphysema-c056--> Both diseases often coexist, so telling which one causes a patient's symptoms is hard.<!--c:emphysema-c057--> The bronchitic "blue bloater" has cyanosis and edema from cor pulmonale.<!--c:emphysema-c058-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What do imaging and tests show?** On a chest radiograph the diaphragms sit low and the lungs look large, a picture of overinflation.<!--c:emphysema-c059--> A barrel chest results from a larger anteroposterior diameter.<!--c:emphysema-c060--> On the radiograph, bronchovascular markings fade out before the lung periphery.<!--c:emphysema-c061-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it progress?** Dyspnea worsens steadily and lung function falls inexorably.<!--c:emphysema-c062--> Lung transplantation is a treatment option for advanced disease.<!--c:emphysema-c063--> Spontaneous pneumothorax, particularly in younger people, can follow rupture of a localized focus.<!--c:emphysema-c064--> Localized emphysema itself is clinically insignificant.<!--c:emphysema-c065-->

## High-yield summary

- **Centrilobular** emphysema is the smoker's type and is worst in the upper lobes.<!--c:emphysema-c070-->
- **Panacinar** emphysema is typically linked with α1-AT deficiency.<!--c:emphysema-c071-->
- **Paraseptal** emphysema is subpleural, apical, and can cause pneumothorax.<!--c:emphysema-c072-->
- The mechanism is an **elastase** versus **α1-antitrypsin** imbalance.<!--c:emphysema-c073-->
- In **PiZZ** homozygotes, plasma α1-AT is 15% to 20% of normal.<!--c:emphysema-c074-->
- The **pink puffer** keeps near-normal hemoglobin saturation.<!--c:emphysema-c075-->
- Radiographs show **overinflation** and a barrel chest.<!--c:emphysema-c076-->
- **Bullae** range from 2 cm to a whole hemithorax.<!--c:emphysema-c077-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is emphysema a fibrotic scarring disease?** No. Airspace walls are destroyed without fibrosis.<!--c:emphysema-c078-->

**Is the anatomic type more important than severity?** No. Severity matters more.<!--c:emphysema-c079-->

**Do all PiZZ nonsmokers get emphysema?** No. Most show no evidence of it.<!--c:emphysema-c080-->

**Are the pink puffer and the blue bloater the same patient?** No. The emphysema patient keeps near-normal oxygen saturation.<!--c:emphysema-c081--> The cyanotic, edematous blue bloater is the chronic bronchitis picture.<!--c:emphysema-c082-->

**Is every centrilobular-looking emphysema caused by smoking?** No. In coal miners, focal dust emphysema looks centrilobular but has no bronchiolar inflammation, and its spaces are smaller and more even.<!--c:emphysema-c083-->

## Mnemonics

**Where does each type sit?** "Center goes up, whole goes down": centrilobular emphysema, the acinus center, favors the upper lobes.<!--c:emphysema-c084--> Panacinar emphysema, the whole acinus, favors the lower zones when both coexist in a smoker.<!--c:emphysema-c085--> "Paraseptal, Pleura, Pneumothorax": the three P's of the localized type.<!--c:emphysema-c086-->
