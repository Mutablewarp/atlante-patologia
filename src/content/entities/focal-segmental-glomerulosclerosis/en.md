## Definition and epidemiology

**What is focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)?** Patients with FSGS differ in outcome, cause and response to treatment, because the term names a pattern of glomerular injury, not one disease.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c001--> "Focal" means that some glomeruli are affected and others are not.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c002--> "Segmental" means that, at first, only part of the capillary tuft is involved.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c003--> Sclerosis is the extra collagenous matrix that often accumulates in consolidated segments.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c004--> The injury is either primary (idiopathic) or secondary to another condition.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c005--> FSGS accounts for 10% of childhood primary nephrotic syndrome and about a third of adult cases.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c006--> Among Black patients FSGS is the most frequent primary cause of nephrotic syndrome, and it affects them more than white patients.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c007--> Its frequency has risen in recent decades, for unknown reasons.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes FSGS?** Primary FSGS has no identified cause.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c009--> Secondary FSGS follows a genetic defect, a loss of nephrons, obesity, low blood oxygen, a virus or a drug.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c010--> Some forms arise from mutations in collagen IV, TRPC6, α-actinin-4, nephrin or podocin, all podocyte proteins.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c011--> Renal mass can be lost through reflux nephropathy (acquired) or unilateral agenesis with contralateral hypoplasia (congenital).<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c012--> Obesity can cause FSGS even when the amount of kidney tissue is normal.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c013--> Hypoxemia from sickle cell disease or cyanotic congenital heart disease can injure glomeruli in a comparable pattern.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c014--> The collapsing variant is the one tied to HIV infection, and Black patients are especially affected.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c015--> Some cancer patients given pamidronate for osteolytic bone disease develop collapsing FSGS.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c016--> Intravenous drug abuse is another setting of collapsing FSGS.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c017--> APOL1 variants are linked to FSGS in Black patients; they probably persist by protecting against African sleeping sickness.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c018-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do glomeruli scar?** Whatever the trigger, damage converges on a final common pathway, probably through the podocyte in every type.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c019--> Fewer nephrons mean the survivors are overworked: their glomeruli enlarge, and capillary pressure and filtration rise.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c020--> Podocytes barely proliferate, so enlargement overstresses them.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c021--> A cause outside the kidney is suggested by a circulating permeability factor found in some patients.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c022--> FSGS most often recurs after transplantation in patients with the factor, which supports a systemic cause.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c023--> HIV nephropathy may come from HIV itself in kidney tissue, or from a different virus in an immunodeficient patient's kidney.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c024--> Viral infection of podocytes may also cause collapsing FSGS.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c025-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the glomeruli look like?** Matrix, cells or both fill part of the tuft, in some glomeruli only.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c026--> Hyalinosis is the glassy look of a lesion infiltrated by plasma proteins and lipid.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c027--> Next to sclerotic lesions, the tuft sticks to Bowman capsule.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c028--> Uninvolved glomeruli can have a few extra mesangial cells but are otherwise normal.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c029--> The histologic variants are:
- **Perihilar:** the scars cluster at the hilum of the tuft and in deep juxtamedullary glomeruli, mainly with obesity or reduced renal mass.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c030-->
- **Collapsing:** the sclerotic segments have collapsed capillaries, and nearby podocytes are enlarged and more numerous.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c031-->
- **Tip lesion:** sclerosis is confined to the tuft near the tubular pole.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c032-->
- **Cellular:** the consolidated areas contain many lipid-laden cells.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c033-->
- **HIV-associated nephropathy:** tubules are commonly atrophic or cystically dilated with protein casts; the interstitium is fibrotic with mononuclear cells.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c034-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** a kidney biopsy examined by light microscopy, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c035-->
- On PAS, a scar near the hilum of the tuft is seen fused to Bowman capsule.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c036-->
- On silver stain, collapsing FSGS shows hypertrophic podocytes, and the capillaries cave in as matrix accumulates.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c037-->
- Since lesions are focal, a small sample with no sclerotic glomeruli can pass for minimal-change disease.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c038-->
- Patchy IgM and C3 deposits, found by immunofluorescence, sit in the scarred, hyalinized segments.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c039-->
- In scars, IgG, C4 and C1q are rarer than IgM and C3; unscarred segments barely stain, mostly for mesangial IgM and C3.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c040-->
- On electron microscopy, podocytes sometimes peel off or are lost from the basement membrane, and the effacement of foot processes is diffuse.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c041-->
- Scars are matrix-rich, with capillary collapse and thick, wrinkled basement membranes.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c042-->
- Hyalinosis on light microscopy matches electron-dense deposits of trapped plasma protein in sclerotic segments.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c043-->
- Immune complexes are absent.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c044-->
- In HIV-associated nephropathy, endothelial cells contain many tubuloreticular inclusions.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c045-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** Typically, nephrotic syndrome develops from proteinuria that began unnoticed.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c046--> Microscopic hematuria is frequent, and hypertension is present in many patients.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c047--> ACE inhibitors or ARBs help when FSGS comes from obesity or reduced renal mass; the course is usually indolent, with less proteinuria.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c048--> The tip lesion resembles minimal-change disease and often starts with severe nephrotic syndrome.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c049--> Renal failure and severe nephrotic syndrome typically accompany collapsing FSGS, HIV-associated or idiopathic.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c050-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What points to FSGS?** The clinical picture combines hypertension and microscopic hematuria with proteinuria that progresses to nephrotic syndrome.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c051--> A patient labeled minimal-change disease who gets no response to steroids may have FSGS that the biopsy missed.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c052--> Azotemia in known minimal-change disease may come from drug-induced interstitial nephritis, from FSGS missed by the first biopsy, or from progression to FSGS.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c053-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens over time?** Proteinuria usually stays and the kidneys lose function bit by bit.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c054--> Many reach end-stage renal disease after 5 to 20 years.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c055--> Corticosteroids help some patients, but not all.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c056--> Steroids work more often in the tip lesion than in other variants.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c057--> Within 2 years, half of the patients with collapsing FSGS reach end-stage disease, and this variant has the worst prognosis.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c058--> Half of transplanted kidneys develop recurrent FSGS, though transplantation is the preferred treatment.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c059-->

## High-yield summary

- **FSGS** is a pattern of injury with many causes, not one disease.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c064-->
- **Focal** means some glomeruli, **segmental** means part of the tuft.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c065-->
- **Secondary** causes include genetic podocyte defects, reduced renal mass, obesity, HIV and pamidronate.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c066-->
- The **collapsing** variant, typical of HIV, has the worst prognosis.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c067-->
- The **tip lesion** responds best to steroids.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c068-->
- **Recurrence in grafts** is frequent, especially in patients with a serum permeability factor.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c069-->
- A small biopsy can mimic **minimal-change disease**.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c070-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is FSGS one disease with one cause?** No. It is a pattern of injury with many causes and outcomes.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c071-->

**Does a biopsy without sclerotic glomeruli rule FSGS out?** No. The lesion is focal, and normal-looking glomeruli can make it look like minimal-change disease.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c072-->

**Does FSGS respond to steroids like minimal-change disease?** Not reliably. Only some patients improve, and the tip lesion responds better than other variants.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c073-->

**Does a kidney transplant cure FSGS?** No. The disease often recurs in the graft.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c074-->

## Mnemonics

**FSGS = "Few, Scarred, Graft, Steroids?":** few glomeruli affected, scarred segments, graft recurrence, steroid response in only some patients.<!--c:focal-segmental-glomerulosclerosis-c075-->
