## Definition and epidemiology

**Which cancer is "laryngeal cancer", and who gets it?** Squamous cell carcinoma accounts for nearly every cancer of the larynx.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c001--> Men are affected far more often than women.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c002--> The majority of patients are cigarette smokers.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What puts the larynx at risk?** Most patients smoke cigarettes.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c004--> Roughly one tumor in four contains human papillomavirus (HPV).<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c005--> An HPV 6 or 11 papilloma seldom becomes carcinoma; smokers and previously irradiated patients are the usual cases.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c006-->

## Gross morphology

**Where in the larynx does the tumor sit, and why does it matter?**

- **Glottic:** a glottic tumor stays on the true vocal cords, on one side or on both.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c007--> It is the most frequent site: about two of every three cases.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c008-->
- **Supraglottic:** the true cords are spared by definition; the tumor starts in the epiglottis, the false cords or the ventricle.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c009--> No more than about one-third of cases begin here.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c010-->
- **Transglottic:** the tumor is called transglottic when both the true and the false cords are involved.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c011-->
- **Infraglottic:** these rare tumors often reach the trachea; they begin under the true cords, or on them with downward spread.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c012-->

In a supraglottic laryngectomy specimen the tumor sits above the mucosa as a granular patch with ragged borders.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c013-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does each site spread, and what surgery follows?**

- Glottic tumors have a good prognosis; they reach the lymph nodes only late.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c014-->
- Compared with glottic tumors, supraglottic ones are more often found with nodal metastases.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c015-->
- Total laryngectomy is often needed for transglottic tumors, which spread to lymph nodes.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c016-->
- Infraglottic tumors usually call for total laryngectomy, and they commonly spread to lymph nodes.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c017-->

## High-yield summary

- **Squamous cell carcinoma** makes up almost all laryngeal cancers.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c021-->
- **Cigarette smoking** and male sex are the typical background.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c022-->
- **Glottic** tumors are the commonest and sit on the true cords; nodal spread comes late and the prognosis is good.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c023-->
- **Supraglottic** tumors spare the true cords by definition, and they metastasize to nodes more often than glottic ones.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c024-->
- **Transglottic** and **infraglottic** tumors spread to the nodes; total laryngectomy is often needed for the first and usually for the second.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c025-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a supraglottic carcinoma one that touches the true cords?** No. By definition it does not involve them.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c026-->

**Are glottic and supraglottic cancers equally likely to reach the nodes early?** No. Supraglottic tumors metastasize to nodes more often, while glottic tumors do so late.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c027-->

**Are laryngeal cancers a mix of many cell types?** No. Almost all are squamous cell carcinomas.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c028-->

## Mnemonics

**Which site is slow to reach the nodes?** Glottic = **G**ood prognosis, **G**lacial nodal spread; Supraglottic = **S**ooner nodal spread.<!--c:laryngeal-squamous-cell-carcinoma-c029-->
