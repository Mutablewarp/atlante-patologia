## Definition and epidemiology

**What is hereditary hemochromatosis?** Hereditary hemochromatosis (HH) is an inherited disorder with excess iron absorption and slow iron accumulation.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c001--> It is systemic; parenchymal cells of liver, heart and pancreas suffer most.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c002--> HH tops the list of inherited metabolic diseases among white people.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c003--> Onset is most often between 40 and 60 years of age.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c004--> Menstrual iron loss probably protects women, so men are affected far more often.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c005--> The gut absorbs little iron per day, so overload takes years.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which genes are mutated, and what else causes iron overload?** The usual culprit is the *HFE* gene (chromosome 6, short arm).<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c007--> In 90% of patients, both *HFE* copies carry the C282Y change.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c008--> H63D is a less common *HFE* mutation.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c009--> *HAMP*, the hepcidin gene, is only very rarely mutated; *TfR2* and hemojuvelin (*HJV*) mutations cause other rare forms.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c010--> Mutations of the ferroportin gene can also cause hemochromatosis.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c011--> Genetic or epigenetic modifiers probably explain why some C282Y homozygotes never develop disease or iron overload.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c012-->

**What is secondary iron overload?** It is iron excess in people without HH mutations.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c013--> Causes include ineffective red cell production, very high dietary iron, injected iron and repeated transfusions.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c014--> Porphyria cutanea tarda and chronic liver disease, such as alcoholic liver disease, also increase iron absorption.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c015--> Alcohol seems to worsen iron storage and cell injury, and long-term heavy drinking is common in these patients.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c016-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does iron pile up in HH?** The body cannot excrete iron, so the amount absorbed in the duodenum sets the total.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c017--> Ferroportin is the iron exit of enterocytes, hepatocytes and macrophages; liver-made hepcidin binds it, halting export and triggering its breakdown.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c018--> Hepcidin synthesis depends on HFE, TfR2 and hemojuvelin.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c019--> Because hepcidin is too low in HH, the gut behaves as if iron were scarce, though it is abundant.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c020--> Without hepcidin to restrain ferroportin, more iron crosses enterocytes and is exported by macrophages and hepatocytes.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c021--> More iron arrives than transferrin can carry, so free iron in the blood becomes very high.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c022--> Storage and export fall behind once hepatocytes take in free iron, by a route still unclear.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c023--> Overloaded cells probably tolerate [[oxidative-injury]] poorly, since iron fuels reactive oxygen species damage.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c024-->

**And in secondary overload?** On a low-iron diet overload is unlikely, because absorption partly depends on how much iron is eaten.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c025--> In sickle cell anemia and thalassemia major, absorption rises even though transferrin is saturated; the iron comes from hemolysis and transfusions.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c026-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the organs look like?** Late in the disease the liver shows small-nodule cirrhosis; it is enlarged and reddish brown.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c027--> The pancreas is rust colored and fibrotic.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c028-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Where is the iron, and what does it do to the tissue?** Iron granules ([[g:hemosiderin]]), mostly ferric iron held in lysosomes, fill liver cells and the lining of the bile ducts.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c029--> Kupffer cells engulf necrotic hepatocytes and so become conspicuously iron-laden late in the disease.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c030--> Bile ductules and macrophages in the fibrous bands also stand out for their iron.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c031--> The small-nodule cirrhosis eventually becomes large-nodule.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c032--> Acini and islets of Langerhans both lose cells in the iron-laden pancreas.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c033--> Ventricular muscle holds more iron pigment than atrial muscle, and myocyte death often leaves fibrosis.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c034--> In most patients, iron nonspecifically drives basal melanocytes to make extra melanin.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c035--> Only some patients show marked iron deposits in the skin itself.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c036-->

**How does secondary overload differ?** Hepatocytes in secondary siderosis receive iron only late, after Kupffer cells are uniformly loaded.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c037--> Cirrhotic nodules often have iron only at the periphery, and hepatic iron is generally lower than in HH.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c038-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** Perls staining ([[g:perls-prussian-blue]]) turns HH iron blue; it lines the bile canaliculi of the hepatocytes.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c039-->
- Molecular findings: *HFE* C282Y or H63D homozygosity, or C282Y/H63D compound heterozygosity.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c040-->
- Specimen, sampling, IHC and electron microscopy are not covered by the pages used.

## Clinical presentation

**What does a patient with advanced HH look like?** Advanced HH shows up as a failing heart, bronzed skin, diabetes and a cirrhotic liver.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c041--> Symptoms typically begin in midlife, and the liver damage advances slowly.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c042--> Pancreatic iron commonly causes diabetes; with pigmented skin and glucose intolerance it is called bronze diabetes.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c043--> Death in HH often comes from heart failure, which usually looks like [[dilated-cardiomyopathy]].<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c044--> Restrictive physiology may occur too.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c045--> Atrial fibrillation is common.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c046--> Reduced pituitary gonadotropin release leaves men impotent with little body hair, and women with no periods and low libido.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c047--> Arthropathy affects many patients, hitting the fingers and hands hardest.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c048--> In HH, arthritis can also cripple big joints, the knee for one.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c049-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which blood tests point to HH?** Transferrin is fully saturated and serum iron is far above normal.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c050--> Normally transferrin is only about one-third saturated.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c051--> Ferritin in the blood is greatly increased in HH, in line with the large iron stores.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c052--> Ferritin also rises in acute inflammation, so it is not specific.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c053-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens to untreated and treated patients?** Cirrhosis may lead to [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]].<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c054--> Untreated, patients can die of gastrointestinal bleeding or liver-failure coma.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c055--> Repeated phlebotomy is the best way to remove iron.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c056--> Iron-depleted homozygotes without cirrhosis or diabetes live as long as the general population.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c057-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What else can look like HH?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secondary iron overload | Iron starts in Kupffer cells and is usually less than in HH; no HH mutation; the cause is transfusion, iron intake or ineffective erythropoiesis.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c058--> | Perls stain pattern and history |
| [[alcoholic-liver-disease]] | Heavy alcohol use is common in secondary overload; chronic liver disease raises iron absorption.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c059--> | History and Perls stain |
| [[wilson-disease]] | Wilson disease loads brain and liver with copper, not iron, and is autosomal recessive.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c060--> | See the Wilson disease card |
| Anemia of chronic inflammation | High hepcidin blocks ferroportin: blood iron is low, yet iron is held in stores.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c061--> | Clinical context and iron studies |

## High-yield summary

- **HFE** C282Y homozygosity underlies most HH.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c062-->
- **Low hepcidin** lets ferroportin run unchecked, so absorption is excessive.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c063-->
- **Perls stain** shows blue iron in hepatocytes.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c064-->
- Advanced HH shows **bronzed skin, diabetes, a failing heart and cirrhosis**.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c065-->
- **Bronze diabetes**: glucose intolerance with pigmented skin.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c066-->
- **Phlebotomy** is the most effective treatment.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c067-->
- Cirrhosis can lead to **hepatocellular carcinoma**.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c068-->
- In **secondary overload**, iron starts in Kupffer cells and HH mutations are absent.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c069-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does everyone with two C282Y copies get the disease?** No. Some homozygotes never develop the disease or iron overload.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c070-->

**Is HH caused by eating too much iron?** No. Faulty hepcidin regulation raises absorption; diet matters mainly in secondary overload.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c071-->

**Is the bronze skin just iron in the skin?** No. Most patients have extra melanin, and only about half have marked iron deposits in the skin.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c072-->

**Does a high ferritin always mean iron overload?** No. Acute inflammation raises it too.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c073-->

**Do many transfusions alone cause iron cirrhosis?** Usually not. Cirrhosis is rare, since the iron stays mostly inside mononuclear phagocytes.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c074-->

**Is "Bantu siderosis" hereditary?** No. It is secondary overload from iron-rich home-brewed drinks.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c075-->

## Mnemonics

**Five targets: "Large Pink Hippos Sip Juice."** Liver, Pancreas, Heart, Skin, Joints.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c076-->

**Mechanism: "Hepcidin low, iron flows."** Too little hepcidin, too much absorption.<!--c:hereditary-hemochromatosis-c077-->
