## Definition and epidemiology

**What is polycystic kidney disease, and what else is cystic?** Among inherited diseases that fill the kidneys with cysts, ADPKD (autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease) is the most frequent.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c001--> In the United States, 1 in 400 to 1 in 1,000 people have it.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c002--> Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD) is far rarer and appears in infancy.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c003--> Others are nephronophthisis, medullary cystic disease, medullary sponge kidney, glomerulocystic disease, simple cysts and acquired cysts of dialysis.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which gene is behind each disease?**
- ADPKD: PKD2 mutations account for about 15% of cases, PKD1 for the other 85%.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c005-->
- ARPKD: mutations of PKHD1.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c006-->
- Nephronophthisis is autosomal recessive and arises from mutations in NPHP genes.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c007-->
- Medullary cystic kidney disease is autosomal dominant: MUC1 mutations cause type 1 and UMOD mutations type 2.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c008-->
- Familial glomerulocystic disease is linked to mutations in HNF-1β (TCF2).<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these mutations produce cysts?** Primary cilia of tubular cells carry polycystin-1 and polycystin-2, encoded by PKD1 and PKD2.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c010--> Through them the cell reads urine flow and controls its calcium.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c011--> When they are defective, tubular cells proliferate excessively; because the fault lies in the cilia, this is a ciliopathy.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c012--> A handful of abnormally multiplying tubule cells is thought to seed each cyst.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c013--> Where the lining cells are abnormal, a faulty basement membrane lets the tubule widen.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c014--> Most cysts end up disconnected from the tubule, and their lining then secretes the fluid; at first it was glomerular filtrate.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c015--> Fewer than 2% of nephrons form cysts, so crowding alone probably does not explain the functional loss.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c016--> In ARPKD, fibrocystin, encoded by PKHD1, sits in cilia of pancreatic ducts, bile ducts and renal collecting ducts.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c017--> Mutated PKHD1 therefore also malforms the bile ducts, with liver fibrosis.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c018-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the kidneys look like?**
- ADPKD: kidney weight can reach 4,500 g, with both markedly enlarged.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c019-->
- The cysts hold straw-colored fluid, and the largest measure 5 cm.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c020-->
- Over the years, tissue left between cysts wastes away and scars.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c021-->
- ARPKD is always bilateral.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c022-->
- Where cortex meets medulla, nephronophthisis kidneys often, not always, contain cysts reaching 1 cm.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c024-->
- In medullary sponge kidney, the papillae hold many small cysts, each under 5 mm.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c025-->
- Both kidneys are involved in 75% of these patients.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c026-->
- Simple cysts push out the capsule because they sit mostly in the outer cortex; there may be one or several.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c027-->
- Over 75% of patients dialyzed for 5 years have cysts in both kidneys.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c028-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What lines the cysts, and what surrounds them?**
- ADPKD cysts have a cuboidal or columnar lining.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c029-->
- Radiating at right angles to the capsule, the cysts of ARPKD are stretched collecting ducts of both cortex and medulla.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c031-->
- In ARPKD with later onset, atrophic tubules and scarred interstitium are especially common.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c032-->
- The usual liver lesion in ARPKD is congenital hepatic fibrosis: bile ducts multiply and fibrous tissue enlarges the portal tracts.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c033-->
- In early nephronophthisis, tubular basement membranes thicken and split into layers.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c034-->
- Late nephronophthisis looks nonspecific: inflammatory cells sit among scarred glomeruli and interstitium.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c035-->
- Medullary sponge kidney cysts develop from papillary collecting ducts and have a cuboidal or columnar lining.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c036-->
- Acquired-cyst lining can become hyperplastic or neoplastic during the first 10 years of dialysis.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c038-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at?**
- **How it is studied:** note where the cysts lie: radial collecting-duct cysts in ARPKD, corticomedullary cysts in nephronophthisis, papillary cysts in medullary sponge kidney.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c039-->
- Molecular findings identify the causal gene: PKD1 or PKD2, PKHD1, NPHP genes, MUC1 or UMOD.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c040-->

The reference pages name no special stain or sampling method.

## Clinical presentation

**How do they present?** The old name, adult polycystic kidney disease, reflects that most patients have no signs until the fourth decade.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c041--> Childhood symptoms occur in a small minority, and presentation at birth is rare.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c042--> ADPKD may cause a mass in the abdomen, pain in the side or belly, or a dragging weight in the lumbar region.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c043--> Hypertension is both early and frequent.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c044--> In time, kidney function declines steadily, with blood and a little protein in the urine.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c045-->

**Which organs outside the kidney are involved in ADPKD?**
- Liver cysts, lined like bile duct epithelium, occur in a third of patients.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c046-->
- About 1 in 5 patients have a cerebral aneurysm.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c048-->
- Colonic diverticula are common.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c049-->

**And in the other diseases?**
- In ARPKD, huge kidneys and oligohydramnios (Potter sequence) both stunt lung development, leaving pulmonary hypoplasia.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c050-->
- One infant in four does not survive the neonatal period.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c051-->
- Survivors have liver fibrosis with portal hypertension, and renal insufficiency whose onset and pace vary.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c052-->
- Kidney failure comes last in nephronophthisis; first come tubular dysfunction, bed-wetting, thirst and large urine volumes.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c053-->
- Juvenile nephronophthisis, the commonest of three forms, causes 5% to 10% of pediatric ESRD.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c054-->
- Polyuria is the usual first sign of medullary cystic disease, whose renal failure sets in after the fourth decade.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c055-->
- Symptoms, when present, appear at 30 to 60 years: flank pain, dysuria, and hematuria or gravel from cyst stones.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c056-->
- Only very large simple cysts cause symptoms; most are chance findings.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c057-->
- One person in two beyond 50 has one.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c058-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the type?** A smooth outer surface separates ARPKD from ADPKD, whose surface cysts distort.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c059--> The reference pages give no imaging or laboratory criteria.

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outcome?** Half of ADPKD patients reach ESRD.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c060--> About 5% of ESRD treated by dialysis or transplant is due to ADPKD.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c061--> Intracranial hemorrhage kills 15% of ADPKD patients.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c062--> Juvenile nephronophthisis usually reaches ESRD within 10 years of onset.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c063--> Medullary sponge kidney is harmless itself, but its cysts may favor pyelonephritis.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c064--> Renal cell carcinoma arises in about 5% of acquired cystic disease.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c065-->

## High-yield summary

- **ADPKD** is dominant, mostly **PKD1**, with huge cystic kidneys and onset in the fourth decade.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c073-->
- **ARPKD** is recessive (**PKHD1**): smooth large kidneys, radial cysts and **hepatic fibrosis**.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c074-->
- ADPKD **extrarenal lesions**: liver cysts (a third), **cerebral aneurysms** (a fifth), diverticula.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c075-->
- **Medullary cystic disease** is dominant and causes renal failure in adulthood; **nephronophthisis** is recessive and starts between infancy and adolescence.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c076-->
- **Medullary sponge kidney** has papillary cysts, with stones and secondary pyelonephritis.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c077-->
- **Acquired cysts** follow long dialysis and carry a risk of **renal cell carcinoma**.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c078-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is ADPKD only a disease of adults?** No. It is usually silent until the fourth decade, but a minority have childhood symptoms.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c079-->

**Does ARPKD affect only the kidney?** No. Congenital hepatic fibrosis is the usual liver lesion.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c080-->

**Are nephronophthisis and medullary cystic disease the same disease?** No. The pathology is similar, but genes and inheritance differ.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c081-->

## Mnemonics

**SRL:** in ARPKD the surface is Smooth, the cysts are Radial and the Liver is fibrotic.<!--c:polycystic-kidney-disease-c082-->
