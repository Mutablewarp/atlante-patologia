## Definition and epidemiology

**What does "hyaline" mean on a slide?** Under the light microscope, arterioles with hyaline arteriolosclerosis have a glassy-looking wall.<!--c:hyaline-change-c001-->
The same descriptive word is used for deposits in arterioles, kidney glomeruli, pancreatic islets and pituitary tumor cells.<!--c:hyaline-change-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which conditions go with hyaline change?** Hyaline arteriolosclerosis is a vascular change seen in chronic hypertension.<!--c:hyaline-change-c003-->
It can also be present in diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c004-->
When blood cortisol is high enough to switch off ACTH release, the corticotroph cells show Crooke hyalinization.<!--c:hyaline-change-c005-->

## Pathogenesis

**What builds up in the tissue?** What thickens the wall of a hyaline arteriole is a mixture: plasma proteins that have built up, and basement membrane material.<!--c:hyaline-change-c006-->
In the diabetic kidney, plasma proteins leak into the walls of the afferent and efferent arterioles, which also become sclerotic; together this is hyaline arteriolosclerosis.<!--c:hyaline-change-c007-->
Light microscopy can pick up Crooke hyalinization because the keratin filament bundles that sit around the nucleus are so numerous.<!--c:hyaline-change-c008-->
Amylin, released by β-cells along with insulin, is the polypeptide that makes up islet amyloid in type 2 diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c009-->

## Gross morphology

**Can the naked eye see it?** The vessel change itself is microscopic, but a kidney with hypertensive nephrosclerosis is smaller than normal and its surface looks fine-grained.<!--c:hyaline-change-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Where do you find hyaline material?**
- In nephrosclerosis the smooth muscle cells in the wall of these arterioles may be absent or pushed toward the outer edge, while hyaline thickens the wall all the way around.<!--c:hyaline-change-c011-->
- A capsular drop is a round lump of leaked protein lying between Bowman capsule and its parietal epithelial cells, a feature of diabetic glomeruli.<!--c:hyaline-change-c012-->
- Hyaline caps are protein deposits lying under the endothelium of glomerular capillary loops.<!--c:hyaline-change-c013-->
- In the liver, Mallory-Denk bodies are hyaline inclusions in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes, made mainly of keratin aggregates.<!--c:hyaline-change-c035-->
- Amyloid laid down in an islet of a patient with type 2 diabetes is described as hyalinization.<!--c:hyaline-change-c014-->
- In corticotroph adenomas, the Crooke change is visible by light microscopy.<!--c:hyaline-change-c015-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the PAS stain shows hyaline sclerosis of arterioles in nephrosclerosis.<!--c:hyaline-change-c016-->
- Lipid debris lies inside the dense clumps that electron microscopy shows for the hyaline protein deposits of diabetic glomeruli.<!--c:hyaline-change-c017-->
- Plasma proteins such as IgG and albumin get trapped in diabetic glomeruli, and immunofluorescence shows this as a diffuse linear signal.<!--c:hyaline-change-c018-->
- Staining for ACTH and keratin is usually found at the cell edge in Crooke adenomas.<!--c:hyaline-change-c019-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does it cause in patients?** The vessels hit hardest by chronic hypertension are the small arteries and arterioles, and the kidney is a particularly affected site.<!--c:hyaline-change-c020-->
In most people, hypertensive nephrosclerosis leaves renal function intact.<!--c:hyaline-change-c021-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

Not applicable.

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** End-stage renal disease can be the outcome in a minority of hypertensive patients, whose kidney failure keeps worsening.<!--c:hyaline-change-c022-->
Papillary necrosis and pyelonephritis are more likely in diabetic kidneys because narrowed vessels reduce the blood reaching the medulla.<!--c:hyaline-change-c023-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What can be mistaken for hyaline change?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[amyloidosis]] | The hyalinized material of a diabetic islet is amyloid made of amylin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c024--> | Identify the deposited protein |
| Arteriosclerosis of small muscular arteries | The internal elastic lamina of small muscular arteries is doubled, and the intima thickens with fibrous and muscular tissue and extra elastin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c025--> Aging produces comparable changes, so this lesion cannot be used to diagnose hypertension.<!--c:hyaline-change-c026--> | Light microscopy of the vessel wall |

## High-yield summary

- **Hyaline arteriolosclerosis** is found in hypertension and diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c027-->
- **Capsular drops** and **hyaline caps** are glomerular lesions of diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c028-->
- **Crooke hyalinization** is a cytoplasmic change of corticotroph adenoma cells.<!--c:hyaline-change-c029-->
- **Islet amyloid** (amylin) is the hyalinized material in type 2 diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c030-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hyaline arteriolosclerosis specific for hypertension?** No. It may also be present in diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c031-->

**Is Crooke hyaline an extracellular deposit?** Not in the first place: the change reflects keratin filaments gathered around the nucleus.<!--c:hyaline-change-c032-->

**Is "hyaline" a diagnosis?** No. In a diabetic islet the hyalinized material is amyloid.<!--c:hyaline-change-c033-->
