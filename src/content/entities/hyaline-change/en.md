## Definition and epidemiology

**What does "hyaline" mean on a slide?** In hyaline arteriolosclerosis the arteriole wall looks glassy under the light microscope.<!--c:hyaline-change-c001-->
The same descriptive word is used for deposits in arterioles, kidney glomeruli, pancreatic islets and pituitary tumor cells.<!--c:hyaline-change-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which conditions go with hyaline change?** Hyaline arteriolosclerosis is a vascular change seen in chronic hypertension.<!--c:hyaline-change-c003-->
It can also be present in diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c004-->
Crooke hyalinization is seen in corticotroph cells whose ACTH release has been shut down by excess cortisol in the blood.<!--c:hyaline-change-c005-->

## Pathogenesis

**What builds up in the tissue?** The thick wall of a hyaline arteriole contains plasma proteins that have collected there, along with material like that of the basement membrane.<!--c:hyaline-change-c006-->
In diabetic kidneys the afferent and efferent arterioles harden and take up leaked plasma proteins in their walls, which gives hyaline arteriolosclerosis.<!--c:hyaline-change-c007-->
Crooke hyalinization comes from bundles of keratin filaments around the nucleus; they can be so many that the light microscope resolves them.<!--c:hyaline-change-c008-->
Islet amyloid in type 2 diabetes is made of amylin, which the β-cells secrete together with insulin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c009-->

## Gross morphology

**Can the naked eye see it?** The vessel change itself is microscopic, but a kidney with hypertensive nephrosclerosis is smaller than normal and its surface looks fine-grained.<!--c:hyaline-change-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Where do you find hyaline material?**
- In kidney arterioles of hypertensive nephrosclerosis, the smooth muscle cells are often lost or pushed outward, and hyaline thickens the whole circumference of the wall.<!--c:hyaline-change-c011-->
- In diabetic glomeruli, capsular drops are round deposits of leaked protein that lie against the parietal lining of Bowman capsule.<!--c:hyaline-change-c012-->
- "Hyaline caps" are subendothelial accumulations along capillary loops.<!--c:hyaline-change-c013-->
- In the liver, Mallory-Denk bodies are hyaline inclusions in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes, made mainly of keratin aggregates.<!--c:hyaline-change-c035-->
- In type 2 diabetes an islet can show amyloid deposition, described as hyalinization.<!--c:hyaline-change-c014-->
- In corticotroph adenomas, the Crooke change is visible by light microscopy.<!--c:hyaline-change-c015-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the PAS stain shows hyaline sclerosis of arterioles in nephrosclerosis.<!--c:hyaline-change-c016-->
- On electron microscopy, the hyaline protein deposits in diabetic glomeruli look like dense clumps with fatty debris inside.<!--c:hyaline-change-c017-->
- On immunofluorescence, diabetic glomeruli show a diffuse, linear pattern from trapped plasma proteins such as IgG and albumin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c018-->
- In Crooke adenomas, stains for ACTH and keratin usually appear at the periphery of the cell.<!--c:hyaline-change-c019-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does it cause in patients?** In long-standing hypertension the vessel damage is mainly in small arteries and arterioles, and the kidney is the organ most affected.<!--c:hyaline-change-c020-->
Most people with hypertensive nephrosclerosis keep their kidney function.<!--c:hyaline-change-c021-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

Not applicable.

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** A minority of hypertensive patients go on to worsening kidney failure, up to end-stage disease.<!--c:hyaline-change-c022-->
In diabetic kidneys, narrowed vessels cut the blood supply to the medulla, which makes papillary necrosis and pyelonephritis more likely.<!--c:hyaline-change-c023-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What can be mistaken for hyaline change?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[amyloidosis]] | The hyalinized material of a diabetic islet is amyloid made of amylin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c024--> | Identify the deposited protein |
| Arteriosclerosis of small muscular arteries | The internal elastic lamina of small muscular arteries is doubled, and the intima thickens with fibrous and muscular tissue and extra elastin.<!--c:hyaline-change-c025--> Similar changes occur with aging, so it is not diagnostic of hypertension.<!--c:hyaline-change-c026--> | Light microscopy of the vessel wall |

## High-yield summary

- **Hyaline arteriolosclerosis** is found in hypertension and diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c027-->
- **Capsular drops** and **hyaline caps** are glomerular lesions of diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c028-->
- **Crooke hyalinization** is a cytoplasmic change of corticotroph adenoma cells.<!--c:hyaline-change-c029-->
- **Islet amyloid** (amylin) is the hyalinized material in type 2 diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c030-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hyaline arteriolosclerosis specific for hypertension?** No. It may also be present in diabetes.<!--c:hyaline-change-c031-->

**Is Crooke hyaline an extracellular deposit?** No. It reflects keratin filaments gathered around the nucleus.<!--c:hyaline-change-c032-->

**Is "hyaline" a diagnosis?** No. In a diabetic islet the hyalinized material is amyloid.<!--c:hyaline-change-c033-->
