## Definition and epidemiology

**Which type of esophageal cancer is the common one, and where?** Among all cancers worldwide, esophageal cancer ranks eighth in frequency.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c001--> Worldwide, squamous cell carcinoma is the leading type of esophageal cancer.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c002--> In Australia, Western Europe and North America the picture reverses: adenocarcinoma clearly outnumbers squamous carcinoma.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c003-->

Squamous carcinoma is strikingly uneven across the globe; South Africa, South America, Iran and China have the highest frequency.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c004--> It is more common in older men.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c005--> Adenocarcinoma also affects men more often than women.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c006--> In the US its incidence has multiplied by seven in 30 years, the steepest rise of any solid tumor.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c007-->

<aside class="edition">Classification, grade and stage cut-offs change between editions: check the current TNM edition before quoting a stage.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes squamous carcinoma?** Environmental exposure is the main suspect: its frequency differs even between populations that are fairly alike.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c008--> Smoking and alcohol lead the list, and together they raise risk more than their separate effects added up.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c009--> Risk is also raised by HPV and large intakes of hot beverages, and by Plummer–Vinson syndrome, achalasia and earlier caustic injury.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c010-->

**And adenocarcinoma?** Barrett esophagus with dysplasia is where most adenocarcinomas start, so the risk factors of Barrett apply to them too.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c011--> The main ones are GERD, obesity, male sex and white race.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c012--> Anything that increases gastric acid or reflux adds risk; examples are scleroderma and Zollinger–Ellison syndrome.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c013-->

## Pathogenesis

**What is the precursor of esophageal adenocarcinoma?** Next to the invasive tumor one often finds Barrett mucosa, in keeping with the sequence Barrett esophagus, dysplasia, adenocarcinoma.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c014--> Reflux is also promoted by medications that relax the lower esophageal sphincter and by dilation or myotomy of it, and these raise adenocarcinoma risk.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does each type sit, and what does it look like?** In squamous carcinoma, the middle and upper thirds are involved in roughly half of cases.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c016--> The distal esophagus and gastroesophageal junction are the usual home of adenocarcinoma, and growth into the upper stomach can follow.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c017-->

Squamous tumors may bulge into the lumen, burrow into the wall, or infiltrate mainly inside the wall.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c018--> Adenocarcinoma may grow as a raised mass, as an ulcer, or as a flat lesion.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c019--> Tumor-free Barrett mucosa often lies around the lesion and may be visible to the naked eye.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the tumor cells look like?** A squamous carcinoma with keratin pearls is well differentiated; when the cells look nothing like squamous cells, it is poorly differentiated.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c021--> Some tumors are mostly made of spindle cells.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c022--> Gland formation in adenocarcinoma is graded: clear glands in well differentiated cases, hardly any in poorly differentiated ones.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c023--> Signet ring cells are seen in some poorly differentiated adenocarcinomas.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c024-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, the two types are separated by keratin pearls (squamous differentiation) versus gland formation (glandular differentiation).<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c025-->
- **How it is studied:** endoscopy combined with ultrasound is the usual way to diagnose and stage adenocarcinoma.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c026-->

This card does not cover special stains, immunohistochemistry or molecular tests such as HER2 and PD-L1.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** Patients most often seek care because of dysphagia (difficulty swallowing).<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c027--> Cachexia may develop.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c028--> Half of patients have painful swallowing (odynophagia).<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c029--> If pain persists, suspect that the tumor has reached the mediastinum or spinal nerves.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c030--> Hoarseness results when the recurrent laryngeal nerve is compressed.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c031-->

Adenocarcinoma presents like squamous carcinoma; the typical patient is an obese white man with a history of GERD.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c032-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Where does it spread?** Most metastases are carried by the abundant lymphatic vessels of the esophagus.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c033--> Cervical, internal jugular and supraclavicular nodes receive metastases from upper-third tumors.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c034--> Paraesophageal, cardiac, aortic, hilar and paratracheal nodes are the targets of middle-third tumors.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c035--> Left gastric, celiac and retroperitoneal nodes receive metastases from lower-third tumors, whose lymphatics run alongside the left gastric artery.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c036--> Liver and lung metastases are common.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c037-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What goes wrong, and what is the outlook?** Most tumors are inoperable once dysphagia has developed.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c038-->
- Obstruction comes early with bulky, polypoid tumors.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c039-->
- Bleeding is likelier with ulcerated tumors.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c040-->
- Infiltrating tumors close in on the lumen from every side, so it narrows gradually.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c041-->
- A tracheoesophageal fistula shows up as a persistent cough.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c042-->
- Serious trouble often follows spread to the mediastinum.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c043-->

**How is it treated?** Endoscopic mucosal resection is an option for early invasive (T1) cancers.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c044--> In advanced disease, a good response to chemotherapy and radiation can lead on to surgery.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c045--> Squamous carcinoma is treated much like adenocarcinoma.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c046-->

| [[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor]] | A leiomyoma is almost always benign and is the commonest esophageal tumor made of spindle cells in the submucosa.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c048--> | Histology of the lesion |

## High-yield summary

- **Squamous cell carcinoma** is the most common type worldwide; **adenocarcinoma** predominates in North America, Western Europe and Australia.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c050-->
- **Smoking and alcohol** act synergistically in squamous carcinoma.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c051-->
- Most adenocarcinomas start from **dysplastic Barrett esophagus**.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c052-->
- Squamous carcinoma involves the **middle and upper thirds** in about half of cases, adenocarcinoma mostly the **distal esophagus or gastroesophageal junction**.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c053-->
- **Lymphatics** carry most metastases, by the third of the esophagus involved.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c054-->
- **Dysphagia** is a late sign: most tumors are already inoperable.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c055-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is adenocarcinoma the common esophageal cancer everywhere?** No. Squamous carcinoma is the most common worldwide; adenocarcinoma leads in the United States and other Western regions.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c056-->

**Does dysphagia mean the cancer is early?** No. It usually appears when the tumor is already inoperable.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c057-->

**Do smoking and alcohol simply add up?** No. Their effect on squamous carcinoma is synergistic.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c058-->

## Mnemonics

- **SHAC** for squamous risks: Smoking and alcohol, Hot drinks, Achalasia, Caustic injury.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c059-->
- **GOB** for adenocarcinoma risks: GERD, Obesity, Barrett esophagus.<!--c:esophageal-carcinoma-c060-->
