## Definition and epidemiology

Hypertrophy is an increase in cell size that, summed over many cells, enlarges the organ.<!--c:hypertrophy-c001--> It is the converse of atrophy, and the bigger tissue usually has a greater working capacity.<!--c:hypertrophy-c002--> Cells hypertrophy when the work asked of them or the trophic signals they receive increase.<!--c:hypertrophy-c003--> In the heart and skeletal muscle, organ growth comes mainly from larger cells; in organs such as the kidney, cell size and cell number can both rise.<!--c:hypertrophy-c004--> Hypertrophy and hyperplasia can therefore occur together.<!--c:hypertrophy-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Physiologic hypertrophy**

- **Increased workload:** the enlarged skeletal muscles of athletes are the textbook example.<!--c:hypertrophy-c006-->
- **Puberty:** the rise in androgens and growth hormone, which acts through IGF-1, builds muscle and bone mass, most markedly in boys.<!--c:hypertrophy-c007-->
- **Hormonal stimulation:** hormone-responsive organs such as the breast and thyroid can enlarge when their stimulating hormones increase.<!--c:hypertrophy-c008-->
- **Athlete's heart:** intensive training produces a large but highly efficient heart.<!--c:hypertrophy-c009-->

**Pathologic (compensatory) hypertrophy of the heart**

- **Pressure overload:** chronic hypertension or a stenotic valve.<!--c:hypertrophy-c010-->
- **Volume overload:** an incompetent (regurgitant) valve.<!--c:hypertrophy-c011-->
- **Myocardial injury** and primary diseases of cardiac myocytes (cardiomyopathies).<!--c:hypertrophy-c012-->
- **Right ventricular overload:** pulmonary hypertension (cor pulmonale) or a failing left ventricle, which shifts extra work onto the right one.<!--c:hypertrophy-c074-->

**Compensatory hypertrophy of a paired organ**

- **Single kidney:** when one kidney never develops (unilateral renal agenesis), the other takes on much of the missing kidney's work by growing larger.<!--c:hypertrophy-c075-->

## Pathogenesis

**Net gain of protein.** In hypertrophy protein synthesis rises and protein breakdown falls, the reverse of atrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c013--> Synthesis is boosted at each level, from transcription of selected genes to translation on ribosomes that become more numerous and more efficient.<!--c:hypertrophy-c014--> The process is selective, not a simple build-up of mass: early on, the cell destroys proteins it no longer needs while making more of those the new task requires.<!--c:hypertrophy-c015-->

**Inputs.** Mechanical load, growth factors, neuroendocrine signals (such as β-adrenergic stimulation), calcium fluxes and small-molecule mediators all feed in, and new vessels form to supply the extra oxygen and nutrients.<!--c:hypertrophy-c016--> The sensor that converts resistance exercise into growth is still unknown; membrane phospholipid products and G protein–coupled receptors are candidates.<!--c:hypertrophy-c017-->

**The Akt–mTOR switch in skeletal muscle.**

- IGF-1 binding its receptor activates IRS-1, then PI3K and Akt, which promote mTORC1 and protein synthesis.<!--c:hypertrophy-c018-->
- In adults, IGF-1 and Akt act mostly as permissive signals that block atrophy (Akt restrains FOXO) rather than as direct growth triggers; Akt can amplify the mTORC1 activity that resistance training induces.<!--c:hypertrophy-c019-->
- FOXO raises E3 ubiquitin ligases and inhibits mTORC1, so silencing FOXO lets synthesis proceed.<!--c:hypertrophy-c020-->
- Brakes on growth: myostatin (TGF-β family) acts through Smads to inhibit Akt, and the E3 ligase Cbl-b marks IRS-1 for destruction.<!--c:hypertrophy-c021-->
- Supporting processes: Akt and PI3K also suppress cell death, the extracellular matrix is remodeled, and satellite cells supply extra myocytes or fuse with fibers to add nuclei.<!--c:hypertrophy-c022-->

**Fiber type decides the response.**

- **Light loads, many repetitions (endurance):** type I fibers adapt through AMPK and p38 MAPK, which raise PGC-1α; downstream, nuclear respiratory factors and TFAM multiply mitochondria, and muscle mass does not change.<!--c:hypertrophy-c023--> Mitochondria damaged by the extra oxidative work are removed by mitophagy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c024-->
- **Heavy loads (resistance):** type II fibers enlarge, and their metabolism shifts toward anaerobic glycolysis.<!--c:hypertrophy-c025-->

**The heart.** Stretched or pressure-loaded myocytes release angiotensin II, endothelin-1, norepinephrine and growth factors such as IGF-1 and TGF-β, which act on the same and neighboring cells.<!--c:hypertrophy-c026--> The IGF-1–PI3K–Akt route drives exercise-induced (physiologic) growth and favors myocyte survival and angiogenesis.<!--c:hypertrophy-c027--> Angiotensin II and endothelin-1 act through Gq-coupled receptors on MAPK (especially JNK and p38) and CaMK pathways, which promote maladaptive growth and apoptosis.<!--c:hypertrophy-c028--> Sustained β-adrenergic stimulation causes hypertrophy and desensitizes the receptors.<!--c:hypertrophy-c029--> Even tissues once considered postmitotic turn over their cells slowly; where progenitor input outpaces cell loss, as in skeletal muscle, the organ can enlarge, whereas cell renewal in the adult heart stays low.<!--c:hypertrophy-c030-->

## Gross morphology

- The organ is heavier and larger.<!--c:hypertrophy-c031-->
- **Physiologic** cardiac hypertrophy: wall thickness and chamber volume grow in proportion.<!--c:hypertrophy-c032-->
- **Concentric** hypertrophy (pressure overload): the left ventricular free wall and septum are uniformly thick, the cavity is not enlarged, and the right ventricle is normal.<!--c:hypertrophy-c033--> In hypertensive heart disease the heart weighs more than 375 g in men and 350 g in women.<!--c:hypertrophy-c034-->
- **Eccentric** hypertrophy: the cavity dilates as hypertrophy decompensates.<!--c:hypertrophy-c035-->
- **Right ventricle** (chronic cor pulmonale): its free wall, normally 0.3–0.5 cm thick, can exceed 1.0 cm, and when the hypertrophy is severe the interventricular septum flattens and may even bulge into the left ventricle.<!--c:hypertrophy-c076-->

## Microscopic morphology

- Hypertrophic cardiac myocytes are wider, with large, dark (hyperchromatic), rectangular "boxcar" nuclei.<!--c:hypertrophy-c036-->
- These nuclei are large and dark because the hypertrophic myocyte copies its DNA, and sometimes splits its nucleus, without dividing; binucleated myocytes therefore become more common in pathologic hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c077-->
- Myocyte shape follows the pattern: wider and longer in physiologic growth, wider only in concentric hypertrophy, longer in eccentric hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c037-->
- Interstitial fibrosis accompanies essentially every case of pathologic cardiac hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c038-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **Gross, on the whole heart:** the heart is weighed and then cut transversely across both ventricles, so that wall thickness can be measured and the cavities checked for dilation.<!--c:hypertrophy-c078--> Normal adult values for comparison: 280–340 g in men and 230–280 g in women, with a left ventricular wall of 1.3–1.5 cm and a right one under 0.5 cm.<!--c:hypertrophy-c079-->
- **H&E:** judge fiber width and nuclear size against normal myocardium at the same magnification.<!--c:hypertrophy-c080-->
- **Trichrome stain:** it colors fibrous tissue blue and myocytes red, and so brings out the interstitial fibrosis of pathologic hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c081-->
- **Molecular tests:** when no load explains the hypertrophy, the sarcomere genes are tested for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; a disease-causing variant, most often in MYH7 or MYBPC3, turns up more often when other relatives are affected, and a negative result does not exclude the disease.<!--c:hypertrophy-c082-->
- **Limits:** a failing heart looks much the same whatever damaged it, unless it carries the marks of a specific disease such as an old infarct or amyloid; hypertensive heart disease and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy are diagnosed only after the other causes of hypertrophy have been ruled out (see Diagnosis and Differential diagnosis).<!--c:hypertrophy-c083-->

**Molecular changes in the hypertrophic myocyte:**

- Under acute pressure overload, myocytes switch on immediate-early genes (c-fos, c-jun) and Hsp70.<!--c:hypertrophy-c039-->
- Pathologic hypertrophy reactivates a **fetal gene program**: ventricles again make atrial natriuretic factor and BNP.<!--c:hypertrophy-c040-->
- Fetal contractile isoforms reappear: hypertrophied atria switch to the slow myosin that ventricles already use, and ventricles re-express fetal actin and tropomyosin; slow myosin generates less force but saves energy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c041-->

## Clinical presentation

- Cardiac hypertrophy is initially compensatory: it lets the ventricle cope with a higher load and brings wall stress back toward normal.<!--c:hypertrophy-c042-->
- Fibrosis stiffens the hypertrophic ventricle, so diastolic dysfunction is the most common functional disorder in hypertensive heart disease and can on its own cause heart failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c043-->
- Higher workload, poor relaxation and the coronary atherosclerosis that accompanies hypertension together raise the risk of ischemia, infarction and heart failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c044-->
- In skeletal muscle, measures that promote hypertrophy can slow or prevent cachexia and age-related muscle loss.<!--c:hypertrophy-c045-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

- Blood BNP is a useful marker of how severe heart failure is.<!--c:hypertrophy-c046-->
- Hypertensive heart disease is diagnosed only when hypertension is the sole explanation for the enlarged heart.<!--c:hypertrophy-c047-->

## Complications and prognosis

- **Limits of adaptation:** beyond a certain size hypertrophy stops compensating, probably because oxygen must diffuse too far from the interstitium to the core of each fiber.<!--c:hypertrophy-c048-->
- Untreated pressure-overload hypertrophy tends to progress to eccentric hypertrophy with heart failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c049-->
- Increased myocyte apoptosis may help tip compensated hypertrophy into failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c050-->
- Myocardial fibrosis disturbs conduction and favors atrial fibrillation and ventricular tachycardia.<!--c:hypertrophy-c051-->
- **Sudden death:** when acute ischemia strikes a hypertrophic ventricle, a lethal ventricular arrhythmia is more likely to start.<!--c:hypertrophy-c084-->
- Heart failure is the main cause of death in untreated hypertension.<!--c:hypertrophy-c052-->
- ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists may limit pathologic hypertrophy; β-blockers lower mortality in advanced heart failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c053-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[hyperplasia]] | More cells rather than bigger cells; the two may coexist in the same organ.<!--c:hypertrophy-c054--> | Cell count versus cell size |
| [[atrophy]] | The opposite change: smaller cells, with breakdown of protein exceeding synthesis.<!--c:hypertrophy-c055--> | Cell size compared with normal tissue |
| Endurance-trained muscle | More mitochondria in type I fibers without an increase in muscle mass.<!--c:hypertrophy-c056--> | Muscle mass and fiber size are unchanged |
| [[hypertensive-heart-disease]] | Concentric left ventricular hypertrophy with no cause other than hypertension; fibrosis and boxcar nuclei, unlike the efficient athlete's heart.<!--c:hypertrophy-c057--> | Blood pressure history; exclusion of other causes |
| Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy | Left ventricular hypertrophy that neither a pressure load nor athletic training explains; the septum is often more than 1.5 times as thick as the free wall (asymmetric hypertrophy), and histology shows myocyte disarray.<!--c:hypertrophy-c085--> | Exclusion of secondary causes; family screening and sarcomere gene testing |

## High-yield summary

- Hypertrophy = increased **cell size** leading to increased **organ size**.<!--c:hypertrophy-c058-->
- Heart and skeletal muscle enlarge mainly by **hypertrophy**, not by adding cells.<!--c:hypertrophy-c059-->
- Mechanism: protein **synthesis exceeds degradation**, with **selective** removal of unneeded proteins.<!--c:hypertrophy-c060-->
- **IGF-1 → PI3K → Akt → mTORC1** promotes growth; **FOXO**, **myostatin** and **Cbl-b** oppose it.<!--c:hypertrophy-c061-->
- **Endurance** → type I fibers gain **mitochondria** (PGC-1α), no mass gain; **resistance** → **type II fibers** hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertrophy-c062-->
- **Pressure overload** → **concentric** LVH; decompensation → **eccentric** hypertrophy and failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c063-->
- Pathologic cardiac hypertrophy: **boxcar nuclei**, **interstitial fibrosis**, **fetal gene program** (ANF, BNP).<!--c:hypertrophy-c064-->
- The **limit** of useful hypertrophy is set by **oxygen diffusion** distance.<!--c:hypertrophy-c065-->
- **Cor pulmonale** = **right ventricular** hypertrophy from **pulmonary hypertension**; a failing left ventricle can also overload the right.<!--c:hypertrophy-c086-->
- **Unexplained** left ventricular hypertrophy (no load, no training) → think of **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy**: asymmetric septum, **myocyte disarray**.<!--c:hypertrophy-c087-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- **Bigger is not always better:** a diseased heart can weigh as much as an athlete's heart yet have a damaged structure and work poorly.<!--c:hypertrophy-c066-->
- Endurance training does not enlarge muscle fibers; it multiplies mitochondria.<!--c:hypertrophy-c067-->
- Overeating does not enlarge muscle: extra calories build fat, not muscle, whereas starvation shrinks both.<!--c:hypertrophy-c068-->
- After puberty, normal levels of androgens and IGF-1 do little to drive muscle hypertrophy directly.<!--c:hypertrophy-c069-->
- Concentric hypertrophy has a normal or small cavity; a dilated cavity signals eccentric hypertrophy, often with failure.<!--c:hypertrophy-c070-->
- Fetal isoform re-expression is not purely harmful: slow myosin reduces contractility but improves energy efficiency.<!--c:hypertrophy-c071-->
- In "benign prostatic hypertrophy", a traditional name still in use, the gland actually enlarges by [[hyperplasia|hyperplasia]]: its glands and stroma proliferate.<!--c:hypertrophy-c088-->

## Mnemonics

**"Concentric = Cramped cavity, Eccentric = Expanded cavity"** (original): concentric hypertrophy thickens the wall around an unchanged cavity; eccentric hypertrophy comes with a dilated one.<!--c:hypertrophy-c072-->

**"TROPHY is for size, PLASIA is for plenty"** (original): hypertrophy makes cells bigger, hyperplasia makes more of them.<!--c:hypertrophy-c073-->
