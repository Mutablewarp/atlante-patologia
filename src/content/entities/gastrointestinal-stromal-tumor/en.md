## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a GIST and where does it come from?** A gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST) derives from the interstitial cells of Cajal.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c001--> These pacemaker cells sit in the muscularis propria.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c002--> They link the enteric nerves to the gut muscle, which keeps peristalsis coordinated.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c003--> GISTs can occur anywhere along the gastrointestinal tract.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c004--> The stomach is the commonest site, followed by the small bowel.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c005--> Among mesenchymal tumors of the small intestine, GIST ranks first.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c006--> A GIST is a much rarer finding in the colon than a benign lipoma or leiomyoma.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which genes are mutated?** In most GISTs the key mutation is in *KIT*.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c008--> *PDGFRA* mutations are linked to a minority.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c009--> Mutations of the succinate dehydrogenase gene complex are linked to another minority.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**What do the mutated genes do?** *KIT* encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c011--> *PDGFRA* also encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c012-->

## Gross morphology

**What does a GIST look like to the naked eye?** A GIST is usually a large, bulky mass.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c013--> Its origin is the muscularis propria, which lies deep to the mucosa.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c014--> The mucosa over a GIST may ulcerate, and the ulcer sits centrally.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c015-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the tumor cells look like?** The tumor grows as a solid mass of neoplastic cells.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c016--> The cells are spindled or epithelioid.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c017--> Spindle cells usually make up small intestinal GISTs.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c018-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- **Immunohistochemistry:** Most GISTs are CD117-positive on immunohistochemistry; this antibody recognizes the KIT protein.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c019-->
- **Molecular:** the mutation found (*KIT*, *PDGFRA* or succinate dehydrogenase genes) decides which drug is chosen.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c020-->
- The pages used for this card do not describe specimen type, sampling, special stains, electron microscopy or DOG1.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a GIST present?** A GIST can first show itself through bleeding from the ulcerated mucosa above it.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c021--> In the small intestine this bleeding can be severe.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c022-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** The pages used for this card give no imaging or laboratory criteria. The tissue findings are in the microscopic and special-stain sections above.

## Complications and prognosis

**What predicts behavior, and how is a GIST treated?** A GIST's likely behavior is judged mainly from where it arises, how large it is and how many mitoses it shows.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c023--> Aggressive behavior is more probable in GISTs of the small intestine than in gastric GISTs.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c024--> Managing a GIST involves both resection of the tumor and drug therapy.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c025--> Imatinib blocks the KIT kinase, so it fits the majority of GISTs, which carry KIT mutations.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c026--> BCR-ABL1, the fusion protein of chronic myelogenous leukemia, is also an imatinib target.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c027-->

## High-yield summary

- GIST derives from the **interstitial cells of Cajal**, the pacemaker cells of the gut.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c031-->
- Most GISTs are driven by **KIT** mutations.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c032-->
- Most GISTs stain positive for **CD117**.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c033-->
- Fewer GISTs involve **PDGFRA** or succinate dehydrogenase genes instead.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c034-->
- The commonest site is the **stomach**, then the small bowel.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c035-->
- Behavior is predicted mainly by **site, size and mitotic count**.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c036-->
- **Imatinib** inhibits the kinase of KIT-driven GISTs.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c037-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a GIST a smooth muscle tumor?** No. It derives from the interstitial cells of Cajal, the pacemaker cells of the muscularis propria.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c038--> Colonic leiomyomas come from a different layer, the muscularis mucosae.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c039-->

**Does every GIST carry a KIT mutation?** No. Fewer GISTs involve *PDGFRA* or succinate dehydrogenase genes instead.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c040-->

**Is imatinib only a leukemia drug?** No. It is used in GIST to inhibit the kinase.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c041--> It is also used in chronic myelogenous leukemia, against BCR-ABL1.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c042-->

**Is size alone enough to judge risk?** No. Tumor site and mitotic count also predict behavior.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c043-->

## Mnemonics

**CAJAL-KIT:** Cajal cell origin, KIT mutation, CD117 positive, imatinib treatment.<!--c:gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor-c044-->
