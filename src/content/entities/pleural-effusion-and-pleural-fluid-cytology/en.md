## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a pleural effusion?** It is an excess of fluid in the pleural cavity.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c001--> Pleuritis, inflammation of the pleura, often comes together with an effusion.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c002--> Pneumothorax is a different problem: air, not fluid, in the pleural cavity.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which diseases lead to an effusion?**

- Heart failure and other edema states, systemic or pulmonary, raise capillary pressure and can cause hydrothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c004-->
- Severe starvation, cirrhosis and nephrotic syndrome lower the serum oncotic pressure, and hydrothorax can follow.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c005-->
- Asbestos exposure can also cause hydrothorax, as can connective-tissue diseases such as lupus and rheumatoid arthritis.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c006-->
- Pneumococcal pneumonia is the classic source of pyothorax: a bacterial lung infection that reaches the pleura is its usual route.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c007-->
- Occasionally, a penetrating wound carries pus-forming bacteria into the pleura.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c008-->
- Pyothorax seldom complicates a medical procedure on the pleura.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c009-->
- Chest trauma, or a dissecting aortic aneurysm that bursts, fills the pleura with blood: hemothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c010-->
- Pulmonary infarction, pleural cancer and tuberculosis can all tinge an effusion with blood.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c011-->
- A mediastinal tumor such as lymphoma is a rare cause of chylothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c012-->
- Tropical nematode infestation can also produce chylothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c013-->
- Pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM) is another setting for chylothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c014-->

**What causes pleuritis?** Viral or bacterial infection, local or spreading from the lung, can cause pleuritis, as can collagen vascular disease or a peripheral lung infarct.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c015-->

**And pneumothorax?**

- Pneumothorax can be traumatic: a stabbing or a fractured rib may perforate the pleura.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c016-->
- Medical procedures cause most traumatic pneumothoraces: draining pleural fluid, lung biopsy or ventilation under pressure.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c017-->
- Spontaneous pneumothorax typically affects young adults.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c018-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does pleural fluid normally move?** The visceral pleura takes up again what the parietal pleura secretes.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c019--> A small amount of pleural fluid normally lets the lung slide smoothly against the chest wall.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c020-->

**What upsets that balance?**

- Hydrothorax is the same process as edema, but in the pleural space.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c021-->
- Chylothorax results from obstruction of lymphatic drainage.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c022-->

## Gross morphology

**How big can an effusion be, and how does the fluid look?** Effusions can be huge, or as small as a few milliliters.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c023--> A very large effusion pushes the mediastinum, trachea included, over to the contralateral side.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c024-->

- Hydrothorax fluid looks like water.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c025-->
- Pyothorax fluid is turbid.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c026-->
- Fibrosis and loculation are common in empyema, where the pleural pus is thick.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c027-->
- Chyle, the fluid of chylothorax, is lipid-rich and milky in appearance.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c028-->
- Hemothorax is blood in the pleural cavity.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c029-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is in the fluid under the microscope?** Polymorphonuclear leukocytes fill the fluid of pyothorax.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c030--> Cytology of reactive mesothelium, metastatic carcinoma and mesothelioma is not covered in this card yet.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient feel?** Pleuritis typically hurts: the chest pain is sharp and comes on when the patient breathes in.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c031-->

**What does a spontaneous pneumothorax look like at the bedside?** For example, a young adult may suddenly feel chest pain and breathlessness while exercising hard.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c032-->

**What are tension pneumothorax and bronchopleural fistula?**

- In tension pneumothorax, the opposite lung is compressed and the mediastinum shifts away, because the air collection on one side is so large.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c033-->
- In a bronchopleural fistula, an airway opens freely into the pleural space.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c034-->
- Most often a biopsy or an operation breaks the bronchial wall, so the fistula is iatrogenic.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c035-->
- When tissue-destroying lung infection causes the fistula, the infection matters most.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c036-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it found?** A tiny effusion may be seen only on imaging, where the costophrenic angle looks blunted.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c037--> On chest X-ray, the painful side shows a collapsed lung surrounded by air in the pleural space.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c038--> Fluid analysis, including transudate versus exudate, is not covered in this card yet.

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?**

- Chylothorax is ominous because lymphatic obstruction hints at diseased posterior mediastinal nodes.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c039-->
- Immediate drainage is needed for tension pneumothorax, which can threaten life.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c040-->
- Some spontaneous pneumothoraces need the air drawn off, but most clear without help.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c041-->
- A bronchopleural fistula is a grave problem.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c042-->

## High-yield summary

- **Hydrothorax** is the watery effusion of pleural edema.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c047-->
- **Pyothorax** is the neutrophil-filled effusion of pleural infection, classically after pneumococcal pneumonia.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c048-->
- **Empyema** is pyothorax with thick pus; the pleura often scars and divides into pockets.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c049-->
- A ruptured vessel, for instance in a dissecting aortic aneurysm, or trauma leads to **hemothorax**.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c050-->
- **Chylothorax** points to lymphatic obstruction, for example mediastinal lymphoma.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c051-->
- **Pleuritis** typically hurts sharply on inspiration.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c052-->
- **Tension pneumothorax** shifts the mediastinum and needs immediate drainage.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c053-->
- Ruptured subpleural emphysema blebs usually cause **spontaneous pneumothorax**.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c054-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does an effusion always mean pleural disease?** No. Heart failure, nephrotic syndrome or cirrhosis can produce hydrothorax through pressure changes alone.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c055-->

**Is empyema the same as pyothorax?** No. Empyema is pyothorax with thick pus; the pleura often scars and divides into pockets.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c056-->

**Is every blood-stained effusion a hemothorax?** No. Infarction, pleural cancer and tuberculosis can give a blood-tinged effusion, but hemothorax means frank blood from trauma or a ruptured vessel.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c057-->

## Mnemonics

**Water, pus, blood, milk.** Hydrothorax is watery, pyothorax is pus, hemothorax is blood and chylothorax is milk-white.<!--c:pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology-c058-->
