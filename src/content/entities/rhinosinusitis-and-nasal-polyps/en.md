## Definition and epidemiology

**What are rhinitis, sinusitis and nasal polyps?** Rhinitis is inflammation of the lining of the nasal cavity and sinuses.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c001--> Sinusitis, an inflamed sinus lining, is usually the result of a bacterial infection.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c002--> Inflammatory polyps are swellings of the nose and sinuses that are not tumors.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes each of these conditions?**
- Viruses, typically the common cold, lead the causes of acute rhinitis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c004-->
- Seasonal or perennial allergic rhinitis arises from sensitization to an environmental allergen.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c005-->
- Chronic rhinitis can develop in people who have had acute rhinitis again and again.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c006-->
- A sinus whose drainage or ventilation is obstructed becomes prone to infection.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c007-->
- Acute sinusitis lasts less than 3 weeks.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c008-->
- Most cases begin with infection that extends from the nasal mucosa.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c009-->
- The usual culprits are Haemophilus influenzae and Branhamella catarrhalis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c010-->
- A dental infection at the first or second molar can spread into the maxillary sinus through a thin bone plate.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c011-->
- Chronic sinusitis can develop from repeated acute sinusitis or an infection that never fully clears.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c012-->
- Anaerobic bacteria are almost always present in the pus of chronic sinusitis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c013-->
- Allergy and cystic fibrosis are among the many causes of inflammatory polyps.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c014-->
- Fungal disease of the nose and sinuses mostly strikes opportunistically, when immunity is low.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c015-->
- Invasive fungal sinusitis is usually seen in immunocompromised patients.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c016-->
- Diabetic and immunosuppressed patients are most at risk from mucormycosis, which can be fatal.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c017-->

## Pathogenesis

**What happens in the mucosa?**
- Viral rhinitis sheds damaged epithelial cells after the virus replicates in them.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c018-->
- Neutrophils and mononuclear cells infiltrate the edematous, congested mucosa.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c019-->
- Leaky vessels and heavy mucus output produce the thin watery discharge called rhinorrhea.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c020-->
- Upper-airway bacteria can superinfect days into a cold, making the discharge mucopurulent.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c021-->
- Epithelial regrowth is fast after the inflammation ends.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c022-->
- Nasal mast cells carry IgE on specific receptors, and plasma cells in the mucosa make that IgE.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c023-->
- Mast cells empty their mediator granules when their bound IgE meets an inhaled allergen such as pollen.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c024-->
- Histamine acts at once; slower mediators such as leukotrienes sustain the reaction.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c025-->
- Many symptoms are mediated through the H1 receptor, where histamine acts.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c026-->
- Mucus and pus stagnate when a sinus opening is blocked.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c027-->
- In allergic fungal sinusitis the immune system overreacts to fungal antigens.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c028-->
- Packed hyphae in a poorly draining sinus form a fungus ball, in hosts with normal immunity.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c029-->
- Rhinocerebral aspergillosis reaches the meninges and brain after the organisms get through the venous sinuses.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c030-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lesions look like?**
- The ethmoid recess and the lateral nasal wall are the usual origins of inflammatory polyps.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c031-->
- They can be single or multiple, on one side or both.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c032-->
- Mucus that accumulates in a sinus forms a mucocele, and infection of it produces a pyocele.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c033-->
- Pus in a sinus is called an empyema.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c034-->
- Mucoceles grow slowly, usually in the anterior frontal and ethmoid cells, and the expanding mass erodes bone.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c035-->
- Saddle nose is the end stage of granulomatosis with polyangiitis, after the septum perforates, the mucosa ulcerates and the sinuses are destroyed.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c036-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen under the microscope?**
- The inferior turbinates are where mucosal edema is most marked in allergic rhinitis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c037-->
- Eosinophils can be numerous, in nasal secretions or in the mucosa itself.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c038-->
- In chronic rhinitis the mucosa is thickened.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c039-->
- Lymphocytes and plasma cells infiltrate; mucous glands enlarge.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c040-->
- A polyp's loose mucoid stroma holds mucous glands under a respiratory epithelial cover.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c041-->
- Eosinophils and other chronic inflammatory cells infiltrate the stroma.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c042-->
- In granulomatosis with polyangiitis, the nasal tissue has vasculitis and ischemic-type necrosis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c043-->
- Microabscesses and a few multinucleated giant cells lie in a mixed infiltrate.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c044-->
- No well-formed granulomas are present.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c045-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- On routine microscopy, a polyp has respiratory epithelium over a loose stroma with mucous glands and eosinophils.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c046-->
- On microscopy, Aspergillus in the nose may show fruiting bodies.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c047-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient notice?**
- Patients with viral rhinitis have a stuffy nose because the mucosa swells.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c048-->
- In allergic rhinitis, persistent edema in the late phase is felt as nasal obstruction.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c049-->
- Polyps may cause headaches, a runny nose and blockage.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c050-->
- Atopic children and young adults are the typical patients with allergic fungal sinusitis, although any age can be affected.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c051-->
- The maxillary and ethmoid sinuses lead the list, though any sinus may be affected.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c052-->
- A fungus ball causes nasal obstruction.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c053-->
- A fungus ball may also erode bone and cause eye symptoms.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c054-->
- Granulomatosis with polyangiitis may also make patients feel ill, with fever and weight loss.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c055-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What supports the diagnosis?** Active granulomatosis with polyangiitis is accompanied by raised serum levels of ANCA and proteinase 3 (PR3).<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c056-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?**
- Sinus infection can extend to the sinus wall, the orbit, the veins and the cranial cavity.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c057-->
- A big frontal or anterior ethmoid mucocele can press on the orbit and occasionally erode bone to reach the CNS.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c058-->
- Orbital cellulitis or abscess can follow when sinus-wall osteomyelitis spreads along Volkmann canals to the orbital periosteum.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c059-->
- Septic thrombophlebitis reaching the cavernous sinus is potentially life-threatening.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c060-->
- Intracranial spread can produce abscesses or purulent meningitis without extensive bone destruction.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c061-->
- Few patients with rhinocerebral aspergillosis survive.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c062-->
- Brain, orbit, bone and skin can all be invaded by mucormycosis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c063-->

## High-yield summary

- **Viral rhinitis**: neutrophils and mononuclear cells, watery **rhinorrhea**, later mucopurulent if secondarily infected.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c068-->
- **Allergic rhinitis**: **IgE on mast cells**, **histamine**, many **eosinophils**, late-phase edema.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c069-->
- **Inflammatory polyp**: respiratory epithelium over a loose stroma that contains **eosinophils**, not a tumor.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c070-->
- **Sinusitis**: a blocked **ostium** predisposes; **Haemophilus influenzae** is common in acute disease, **anaerobes** are nearly always present in chronic disease.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c071-->
- Complications: intracranial abscess, **cavernous sinus** thrombophlebitis, **osteomyelitis**, **mucocele**.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c072-->
- Fungi: **allergic** (atopic host), **fungus ball** (normal host), **invasive** (immunocompromised host).<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c073-->
- **Granulomatosis with polyangiitis**: **saddle nose**, necrosis and vasculitis, raised **ANCA/PR3**.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c074-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a nasal polyp a tumor?** No. An inflammatory polyp is a nonneoplastic swelling.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c075-->

**Does a nasal biopsy in granulomatosis with polyangiitis show granulomas?** Not well-formed ones; expect giant cells, vasculitis and necrosis.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c076-->

**Is allergic fungal sinusitis an invasive infection?** No. Like the fungus ball it is noninvasive, and it is a hypersensitivity reaction.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c077-->

**Must bone be destroyed for sinusitis to reach the brain?** No. Spread may occur through lymphatics and veins.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c078-->

## Mnemonics

**ABI** (original), the three fungal forms by host: **A**llergic in the atopic young, **B**all in the healthy host, **I**nvasive in the immunocompromised.<!--c:rhinosinusitis-and-nasal-polyps-c079-->
