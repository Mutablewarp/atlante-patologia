## Definition and epidemiology

**What is primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)?** In PBC, the bile ducts inside the liver are gradually destroyed over many years by an immune attack.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c001--> The liver is injured by cholestasis, which arises because bile secretion fails once the ducts are lost.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c002--> The typical patient is a woman between 30 and 65 years old, and women make up 90% to 95% of cases.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c003--> Cirrhosis appears only at the last of four stages.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What predisposes to PBC?** Relatives of patients get PBC much more often than the general population, hinting at a genetic predisposition even though cases are sporadic.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c005--> Most patients also carry another autoimmune condition, for example chronic thyroiditis or Sjögren syndrome.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c006--> Carriers of DRB1*11 or DRB1*13 get PBC less often, whereas certain other HLA-DRB1 alleles are associated with it.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c007--> Women are affected far more often, and X monosomy found more often in them may partly explain why.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do the bile ducts get destroyed?** Serum immunoglobulins are raised, IgM above all, and both humoral and cellular immunity are abnormal.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c009--> Antimitochondrial antibodies (AMA) circulate in more than 95% of patients.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c010--> They recognize the mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase complex.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c011--> AMA are a marker, not a cause: nothing links them to starting or worsening PBC, and mitochondria work normally despite them.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c012--> Antithyroid and antinuclear autoantibodies, among others, may also circulate.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c013--> Complement stays continuously active.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c014--> CD8+ lymphocytes are suspected of destroying the ducts, as they form most of the cells gathered at the damage.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the liver look like at the end stage?** The end-stage liver is cirrhotic and nodular, and bile colors it dark green.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c016--> Chronic cholestasis puts a "halo" around each nodule, and the bridging fibrous septa give the parenchyma a "jigsaw puzzle" look.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes at each of the four stages?**
- **Stage 1, portal stage (florid duct lesion):** small and mid-sized bile ducts inside the liver are destroyed by chronic inflammation.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c018--> In a tissue section the lesion looks focal, because the injury of the ducts is segmental.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c019--> The infiltrate around the duct includes lymphocytes and breaks its basement membrane.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c020--> Small papillae sometimes grow inward from the layered, overgrown lining of the affected duct.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c021--> A duct may be pressed on by epithelioid granulomas, which are frequent in portal tracts.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c022-->
- **Stage 2, periportal stage:** inflammation is milder than in stage 1; at the edge of the portal tracts ductules proliferate while the number of ducts falls.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c023-->
- **Stage 3, septal stage:** medium-sized ducts are commonly scarred, and small ducts have nearly all disappeared (ductopenia).<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c024--> A few lobules are already ringed by fibrous bands that extend outward from the portal tracts.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c025-->
- **Stage 4, cirrhosis:** the septa and nodules show only slight inflammation; small ducts are scarce and medium-sized ones reduced.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c026-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** the specimen is a liver biopsy, read for the typical histology.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c027-->
- Looking for AMA in the serum is a routine step in diagnosing PBC.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c028-->
- Special stains, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy and molecular tests are not covered in this card.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient notice, and what do the labs show?** Many patients have no symptoms in early disease; when symptoms start, fatigue and itching are the commonest.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c029--> In the early phase, alkaline phosphatase is typically raised, whereas bilirubin is normal or barely above normal.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c030--> Most patients see bilirubin creep upward as the disease progresses.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c031--> Transaminases (AST, ALT) rise only moderately.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c032--> Lipoprotein-X, an abnormal lipoprotein, appears in the blood, and cholesterol climbs steeply.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c033--> Xanthomas are localized lesions beneath the skin made of macrophages that have taken up cholesterol.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c034--> Stools become fatty (steatorrhea) when too little bile reaches the intestine and fat goes unabsorbed.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c035--> Poor absorption of vitamin D and calcium can soften or thin the bones (osteomalacia, osteoporosis).<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c036-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**When is PBC confirmed?** With an AMA titer of 1:40 or higher, either 6 months of raised alkaline phosphatase or typical biopsy histology completes the diagnosis.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c037--> When AMA are absent (a rare situation), the elevated alkaline phosphatase and the biopsy findings must carry the diagnosis.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c038-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does PBC evolve, and how is it treated?** The disease usually moves slowly and can last 20 to 30 years.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c039--> The outcome varies: some patients progress to advanced cirrhosis, while others stay symptom-free and have an excellent prognosis.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c040--> Once cirrhosis is established, death results from portal hypertension and its complications, or from liver failure.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c041--> Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) is given to improve the flow of bile through the liver.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c042--> Patients on UDCA live longer before needing a transplant, although the underlying cause is not treated.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c043--> Liver transplantation is a very effective option once PBC reaches its end stage.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c044-->

| [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|Primary sclerosing cholangitis]] | Ulcerative colitis is common; mostly men; periductal "onion skin" fibrosis narrows intra- and extrahepatic ducts.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c045--> | On cholangiography PSC gives the bile ducts a beaded look, because the narrowing is patchy.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c046--> |

## High-yield summary

- **AMA** are found in nearly all patients but are a marker, not the cause.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c047-->
- The **florid duct lesion** of stage 1 is patchy and hits small and medium intrahepatic ducts.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c048-->
- **Ductopenia** is established by stage 3, when nearly all small ducts are gone.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c049-->
- Diagnosis follows a **two-of-three** rule that mixes a blood antibody, a liver enzyme and the biopsy.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c050-->
- **UDCA** improves transplant-free survival but does not remove the cause.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c051-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do AMA destroy the bile ducts?** No. They do not affect mitochondrial function, and CD8+ T cells are the likely mediators.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c052-->

**Is PBC cirrhosis from the start?** No. Cirrhosis is stage 4 of four.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c053-->

**Does a negative AMA exclude PBC?** No. AMA-negative PBC is diagnosed from alkaline phosphatase and histology.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c054-->

**Does UDCA cure PBC?** No. It improves bile transport and survival without treating the cause.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c055-->

**Is the florid duct lesion seen in every section?** No. The injury is segmental, so a section may miss it.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c056-->

## Mnemonics

**"For Patients, Stop Cholestasis":** Florid duct lesion, Periportal, Septal, Cirrhosis are the four stages in order.<!--c:primary-biliary-cholangitis-c057-->
