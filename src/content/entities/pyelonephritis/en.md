## Definition and epidemiology

**What is pyelonephritis?** It is a bacterial infection of the kidney that involves the pelvis, the calyces and the parenchyma.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c001--> The acute form is a neutrophilic inflammation, while the chronic form leaves atrophy, scarring and dilated calyces.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c002--> Women are affected more often, since the bladder infection that usually comes first is commoner in them.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it, and who is at risk?** Most acute pyelonephritis is caused by *Escherichia coli*, a gram-negative organism from the bowel.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c004--> Uropathogenic *E. coli* bind urothelial and kidney epithelial cells with pilus adhesins, which PAP genes encode.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c005--> A short urethra and the lack of antibacterial prostatic secretions favor bladder infection in women.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c006--> Sexual intercourse can move bacteria toward the bladder.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c007--> In pregnancy, progesterone slackens the bladder wall so that more urine stays behind, and asymptomatic bacteriuria is common.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c008--> An atonic bladder from nerve damage (paraplegia, diabetic neuropathy), or a prostate blocking outflow, leaves residual urine.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c009--> Glucose in the urine of diabetic patients feeds bacterial growth.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c010--> Blood-borne seeding of the kidney is seen with *Aspergillus* in immunosuppressed hosts, with miliary tuberculosis, and with staphylococci shed from an infected valve.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c011--> Obstruction, reflux, or both keep infection recurring or persisting, and this produces chronic pyelonephritis.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do bacteria climb up to the kidney?** Bacteria from an infected bladder must be pushed up the ureter to the pelvis and calyces, then enter the parenchyma through the papillae.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c013--> The ureter runs obliquely through the bladder wall, so when the bladder contracts it is squeezed shut and urine stays out.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c014--> Urine is driven up the ureter at voiding when, from birth, the ureter enters the bladder almost at a right angle after a short intramural run.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c015--> Refluxed urine enters the kidney more easily through concave compound papillae, mostly at the poles, than through the convex simple papillae of the central calyces.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c016--> In obstruction, sustained high pressure eventually lets urine into simple papillae too.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c017--> Once in the collecting ducts, bacteria spread into the interstitium and the tubules.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c018--> The medulla, and the cortex over it, slowly atrophy and scar from repeated inflammation in reflux or obstruction.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c019--> Experts disagree on whether sterile reflux alone can lead to chronic pyelonephritis.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c020-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the kidney look like?**
- Acute: the kidney is swollen.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c021-->
- The site of abscesses shows the route of infection: cortical if blood-borne, medullary if ascending.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c022-->
- Pus coats the urothelium of the pelvis and calyces, which may also be congested.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c023-->
- Much of the kidney can look normal, since the disease is often patchy.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c024-->
- Necrotic papillae look yellowish; they are seen when obstruction has widened the pelvis and calyces.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c025-->
- In reflux, each polar calyx widens under a sharply bounded, rough scar that dents the surface.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c026-->
- Chronic, with obstruction: the whole collecting system is dilated, and the parenchyma is thinned to the same degree everywhere.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c027-->
- Dilated calyces with scarring above them mean chronic pyelonephritis or analgesic nephropathy, since no other disease gives both.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c028-->
- Xanthogranulomatous form: yellow nodular lesions, usually in one kidney, that can look like a mass.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c029-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen under the microscope?** Acute disease is recognized by tubules, above all collecting ducts, plugged with neutrophils; more neutrophils lie in the interstitium.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c030--> Vessels and glomeruli often survive, while the cortex above all shows patchy, severe inflammatory destruction.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c031--> Chronic histology is nonspecific: many diseases produce the same chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis picture.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c032--> Scars are fibrous and inflamed tissue around shrunken, widened tubules.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c033--> **Thyroidization:** atrophic tubules fragment into small round segments whose pink glassy casts make them look like thyroid follicles; it is typical but not specific.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c034--> Glomeruli range from normal to scarred, and some are encircled by fibrosis.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c035--> Arterial walls are often fibrotic, and the lining of the calyces is badly scarred and inflamed.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c036--> In the xanthogranulomatous form, lipid-laden foamy macrophages (xanthoma cells) make the lesions yellow.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c037-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E in acute disease, look for neutrophils packing the collecting ducts and tubules.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c038-->
- On H&E in chronic disease, look for thyroidized tubules, interstitial fibrosis and chronic inflammation.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c039-->
- The pages give no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test for this entity, and say nothing about sampling.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Acute pyelonephritis causes fever with chills, flank pain and tenderness over the costovertebral angle.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c040--> Neutrophilia in the blood is common.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c041--> Fever and flank pain return in repeated attacks, which in most patients look like a urinary infection or acute pyelonephritis.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c042--> Some patients have no symptoms until end-stage renal disease.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c043-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests help?** Leukocyte casts in the urine point to the kidney, since symptoms alone seldom tell an upper from a lower urinary infection.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c044--> Imaging in chronic disease reveals dilated calyces with cortical scars, and urinalysis finds leukocytes.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c045--> A xanthogranulomatous lesion can be mistaken for renal cell carcinoma.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c046-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** Necrosis of the papillary tips is a possible complication of severe acute pyelonephritis.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c047--> A perinephric abscess forms if the infection spreads past the renal capsule.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c048--> Once most working nephrons are lost, secondary focal segmental glomerulosclerosis may develop.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c049--> Silent progression can end in end-stage renal disease.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c050-->

## High-yield summary

- **E. coli** causes most acute pyelonephritis, by **ascending** infection from the bladder.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c056-->
- **Vesicoureteral reflux** and **obstruction** are the two routes to chronic disease.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c057-->
- Acute disease: **neutrophils in collecting ducts**, **abscesses**, and sometimes **papillary necrosis**.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c058-->
- Chronic disease with reflux: **coarse polar scars** over dilated calyces.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c059-->
- Chronic histology is nonspecific, but **thyroidization** is characteristic.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c060-->
- **Xanthogranulomatous** pyelonephritis: foamy macrophages, yellow mass, mimics **renal cell carcinoma**.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c061-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do bacteria in the bladder normally reach the kidney?** No. The oblique intramural ureter closes during voiding, so reflux needs an anatomic defect such as a short intramural ureter.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c062-->

**Does every case start from below?** No. Blood-borne infection exists and tends to form abscesses in the cortex.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c063-->

**Is the histology of chronic pyelonephritis diagnostic?** No. The gross appearance is the distinctive part.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c064-->

**Is thyroidization a thyroid disease?** No. It describes atrophic kidney tubules whose casts look like thyroid colloid.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c065-->

**Is xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis a tumor?** No. It is a chronic infection that can present as a mass.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c066-->

## Mnemonics

**BURP:** **B**ladder infection, **U**reteral **R**eflux, **P**apillary entry, the three steps of ascending infection.<!--c:pyelonephritis-c067-->
