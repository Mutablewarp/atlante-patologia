## Definition and epidemiology

**What is reflux esophagitis?** Stomach contents flowing back up injure the esophageal lining; the condition is also called gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD).<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c001--> No other form of esophagitis is seen nearly as often.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c002--> GERD affects patients of every age.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c003--> Some patients have erosions and some do not, and in others [[barrett-esophagus|Barrett esophagus]] complicates the picture.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Why does stomach content reach the esophagus?** Reflux is held back mainly by the lower esophageal sphincter.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c005--> After a meal, a few reflux episodes are normal.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c006--> Repeated, long-lasting episodes are what turn reflux into esophagitis.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c007--> Cigarettes and fatty food weaken the sphincter, which makes reflux more likely.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c008--> Pregnancy and a nasogastric tube can also lead to reflux.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c009-->

**How are hiatal hernia and reflux related?** In a sliding hiatal hernia, a widened hiatus lets the upper stomach slip into the chest.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c010--> A faulty sphincter with no anatomic lesion can cause reflux esophagitis by itself, though the disease is often found together with a sliding hernia.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c011--> Which comes first, the hernia or the reflux, is not settled.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c012--> Most hiatal hernias are the sliding type (85%), and they usually cause no symptoms.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c013--> Reflux can lessen in paraesophageal hernia, because the herniated fundus squeezes the esophagus.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c014--> In [[systemic-sclerosis|systemic sclerosis]], the lower sphincter and esophageal muscle work poorly, and patients have heartburn from acid reflux.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c015-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does reflux injure the mucosa?** Acid harms the lining, and adding pepsin makes the damage worse.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c016--> Duodenal bile, often present in refluxed fluid, worsens the damage.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c017--> Direct injury also comes from alcohol and hot drinks.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c018--> Submucosal glands secrete an alkaline fluid that shields the mucosa in part.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c019-->

**What happens when injury continues?** The epithelium becomes hyperplastic.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c020--> Ulcers that last and reach below the lamina propria can leave a scar-narrowed lumen, a stricture.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c021--> In Barrett esophagus, a result of long-standing GERD, goblet-cell columnar epithelium takes the place of the squamous lining.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c022-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the lining look like?** Hyperemia, seen as redness, comes before any other macroscopic change.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c023--> Shallow erosions and ulcers often run as vertical streaks.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c024--> Barrett mucosa lining the lower esophagus has a salmon-pink color on endoscopy.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c025-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Hydropic change, a swelling of the squamous cells, marks mild injury.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c026--> Basal hyperplasia, a thickened basal layer, appears when injury persists.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c027--> Elongated lamina propria papillae stretch toward the surface.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c028--> Dilated capillaries are a frequent finding inside the papillae.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c029--> The epithelium contains infiltrating eosinophils, neutrophils and lymphocytes.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c030--> In severe cases the mucosa ulcerates.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c031-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

- **How it is studied:** on a biopsy, look for intraepithelial inflammation along with basal hyperplasia and long papillae.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c032-->
- **Barrett esophagus:** Barrett epithelium is a mixture of intestinal-type and gastric-type cells.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c033-->
- The reference pages describe no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test for reflux esophagitis.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient complain of?** Patients usually present with heartburn and dysphagia.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c034--> Reclining favors acid reflux, so sliding-hernia symptoms classically get worse when the patient lies down.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c035--> Erosive GERD may cause bleeding and stricture.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c036-->

**What relieves the symptoms?** Suppressing acid, above all with PPIs, usually eases symptoms.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c037-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How do we confirm or exclude GERD?** Eosinophilic esophagitis should be diagnosed only after GERD is excluded, by pH monitoring or a multi-week PPI trial.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c038--> Many patients with Barrett esophagus report no reflux symptoms, yet endoscopy with biopsy is what establishes the diagnosis.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c039-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Why is chronic GERD worrying?** Untreated dysplasia can evolve into adenocarcinoma, but only a minority of Barrett patients develop it.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c040--> The more esophagus Barrett epithelium covers, and the worse the dysplasia, the higher the adenocarcinoma risk.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c041--> A Barrett patient has an estimated 0.1% to 0.3% chance per year of developing adenocarcinoma.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c042--> Regular surveillance endoscopy in Barrett esophagus aims to find dysplasia early.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c043-->

## High-yield summary

- The central mechanism is an incompetent **lower esophageal sphincter**; a **sliding hiatal hernia** is often present too.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c048-->
- On histology, expect **eosinophils** among other inflammatory cells, **elongated papillae** and **basal hyperplasia**.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c049-->
- Shallow erosions and ulcers often run as **vertical streaks**; a lasting deep ulcer can leave a **stricture**.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c050-->
- **PPIs** usually ease heartburn and dysphagia.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c051-->
- Chronic GERD can lead to **Barrett esophagus** (**goblet cells**) and then to adenocarcinoma.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c052-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do eosinophils in the biopsy prove eosinophilic esophagitis?** No. GERD can also raise eosinophil counts; they sit mostly in the distal esophagus and subside with antireflux treatment.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c053-->

**Does a hiatal hernia always cause reflux?** No. The common sliding type is usually asymptomatic.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c054-->

**Does a large paraesophageal hernia mean more reflux?** Not necessarily: it can compress the esophagus and reduce reflux.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c055-->

**Is GERD always erosive?** No. It may also be nonerosive.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c056-->

## Mnemonics

**PEB**: **P**apillae long, **E**osinophils in, **B**asal layer thick: the three histologic signs of reflux.<!--c:reflux-esophagitis-c057-->
