## Definition and epidemiology

**What is gastric adenocarcinoma, and where is it common?** It is the most common malignant tumor of the stomach.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c001--> Its incidence differs strongly from one region to another.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c002--> Many Eastern countries report far higher rates than the West, where incidence has fallen steeply during the last hundred years.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c003--> Better detection and treatment of Helicobacter are shrinking the East-West difference.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What raises or lowers the risk?** Regional differences mostly follow chronic gastritis and the local spread and strains of Helicobacter.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c005--> More gastric cancers occur in people who eat a lot of smoked or pickled food.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c006--> Eating plenty of fresh vegetables lowers the risk.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c007--> Heredity contributes substantially to certain types of gastric cancer.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c008--> Germline mutations of CDH1, the E-cadherin gene, are linked to the rare, dominantly inherited hereditary diffuse gastric cancer.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c009--> Carriers can develop cancer at a young age.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c010--> They also have a higher risk of lobular carcinoma of the breast.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the tumor arise?** The intestinal type follows chronic gastritis with intestinal metaplasia.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c012--> Its link to Helicobacter explains why its incidence is falling.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c013--> Across countries, the incidence of the diffuse type has stayed fairly steady.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c014--> Compared with the intestinal type, its genetic basis is better defined.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c015--> Diffuse carcinomas that arise sporadically also lose E-cadherin function.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c016--> Unlike colonic adenomas, which are tightly tied to cancer, gastric adenomas have at most a possible connection to carcinoma.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c017-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the tumor look like on the opened stomach?** The appearance varies.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c018--> Most tumors are ulcerated to a significant degree and bulge into the lumen as large polypoid masses.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c019--> Ulcer size and a stiff, raised margin separate malignant from benign peptic ulcers.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c020--> A few cancers, mostly signet ring ones, grow deep in the wall under a surface that looks intact.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c021--> This pattern is classically called linitis plastica, a stomach made rigid by a thickened wall.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c022-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the pathologist see?** In the Lauren system a gastric carcinoma is intestinal or diffuse, and some tumors overlap between the two.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c023--> The label "intestinal" refers mainly to how the tumor is built, not to its cell type.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c024--> Solid areas may appear among the glands or papillae of intestinal-type tumors.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c025--> The mucosa of an intestinal-type cancer may be taken over by crowded, irregular tumor glands.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c026--> This is the more common pattern.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c027--> The diffuse type permeates the whole gastric wall; most of its cells are signet ring cells that stick together poorly.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c028--> Glands and surface lining are spared; the tumor cells crowd and thicken the lamina propria.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c029--> Signet ring carcinoma in situ is often present in the hereditary form as many tiny foci.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c030-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look for glands or papillae (intestinal type) or poorly cohesive signet ring cells (diffuse type).<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c031-->
- Only careful, extensive microscopy can detect the tiny in situ signet ring foci of hereditary cases.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c032-->
- The reference pages give no detail on special stains, immunohistochemistry (HER2, PD-L1), EBV or microsatellite instability testing; this card does not cover them yet.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** With diffuse infiltration the mucosal surface may show no mass at all, which can mislead.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c033--> The reference pages do not describe symptoms or patterns of spread.

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How do you tell a malignant ulcer from a benign one?** A necrotic floor and a hard raised rim mark a large antral tumor as malignant, not a benign ulcer.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c034--> The reference pages do not cover laboratory or imaging tests.

## Complications and prognosis

**How does depth of invasion change survival?**
<aside class="edition">Survival figures follow the 8th edition of the reference and may be out of date.</aside>
- Early gastric cancer is confined to the mucosa and submucosa.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c035-->
- Its five-year survival is 80% to 90%.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c036-->
- Advanced cancer reaches the muscularis propria or beyond.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c037-->
- Its five-year survival is 16% to 80%.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c038-->
- A tumor that invades deeper than that carries a worse outlook still.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c039-->
- Prophylactic gastrectomy is an option for carriers of germline CDH1 mutations.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c040-->

## High-yield summary

- The **intestinal type** follows Helicobacter-related chronic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c045-->
- The **diffuse type** is mostly **signet ring** cells with weak cohesion.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c046-->
- **Linitis plastica** is a rigid, thick stomach under an intact-looking surface.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c047-->
- Germline **CDH1** mutation is linked to hereditary diffuse gastric cancer.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c048-->
- CDH1 carriers have a higher risk of **lobular breast carcinoma**.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c049-->
- **Early gastric cancer** has a five-year survival of 80% to 90%.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c050-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does "intestinal" mean the tumor arises from intestinal cells?** No. The word refers to the glandular pattern of growth, not to the cell type.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c051-->

**Does an intact mucosal surface rule out diffuse cancer?** No. The tumor can infiltrate the wall under a normal-looking surface and produce linitis plastica.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c052-->

**Is a gastric adenoma as tightly linked to carcinoma as a colonic adenoma?** No. The tight adenoma-carcinoma connection typical of the colon is missing in the stomach.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c053-->

**Is the incidence falling for every gastric cancer?** No. Only the intestinal type is declining; the diffuse type has stayed stable.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c054-->

## Mnemonics

**Three Ds for the diffuse type:** Diffuse spread, Disconnected cells, Dominant CDH1 inheritance in the hereditary form.<!--c:gastric-adenocarcinoma-c055-->
