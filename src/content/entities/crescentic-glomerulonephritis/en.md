## Definition and epidemiology

**What is crescentic glomerulonephritis?** Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis is the clinical picture of kidney failure that worsens fast, with signs of nephritis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c001--> Its histologic counterpart is crescentic glomerulonephritis, defined by crescents in more than half of the glomeruli.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c002--> A crescent is a mass of proliferating parietal epithelial cells, macrophages and fibrin that fills Bowman space.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c003--> Immunopathology sorts cases into three categories: anti-GBM disease, immune complex disease and ANCA disease, with a small remainder that fits none.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c004-->

ANCA disease leads from middle age onward and is the main type in older adults, while immune complex disease leads under age 20.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c005--> Anti-GBM disease is a minority in every age group.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers each type?** Anti-GBM disease is autoimmune, and HLA-DRB1 is strongly associated with susceptibility.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c007--> A viral upper respiratory infection frequently comes first.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c008--> Smoking, for example, may be a second hit that the lungs need before they are involved.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c009--> ANCA disease is caused by autoantibodies against proteins of neutrophils and monocytes, in most cases myeloperoxidase (MPO) or proteinase 3 (PR3).<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c010--> In the immune complex category, injury follows deposition of immune complexes in the glomeruli.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c011--> Because glomeruli showed neither anti-GBM antibodies nor immune complexes, the ANCA type used to be labeled idiopathic crescentic glomerulonephritis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a crescent form?** A crescent starts when the capillary wall of the glomerulus is breached, whichever mechanism is responsible.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c013--> Bowman space receives plasma, with its clotting factors and inflammatory mediators, through the gap.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c014--> Macrophages and fibrin join the proliferating parietal epithelial cells, and together they build the crescent.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c015-->

In anti-GBM disease, the antigen lies in the NC1 domain of the α3 chain of type IV collagen in the GBM, and autoantibodies bind it.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c016--> The epitope is hidden until a change in the three-dimensional shape of the collagen exposes it.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c017--> Complement is one of the mediator systems that the bound antibodies switch on, and acute inflammation follows.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c018--> Vessels may also be injured by T cells aimed at GBM antigens, as experiments suggest.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c019--> Lung bleeding occurs because alveolar capillary basement membranes carry the same antigen.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c020-->

ANCA-activated neutrophils kill endothelial cells by granule release and toxic oxygen products after sticking to them.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c021--> The same neutrophils trigger kinins and alternative complement, which worsens the inflammation.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c022--> ANCAs cause this disease, as was shown after they were found circulating in about 90% of patients.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c023-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the glomerulus look like?** Beside the damaged tuft, the crescent's cells fill Bowman space.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c024-->

In anti-GBM disease, usually over half of the glomeruli carry crescents, and almost every patient has them.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c025--> Parts of some glomeruli often show fibrinoid necrosis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c026--> Bleeding into the alveoli is marked in involved lungs.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c027--> Electron microscopy shows focal GBM breaks and none of the dense deposits seen with immune complexes.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c028-->

In ANCA disease, often more than half of the glomeruli are affected by necrosis and crescents, and nearly all patients have these lesions.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c029--> A crescent develops over time in response to an earlier segmental lesion of fibrinoid necrosis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c030--> Segments away from the necrosis can look normal or show only slight changes.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c031--> ANCA disease has few immune deposits, unlike the other two types, which is why it is called pauci-immune.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c032-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- In anti-GBM disease, on Masson trichrome a cell-rich crescent crowds Bowman space.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c033-->
- In ANCA disease, a silver stain shows breaks in some glomerular basement membranes.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c034-->
- Anti-GBM disease is recognized on immunofluorescence by IgG outlining the GBM in a diffuse linear pattern.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c035-->
- Most immune complex disease gives a granular immunofluorescence pattern instead.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c036-->
- In ANCA disease, immunoglobulin and complement deposits are scarce or missing.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c037-->
- Anti-GBM disease is not the only cause of IgG along basement membranes: diabetic glomerulosclerosis and monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease show it too.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c038-->
- In anti-GBM disease, electron microscopy shows breaks in the GBM and no immune complex deposits.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c039-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does the patient present?** Both anti-GBM and ANCA disease usually announce themselves with kidney failure that worsens quickly and with a nephritic picture.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c040--> Lung bleeding, with hemoptysis, affects about half of anti-GBM patients and can endanger life.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c041--> The eponym Goodpasture applies once both organs, lungs and kidneys, are affected.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c042--> Pulmonary hemorrhage is one of the many systemic effects of the small vessel vasculitis that about three quarters of ANCA disease patients have.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c043--> Goodpasture syndrome is much rarer than the pulmonary–renal syndrome caused by ANCA vasculitis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c044-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests assign the category?** Circulating anti-GBM antibodies are detectable in most patients with anti-GBM disease.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c045--> Most patients with ANCA disease have circulating ANCAs, usually MPO-ANCA or PR3-ANCA.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c046--> The categories can overlap, with more than one marker present in the same patient's serology or biopsy.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c047-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens with and without treatment?** In anti-GBM disease, starting plasma exchange and heavy immunosuppression early, before the kidneys fail badly, gives the best results.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c048--> Waiting for anti-GBM antibodies to clear makes graft loss from recurrence unlikely.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c049--> Without treatment, end-stage renal disease develops within 5 years in more than 80% of patients with ANCA disease.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c050--> Immunosuppression lowers this to under 20%.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c051--> After remission, patients remain at risk of relapse.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c052--> In ANCA disease, 15% of transplant recipients have a recurrence.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c053-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-GBM disease | Linear IgG along the GBM, possible lung hemorrhage, no deposits on electron microscopy.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c054--> | Immunofluorescence for IgG; serum anti-GBM antibodies |
| [[anca-associated-vasculitis\|ANCA glomerulonephritis]] | Necrosis and crescents with little or no immunoglobulin, often with systemic vasculitis.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c055--> | Immunofluorescence; serum MPO-ANCA or PR3-ANCA |
| Immune complex glomerulonephritis | Granular rather than linear immunofluorescence.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c056--> | Immunofluorescence |

## High-yield summary

- A **crescent** is parietal epithelial cells, macrophages and fibrin in Bowman space after the capillary wall breaks.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c057-->
- **Linear IgG** along the GBM points to anti-GBM disease, aimed at the α3 chain of type IV collagen.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c058-->
- A **granular** pattern points to immune complexes.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c059-->
- **Pauci-immune** crescentic disease is the ANCA form, with MPO or PR3 antibodies in most cases.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c060-->
- **Goodpasture syndrome** is anti-GBM disease with both lung and kidney involvement.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c061-->
- **ANCA disease** becomes more common with age and is the leading type in older adults.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c062-->
- **Plasma exchange** with immunosuppression works best early in anti-GBM disease.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c063-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does linear IgG on the GBM prove anti-GBM disease?** Not entirely. Two other diseases show it too: diabetic glomerulosclerosis and monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c064-->

**Is Goodpasture syndrome the same as anti-GBM disease?** No. Goodpasture syndrome is the lung-and-kidney form, while anti-GBM disease can also stay confined to the kidneys.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c065-->

**Is Goodpasture syndrome the usual cause of a lung–kidney syndrome?** No. The ANCA pulmonary–renal syndrome is far more frequent.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c066-->

**Does pauci-immune mean a mild disease?** No. The term refers to scant immune deposits, and ANCA disease is aggressive, with necrosis and crescents.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c067-->

**Is crescentic glomerulonephritis a single disease?** No. It is a pattern of injury that can be reached by anti-GBM, immune complex or ANCA mechanisms.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c068-->

## Mnemonics

**Three L's:** a **Line** of IgG means anti-GBM, **Lumps** (granular) mean immune complexes, and **Little** or nothing means ANCA.<!--c:crescentic-glomerulonephritis-c069-->
