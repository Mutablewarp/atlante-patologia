## Definition and epidemiology

**What are vocal cord nodules, polyps and laryngeal papillomas?** Inflammation or trauma of the vocal cord can leave a stromal reaction: the vocal cord nodule or polyp, also called a singer's nodule, which is not a tumor.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c001--> A squamous papilloma is a papillary growth of mature squamous epithelium.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c002-->

**Who gets them?** Nodules and polyps appear at any age.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c003--> Adults, mostly men, are the patients who develop a single laryngeal papilloma.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c004--> Children and adolescents can have many papillomas at once (juvenile laryngeal papillomatosis).<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c005-->

<aside class="edition">Leukoplakia, keratosis and the grading of laryngeal epithelial dysplasia changed between classification editions. The reference pages do not cover them; check the current WHO classification before using any grade.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers these lesions?** Nodules and polyps follow vocal overuse, irritants, laryngitis or an underactive thyroid.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c006--> Papillomas are caused mainly by HPV types 6 and 11.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why is papillomatosis hard to eradicate?** HPV often involves wide areas, so removing visible growths does not reliably cure papillomatosis.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c008--> Papillomas can spread down to the trachea and bronchi.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c009-->

## Gross morphology

**What do they look like?** The pictured polyp is a lone glossy mass on a true vocal cord.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c010--> Papillomas are papillary growths, single or multiple.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c011-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the stroma show?** In early nodules and polyps the stroma is edematous and fibromyxoid.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c012--> With time the stroma turns hyalinized and densely fibrotic.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c013--> Under the microscope, each papilloma frond has a fibrovascular core covered by mature squamous epithelium.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c014-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the reference pages give no specimen, stain, immunohistochemistry or molecular method for these lesions, and no grading of dysplasia.

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** The voice becomes hoarse or cracks in patients with nodules and polyps.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c015--> Papillomatosis can cause life-threatening airway obstruction.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c016-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** The reference pages do not describe a diagnostic work-up for these lesions.

## Complications and prognosis

**Do they come back or turn malignant?** Papillomatosis can relapse repeatedly for years.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c017--> A solitary papilloma in an adult is usually cured by surgery.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c018--> Smokers and patients who have had radiotherapy are those in whom papillomatosis may, rarely, become squamous cell carcinoma.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c019-->

## High-yield summary

- **Vocal cord nodules and polyps** are reactive stromal lesions, not neoplasms.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c021-->
- **HPV 6 and 11** are the main cause of laryngeal papillomas.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c022-->
- **Juvenile papillomatosis** is multiple, recurs and can obstruct the airway.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c023-->
- A **solitary papilloma** in an adult is usually cured by surgery.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c024-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a vocal cord polyp a precancerous lesion?** No. It is a reactive stromal change after inflammation or trauma.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c025-->

**Does excision cure laryngeal papillomatosis?** Excision is not a reliable cure: the infection is often extensive, so growths keep coming back for years.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c026-->

## Mnemonics

**Triggers of nodules and polyps: VASIT.** Voice abuse, Alcohol, Smoking, Infection, Thyroid underactivity.<!--c:laryngeal-precursor-lesions-c027-->
