## Definition and epidemiology

**What is light-chain cast nephropathy?** The disease is defined by tubular casts and tubular cell damage, both caused by monoclonal light chains passing into the urine.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c001--> Among myeloma patients it is the kidney disease seen most often, arising as a complication of the tumor.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which kidney diseases can myeloma cause?** Four are possible: cast nephropathy, AL amyloidosis, and deposition disease of either light chains or heavy chains.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c003--> Which of these a patient gets depends on the structure of the light chain.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c004--> Some patients have more than one at the same time.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c005-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do free light chains hurt the kidney?** The glomeruli filter the light chains out of the blood.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c006--> Distal tubular cells secrete Tamm–Horsfall glycoprotein; because urine is acidic, the light chains attach to it and aggregate into casts.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c007--> The casts block the tubules, and the free light chains also poison the tubular epithelium; both lower kidney function.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c008-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the casts look like?** Casts pack the distal tubules and collecting ducts, looking solid and glass-like and staining deep pink with eosin.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c009--> Casts often break up, leaving jagged corners that give a crystal-like look.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c010--> Macrophages and multinucleated giant cells may gather around casts as a foreign-body reaction.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c011--> Tubular damage is usually accompanied by edema and long-standing inflammation of the surrounding interstitium.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c012--> Tubular atrophy and interstitial fibrosis mark the lesions that have been present longer.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c013--> Where the interstitium is scarred, patchy calcification (nephrocalcinosis) is often found.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c014-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on light microscopy, look for casts inside the tubular lumina.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c015-->
- Antibody staining can detect light chains and Tamm–Horsfall protein.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c016-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** It can appear as acute or chronic renal failure.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c017--> Proteinuria is usually present but may stay below nephrotic range; most of it is light chain.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c018-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What should I think if a myeloma patient has nephrotic-range proteinuria?** Cast nephropathy becomes the less likely cause; AL amyloidosis or light-chain deposition disease fits better.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c019-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What does long-standing injury leave behind?** Long-standing injury leaves a scarred interstitium and atrophic tubules.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c020-->

**What else can myeloma do to the kidney?**

## High-yield summary

- **Light-chain cast nephropathy** is the most common kidney disease of **multiple myeloma**.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c025-->
- Light chains bind **Tamm–Horsfall** protein in acidic urine and form casts in distal tubules.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c026-->
- The casts are **fractured**, sharp-edged and may attract **giant cells**.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c027-->
- **Nephrotic-range** proteinuria in a myeloma patient shifts suspicion to AL amyloidosis or light-chain deposition disease.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c028-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Must proteinuria in cast nephropathy be nephrotic?** No. It is mostly light chains and may fall below the nephrotic range.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c029-->

**Does myeloma cause only one kind of kidney disease?** No. Four are possible, and some patients have several together.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c030-->

**Are the casts only a plumbing problem?** No. Free light chains also poison tubular cells directly.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c031-->

## Mnemonics

**CAST:** **C**hains in acid urine + **A**ttach to Tamm–Horsfall = **S**topped **T**ubules.<!--c:light-chain-cast-nephropathy-c032-->
