## Definition and epidemiology

**What are serrated colorectal lesions?** They are colorectal polyps: hyperplastic polyps, sessile serrated adenomas (also called sessile serrated polyps) and traditional serrated adenomas.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c001-->

<aside class="edition">Names and criteria changed between editions. This card follows the 8th-edition reference; check current terminology.</aside>

Hyperplastic polyps are benign; their crypts are overbuilt and the lesion is a small sessile bump.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c002-->
No other colon polyp is as common, and the rectum is where it turns up most.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c003-->
A sessile serrated adenoma looks similar but has malignant potential.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c004-->
Traditional serrated adenomas, much rarer than the other two, mostly occur in the distal colon and can be premalignant.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who gets these polyps?**
- Colons with adenomatous polyps tend to hold more hyperplastic polyps too.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c006-->
- They are also commoner where colorectal cancer rates are higher.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c007-->
- Hyperplastic and sessile serrated polyps make up most of the many serrated polyps in the rare serrated polyposis syndrome (hyperplastic polyposis syndrome).<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c008-->
- Men and women are equally affected; European descent and older age raise the risk.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c009-->
- It runs in families, yet no specific mutation has been found.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do the crypts become serrated?** Hyperplastic polyps are thought to start when normal epithelium proliferates and matures abnormally.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c011-->
Hyperplastic polyp cells linger longer on the surface: they mature low in the crypt after a slow climb from the dividing zone at its base.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c012-->

**What is the serrated pathway?**
- In sessile serrated adenomas, the promoter of the mismatch-repair gene MLH1 is hypermethylated.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c013-->
- BRAF mutations are a feature of these lesions too.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c014-->
- Microsatellite instability (MSI) is frequent in them.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c015-->
- Some traditional serrated adenomas carry KRAS mutations, others BRAF mutations, so they are molecularly diverse.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c016-->
- A subset shows MGMT promoter methylation as part of the CpG island methylator phenotype (CIMP).<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c017-->

## Gross morphology

**What do they look like at endoscopy?** Hyperplastic polyps are almost always multiple, each a small pale sessile nodule.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c018-->
Sessile serrated adenomas are sessile or flat.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c019-->
Often under a mucin film, they can mimic a deformed mucosal fold.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c020-->
They tend to be larger than hyperplastic polyps.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c021-->

## Microscopic morphology

**How does each type look on H&E?**
- Hyperplastic polyp: the crypt bases look relatively normal, while the crypts themselves are elongated.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c022-->
- There is no dysplasia; the upper third of each crypt holds absorptive cells and overgrown mucus-secreting cells.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c023-->
- Together these features produce a tufted surface with a sawtooth outline.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c024-->
- Sessile serrated adenoma: cells can divide at any height of the crypt, and growth is lopsided and haphazard.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c025-->
- Goblet and mucin cells reach the base, so the serration does too.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c026-->
- Some bases are boot-, L- or inverted T-shaped; mucin distends others.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c027-->
- Traditional serrated adenoma: ectopic crypts form, and the architecture is typically tubulovillous or villous.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c028-->
- Traditional serrated adenoma: low-grade dysplasia means stretched nuclei and pink (eosinophilic) cytoplasm in a serrated lining.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c029-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which test separates the types?** Crypt architecture on routine histology separates sessile serrated adenomas from hyperplastic polyps; no special stain does.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c030-->

## Clinical presentation

**Where in the colon should I expect them?** Sessile serrated adenomas usually develop in the right colon.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c031-->
First-degree relatives of a patient with serrated polyposis syndrome face a higher cancer risk, so their colonoscopic screening should begin earlier than usual.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c032-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** Crypt histology separates sessile serrated adenomas from hyperplastic polyps.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c033-->
Subtle features can make the two hard to tell apart.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c034-->
Both sessile and traditional serrated adenomas call for complete removal.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c035-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Which of these polyps can turn into cancer?** Sessile serrated adenomas are an important carcinoma precursor; hyperplastic polyps are not.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c036-->
Invasive carcinoma is the end stage, after dysplasia worsens from low to high grade.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c037-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperplastic polyp | No dysplasia; crypt bases look normal.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c038--> | H&E histology |
| Sessile serrated adenoma | Serration to the base, with boot-, L- or inverted T-shaped crypts.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c039--> | H&E histology |
| Traditional serrated adenoma | Pink (eosinophilic) cytoplasm, ectopic crypts and a villous or tubulovillous pattern.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c040--> | H&E histology |

## High-yield summary

- **Hyperplastic polyp**: benign, no dysplasia, commonest in the rectum.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c042-->
- **Sessile serrated adenoma**: right colon, boot-shaped crypts, premalignant.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c043-->
- **BRAF** mutation, **MLH1** promoter methylation and **MSI** mark the sessile serrated adenoma pathway.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c044-->
- **Traditional serrated adenoma**: distal colon, ectopic crypts, variable BRAF, KRAS or CIMP changes.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c045-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a sessile serrated adenoma just a bigger hyperplastic polyp?** No. It looks similar but is premalignant and has distinct crypt architecture.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c046-->

**Are all serrated polyps harmless?** No. Only hyperplastic polyps are benign; sessile and traditional serrated adenomas should be removed entirely.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c047-->

**Do the two serrated adenomas share one molecular profile?** No. Sessile ones show MLH1 methylation, BRAF mutation and MSI, while traditional ones vary.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c048-->

## Mnemonics

**RBB:** the sessile serrated adenoma is Right-sided, BRAF-mutated and has Boot-shaped crypts.<!--c:serrated-colorectal-lesions-c049-->
