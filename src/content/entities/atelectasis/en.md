## Definition and epidemiology

**What is atelectasis?** Lung tissue that had been inflated with air has collapsed.<!--c:atelectasis-c001-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What makes a lung segment collapse?** Most often a blocked bronchus.<!--c:atelectasis-c002-->

- In adults, a primary lung tumor that grows into the bronchus is the usual culprit.<!--c:atelectasis-c003-->
- In children, an inhaled object, a mucus plug or aspirated stomach contents often blocks the bronchus.<!--c:atelectasis-c004-->
- A lung can also collapse when something presses on it from outside, such as fluid (hydrothorax) or air (pneumothorax) in the pleural space.<!--c:atelectasis-c005-->
- Pain after abdominal surgery limits breathing movements, and mucus can block a bronchus; either can bring on atelectasis.<!--c:atelectasis-c006-->

**What is right middle lobe syndrome?** Enlarged lymph nodes at the hilum can press on the bronchus of the right middle lobe from outside, and the lobe then collapses: right middle lobe syndrome.<!--c:atelectasis-c007-->
Surrounded by lymph nodes, this thin, long bronchus is easily squeezed from outside.<!--c:atelectasis-c008-->
In many cases no cause for the blockage is found; when one is, tuberculous lymphadenitis or metastatic lung cancer can be behind the node enlargement.<!--c:atelectasis-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does air disappear from a blocked segment?** Collapse follows because the blood absorbs the alveolar gas once the air supply is cut off.<!--c:atelectasis-c010-->

**Does every obstruction cause collapse?** No. Only a complete block ends in atelectasis, whereas with a partial block air can be trapped and the segment beyond it becomes overdistended.<!--c:atelectasis-c011-->

## Gross morphology

**How does a collapsed lung look?** It is deflated next to an air-filled lung, as in an infant's lungs where one side has collapsed.<!--c:atelectasis-c012-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What happens to the collapsed tissue over time?** Infection beyond the block contributes to widening of the bronchi, and if the collapse persists the airless lung also becomes fibrotic.<!--c:atelectasis-c013-->
Microscopically, in right middle lobe syndrome the lobe shows scarring and dilated, chronically inflamed bronchi.<!--c:atelectasis-c014-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient notice?** Often nothing.<!--c:atelectasis-c015-->
In severe collapse the mediastinum is pulled toward the collapsed side, and blood oxygen may fall (hypoxemia).<!--c:atelectasis-c016-->
Severe compression from outside can displace the mediastinum away from the affected side and endanger the function of that lung.<!--c:atelectasis-c017-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Can the lung beyond a block become infected?** Yes. Beyond an obstruction, bronchiectasis, abscesses or pneumonia may develop.<!--c:atelectasis-c018-->
The term bronchiectasis describes bronchi that stay permanently widened.<!--c:atelectasis-c019-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[bronchiectasis]] | Irreversible widening of bronchi, whereas atelectasis is collapse of lung tissue; it can follow long-standing atelectasis.<!--c:atelectasis-c020--> | Thick-walled, markedly enlarged bronchi are seen in a surgically removed bronchiectatic lobe.<!--c:atelectasis-c021--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Atelectasis** is airless, collapsed lung that was once inflated.<!--c:atelectasis-c022-->
- Most cases follow **bronchial obstruction**; the trapped gas is absorbed into the blood.<!--c:atelectasis-c023-->
- **Outside compression** (hydrothorax, pneumothorax) also collapses lung.<!--c:atelectasis-c024-->
- Chronic collapse makes the lung **fibrotic**; infection helps widen its bronchi.<!--c:atelectasis-c025-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does the mediastinum always shift the same way?** No. Severe collapse from obstruction pulls it toward the affected side.<!--c:atelectasis-c026-->
With severe outside compression it moves away from the affected side.<!--c:atelectasis-c027-->

**Does any bronchial block cause atelectasis?** No. Only a complete block does; a partial one can overdistend the segment instead.<!--c:atelectasis-c028-->

**Does atelectasis always cause symptoms?** No. It is often silent.<!--c:atelectasis-c029-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the two main mechanisms?** Think "Plug or Press": a plug in the bronchus, or pressure on the lung from outside.<!--c:atelectasis-c030-->
