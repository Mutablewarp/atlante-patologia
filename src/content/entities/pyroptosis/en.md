## Definition and epidemiology

Pyroptosis is a programmed cell death that ends with rupture of the plasma membrane and a strong inflammatory signal, in contrast to the quiet death of apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c001--> Its name says as much: *pyro-* is Greek for fire, and so fever, while *ptosis* means falling.<!--c:pyroptosis-c051--> Its hallmark enzyme is caspase-1, a pro-inflammatory protease activated inside the inflammasome.<!--c:pyroptosis-c002--> It is chiefly a weapon of innate immunity: a cell invaded by an intracellular pathogen destroys itself and, in doing so, calls in more immune cells.<!--c:pyroptosis-c003--> Pyroptosis is one of the regulated forms of cell death and is related to necroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

- **Intracellular microbes:** viruses and bacteria that invade the cell are the classic triggers.<!--c:pyroptosis-c005-->
- **Bacterial flagellin:** detecting this bacterial protein is what drives *Salmonella*-infected macrophages into pyroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c006-->
- **Cytosolic LPS:** human caspase-4 and caspase-5 (caspase-11 in mice) recognize bacterial lipopolysaccharide directly and set off pyroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c007-->
- **Sterile triggers:** irritants such as mineral crystals can set it off without any microbe; monosodium urate crystals, for instance, lead to inflammasome assembly through NLRs.<!--c:pyroptosis-c008-->
- **Metabolic disease:** it is also thought to contribute to obesity and to type 2 diabetes.<!--c:pyroptosis-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**1. Sensing.** A pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) or another danger signal inside the cell switches on a cytosolic NOD-like receptor (NLR).<!--c:pyroptosis-c011--> Inflammasomes, the large multiprotein platforms in which some pro-inflammatory cytokines are cut into their active form, are what these receptors then assemble.<!--c:pyroptosis-c010-->

**2. The inflammasome.** Innate immune cells such as macrophages carry inflammasomes; the one studied in most detail, NLRP3, is built from an NLR sensor, the adaptor ASC and caspase-1.<!--c:pyroptosis-c012--> An activating stimulus such as extracellular ATP or urate crystals usually switches NLRP3 on only in a cell that has been primed, that is, made to produce NLRP3 and pro-IL-1β by a first signal through a pattern recognition receptor, for instance a Toll-like receptor engaged by bacterial LPS.<!--c:pyroptosis-c052--> Once the sensor is triggered, the inactive procaspase-1 held in the complex becomes active caspase-1.<!--c:pyroptosis-c013-->

**3. A caspase outside apoptosis.** Chemically, caspase-1 is a cysteine protease like the apoptotic caspases; functionally, it normally pushes the cell toward pyroptosis rather than apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c014--> Pyroptosis has caspases of its own, caspase-1 among them: of the roughly 14 caspases known, only about half work in apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c015-->

**4. Cytokine maturation.** Caspase-1 processes inactive precursors into mature IL-1β and IL-18, the cytokines that spread the inflammatory alarm.<!--c:pyroptosis-c016-->

**5. Gasdermin D pores.** Gasdermin D has an N-terminal and a C-terminal domain joined by a linker, and the inflammatory caspases (caspase-1, -4, -5 and -11) cut it right there.<!--c:pyroptosis-c017--> The C-terminal domain normally restrains the N-terminal one; once separated, the N-terminal fragment is enough to kill the cell.<!--c:pyroptosis-c018--> Large pores form as the fragment assembles in the plasma membrane, where it anchors itself by binding lipids such as phosphoinositides.<!--c:pyroptosis-c019-->

**6. Swelling and bursting.** The cell bursts after swelling with the water and solutes that pour in through the pores.<!--c:pyroptosis-c021--> Energy runs out as well, because caspase-1 also cuts glycolytic enzymes, along with structural proteins of the cytoskeleton.<!--c:pyroptosis-c022-->

**7. Release of IL-1β and DAMPs.** Gasdermin D pores are the exit route for IL-1β: cells lacking gasdermin D still process IL-1β through caspase-1 but release much less of it.<!--c:pyroptosis-c023--> Further immune cells are called in to clear the infection by the damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) that leak out of the ruptured cell.<!--c:pyroptosis-c024-->

## Microscopic morphology

- The dying cell swells and its plasma membrane breaks, whereas an apoptotic cell blebs without rupturing.<!--c:pyroptosis-c025-->
- The nucleus shows pyknosis and DNA fragmentation.<!--c:pyroptosis-c026--> Unlike the apoptotic nucleus, which breaks into fragments, it stays whole while its chromatin condenses.<!--c:pyroptosis-c053-->
- Because the lysed cell releases active cytokines, inflammation follows its death.<!--c:pyroptosis-c027-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **In tissue:** pyroptosis is hard to catch in the act, because the dying cells burst quickly and macrophages soon clear them away.<!--c:pyroptosis-c054--> On H&E it shows at most the lytic picture described under Microscopic morphology, and appearance alone is not enough to prove it.<!--c:pyroptosis-c055-->
- **What defines it:** inflammatory caspases often, though not always, start it; for the Nomenclature Committee on Cell Death (NCCD, 2018) the defining step is the opening of pores in the plasma membrane by gasdermins.<!--c:pyroptosis-c056-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** on a tissue section a gasdermin stain says little about activation, because current methods struggle to tell the cleaved fragment from the full-length protein.<!--c:pyroptosis-c057-->
- **Immunoblot and ELISA:** in cultured cells, the strongest proof that caspase-1 has acted is a Western blot showing cleaved gasdermin D and cleaved caspase-1, while an ELISA measures the mature IL-1β released into the medium.<!--c:pyroptosis-c058-->
- **Membrane rupture:** lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), an enzyme of the cytosol, turns up in the culture medium once the cell has burst, while ethidium bromide and similar dyes, which an intact membrane keeps out, get in through the gasdermin pores.<!--c:pyroptosis-c059--> Cells genetically lacking caspase-1 or an inflammasome component are the control that ties these results to pyroptosis, because lysis is also the final step in many other ways of dying.<!--c:pyroptosis-c060-->
- **DNA tests:** pyroptotic nuclei can be TUNEL-positive, like apoptotic and necroptotic ones, so a positive TUNEL does not by itself mean apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c061--> On a gel, pyroptotic DNA does not form the ladder of fragments about 180 base pairs apart that is typical of apoptosis, although a nuclease activated by caspase-1 does cut it.<!--c:pyroptosis-c062-->

## Clinical presentation

- **Host defense:** pyroptosis is protective for the host when the enemy is an intracellular pathogen.<!--c:pyroptosis-c028-->
- **HIV infection:** pyroptosis is one of the main drivers of CD4+ T-cell loss and also fuels inflammation.<!--c:pyroptosis-c029-->
- **Autoinflammatory syndromes:** several periodic fever syndromes stem from inherited faults in inflammasome regulation, and mutations affecting NLRP3 are a recognized cause of hereditary periodic fevers.<!--c:pyroptosis-c030-->
- **NOMID:** in neonatal-onset multisystem inflammatory disease, an inherited autoinflammatory disorder, the skin and joints are inflamed by too much IL-1, because a mutation keeps cryopyrin (NLRP3), a part of the inflammasome, permanently switched on.<!--c:pyroptosis-c031-->
- **Gout and pseudogout:** active IL-1β and IL-18 are made when monosodium urate crystals (gout) or calcium pyrophosphate crystals (pseudogout) switch on the NLRP3 (cryopyrin) inflammasome, a mechanism that helps explain the inflammation of these arthritides.<!--c:pyroptosis-c065-->

## Complications and prognosis

- The same machinery that clears infection harms the host when inflammasome activity is excessive or poorly controlled.<!--c:pyroptosis-c032-->
- Inflammatory caspases that sense cytosolic LPS are central not only to antibacterial defense but also to endotoxic shock.<!--c:pyroptosis-c033-->
- Blocking the IL-1 receptor with anakinra brought a rapid response in all 18 patients with NOMID enrolled in one study, and the disease came back within days whenever the drug was stopped.<!--c:pyroptosis-c066-->

## High-yield summary

- Pyroptosis = **lytic, inflammatory** programmed death that mainly targets **intracellular pathogens**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c038-->
- **Inflammasome** = sensor **NLR** (e.g. **NLRP3**) + adaptor **ASC** + **procaspase-1**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c039-->
- **NLRP3** needs **two signals**: an **activating stimulus** (e.g. **ATP**, **urate crystals**) usually works only after **priming** through a pattern recognition receptor (e.g. LPS on a Toll-like receptor).<!--c:pyroptosis-c064-->
- **Caspase-1** matures **IL-1β** and **IL-18** and normally drives **pyroptosis**, not apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c040-->
- Inflammatory caspases cleave **gasdermin D**; its **N-terminal fragment** forms **plasma membrane pores**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c041-->
- **Caspase-4/5** (mouse caspase-11) sense **cytosolic LPS** directly.<!--c:pyroptosis-c042-->
- **Swelling**, **rupture** and **DAMP** release recruit more immune cells.<!--c:pyroptosis-c043-->
- Disease links: **HIV** CD4+ T-cell loss, **metabolic syndrome** and type 2 diabetes, **periodic fever syndromes**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c044-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- **Caspase-1 is not an apoptotic caspase:** its activation normally ends in pyroptosis, not apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c045-->
- **Pyroptosis is not a quiet death:** DAMPs escape through the ruptured membrane, which does not happen in apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c046-->
- **IL-1β release does not always require death:** gasdermin D pores can let IL-1β out of hyperactivated macrophages that stay alive.<!--c:pyroptosis-c047-->
- **Not only infection:** crystals and metabolic stress can trigger it as well.<!--c:pyroptosis-c048-->
- **Not the same as necroptosis:** necroptosis runs through RIP3 and MLKL without caspases, whereas pyroptosis depends on caspase-1.<!--c:pyroptosis-c049-->
- **Swelling or a positive TUNEL does not prove it:** both are found in other deaths as well; the proof is its machinery at work, such as cleaved gasdermin D.<!--c:pyroptosis-c063-->

## Mnemonics

**"Sense, Assemble, Cut, Punch, Burst"** (original): the NLR senses, the inflammasome assembles, caspase-1 cuts its substrates (IL-1β, IL-18), pores punch the membrane, and the cell bursts.<!--c:pyroptosis-c050-->
