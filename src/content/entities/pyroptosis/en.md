## Definition and epidemiology

Pyroptosis is a programmed cell death that ends with rupture of the plasma membrane and a strong inflammatory signal, in contrast to the quiet death of apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c001--> Its hallmark enzyme is caspase-1, a pro-inflammatory protease activated inside the inflammasome.<!--c:pyroptosis-c002--> It is chiefly a weapon of innate immunity: a cell invaded by an intracellular pathogen destroys itself and, in doing so, calls in more immune cells.<!--c:pyroptosis-c003--> Pyroptosis is one of the regulated forms of cell death and is related to necroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

- **Intracellular microbes:** bacteria, flagellated organisms and DNA or RNA viruses are the classic triggers.<!--c:pyroptosis-c005-->
- **Bacterial flagellin:** macrophages that harbor *Salmonella* undergo pyroptosis once they detect its flagellin.<!--c:pyroptosis-c006-->
- **Cytosolic LPS:** human caspase-4 and caspase-5 (caspase-11 in mice) recognize bacterial lipopolysaccharide directly and set off pyroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c007-->
- **Sterile irritants:** crystals and other inanimate particles can also drive it; monosodium urate crystals, for example, engage NLRs and assemble the inflammasome.<!--c:pyroptosis-c008-->
- **Metabolic stress:** beyond infection, pyroptosis appears to play a part in obesity and its metabolic complications, such as metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes.<!--c:pyroptosis-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**1. Sensing.** Some cytosolic NOD-like receptors (NLRs), NLRP3 being the best known, can gather into large multiprotein platforms, the inflammasomes, whose task is to cut pro-inflammatory cytokines into their active form.<!--c:pyroptosis-c010--> The trigger is a pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) or another danger signal inside the cell that activates an NLR.<!--c:pyroptosis-c011-->

**2. The inflammasome.** The best studied one, NLRP3, groups an NLR sensor, the adaptor ASC and caspase-1 in macrophages and other innate immune cells.<!--c:pyroptosis-c012--> Once the sensor is triggered, the inactive procaspase-1 held in the complex becomes active caspase-1.<!--c:pyroptosis-c013-->

**3. A caspase outside apoptosis.** Chemically, caspase-1 is a cysteine protease like the apoptotic caspases; functionally, it normally pushes the cell toward pyroptosis rather than apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c014--> About 14 caspases are known; only about half are involved in apoptosis, and others, such as caspase-1, mediate pyroptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c015-->

**4. Cytokine maturation.** Caspase-1 processes inactive precursors into mature IL-1β and IL-18, the cytokines that spread the inflammatory alarm.<!--c:pyroptosis-c016-->

**5. Gasdermin D pores.** The inflammatory caspases (caspase-1, -4, -5 and -11) cut gasdermin D in the linker that joins its N-terminal and C-terminal domains.<!--c:pyroptosis-c017--> The C-terminal domain normally restrains the N-terminal one; once separated, the N-terminal fragment is enough to kill the cell.<!--c:pyroptosis-c018--> This fragment binds membrane lipids such as phosphoinositides, moves to the plasma membrane and assembles into large pores.<!--c:pyroptosis-c019-->

**6. Swelling and bursting.** Water and solutes flood in through the pores, so the cell swells and then bursts.<!--c:pyroptosis-c021--> Caspase-1 also cleaves glycolytic enzymes, which drains the cell's energy, along with cytoskeletal proteins, chaperones and caspase-7.<!--c:pyroptosis-c022-->

**7. Release of IL-1β and DAMPs.** Gasdermin D pores are the exit route for IL-1β: cells lacking gasdermin D still process IL-1β through caspase-1 but release much less of it.<!--c:pyroptosis-c023--> Rupture of the membrane spills damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) outside the cell, and these recruit further immune cells to clear the infection.<!--c:pyroptosis-c024-->

## Microscopic morphology

- The dying cell swells and its plasma membrane breaks, whereas an apoptotic cell blebs without rupturing.<!--c:pyroptosis-c025-->
- The nucleus shows pyknosis and DNA fragmentation.<!--c:pyroptosis-c026-->
- Because the lysed cell releases active cytokines, inflammation follows its death.<!--c:pyroptosis-c027-->

## Clinical presentation

- **Host defense:** pyroptosis is protective for the host when the enemy is an intracellular pathogen.<!--c:pyroptosis-c028-->
- **HIV infection:** pyroptosis is one of the main drivers of CD4+ T-cell loss and also fuels inflammation.<!--c:pyroptosis-c029-->
- **Autoinflammatory syndromes:** several periodic fever syndromes stem from inherited faults in inflammasome regulation, and mutations affecting NLRP3 are a recognized cause of hereditary periodic fevers.<!--c:pyroptosis-c030-->
- **NOMID:** neonatal-onset multisystem inflammatory disease is an inherited autoinflammatory disorder: a mutation leaves cryopyrin (NLRP3), an inflammasome component, switched on all the time, and the resulting excess of IL-1 inflames the skin and joints.<!--c:pyroptosis-c031-->

## Complications and prognosis

- The same machinery that clears infection harms the host when inflammasome activity is excessive or poorly controlled.<!--c:pyroptosis-c032-->
- Inflammatory caspases that sense cytosolic LPS are central not only to antibacterial defense but also to endotoxic shock.<!--c:pyroptosis-c033-->

## High-yield summary

- Pyroptosis = **lytic, inflammatory** programmed death that mainly targets **intracellular pathogens**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c038-->
- **Inflammasome** = sensor **NLR** (e.g. **NLRP3**) + adaptor **ASC** + **procaspase-1**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c039-->
- **Caspase-1** matures **IL-1β** and **IL-18** and normally drives **pyroptosis**, not apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c040-->
- Inflammatory caspases cleave **gasdermin D**; its **N-terminal fragment** forms **plasma membrane pores**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c041-->
- **Caspase-4/5** (mouse caspase-11) sense **cytosolic LPS** directly.<!--c:pyroptosis-c042-->
- **Swelling**, **rupture** and **DAMP** release recruit more immune cells.<!--c:pyroptosis-c043-->
- Disease links: **HIV** CD4+ T-cell loss, **metabolic syndrome** and type 2 diabetes, **periodic fever syndromes**.<!--c:pyroptosis-c044-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- **Caspase-1 is not an apoptotic caspase:** its activation normally ends in pyroptosis, not apoptosis.<!--c:pyroptosis-c045-->
- **Pyroptosis is not a quiet death:** unlike apoptosis, the membrane ruptures and DAMPs escape.<!--c:pyroptosis-c046-->
- **IL-1β release does not always require death:** gasdermin D pores can let IL-1β out of hyperactivated macrophages that stay alive.<!--c:pyroptosis-c047-->
- **Not only infection:** crystals and metabolic stress can trigger it as well.<!--c:pyroptosis-c048-->
- **Not the same as necroptosis:** necroptosis runs through RIP3 and MLKL without caspases, whereas pyroptosis depends on caspase-1.<!--c:pyroptosis-c049-->

## Mnemonics

**"Sense, Assemble, Cut, Punch, Burst"** (original): the NLR senses, the inflammasome assembles, caspase-1 cuts its substrates (IL-1β, IL-18), pores punch the membrane, and the cell bursts.<!--c:pyroptosis-c050-->
