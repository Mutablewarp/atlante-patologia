## Definition and epidemiology
**What is renal cell carcinoma, and how common is each type?** RCC is a malignant tumor that grows from the lining cells of the renal tubules and collecting ducts.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c001--> Roughly 80% to 90% of primary kidney cancers are RCCs.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c002--> Four main types differ in cell of origin and in outcome.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c003--> Incidence peaks in the sixth decade.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c004--> Men are affected about twice as often as women.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c005-->

<aside class="edition">Types, frequencies and grades follow one textbook edition; check current WHO manuals.</aside>

- Clear cell: 70% to 80% of cases.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c006-->
- Papillary: 10% to 15%.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c007-->
- Chromophobe: about 5%.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c008-->
- Collecting duct: about 1%.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c009-->

## Etiology and risk factors
**Which factors raise the risk of RCC?** About 5% of RCCs are inherited; the large majority are sporadic.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c010--> One RCC in three is linked to tobacco, smoked or chewed.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c011--> RCC, especially papillary, can follow inherited or acquired cystic kidney disease.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c012--> Analgesic nephropathy is another associated condition.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c013--> A relative with RCC raises one's own risk four- to fivefold.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c014--> Hereditary RCC strikes younger patients than the sporadic form and often involves both kidneys at several sites.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c015-->

**Which inherited syndromes carry RCC?**
- VHL syndrome, autosomal dominant, combines clear cell RCC and pheochromocytoma with cerebellar and retinal vascular tumors and organ cysts.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c016-->
- Hereditary papillary RCC is autosomal dominant: both kidneys develop multiple papillary tumors.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c017-->
- Birt–Hogg–Dubé (BHD) syndrome puts patients at risk of multifocal chromophobe RCC in both kidneys.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c018-->

## Pathogenesis
**How does losing VHL lead to clear cell RCC?** The VHL tumor suppressor gene lies at 3p25.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c019--> Loss of one VHL allele is found in 98% of sporadic clear cell RCCs, nearly all.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c020--> Without working VHL, hypoxia-inducible factor-α (HIF-α) builds up.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c021--> HIF-α buildup turns up genes whose products drive kinase pathways.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c022--> Drugs that block kinases or mTOR hit these pathways and help treat RCC.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c023-->

**What drives the other types?**
- In hereditary papillary RCC the implicated gene is MET at 7q31, not VHL.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c024-->
- Many hereditary papillary RCCs lose the Y chromosome and carry extra copies of chromosomes 7, 16 and 17.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c025-->
- Chromophobe RCC arises from intercalated cells of the collecting ducts.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c026-->
- Collecting duct RCC starts in the medullary ducts of Bellini.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c027-->
- The BHD gene product folliculin (FLCN) seems to be a tumor suppressor; germline inactivating mutations occur in BHD syndrome.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c028-->

## Gross morphology
**What does a clear cell RCC look like on the cut surface?** Clear cell RCC is solid, sometimes partly cystic, with a yellow-orange cut surface.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c029--> Hemorrhage and necrosis often occur focally.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c030--> The yellow areas are tumor cells rich in lipid.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c031-->
## Microscopic morphology
**What does each type look like?**
- Clear cell RCC arises from proximal tubular epithelium.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c032--> Under the light microscope the cytoplasm looks clear because its lipid and glycogen are lost when the tissue is processed.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c033--> Tumor cells form round or elongated nests ringed by vessels.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c034--> Pleomorphism is slight.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c035-->
- Fibrovascular stalks carry the tumor cells of papillary RCC.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c036--> It also arises from proximal tubular epithelium.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c037--> Type 1 cells are small and blue-purple; type 2 cells are large and pink.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c038--> Type 2 has the worse prognosis and is more aggressive than type 1.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c039-->
- Chromophobe RCC cells have sharp borders, and the mix of pale and pink granular cells resembles plant cells.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c040--> The cell center looks pale because mucopolysaccharide vesicles shove organelles aside.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c041-->
- Collecting duct RCC has hobnail cuboidal cells in a single layer lining tubules and papillae.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c042-->
- Nearly all renal medullary carcinomas, a collecting duct variant, occur with sickle cell trait or disease.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c043-->
- A worse prognosis goes with sarcomatoid change, which can occur in any RCC type.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c044-->

**How is the nuclear grade set?** Clear cell and papillary RCC are where WHO/ISUP nuclear grading predicts outcome best.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c045-->

<aside class="edition">

- Grade 1: at 400× the nucleoli are basophilic and either absent or hard to see.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c046-->
- Grade 2: pink nucleoli stand out at 400× but are only just visible at 100×.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c047-->
- Grade 3: the nucleoli are obvious and pink already at 100×.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c048-->
- Grade 4: sarcomatoid or rhabdoid differentiation, extreme nuclear pleomorphism, or multinucleated giant cells.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c049-->

</aside>

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings
**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** the reference pages cover no specimen, sampling, IHC or electron microscopy.
- Light microscopy shows the cell type, growth pattern and nucleoli used to assign type and grade.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c050-->
- Chromophobe RCC is Hale colloidal iron positive, marking the mucopolysaccharide in its vesicles.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c051-->
- The short arm of chromosome 3 may be deleted or show loss of heterozygosity in sporadic RCC tissue.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c052-->

## Clinical presentation
**How does RCC present?** The most frequent presenting sign is blood in the urine (hematuria).<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c053--> Imaging done for another reason often finds the tumor by chance.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c054--> Fewer than 10% of patients have the full classic triad: hematuria, flank pain and a palpable mass.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c055--> Called a "great mimic", RCC can cause fever and paraneoplastic syndromes through ectopic hormones.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c056-->

**Which paraneoplastic syndromes occur?**
- Hyperparathyroid symptoms can come from a PTH-like substance.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c057-->
- Erythropoietin from the tumor produces erythrocytosis.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c058-->
- Renin secretion makes the patient hypertensive.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c059-->

Many patients first seek care for a metastasis, such as a seizure from a brain deposit or a cough from a lung deposit.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c060-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**How is RCC found?** Abdominal imaging often detects RCC incidentally.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c061--> The reference pages give no imaging criteria or laboratory work-up.

## Complications and prognosis
**What decides the outcome?** Nuclear grade, histologic type, tumor size and spread determine the outcome.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c062--> No other prognostic factor matters more than stage.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c063--> Five-year survival is 90% for tumors confined to the renal capsule.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c064--> With distant metastases it is 30%.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c065--> Clear cell RCC has a poorer outlook than papillary and chromophobe.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c066--> Collecting duct tumors do worse than clear cell tumors.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c067--> Survival beyond 1 year is rare when sarcomatoid features are prominent.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c068--> Metastases go most often to the lungs and bones.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c069--> The reference pages give no TNM categories.

## High-yield summary
- **Clear cell** is the most common type.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c072-->
- Loss of **VHL** with **HIF-α** buildup underlies clear cell tumorigenesis.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c073-->
- **Hematuria** is the most frequent presenting sign.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c074-->
- RCC can secrete **renin**, **erythropoietin** or a **PTH-like** substance.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c075-->
- **Stage** is the best prognostic factor.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c076-->
- **Sarcomatoid** change and the **collecting duct** type signal a worse prognosis.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c077-->
- **Nucleoli** set ISUP grades 1 to 3; grade 4 rests on extreme pleomorphism or sarcomatoid or rhabdoid change.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c078-->
- **Hale colloidal iron** stains chromophobe RCC.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c079-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Is the cytoplasm of clear cell RCC really empty?** No. Lipid and glycogen are lost during tissue processing.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c080-->

**Does the classic triad usually appear?** No. Fewer than 10% of patients show all three signs.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c081-->

**Is RCC always sporadic?** No. About 5% are inherited, and these tend to appear in younger patients.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c082-->

**Do all RCC types come from the same cell?** No. Clear cell and papillary arise from the proximal tubule, chromophobe from intercalated cells, collecting duct from the ducts of Bellini.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c083-->

**Do all papillary RCCs behave alike?** No. Type 2 is the more aggressive one.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c084-->

## Mnemonics
**CPCC, "Cats Prefer Comfy Couches":** clear cell, papillary, chromophobe, collecting duct, by falling frequency.<!--c:renal-cell-carcinoma-c085-->
