## Definition and epidemiology

**What is Buerger disease, and who gets it?** In Buerger disease, small and medium-sized arteries toward the ends of the arms and legs are blocked by a mix of inflammation and thrombus.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c001--> Women are now diagnosed too, unlike the old picture of a young or middle-aged heavy-smoking man.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c002--> It is seen more often in Asia and around the Mediterranean, Middle East included.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c003-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the artery look like under the microscope?** First, medium and small arteries become acutely inflamed (arteritis); a thrombus follows.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c004--> The same neutrophils also reach the veins and nerves beside the artery.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c005--> The lumen ends up obliterated by thrombus, which forms once the endothelium is involved.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c006--> Look for wall microabscesses, where Langhans giant cells and fibroblasts rim a neutrophil core.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c007--> Their presence tells the lesion apart from a thrombus complicating atherosclerosis.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c008--> Late lesions differ: organization of the thrombi is complete, while recanalization is only partial.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c009-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which clinical clues point to the diagnosis?** Intermittent claudication, exertional muscle cramping relieved by rest, usually begins at 25 to 40 years.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c010--> A finger or toe may first show a painful ulcer, and the tip can then be destroyed.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c011--> Gangrene of an extremity, which can lead to amputation, may complicate even early lesions.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c012--> Hands and feet on both sides may be lost gradually if smoking continues.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c013-->

## High-yield summary

- Inflammation occludes **medium and small arteries** in the distal limbs.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c015-->
- Classic patient: a young or middle-aged **heavy smoker**.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c016-->
- Histology: **thrombosing vasculitis** with neutrophilic **microabscesses** and Langhans giant cells.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c017-->
- Resuming smoking can bring an exacerbation; stopping can bring **remission**.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c018-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is Buerger disease only a disease of men?** No. Women are affected too, even though almost all earlier cases were in men.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c019-->

**Do we know how tobacco causes it?** No. Smoking clearly matters, since remission can follow quitting, but the mechanism by which tobacco smoke acts remains obscure.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c020-->

**Is the thrombus the same as in atherosclerosis?** No. A neutrophil-centered microabscess with giant cells in the wall is what sets it apart.<!--c:thromboangiitis-obliterans-c021-->
