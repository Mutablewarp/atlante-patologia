## Definition and epidemiology

**What is gastric MALT lymphoma?** It is a B-cell tumor of the lymphoid tissue in the stomach mucosa, classed as an extranodal marginal zone lymphoma.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c001--> Gastric lymphomas are most often of the MALT type.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c002--> Among extranodal sites of lymphoma the gastrointestinal tract ranks first.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c003--> Within that tract, the stomach is the part most often affected.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c004--> Of all B-cell lymphomas, 5% to 10% are MALT lymphomas.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c005--> The median age at presentation is 60 years, and most patients are adults.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What sets it off?** Long-standing inflammation from infection or autoimmunity usually comes first.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c007--> In the stomach, *Helicobacter pylori* gastritis is the classic infectious setting.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does an inflammatory reaction turn into a lymphoma?** First comes a benign, polyclonal B-cell response.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c009--> Gradually, genetic damage in those B cells, such as mutations and chromosomal abnormalities, makes them malignant.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c010--> The resulting tumor is monoclonal.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c011--> Antibiotics that clear *H. pylori* can make a gastric MALT lymphoma regress, provided it is still in its earliest phase.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c012--> Once a tumor has acquired t(11;18)(q21;q21), which happens in some lymphomas, eradication therapy no longer works.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c013--> Antibiotics alone also fail once a tumor has picked up some other translocation.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c014--> Extra genetic hits can let the tumor spread elsewhere or turn into DLBCL.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What does it look like to the naked eye?** Rather than forming a discrete mass, the lymphoma spreads through a large area of the mucosa.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c016--> Fine mucosal detail is lost in the infiltrated area.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the pathologist see?** Early on, extra marginal zone cells invade germinal centers and ring the reactive follicle.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c018--> Lymphoid cells that look alike crowd the lamina propria.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c019--> Some tumor cells are monocytoid lymphocytes, mid-sized and rich in cytoplasm.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c020--> Some tumor cells may differentiate toward plasma cells.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c021--> Lymphoepithelial lesions result when tumor cells penetrate the epithelium of glands.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c022-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- Lymphoepithelial lesions, characteristic of the tumor, can be seen on a biopsy of the stomach.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c023-->
- No single immunophenotype is specific for the tumor.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c024-->
- The tumor cells are mostly IgM-positive and restricted to one light chain.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c025-->
- The cells carry B-cell antigens.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c026-->
- Cyclin D1, CD23, CD10 and CD5 are all absent, a pattern that helps separate the tumor from other small B-cell lymphomas.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c027-->
- If the gastric infiltrate is subtle, the diagnosis can be supported by finding clonal IgH gene rearrangement and common cytogenetic abnormalities.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c028-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it behave in the patient?** MALT lymphoma can also arise outside the stomach, for example in the salivary glands, thyroid or skin.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c029--> It tends to stay in one place for a long time.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c030--> Clinically the course is indolent.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c031-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it told apart from gastritis?** Chronic gastritis with lymphoid hyperplasia can look similar to MALT lymphoma.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c032--> Glands invaded by lymphocytes point to lymphoma.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c033--> So does a lamina propria filled by one uniform type of monocytoid cell.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c034-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Can it regress, and when does it become dangerous?** Removing *H. pylori* puts as many as 80% of the associated gastric lymphomas into remission.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c035--> A large B-cell lymphoma can occasionally develop from a MALT lymphoma that was indolent.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c036--> When the tumor turns into DLBCL, it behaves more aggressively.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c037-->

## High-yield summary

- ***H. pylori* gastritis** is the prototypical trigger.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c043-->
- **Lymphoepithelial lesions**, lymphocytes invading gastric glands, are the hallmark.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c044-->
- Early tumors may **regress after eradication** of *H. pylori*.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c045-->
- Tumors with **t(11;18)(q21;q21)** no longer respond to eradication.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c046-->
- More genetic lesions can lead to **transformation to DLBCL**.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c047-->
- The immunophenotype is not specific, but the cells are **cyclin D1-, CD23-, CD10- and CD5-negative**.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c048-->
- It is the most common **gastric lymphoma** and its course is indolent.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c049-->
- **Clonal IgH rearrangement** helps when the infiltrate is subtle.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c050-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does eradication cure every gastric MALT lymphoma?** No. With t(11;18) or another acquired translocation, antibiotics by themselves fail.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c051-->

**Is MALT lymphoma an aggressive tumor?** No. It is slow and tends to stay in place; it turns aggressive when it becomes DLBCL.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c052-->

**Is a lymphoid infiltrate in the gastric mucosa always gastritis?** No. Gastritis with lymphoid hyperplasia can mimic lymphoma, and lymphoepithelial lesions are the clue.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c053-->

**Are all gastric lymphomas MALT lymphomas?** No. DLBCL is the other main type, and together the two account for most gastric lymphomas.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c054-->

## Mnemonics

**HELP:** *H. pylori* drives it, Epithelium is invaded by lymphocytes (lymphoepithelial lesions), Localized for long and indolent, Pills (antibiotics) can make early tumors regress.<!--c:gastric-malt-lymphoma-c055-->
