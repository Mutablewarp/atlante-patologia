## Definition and epidemiology

**What are myocardial hypertrophy and heart failure, and how are they linked?** Hypertrophy is adaptive: enlarged myocytes hold wall stress down and contract harder.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c001--> Heart failure produces its signs and symptoms because the heart keeps output up only by filling at a higher pressure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c002--> Compensation lets many patients live with heart failure for years.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c003--> Ejection fraction is the share of end-diastolic blood that a ventricle ejects; in a normal ventricle it is about 60%.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What overloads or damages the heart?** Heart failure can follow structural damage to the heart, or a workload that stays raised for a long time.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c005--> No other cause of heart failure is as common as coronary artery disease.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c006--> Hypertension and valve disease are also major causes of heart failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c007--> Leaky valves and injured myocardium overload the heart with volume, stenotic valves and hypertension with pressure; pathologic hypertrophy compensates for either.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c009--> Coronary disease and hypertension load the left ventricle first, which is why the left side fails more often.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c011--> Lung disease or pulmonary hypertension can cause right-sided failure alone, but usually it follows left-sided failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the heart compensate, and why does it end up failing?** By the Frank–Starling mechanism, a healthy ventricle that fills more ejects more blood per beat, up to a limit.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c013--> A failing heart needs a high atrial filling pressure to deliver a given stroke volume.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c016-->

**Which signals drive hypertrophy?**
- Stretched or overloaded myocytes release angiotensin II and endothelin-1, which bind receptors and drive hypertrophy.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c017-->
- Over-stimulated β-receptors lose sensitivity as myocytes enlarge, and are tied to the shift to failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c019-->
- Scarring between the myocytes (interstitial fibrosis) accompanies pathologic hypertrophy and nearly every form of failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c024-->
- Apoptosis may help push a compensated heart into failure, and it is more frequent in pathologic hypertrophy.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c028-->

**How does failure produce systemic fluid retention?** Low output starves the kidney of flow, and its renin–angiotensin–aldosterone response makes the body hold sodium and fluid.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c030--> Fluid leaves the capillaries as edema because capillary pressure is up while plasma oncotic pressure is down.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c032-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the failing heart look like?** Ventricular hypertrophy accompanies nearly all chronic heart failure; apart from lesions of the underlying disease, nothing else about the failing heart is specific.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c033--> In concentric hypertrophy the wall gets thicker while the chamber stays about the same size.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c034--> Heart failure is often present clinically when hypertrophy is eccentric, with a dilated chamber and lengthened myocytes.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c035--> The right ventricle usually thickens once left-sided failure overloads it; before then, only the left may be enlarged.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c037--> Most patients with severe systolic failure have clearly widened ventricles.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c038--> Nutmeg liver looks mottled on section: congested central veins are dark red, the tissue around them yellow.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c039-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes at the microscope?** Large dark nuclei and extra binucleate myocytes in hypertrophy come from new DNA synthesis and nuclear division.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c040--> Myocytolysis is the vacuolated look of chronically injured myocytes that have traded sarcomeres for cytosol and glycogen.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c041--> Heart failure cells are alveolar macrophages carrying iron pigment (hemosiderin) from red cells that leaked out of congested lung capillaries.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c044-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** an autopsy shows a large heart with dilated chambers.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c047-->
- Under the microscope, look for enlarged dark nuclei, binucleated cells and vacuolated myocytes.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c048-->
- Blood BNP level serves clinicians as a marker of how severe the failure is.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c050-->
- No special stain, immunohistochemistry or electron microscopy is covered here.

## Clinical presentation

**What do patients complain of, and what do doctors find?** Left-sided failure causes dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c051--> Lying flat moves blood out of the legs and into the lungs, which causes orthopnea and nocturnal attacks; exertional dyspnea reflects pulmonary congestion.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c052--> In pulmonary edema, fluid-filled alveoli expand with a crackle that the doctor hears as rales.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c053--> Right-sided failure congests the liver, which becomes big and sore, and the spleen; neck veins bulge, the legs pit on pressure and the abdomen may fill with fluid (ascites).<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c054--> Sodium and water retention is typical of left-sided failure: poor renal perfusion triggers renin–angiotensin–aldosterone activity.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c055--> Confusion and memory loss can result when too little blood reaches the brain.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c056--> Two mechanisms act together: organs get too little blood (forward failure), but congestion upstream (backward failure) explains most symptoms.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c058--> HFpEF, with normal ejection fraction, is nearly half of failure; it mostly affects older people with hypertension, more often women.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c059-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is failure recognized and typed?** HFpEF is defined by an ejection fraction of 50% or more.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c061-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** A heart badly hit at the outset may go into acute, life-threatening cardiogenic shock.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c064--> Clinical failure often goes with eccentric remodeling, which develops if injury continues: a dilated chamber and longer myocytes.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c065--> Right-sided failure eventually follows long-standing left-sided failure, with pulmonary hypertension as the link.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c067--> Within a year of hospital admission for congestive failure, half of patients die.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c068--> β-blockers lower mortality in advanced failure, and few treatments do it better.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c069-->

## High-yield summary

- **Concentric** hypertrophy: thicker wall, chamber not proportionately larger; **eccentric** hypertrophy: dilated chamber and longer myocytes.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c075-->
- In **heart failure**, getting a normal stroke volume takes an abnormally high atrial pressure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c076-->
- **Left-sided failure** gives pulmonary congestion, orthopnea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c077-->
- **Right-sided failure** gives hepatomegaly, ascites, leg edema and distended neck veins.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c078-->
- **Nutmeg liver** and **heart failure cells** mark chronic congestion of liver and lung.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c079-->
- The commonest cause of failure is **coronary artery disease**.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c080-->
- Blood **BNP** tracks the severity of failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c081-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hypertrophy always harmful?** No. An athlete's physiologic hypertrophy is proportional and efficient, unlike pathologic hypertrophy.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c082-->

**Does heart failure mean the heart has stopped pumping?** No. In absolute terms, output is usually preserved; what rises is the filling pressure needed to preserve it.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c083-->

**Does failure always mean a low ejection fraction?** No. HFpEF has a normal ejection fraction.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c084-->

**Is right-sided failure always a lung problem?** No. It most commonly complicates left-sided failure.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c085-->

**Are heart failure cells cardiac cells?** No. They are alveolar macrophages full of hemosiderin.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c086-->

## Mnemonics

**LEFT, RIGHT:** LEFT-sided failure leaves the Lungs wet, RIGHT-sided failure soaks the Rest of the body.<!--c:cardiac-hypertrophy-and-heart-failure-c087-->
