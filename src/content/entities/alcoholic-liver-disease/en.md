## Definition and epidemiology

**What is alcoholic liver disease?** Excess ethanol damages the liver, both directly and indirectly.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c001--> Three lesions make up the disease: steatosis, acute alcoholic hepatitis and cirrhosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c002--> The three can coexist in any combination and may be unrelated, though they usually appear in sequence.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c003--> Cirrhosis spares most heavy drinkers; lifetime intake (years times daily amount) sets the odds.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c004--> Cirrhosis affects about 15% of alcoholics.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c005-->
## Etiology and risk factors

**How much alcohol is enough, and who is more at risk?** The lowest daily intake linked to chronic liver disease is about 20 g of alcohol in women and 40 g in men.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c007--> Cirrhosis generally takes over 10 years of drinking at that intake.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c008--> Women are harmed more easily, for unclear reasons.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c009--> Hepatitis C infection raises the chance of liver disease in heavy drinkers.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c011--> Alcoholics more often than controls have serologic evidence of HBV.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c012-->
## Pathogenesis

**How does ethanol damage the liver?** Most ingested ethanol reaches acetaldehyde and acetate through cytosolic alcohol dehydrogenase in the liver.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c014--> Dietary fat supplies most of the lipid that accumulates in the liver of chronic drinkers.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c016--> Free fatty acids arrive in the liver in greater amounts because ethanol drives lipolysis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c017--> Fat then stays in the cell because ethanol slows mitochondrial oxidation of fatty acids and impairs lipoprotein release.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c019--> Inside hepatocytes it also boosts the synthesis of fatty acids and triglycerides.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c018--> Steatosis reverses fully and fast once drinking stops, so it is driven by alcohol intake.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c020--> Chronic drinking induces cytochrome P450 enzymes, which makes drugs with toxic metabolites, such as acetaminophen, more dangerous.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c022-->

**Why does hepatitis develop in some drinkers?** Nobody knows.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c023--> A steady drinker with years of mild fatty liver can suddenly develop hepatitis, hinting at a cofactor not yet found.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c024--> The fat itself has not been shown to make alcoholic hepatitis more likely.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c025--> Long-term alcohol activates stellate cells; the collagen they deposit in the sinusoids probably contributes to cirrhosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c026-->
## Gross morphology

**What does the liver look like?** A fatty liver is yellow and enlarged.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c028--> In cirrhosis, the parenchyma is divided into hepatocellular nodules circled by fibrous septa.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c030-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the lesions look like on H&E?**
- **Steatosis:** in heavy cases most hepatocytes are swollen with merged fat droplets; in mild cases only a few cells show small ones.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c031--> At the extreme the nucleus is flattened to the cell edge and the cell looks clear, like an adipocyte.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c032-->
- **Steatohepatitis, necrosis:** hepatocyte necrosis is mainly centrilobular.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c033--> Swelling varies between hepatocytes, so the tissue looks patchy.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c034-->
- **Steatohepatitis, Mallory–Denk bodies** ([[g:mallory-denk-bodies]]): look near the nucleus for pink cytoplasmic clumps or tangled threads: these are the Mallory–Denk bodies.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c035-->
- **Steatohepatitis, neutrophils:** satellitosis means neutrophils clustering around ballooned hepatocytes, especially those with these inclusions.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c036--> Inflammatory cells, mostly neutrophils, also lie more diffusely in the lobule.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c037-->
- **Architecture** is typically preserved.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c038-->
- **Fibrosis:** collagen builds up most around central veins and is present in every case.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c039--> It also surrounds single hepatocytes (pericellular fibrosis).<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c040--> In severe disease the central venule is scarred shut, a change called central hyaline sclerosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c041-->
- **Portal tracts** vary: some are enlarged by mononuclear cells and ductular reaction, others look nearly normal.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c042-->
- **Cirrhosis:** the liver is rebuilt as regenerated nodules separated by fibrous septa after hepatocytes die and scar.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c044--> Fatty liver and alcoholic hepatitis are often seen alongside it.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c045-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look for fat that displaces nuclei, swollen hepatocytes, Mallory–Denk bodies and neutrophils around them.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c046-->
- Trichrome stains collagen blue, outlining the pericellular scar around the central vein; NASH mimics this.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c047-->
- Electron microscopy shows the inclusions are clumps of cytokeratin intermediate filaments.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c048-->
- The source pages do not cover biopsy sampling, immunohistochemistry or molecular tests.

## Clinical presentation

**What do patients feel and show?** A fatty liver without complications can look dramatic yet cause few symptoms.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c050--> Alcoholic hepatitis presents with fever and jaundice.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c051--> Leukocytosis is common.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c053--> Alcoholic hepatitis often strikes suddenly, without any change in drinking.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c054--> Portal hypertension without cirrhosis is a frequent companion of central hyaline sclerosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c055-->
## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which lab pattern points to alcoholic hepatitis?** Compared with viral hepatitis, the transaminase rise in alcoholic hepatitis is smaller, though still moderate.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c057--> AST usually stays under 400.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c058--> AST is typically about twice ALT, a 2:1 ratio.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c059--> Serum alkaline phosphatase tends to be raised.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c060--> Severe disease with a prolonged prothrombin time often means a poor outcome.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c061-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens over time?** Alcoholic fatty liver does not lead to worse disease by itself, and it can be fully reversed.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c062--> Stopping alcohol is the best treatment.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c063--> The more severe the injury to liver cells, the worse the outcome of alcoholic hepatitis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c064--> Some patients rapidly develop fatal liver failure.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c065--> About 10% of patients die during the acute stage of alcoholic hepatitis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c066--> Abstinence after the acute episode is followed by recovery in most patients.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c067--> Up to 70% of patients who do not give up alcohol end up with cirrhosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c068--> Acute alcoholic hepatitis has no specific treatment.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c069--> Corticosteroids are often given when neither infection nor renal failure is present, because they reduce short-term mortality.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c070--> Abstinence markedly improves prognosis in established alcoholic cirrhosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c072--> Alcoholic liver disease is frequently the reason for a transplant, and many patients still reach end-stage disease.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c073-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What can look like alcoholic liver disease?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease\|NAFLD and NASH]] | The two share centrilobular fibrosis, Mallory–Denk bodies and steatosis on histology.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c074--> Insulin resistance is probably the leading risk factor.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c075--> | Alcohol history against metabolic risk profile |
| [[acute-viral-hepatitis\|Viral hepatitis]] | Aminotransferases rise higher than in alcoholic hepatitis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c076--> | Viral serology, AST and ALT levels |
| [[wilson-disease]] | Mallory–Denk bodies also occur here, so they do not prove an alcohol cause.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c077--> | Clinical context, not the inclusions alone |
| [[cirrhosis\|Cryptogenic cirrhosis]] | Many cryptogenic cirrhoses are probably NAFLD whose fat has vanished as cirrhosis developed.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c078--> | Metabolic and alcohol history |

## High-yield summary

- Three lesions: **fatty liver**, **alcoholic hepatitis** and **cirrhosis**, usually in sequence.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c079-->
- **Steatosis** is fully **reversible** if alcohol stops.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c080-->
- Alcoholic hepatitis shows **Mallory–Denk bodies**, **neutrophils** around damaged cells and **pericellular fibrosis**.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c081-->
- The injury is **centrilobular**, with **central hyaline sclerosis** in severe cases.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c082-->
- **AST:ALT is about 2:1** and AST usually stays under 400.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c083-->
- **Cirrhosis** occurs in about **15%** of alcoholics.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c084-->
- **Abstinence** reverses fatty liver and improves prognosis even in cirrhosis.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c085-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is fatty liver specific to alcohol?** No. NAFLD and many other conditions cause it too, though alcohol is the classic cause.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c086-->

**Do Mallory–Denk bodies prove alcoholic liver disease?** No. NASH and Wilson disease show them too, so they point toward alcohol without proving it.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c087-->

**Does every heavy drinker get cirrhosis?** No. Only a minority do, and the risk follows the lifetime dose.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c088-->

**Does the fat itself cause alcoholic hepatitis?** There is no evidence that it does.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c089-->

**Does alcoholic fatty liver progress to cirrhosis by itself?** No. Fatty liver alone does not advance to worse disease.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c090-->

## Mnemonics

**MAN** for alcoholic hepatitis: **M**allory–Denk bodies, **A**ST above ALT (2:1), **N**eutrophils around the cells.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c091-->

**FHC**, "Few Heavy Cocktails": **F**at, **H**epatitis, **C**irrhosis, the usual order of the three lesions.<!--c:alcoholic-liver-disease-c092-->
