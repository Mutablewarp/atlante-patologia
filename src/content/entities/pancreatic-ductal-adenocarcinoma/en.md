## Definition and epidemiology

**What is pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and how common is it?** It is a gland-forming cancer of the pancreatic ducts, with mucin-producing cells.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c001--> "Pancreatic cancer" usually means this tumor, which makes up about 85% of adult pancreatic neoplasms.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c002--> It ranks fourth among US cancer deaths in both sexes.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c003--> Five-year survival is below 8%.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c004--> The incidence peaks after age 60.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c005--> In old age the sexes are nearly equal, but younger men predominate by up to 3:1.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What raises the risk?**

- Smoking raises the risk two- to threefold.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c007-->
- About a quarter of cases are attributed to smoking.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c008-->
- Diabetes of 5 years or more doubles the risk.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c009-->
- In some patients the tumor comes first and the diabetes follows.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c010-->
- Hereditary and tropical calcifying pancreatitis are the forms with the firmer cancer association.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c011-->
- Common chronic pancreatitis, such as the alcoholic kind, probably explains few cancers.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c012-->
- A duct-blocking cancer can itself cause obstructive pancreatitis, blurring cause and effect.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c013-->
- Dietary nitrates, fat and meat may raise the risk.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c014-->
- Fruit and vegetables seem to protect against it.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c015-->
- Coffee and alcohol are not convincingly linked to it.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c016-->
- Exposure to dry-cleaning agents or coal gas at work goes with a higher incidence.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c017-->
- Inherited syndromes such as Peutz–Jeghers syndrome (STK11) and hereditary breast-ovarian cancer syndrome (BRCA2) raise the risk; see [[inherited-cancer-syndromes]].<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c018-->
- Most cases have no known germline mutation.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c019-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a normal duct become an invasive cancer?** PanIN is the duct lesion behind the stepwise model: abnormal mucin-rich epithelium has displaced the normal one.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c020--> There are two PanIN grades; the higher shows more architectural and cellular disorder.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c021--> Telomere shortening and a KRAS oncogene mutation that switches it on both appear early, already in low-grade PanIN.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c022--> KRAS is mutated in up to 95% of cases.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c023--> CDKN2A (p16), TP53 and SMAD4 are lost later (see [[oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes]]).<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c024--> CDKN2A is inactivated in about 95% of cases.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c025--> TP53 is altered in 50% to 75%.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c026--> SMAD4, a TGF-β signaling gene, is lost in about 55%.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c027--> EGF and its receptor are among the overactive growth factor signals.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c028--> BRCA2 is disabled by mutation in up to 7% of tumors.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c029--> Some cases arise from mucinous cystic or intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (see [[pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms]]).<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c030-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does it grow, and what does it look like?** 60% to 70% arise in the head, 10% to 15% in the tail, about 10% in the body.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c031--> Its borders are indistinct, and the gray, firm mass can be hard to tell from the fibrosis of pancreatitis.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c032--> A head tumor can press on the bile duct or ampulla and block bile flow.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c033--> It may also invade the duodenal wall.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c034--> Obstruction of the main pancreatic duct shrinks the body and tail.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c035--> Tail tumors can reach the spleen or stomach.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c036--> Body and tail tumors are larger and more spread at diagnosis, since head tumors announce themselves earlier.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c037--> The liver and regional lymph nodes are frequent metastatic sites.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c038--> Most cases are unresectable once the tumor reaches distant sites such as the peritoneum or lungs.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c039-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the glands and the stroma look like?** Glands lined by mucin-secreting cells are the pattern in the over 75% of tumors graded well to moderately differentiated.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c040--> Some glands look so bland that they pass for benign ducts, though nuclei can be markedly atypical.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c041--> Desmoplastic stroma usually surrounds the glands.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c042--> Growth is widely infiltrative, so tumor cells often lie beyond the visible margin.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c043--> Typically the tumor invades nerves, hence pain early and lasting.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c044--> Single cells or sheets mark the poorly differentiated remaining 25%.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c045--> Variants include colloid and adenosquamous carcinoma.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c046-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- Tumor sections show malignant glands invading fat, set in fibrous tissue.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c047-->
- The source pages do not describe specimen types, special stains or IHC for ductal adenocarcinoma.
- Acinar cell carcinoma, by contrast, can be shown by IHC to make exocrine enzymes.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c048-->
- Some acinar carcinomas carry BRAF fusions or APC/beta-catenin pathway changes; the usual ductal mutations are absent.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c049-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Patients often have epigastric pain radiating to the back, with weight loss and poor appetite.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c050--> Body and tail tumors rarely cause painless jaundice (under 10%), unlike head tumors (about half).<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c051--> A bile duct blocked by tumor can swell the gallbladder without pain, with jaundice: Courvoisier sign.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c052--> Migratory thrombophlebitis (Trousseau syndrome) affects about 10% of patients, mostly with body or tail tumors.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c053--> Other cancers can cause it too, but it can be the first sign of pancreatic cancer.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c054--> Hidden cancer should be sought whenever a healthy person has thrombophlebitis without cause.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c055--> Proposed mechanisms are procoagulant vesicles shed by tumor cells and a factor X–activating tumor protease.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c056-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the diagnosis?** Serum CA19-9 is not specific: it is usually raised in this cancer, and it is a Lewis blood group antigen.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c057--> A head carcinoma typically dilates the bile duct and pancreatic duct together.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c058--> The tumor stays silent until well advanced, so early detection is rare.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c059-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** Curative surgery is uncommon, since most patients already have metastases or major vessel involvement at diagnosis.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c060--> Half of patients have died by 6 months after diagnosis.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c061--> Death follows a steady decline with cachexia and uncontrollable pain.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c062--> The liver and peritoneum are among the metastatic sites.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c063-->

| Acinar cell carcinoma | Only 1% to 2% of pancreatic carcinomas.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c066--> Lacking desmoplastic stroma, they form large masses with a clear boundary.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c067--> Lipase hypersecretion can cause eosinophilia, joint pain and fat necrosis.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c068--> | IHC for exocrine enzymes |
| Pancreatoblastoma | Close relative of acinar carcinoma, mostly found in the first ten years of life.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c069--> It may occur with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c070--> | Age and clinical setting |
| Benign pancreatic ducts | Bland well-differentiated malignant glands can mimic them.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c071--> | Look for infiltration and desmoplastic stroma |

## High-yield summary

- **KRAS** mutation is an early event in low-grade **PanIN**; later come **CDKN2A, TP53 and SMAD4** losses.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c072-->
- **Head** tumors (60% to 70%) are smaller at diagnosis than body and tail tumors.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c073-->
- **Desmoplastic stroma** and **perineural invasion** are typical; nerve invasion explains the early pain.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c074-->
- **Trousseau syndrome** (migratory thrombophlebitis) can be the first sign.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c075-->
- **Acinar cell carcinoma** lacks desmoplasia and may cause a lipase syndrome.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c076-->
- **Pancreatoblastoma** is a childhood tumor.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c077-->
- Five-year survival is **below 8%**.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c078-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a raised CA19-9 prove pancreatic cancer?** No. It is usually raised but not specific.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c079-->

**Is Trousseau syndrome unique to pancreatic cancer?** No. It is often the first sign of pancreatic cancer, but other cancers can cause it.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c080-->

**Is jaundice always present?** No. It is common in head tumors but rare in body or tail tumors.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c081-->

**Do alcohol and coffee cause pancreatic cancer?** No convincing link has been shown.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c082--> Smoking, by contrast, raises the risk two- to threefold.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c083-->

**Is every pancreatic cancer a ductal adenocarcinoma?** No. Acinar cell carcinoma is rare, and pancreatoblastoma occurs in children.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c084-->

## Mnemonics

**KCTS, "Keep Calm, Tumors Spread":** KRAS is hit first, then CDKN2A, TP53 and SMAD4 follow.<!--c:pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma-c085-->
