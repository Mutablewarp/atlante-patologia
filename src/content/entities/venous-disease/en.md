## Definition and epidemiology

**What are varicose veins and DVT?** The saphenous system is the usual site of varicose veins: enlarged, twisting superficial leg veins.<!--c:venous-disease-c001--> Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a thrombus in the deep veins, mainly of the legs.<!--c:venous-disease-c002--> Varicose veins are common, but only some people develop symptoms.<!--c:venous-disease-c003-->

**Are thrombophlebitis and phlebothrombosis the same?** A local bacterial infection often inflames a vein, which then clots: thrombophlebitis.<!--c:venous-disease-c004--> A clot with no infectious or inflammatory trigger is phlebothrombosis.<!--c:venous-disease-c005--> Inflammation accompanies only a minority of venous clots.<!--c:venous-disease-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What predisposes to varicose veins?** Women, particularly after pregnancy, are more affected; heredity, age, obesity and standing add risk.<!--c:venous-disease-c007--> Varices also follow impeded leg venous return, as in heart failure, pelvic tumors or thrombosed thigh and pelvic veins.<!--c:venous-disease-c008-->

**What predisposes to DVT?** Hypercoagulability, stasis and endothelial injury, as in arterial thrombosis.<!--c:venous-disease-c009-->
- Stasis: prolonged immobility (illness, surgery, travel) and heart failure.<!--c:venous-disease-c010-->
- Injury: infection, childbirth, surgery, trauma.<!--c:venous-disease-c011-->
- Hypercoagulability: inherited thrombophilia, cancer, late pregnancy, estrogen therapy.<!--c:venous-disease-c012-->
- Factor V Leiden is the commonest gene variant.<!--c:venous-disease-c013-->
- Strong but uncommon: lack of antithrombin, protein S or protein C.<!--c:venous-disease-c014-->

**Where else do varices occur?** Hemorrhoids are varices of the anal canal and rectum.<!--c:venous-disease-c015--> Obstructing rectal tumors, pregnancy and constipation contribute.<!--c:venous-disease-c016--> Cirrhosis is the main cause of the portal hypertension behind esophageal varices.<!--c:venous-disease-c017-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do varicose veins get worse?** Valve failure and vein widening feed each other; which starts first is unknown.<!--c:venous-disease-c018--> Each incompetent valve strains the segment below, so varicosity extends along the vein.<!--c:venous-disease-c019-->

**Why do esophageal varices form?** Portal hypertension swells the channels joining portal and systemic veins in the lower esophagus.<!--c:venous-disease-c020-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lesions look like?** Esophageal varices collapse at death, so the mucosa shows only bluish streaks.<!--c:venous-disease-c021--> Calf veins are the usual starting point of a DVT, often in valve sinuses.<!--c:venous-disease-c022--> Pelvic veins are the usual site outside the deep leg veins, which hold nearly all venous thrombosis.<!--c:venous-disease-c023-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does a varicose vein wall show?** The wall is uneven: stretched and thin in some segments, thick in others.<!--c:venous-disease-c024--> Thick segments reflect muscle overgrowth, scarring beneath the intima and absorbed old clots.<!--c:venous-disease-c025--> Valve cusps curl over, losing length and gaining thickness.<!--c:venous-disease-c026-->

**What happens to a venous thrombus?** Venous thrombi may dissolve or organize; in large ones, new channels restore some drainage.<!--c:venous-disease-c027-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Is any molecular test relevant?** A common mutation, prothrombin G20210A, adds only a mild risk.<!--c:venous-disease-c028-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do varicose veins present?** Most are a cosmetic problem.<!--c:venous-disease-c029--> Patients mainly report leg aching that eases on elevation and worsens on standing.<!--c:venous-disease-c030--> Severe cases can cause stasis dermatitis.<!--c:venous-disease-c031-->

**How does DVT present?** Small calf thrombi are usually silent.<!--c:venous-disease-c032--> Homan sign, calf tenderness on forced upward foot flexion, occurs in some patients.<!--c:venous-disease-c033--> A thrombus blocking the femoral or iliac vein congests the leg, which turns blue and swells.<!--c:venous-disease-c034--> Phlegmasia cerulea dolens can follow near-total obstruction of a limb's veins.<!--c:venous-disease-c035-->

**What about hemorrhoids?** They often bleed.<!--c:venous-disease-c036--> A thrombosed hemorrhoid hurts severely.<!--c:venous-disease-c037-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How are they diagnosed and treated?** Inspection alone diagnoses leg varicose veins.<!--c:venous-disease-c038--> Skin ulceration, unprovoked bleeding or wide clotting call for surgery.<!--c:venous-disease-c039--> Anticoagulants treat symptomatic DVT; selected patients also get thrombolysis.<!--c:venous-disease-c040--> An inferior vena cava filter may stop pulmonary emboli recurring.<!--c:venous-disease-c041-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Why is leg DVT an embolic source?** Deep leg veins are the origin of most pulmonary emboli.<!--c:venous-disease-c042--> The iliofemoral veins are where most fatal emboli originate.<!--c:venous-disease-c043--> Indwelling lines, the right heart and the pelvic plexus are further sources.<!--c:venous-disease-c044--> Classically, a bed-rest thrombus breaks off when a postoperative patient first gets up, causing sudden death.<!--c:venous-disease-c045-->

**What are the late consequences?** Thrombosis always damages valves, so lasting deep venous insufficiency nearly always follows.<!--c:venous-disease-c046--> Slow-healing ulcers above the inner ankle, and leg skin that is darkened, puffy and firm, mark extensive disease.<!--c:venous-disease-c047--> Mesenteric or hepatic vein thrombosis can be fatal.<!--c:venous-disease-c048--> In cirrhosis, bleeding esophageal varices can kill.<!--c:venous-disease-c049-->

## High-yield summary

- **Hypercoagulability, stasis and endothelial injury** cause DVT.<!--c:venous-disease-c053-->
- DVT is nearly always in **deep leg veins**; the danger is **pulmonary embolism**.<!--c:venous-disease-c054-->
- **Factor V Leiden** is the commonest gene variant.<!--c:venous-disease-c055-->
- **Esophageal varices** come from portal hypertension and can bleed fatally.<!--c:venous-disease-c056-->
- After DVT, **chronic venous insufficiency** is almost certain.<!--c:venous-disease-c057-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is inflammation the usual cause of venous thrombosis?** No. Most cases have no infection or inflammation.<!--c:venous-disease-c058-->

**Does DVT always cause symptoms?** No. Small calf thrombi are often silent.<!--c:venous-disease-c059-->

**Do normal legs exclude a venous embolic source?** No. Only some patients with pulmonary emboli show signs of DVT.<!--c:venous-disease-c060-->

## Mnemonics

**Slow, Sore, Sticky:** slow flow (stasis), sore wall (injury), sticky blood (hypercoagulability).<!--c:venous-disease-c061-->
