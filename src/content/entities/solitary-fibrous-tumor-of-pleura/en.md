## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a solitary fibrous tumor of the pleura?** It is a localized pleural neoplasm and not a common one.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c001--> Benign behavior is the rule, with only a few malignant cases.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c002--> The visceral pleura is the site of origin in about 80% of cases; the parietal pleura accounts for the remainder.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c003--> The peritoneum and pericardium can also give rise to similar tumors, as can other mesothelial surfaces.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Is it caused by asbestos?** No. The tumor is unrelated to asbestos exposure.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c005--> The reference pages name no other cause.

## Pathogenesis

**Which cells does it come from?** Mesothelial cells are not the origin; submesothelial connective tissue is.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c006--> The reference pages give no molecular mechanism.

## Gross morphology

**What does the tumor look like on the cut specimen?** It is usually attached by a pedicle (pedunculated).<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c007--> Most tumors are larger than 10 cm.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c008--> Cut sections look gray-white, and some show a whorled pattern.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c009--> At the base, where the tumor is anchored to the pleura, an occasional cyst may sit.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the typical pattern under the microscope?** Most often the tumor shows the "patternless pattern": cells and connective tissue are jumbled together without order.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c011--> Some tumors also show storiform (spiral) or herringbone areas.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c012--> Many cells resemble fibroblasts, and their shape runs from spindle to oval.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c013--> Collagen between the cells may appear as a fine net or as heavy strands.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c014-->

**Which features suggest malignancy?** Necrosis and cell pleomorphism are among the features that point to malignancy.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c015-->

## High-yield summary

- A localized, usually **pedunculated** tumor of the pleura.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c019-->
- Mostly **benign**, with a few malignant cases.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c020-->
- Not mesothelial: **submesothelial** connective tissue.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c021-->
- Not related to **asbestos**.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c022-->
- Histology: the **patternless pattern**, with collagen as a fine net or heavy strands.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c023-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is it a mesothelioma because it sits in the pleura?** No. Its origin is submesothelial connective tissue; mesothelium is not involved.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c024-->

**Is it an asbestos-related tumor?** No. It is unrelated to asbestos exposure.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c025-->

**Is every solitary fibrous tumor benign?** No. Most are benign, but a few are malignant.<!--c:solitary-fibrous-tumor-of-pleura-c026-->
