## Definition and epidemiology

**What is the usual malignant tumor of the gallbladder, and who gets it?** The leading malignancy of the gallbladder is adenocarcinoma.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c001--> Among gallbladders removed at cholecystectomy, about 2% harbor an unsuspected adenocarcinoma.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c002--> Groups with a high rate of gallstones, such as Native Americans, see more of these cancers, and so do women.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c003--> Nearly all cancers arising in the extrahepatic bile ducts are adenocarcinomas as well.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c004--> Men and women are affected about equally, and the duct tumor is seen less often than gallbladder cancer.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which conditions raise the risk?**
- Stones and chronic cholecystitis are usually present alongside the cancer.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c006-->
- Cancer arises with special frequency in a gallbladder whose wall has calcified, the so-called porcelain gallbladder.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c007-->
- Many patients with bile duct cancer also have gallstones.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c008-->
- Inflammatory conditions of the large bowel are associated with it.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c009-->
- Choledochal cysts and Caroli disease can be the soil in which it develops.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c010-->
- The liver fluke *C. sinensis*, found in Asia, can infest the ducts and is linked to the cancer.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the tumor spread?** The reference text gives no molecular sequence for these cancers; the setting is described under etiology. Tumor cells spread through lymphatics and by direct growth into nearby tissues, including the liver and peritoneum.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c012-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the specimen look like?**
- The fundus is the usual site, although the tumor can arise anywhere in the gallbladder.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c013-->
- A leathery, thickened wall results from the desmoplastic reaction that most of these tumors provoke.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c014-->
- Some tumors also grow as a mass bulging into the lumen.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c015-->
- Bile duct cancer can arise anywhere along the duct; the hilar form lies at the confluence of the two hepatic ducts, right and left.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c016-->
- Some duct tumors form an intraluminal mass, while others spread diffusely through the wall.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the tumor look like under the microscope?** Most often the cancer is an adenocarcinoma that infiltrates the wall and is well differentiated.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c018--> Rarer variants include adenosquamous and anaplastic carcinoma.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c019-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the reference text describes no special stain, immunohistochemical or molecular test for these tumors, so none is listed here.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?**
- Gallstone disease and gallbladder cancer give a similar set of symptoms.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c020-->
- Bile duct and ampullary cancers come to attention through obstructive jaundice.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c021-->
- In bile duct cancer, symptoms appear at an early stage.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c022-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it found?** The reference text gives no laboratory or imaging criteria. In practice, only tumors found by chance at cholecystectomy are cured.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c023-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?**
- Almost every gallbladder cancer that has reached the symptomatic stage can no longer be cured.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c024-->
- Five-year survival at that stage is below 3%.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c025-->
- The prognosis of bile duct cancer is poor.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c026-->
- Gallbladder cancer has a somewhat worse outlook than this.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c027-->

## High-yield summary

- **Adenocarcinoma** dominates; it is typically well differentiated and infiltrates the wall.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c032-->
- The cancer travels with **gallstones** and chronic cholecystitis, and arises especially often in a **porcelain gallbladder**.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c033-->
- The **fundus** is the favored site, and the wall turns **thick and leathery**.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c034-->
- **Late presentation**: symptoms mimic gallstones and 5-year survival is below 3%.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c035-->
- Cure is realistic almost only for an **incidental** tumor found in the removed gallbladder.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c036-->
- **Bile duct and ampullary carcinoma** present with **obstructive jaundice**.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c037-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do the symptoms of gallbladder cancer give an early warning?** No. The picture is that of gallstone disease, and almost all tumors are already incurable once symptoms appear.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c038-->

**Is a calcified gallbladder a harmless finding?** No. Cancer develops especially often in a porcelain gallbladder.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c039-->

**Do bile duct and gallbladder cancer have the same outlook?** Both are poor. Bile duct cancer gives symptoms earlier, which makes its outlook somewhat better.<!--c:gallbladder-carcinoma-c040-->
