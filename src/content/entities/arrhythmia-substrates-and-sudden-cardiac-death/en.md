## Definition and epidemiology

**What is an arrhythmia substrate, and why does it matter in sudden death?** A substrate is a fixed structural change in the heart, such as a scar or a thick, fibrotic wall, that makes a lethal rhythm disturbance possible.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c001--> In most cases a diseased heart abruptly slips into ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation, and the victim dies.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c002--> Each year in the United States, sudden death claims more than 300,000 lives.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which diseases leave a substrate, and does age change the list?** In athletes under 35, [[hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy]] leads, and with unexplained hypertrophy and coronary anomalies it accounts for over 75% of sudden deaths.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c004--> [[arrhythmogenic-cardiomyopathy]] is a main cause in the young in Italy and other Mediterranean countries.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c005--> After midlife, sudden death in developed nations is mostly due to [[ischemic-heart-disease]].<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c006--> Among victims under 35, roughly 15% to 20% have a coronary artery arising from an abnormal site.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c007--> In athletes over 35, [[mitral-valve-prolapse]] appears as a minor cause.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c008--> Patients with severe congenital aortic stenosis can die suddenly, mostly from ventricular arrhythmia.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c009--> Ebstein malformation can also end in arrhythmia and sudden death.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a lethal rhythm start, and where can the conduction system fail?** Ventricular muscle is the last stop: sinoatrial node, atria, atrioventricular (AV) node, bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibers.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c011--> The AV node is the slowest step in conduction, which gives the ventricles time to fill before they are activated.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c012--> A pacemaker lower in the system beats more slowly, so when the sinoatrial node fails (sick sinus syndrome) a backup site, even ventricular muscle, sets a slower rate.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c013-->

In adults, only the bundle of His normally links atria and ventricles electrically.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c014--> Development can leave a stray strand of myocytes bridging atria and ventricles.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c015--> Supraventricular tachycardia can arise from circuits involving such a pathway; in Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome it also makes the ventricles fire early (pre-excitation).<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c016--> A mother's autoantibodies, as in lupus, may cross the placenta and cause a congenital conduction defect.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c017--> Heart surgery, catheterization, an infarct, or inflammatory or infiltrative disease can all injure the conduction system.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c018-->

Lethal arrhythmias usually start in remodeled working ventricular muscle, driven by triggered activity (ectopic beats), reentry (abnormal conduction) or both.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c019--> By extent, a substrate is diffuse (myocyte hypertrophy with interstitial fibrosis) or localized (healed infarct, ventricular aneurysm).<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c020--> In most cases a substrate alone cannot produce an arrhythmia, though it may be required.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c021--> Arrhythmia is likeliest when a brief trigger (an electrolyte change, ischemia or neurohormonal activation) hits remodeled myocardium.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c022--> Most often, sudden death strikes when acute ischemia hits muscle already holding a healed infarct.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c023-->

A structurally normal heart can still harbor a channelopathy: an inherited fault in an ion channel or in the proteins that move and recycle it.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c024--> Such deaths are rare.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c025--> Slow repolarization, usually from loss-of-function K+ channel mutations, makes afterdepolarizations likelier in long QT syndrome.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c026--> Gain-of-function mutations in SCN5A or CACNA1C can also cause it.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c027--> Brugada syndrome is autosomal dominant.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c028--> Loss-of-function SCN5A mutations occur in about 25% of cases.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c029--> In catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, Ca2+ leaks from the sarcoplasmic reticulum through RyR2 or calsequestrin mutations, and afterdepolarizations set off arrhythmias.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c030-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the heart of a sudden death victim look like?** The usual finding is myocardial change that serves as the anatomic substrate.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c031--> In Ebstein malformation, low, wall-adherent tricuspid leaflets leave an atrialized upper right ventricle and a functional lower part.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c032--> In Brugada syndrome the heart is often structurally normal.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c033-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the microscope show?** Diffuse substrates show myocyte hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c034--> Some Brugada hearts contain fibrosis in differing amounts.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c035--> In congenital heart block the AV node is either cut off from atrial myocardium or walled off from ventricular conduction tissue by fibrosis.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c036--> Myocyte vacuoles suggest a storage disease, not a primary cardiomyopathy.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c037-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- Occasionally an ECG points to the culprit: a new bundle branch block suggests a conduction-system lesion, and a Wolff–Parkinson–White pattern implies an accessory AV connection.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c038-->
- Long QT syndrome is divided into types by the mutated gene.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c039-->
- Histology helps: vacuolated myocytes favor a storage disease over a primary cardiomyopathy.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c040-->
- Sampling, special stains and molecular panels: not covered by the source pages.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Many victims were apparently healthy and collapsed outside a hospital.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c041--> Long QT syndrome means fainting, ventricular arrhythmia or sudden death in someone whose ECG shows a long QT and abnormal T waves.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c042--> Right bundle branch block with ST-segment elevation over the right precordial leads marks Brugada syndrome.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c043--> In catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, exertion or emotional stress releases catecholamines that trigger arrhythmias and sudden death.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c044--> Despite a slow heart rate, isolated congenital heart block seldom limits function.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c045--> Later, Stokes–Adams fainting, hypertrophy, rhythm disorders or heart failure may follow.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c046-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What do the ECG and the autopsy contribute?** At autopsy many victims turn out to have coronary disease that stayed clinically quiet.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c047--> A normal heart at autopsy does not exclude an arrhythmic death, because channelopathies can kill without a structural lesion.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c048-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What raises the risk of a fatal arrhythmia?** Failing hearts acquire a longer QT interval through electrophysiologic remodeling, not a gene, and this probably raises arrhythmia risk.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c049--> Many widely prescribed drugs lengthen the QT interval as a side effect.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c050--> Drug-induced QT lengthening is most dangerous when an inherited trait or acquired condition already delays repolarization.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c051-->

## High-yield summary

- A **substrate** (scar, hypertrophy, fibrosis) is in most cases not sufficient alone; a **trigger** such as acute ischemia makes arrhythmia likely.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c056-->
- A **healed infarct** plus acute ischemia is the most frequent combination.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c057-->
- Lethal rhythms arise by **triggered activity** or **reentry**.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c058-->
- Under 35, **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy**, idiopathic hypertrophy and coronary anomalies dominate; later, **coronary artery disease**.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c059-->
- An **accessory pathway** causes pre-excitation in Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c060-->
- **Channelopathies** (long QT, Brugada, CPVT) can kill a structurally normal heart.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c061-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a scar cause sudden death by itself?** No. A substrate alone usually cannot start an arrhythmia; a transient trigger such as acute ischemia must be added.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c062-->

**Does a normal heart at autopsy mean the death was not cardiac?** No. Channelopathies can cause lethal arrhythmia without a structural lesion.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c063-->

**Is the slow AV node a defect?** No. Its slow conduction is normal and lets the ventricles fill.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c064-->

**Are the leading causes the same at every age?** No. Under 35, heart muscle disease and coronary anomalies lead; in older adults, coronary artery disease.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c065-->

## Mnemonics

**A scar needs a spark:** the fixed **substrate** (scar) is the fuel, and the transient **trigger** (ischemia) is the spark.<!--c:arrhythmia-substrates-and-sudden-cardiac-death-c066-->
