## Definition and epidemiology

**What are vascular tumors?** Lesions of blood or lymphatic vessel cells, from benign hemangiomas to malignant angiosarcoma and Kaposi sarcoma.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c001--> Many benign ones are hamartomas, not true neoplasms.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c002--> Hemangiomas usually arise in skin, sometimes in internal organs.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c003--> Malignant ones are rare.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c004-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does each tumor look like?**
- **Capillary hemangioma:** vessels like ordinary capillaries in caliber and wall.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c005-->
- **Juvenile (strawberry) hemangioma:** No capsule, yet a fairly clear border; crowded capillaries, mostly full of blood.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c006--> On newborn skin; tends to fade.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c007-->
- **Cavernous hemangioma:** blood pools in wide, stroma-poor spaces; unlike capillary type, no spontaneous shrinkage.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c008--> On skin it is a port wine stain.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c009-->
- **Glomus tumor:** tiny bluish-red nodule; forked vessels run between glomus cell clusters.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c010--> Typically painful, under a nail.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c011-->
- **Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma:** Intermediate between hemangioma and angiosarcoma: open lumina, cells with roomy pink cytoplasm, sometimes vacuolated.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c012--> May recur locally; a minority metastasize.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c013-->
- **Angiosarcoma:** Ranges from well-formed vessels to barely recognizable ones.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c014--> Undifferentiated ones are more aggressive, with many mitoses.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c015-->
- **Kaposi sarcoma:** HHV-8 (KSHV) infection produces this endothelial tumor.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c016--> It varies: some lesions look like a hemangioma, others are so cellular that vessels are hard to see.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c017-->
- **Capillary lymphangioma:** thin-walled, endothelium-lined spaces full of lymph.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c018-->
- **Cystic lymphangioma (cystic hygroma):** Often congenital, typically in neck or axilla; its cysts contain fluid, not blood.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c019-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** Histology; endothelial markers (CD31) show endothelial origin.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c020--> Endothelial lineage on immunostaining tells Kaposi sarcoma from fibrosarcoma.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c021--> Most infected people never develop it, so HHV-8 alone is not diagnostic.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c022-->

## High-yield summary

- **Cavernous** hemangioma has large spaces and no regression; **capillary** has capillary-sized vessels.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c027-->
- **Glomus tumor** is a small, painful subungual nodule.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c028-->
- Hepatic **angiosarcoma** is linked to exposures such as **Thorotrast**, vinyl chloride and arsenic.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c029-->
- **HHV-8** causes **Kaposi sarcoma**, which has become epidemic in AIDS and other immune suppression.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c030-->
- **Cystic lymphangioma** is often congenital and typically sits in the neck or axilla.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c031-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do finger-like projections of a juvenile hemangioma mean invasion?** No; they can look invasive, but the lesion is benign and never metastasizes.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c032-->

**Is Kaposi sarcoma deadly?** Rarely: though a cancer found at many sites, patients seldom die of it.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c033-->

**Does HHV-8 infection mean Kaposi sarcoma?** No; few infected people develop it.<!--c:vascular-tumors-c034-->
