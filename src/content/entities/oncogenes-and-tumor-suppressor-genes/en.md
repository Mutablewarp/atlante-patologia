## Definition and epidemiology

**What are oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes?** A proto-oncogene is a normal gene that promotes cell growth; once mutated into an overactive form it is called an oncogene.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c001--> Losing a tumor suppressor gene removes a brake on the cell cycle, and the cell then divides without that check.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c002--> Cancer needs the two changes together: overactive growth-promoting genes, plus suppressors that are bypassed or switched off.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What damages these genes?** DNA polymerases sometimes miscopy bases, and chemicals and radiation damage DNA directly.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c004--> Mutations keep accumulating in the descendants of the single cell from which most cancers arise.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c005--> Some inherited cancer syndromes involve mutated BRCA1, BRCA2 or PALB2; their proteins help carry out homologous recombination.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c006--> A mismatch repair defect can be present from birth or arise later in life.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do oncogenes drive growth?** Any step of the growth-signaling pathway, from the first ligand to the final transcription factor, can carry a driver mutation.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c008--> Most of a tumor's mutations are passengers, whose roles are largely unknown; the drivers are a small minority.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c009-->

- **Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs):** a mutation that locks the receptor in a permanently active state lets it signal without any ligand.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c010--> Examples are **HER2/neu** amplification and **EGFR** mutation or amplification.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c011-->
- **RAS** proteins (K-, N-, H-RAS) switch between GDP and GTP and signal only in the GTP-bound state.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c012--> GAP is the off switch for active RAS: it speeds the splitting of GTP into GDP.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c013--> Mutant RAS never shuts down, and it is found in many malignant tumors.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c014-->
- **BCR::ABL1:** a translocation of Abl produces the mutant protein Bcr-Abl.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c015-->
- **MYC** is a transcription factor activated by amplification or translocation; translocation can land the gene next to a promoter that drives it harder.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c016--> It may control the transcription of a large share of human genes.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c017-->

**Which brakes fail?**

- **RB** keeps E2F, which drives entry into S phase, switched off until cyclin D-CDK4/6 phosphorylates RB and releases it.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c018-->
- **TP53 (p53)**: MDM2 adds ubiquitin to p53 and sends it to the proteasome, so p53 is scarce in an unstressed cell.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c019--> Damage-induced arrest runs through p21, which p53 raises after ATM/Chk2 or ATR/Chk1 phosphorylate it.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c020--> When repair is impossible, p53 pushes the cell into apoptosis (see [[apoptosis]]).<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c021-->
- **Mismatch repair (MMR)** fixes insertions and deletions from polymerase slippage and single mispaired bases, using two overlapping systems.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c022-->
- **BRCA1 and BRCA2** products take part in homologous recombination, one of the two routes that repair double-strand breaks.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c023-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which findings guide testing and treatment?**

- **Microsatellite instability** points to a defect in MMR.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c024-->
- In some tumors, the **ERCC1** level can forecast whether cisplatin-type drugs will work.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c025-->
- After surgery, patients with **hormone receptor-positive** tumors have a lower risk of recurrence if given antiestrogen therapy.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c026-->

See [[predictive-biomarkers]] for the general concept.

## Clinical presentation

**How do these defects show up in patients?** Most prostate cancers eventually become insensitive to androgens, even though removing androgen stimulation initially slows their growth.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c027-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Do these genes affect treatment response?** DNA-targeting chemotherapy works less well against tumor cells with efficient DNA repair.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c028-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Oncogene | Gain of function: activating mutation of a proto-oncogene.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c029--> | Find the activating change (mutation, amplification, translocation) |
| Tumor suppressor | Loss of function: mutation disables the brake that the gene puts on the cell cycle.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c030--> | Find the inactivating change |
| DNA repair gene | When repair fails, mutations are handed on to daughter cells.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c031--> | Microsatellite instability for MMR |

## High-yield summary

- **Oncogenes** come from **proto-oncogenes** by activating mutation; **tumor suppressors** are brakes that are inactivated.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c032-->
- A mutant **RAS** cannot switch itself off: it stays loaded with **GTP**.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c033-->
- **BCR::ABL1** is a **translocation** that produces the mutant Bcr-Abl protein.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c034-->
- **RB** holds **E2F**; many cancers no longer obey the G1 restriction point.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c035-->
- **p53** is called the **guardian of the genome**.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c036-->
- Failed **MMR** appears as **microsatellite instability**.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c037-->
- **BRCA1/2** work in **homologous recombination**.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c038-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is an oncogene a viral gene?** No. Oncogenes were first found in tumor viruses (v-), but human cancers carry altered versions of normal cellular genes (c-).<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c039-->

**Does a mutant oncogene always drive proliferation?** No. The same mutant protein can promote growth in one cell type and cell death in another.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c040-->

**Does MYC only promote growth?** No. MYC can also trigger cell death when p53 and the other death effectors are intact.<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c041-->

## Mnemonics

**Stuck pedal, cut brakes** (original): oncogenes are the accelerator jammed down (gain of function), suppressors are the brakes that were cut (loss of function).<!--c:oncogenes-and-tumor-suppressor-genes-c042-->
