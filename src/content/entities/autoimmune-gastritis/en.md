## Definition and epidemiology

**What is autoimmune gastritis, and who gets it?** It is an immune-mediated gastritis that stays confined to the gastric body and fundus.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c001--> It affects roughly 1 to 2 in 100 older adults.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c002--> Women are affected more often than men.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers it?** The cause is an immune attack on the stomach's own parietal cells, aimed at their H+/K+ ATPase (the acid pump) and at intrinsic factor.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c004--> Serum antibodies against parietal cells and against intrinsic factor are found.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c005--> Helicobacter pylori colonization is uncommon here, unlike in the environmental form of atrophic gastritis, where it is nearly universal.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c006--> Patients and their relatives are prone to other autoimmune diseases, for example Addison disease or type 1 diabetes.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why is only the body and fundus involved, and what follows from losing parietal cells?** Parietal cells exist only in the body and fundus, so the disease stays there.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c008--> Acid output falls once parietal cells are lost, so the patient has hypochlorhydria or achlorhydria.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c009--> Serum gastrin rises because antral G cells make more of it when acid is lost.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c010--> Any parietal cells that survive are driven to secrete acid by histamine, which corpus ECL cells release under gastrin stimulation.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c011--> Intrinsic factor is needed to absorb vitamin B12 in the ileum, so losing it produces B12 deficiency.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c012--> When this deficiency causes a megaloblastic anemia, the anemia is called pernicious anemia.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c013-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Two kinds of metaplasia, intestinal and pseudopyloric, appear in a mucosa that has lost its parietal cells and is heavily infiltrated by mononuclear cells.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c014--> The glands shrink (atrophy) while inflammatory cells crowd the lamina propria.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c015--> Goblet cells mark the intestinal metaplasia.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c016--> Small nodules of ECL (enterochromaffin-like) cells can be seen in the body.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c017--> Marked neuroendocrine hyperplasia is a late change.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c018-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and what do the tests add?**
- **How it is studied:** in a gastric body biopsy, look for a dense mononuclear infiltrate and metaplasia, with parietal cells missing.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c019-->
- Serology: anti-parietal cell and anti-intrinsic factor antibodies are positive.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c020-->
- Blood chemistry: vitamin B12 is low and serum gastrin is high.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c021-->

## Clinical presentation

**What problems does the patient have?** Patients secrete little or no acid (hypochlorhydria or achlorhydria).<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c022--> Low vitamin B12, high gastrin and little acid are expected as a set.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c023-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the autoimmune form?** Antibody positivity, low B12 and high gastrin, together with atrophy limited to the body and fundus, favor the autoimmune form over environmental atrophic gastritis.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c024-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong over time?** Vitamin B12 deficiency can lead to pernicious anemia.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c025--> Years later, neuroendocrine cells may proliferate conspicuously.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c026--> Other autoimmune diseases are more likely in the patient and in the family.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c027-->

## High-yield summary

- Autoimmune gastritis is limited to the **gastric body and fundus**.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c030-->
- The targets are the parietal cell **H+/K+ ATPase** and **intrinsic factor**.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c031-->
- Loss of intrinsic factor causes **vitamin B12 deficiency** and **pernicious anemia**.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c032-->
- Low acid with **hypergastrinemia** is the expected pattern.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c033-->
- **ECL** cell micronodules can be seen in the body.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c034-->
- Other **autoimmune diseases** cluster with it, for example hypothyroidism or type 1 diabetes.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c035-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is pernicious anemia an iron-deficiency anemia?** No. It is a megaloblastic anemia from vitamin B12 deficiency.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c036-->

**Does autoimmune gastritis involve the antrum?** No. Only the body and fundus hold parietal cells, the target of the attack.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c037-->

**Does high gastrin mean too much acid?** No. Gastrin rises because acid is lost.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c038-->

**Is it the same as H. pylori gastritis?** No. The atrophy of the environmental form is antral predominant and multifocal, and its antibodies are absent.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c039-->

## Mnemonics

**BFG: Body and Fundus, Gastrin up, B12 down.** The disease sits in the body and fundus, with high gastrin and low vitamin B12.<!--c:autoimmune-gastritis-c040-->
