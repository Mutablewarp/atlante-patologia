## Definition and epidemiology

**What makes a pneumonia atypical, viral or opportunistic?** An atypical pneumonia lingers, starts slowly, and the white cell count is normal or only slightly raised.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c001--> Viral and Mycoplasma pneumonias are interstitial: mononuclear cells inflame the alveolar walls.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c002--> Opportunistic pneumonias, such as Pneumocystis and invasive Aspergillus, arise when immunity is weakened.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which organisms are responsible?**
- Viruses are the usual cause of interstitial pneumonia, but Mycoplasma, Chlamydia and Rickettsia can produce the same pattern.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c004-->
- Influenza, coronaviruses, adenovirus, herpes simplex, varicella-zoster, measles and CMV can infect the lung.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c005-->
- Slow growth and the lack of a rigid cell wall characterize Mycoplasma pneumoniae.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c006-->
- Transplant and chemotherapy patients on immunosuppressive drugs, and people with HIV/AIDS, are the main victims of Pneumocystis jiroveci pneumonia.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c007-->
- Cytotoxic therapy or AIDS, by weakening immunity, sets up nearly every case of invasive aspergillosis.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a virus injure the lung?** Type I pneumocytes are the fragile alveolar cells that degenerate once infected.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c009--> Type II cells multiply to resurface the alveoli, while edema and hyaline membranes (condensed protein and debris) form where type I cells have degenerated.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c010--> Complete recovery is the usual outcome; interstitial fibrosis is an occasional sequel.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c011--> Lung infarcts in invasive aspergillosis because the fungus invades vessels, chiefly arteries, and blocks them with thrombus.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c012-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the lung look like?** Cavities occasionally appear within the patchy, multifocal consolidation of invasive aspergillosis.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c013--> A fungus ball (aspergilloma) is Aspergillus growing as a mass inside a cavity left by tuberculosis or bronchiectasis.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do you see on H&E?**
- Viral pneumonia shows mononuclear cells in the interstitium.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c015-->
- Viral and non-viral diffuse alveolar damage look alike: hyaline membranes and dead type I cells.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c016-->
- A smoldering viral injury can instead leave chronic interstitial inflammation and type II cell hyperplasia.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c017-->
- In the bronchioles, Mycoplasma brings lymphocytes and plasma cells into the wall and neutrophils into the lumen.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c018-->
- Several faint cytoplasmic inclusions and one haloed dark nuclear inclusion mark the enlarged CMV-infected cell.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c019-->
- Influenza typically shows bronchiolitis and interstitial pneumonitis.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c020-->
- In Pneumocystis pneumonia the interstitium holds plasma cells and lymphocytes, while the alveoli contain a foamy exudate.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c021-->
- Diffuse alveolar damage is the main lesion of SARS and MERS coronavirus pneumonia.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c022-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** a silver stain brings out Pneumocystis cysts, some spherical and some collapsed into crescents.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c023-->
- Pneumocystis is identified in airway samples (sputum, lavage) or in biopsied lung tissue.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c024-->
- Silver-stained sections show Aspergillus hyphae in the lumen and wall of pulmonary artery branches.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c025-->
- Nuclear inclusions mark infection by adenovirus, herpes simplex, varicella and CMV.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c026-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** On X-ray, atypical pneumonia shows scattered patches in the air spaces or the interstitium, and the clinical picture ranges from mild to severe.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c027--> Symptoms of Pneumocystis pneumonia may be minimal, or respiratory failure may progress rapidly.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c028--> Pneumothorax is a risk in AIDS patients who develop thin-walled lung cysts.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c029--> Hemoptysis is the most frequent symptom when a fungus ball becomes evident, but most are silent.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c030-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is Mycoplasma pneumonia diagnosed?** Mycoplasma is hard to culture, so the diagnosis rests on serology: antibodies or cold agglutinins.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c031-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** Mycoplasma deaths are rare, and erythromycin is effective.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c032--> Fatal H1N1 influenza is uncommon and mostly strikes patients with other illnesses; the usual case is mild and clears alone.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c033--> Pneumocystis is treated with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or pentamidine.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c034--> Invasive aspergillosis runs a very rapid, fulminant course.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c035-->

## High-yield summary

- **Interstitial pattern**: mononuclear cells in the alveolar walls, typical of viruses and Mycoplasma.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c039-->
- **Mycoplasma**: inflamed bronchioles with neutrophils filling the lumen.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c040-->
- **Cold agglutinins** and serology support Mycoplasma.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c041-->
- **Pneumocystis**: foamy alveolar exudate, crescent cysts on silver.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c042-->
- **CMV**: enlarged cell with a nuclear inclusion and halo.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c043-->
- **Aspergillus**: invasive disease in the immunosuppressed, with vessel invasion.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c044-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is Pneumocystis a protozoan?** No. Pneumocystis was long thought to be a protozoan, but it belongs with the fungi.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c045-->

**Does influenza show viral inclusions?** No: on microscopy, influenza lacks the usual viral cell changes.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c046-->

**Is bacterial superinfection after influenza rare?** No: a secondary bacterial infection fairly often follows influenza.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c047-->

## Mnemonics

**PA**: Pneumocystis and Aspergillus, the two to think of when immunity is down.<!--c:atypical-and-viral-pneumonia-c048-->
