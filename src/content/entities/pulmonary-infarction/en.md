## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a pulmonary infarct?** It is an area of lung that dies because its blood supply has failed, most often after an embolus blocks a pulmonary artery.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c001--> The lung's two blood supplies explain why most small emboli leave no infarct.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c002--> A blocked pulmonary artery costs the lung tissue only when the bronchial circulation cannot back it up.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who gets a lung infarct?** One of the two arterial routes is already impaired in a patient with heart failure or chronic lung disease, and such patients are the usual victims.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c004--> Pooling of blood in the lung vessels may add to it, but the usual setting is congestive heart failure, where bronchial flow often falls short.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c005--> The blocking embolus usually lodges in a pulmonary artery of middle size.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does a blocked pulmonary artery not always kill the lung?** Ischemia is normally avoided because the lung receives blood from two arterial systems, the pulmonary and the bronchial.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c007--> Bleeding is the usual character of the infarct, since bronchial artery flow still reaches the dead tissue.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c008--> Within two days, blood has leaked into the dead lung tissue.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c009-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the infarct look like?** It is full of blood, so it looks red.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c010--> Its shape is a pyramid whose wide side rests against the pleura.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c011--> Pallor appears in the middle of the lesion once its blood has been taken up again over time.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c012-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the tissue show, and how does it change?** The alveolar spaces of the dead tissue are filled with blood.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c013--> At the margin of the infarct, granulation tissue appears.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c014--> Organization of this tissue converts it into a fibrous scar.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c015-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient complain of?** Hemoptysis occurs only occasionally, while the other features are dyspnea, cough and a sharp pain on breathing from pleural irritation.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c016--> Fluid in the pleural space is common, and often it contains blood.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c017-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is infarction recognized in a patient?** "Partial infarction" leaves no scar, though clinical and imaging findings look like a true infarct.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c018--> Lab tests are not covered in this card.

## Complications and prognosis

**How does a lung infarct end?** Healing finishes with a fibrous scar.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c019--> "Partial infarction" heals without a scar: collateral flow lets the tissue regenerate because its framework was not destroyed.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c020--> A small embolus can clear completely; among the factors that decide this are bronchial collateral flow and thrombolysis.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c021--> An embolus that is not cleared becomes organized and stays as fibrous bands on the artery wall.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c022--> Blockage of the arterial bed by repeated small emboli rarely leads to pulmonary hypertension.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c023-->

## High-yield summary

- The lung has **two arterial supplies**, so infarction needs one of them to fail.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c027-->
- The infarct is **red (hemorrhagic)** and **pyramidal**, with its base on the pleura.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c028-->
- The usual background is **congestive heart failure** or chronic lung disease.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c029-->
- Symptoms: often a **bloody pleural effusion**, with **pleuritic pain**, dyspnea, cough and occasional hemoptysis.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c030-->
- Healing: **granulation tissue**, then **organization** into a fibrous scar.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c031-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does every pulmonary embolus cause an infarct?** No. With two arterial supplies, the lung survives most small emboli without infarcting.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c032-->

**Is a lung infarct pale from the start?** No. It is hemorrhagic first, and the center becomes pale only as the blood is resorbed.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c033-->

**Does a lung infarct always leave a scar?** No. In partial infarction the framework survives and the tissue regenerates.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c034-->

## Mnemonics

**WEDGE:** Wedge on the pleura, Embolus in an artery, Dual supply failed, Granulation then scar, Effusion that is bloody.<!--c:pulmonary-infarction-c035-->
