## Definition and epidemiology

**What is mucoepidermoid carcinoma?** This malignant salivary gland tumor grows from the lining cells of the ducts, which are prone to metaplasia.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c001-->

**How common is it, and where does it arise?**
- Among tumors of the major salivary glands, 5% to 10% are mucoepidermoid carcinomas.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c002-->
- Among minor salivary gland tumors, the share is about 10%.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c003-->
- The parotid is the origin of more than half of the major-gland cases.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c004-->
- Within the minor glands, the tumor mostly develops in the palate.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c005-->
- Adolescents can be affected, but most patients are adult women.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which genetic change drives the tumor?** More than 60% of tumors carry a t(11;19)(q21-22;p13) translocation.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c007-->

**What does the translocation produce?** The result of the translocation is the MECT1–MAML2 fusion gene.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c008-->
- MAML2 is a coactivator of Notch; the fusion places its transactivation domain next to the cAMP-responsive domain of MECT1, another coactivator.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c009-->
- As a result, Notch signaling is probably dysregulated in the tumor cells.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Why is it called "muco-epidermoid"?** The name reflects the mixture of intermediate cells, epidermoid (squamous-like) cells and mucus-secreting cells (mucocytes).<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c011-->

**How do I recognize each cell type?**
- An off-center nucleus and clear cytoplasm identify mucocytes, which gather in groups.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c012-->
- Lacking keratin and intercellular bridges, epidermoid cells merely look squamous.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c013-->
- Compared with epidermoid cells, intermediate cells are smaller.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c014-->

**How does the picture change with grade?**
- Low grade: cystic, ductal and solid areas each hold all three cell types.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c015-->
- Intermediate grade: growth is more solid, and the share of mucus-secreting cells falls as epidermoid and intermediate cells become more numerous.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c016-->
- High grade: only occasional glandular cells remain, and the poorly differentiated cells vary widely in shape.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c017-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look at the mix of mucocytes, epidermoid and intermediate cells and at how solid the growth is: this mix tracks the grade.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c018-->
- **Molecular finding:** the characteristic change is the MECT1–MAML2 fusion produced by t(11;19).<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c019-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** A firm, painless mass that enlarges slowly is the presentation.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c020-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Can a low-grade tumor spread?** Yes: metastasis can occur even when the tumor is low grade.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c021-->

**What is the outlook?**
- Whatever the primary site, over 90% of patients are alive at 5 years.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c022-->
- High-grade tumors do far worse: 5-year survival is 20% to 40%.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c023-->

**How is it treated?** Grade determines how the tumor is treated.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c024-->
- For low-grade tumors, surgery is the treatment.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c025-->
- For high-grade tumors, surgery must be combined with radiation therapy.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c026-->

## High-yield summary

- **Three cell types**: the tumor is built from intermediate, epidermoid and mucus-secreting cells.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c031-->
- **MECT1–MAML2** fusion, created by t(11;19), is present in more than 60% of cases.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c032-->
- **Grade** drives the picture: more mucus-secreting cells in low grade, more solid growth and pleomorphism as grade rises.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c033-->
- **Treatment follows grade**: surgery for low grade, surgery plus radiation for high grade.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c034-->
- **Site**: the parotid is the commonest major gland site, the palate the commonest minor gland site.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c035-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a low-grade mucoepidermoid carcinoma harmless?** No. Metastasis can occur even in low-grade tumors.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c036-->

**Are the epidermoid cells a keratinizing squamous carcinoma?** No. Lacking keratin and bridges, they merely look squamous.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c037-->

**Is it a tumor only of adults?** No. It also occurs in adolescents.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c038-->

## Mnemonics

**MIX** stands for Mucous, Intermediate and epidermoid (X-squamous) cells, the three populations of the tumor.<!--c:mucoepidermoid-carcinoma-c039-->
