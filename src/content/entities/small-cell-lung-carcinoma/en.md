## Definition and epidemiology

**What is small cell lung carcinoma?** Neuroendocrine features mark this aggressive cancer, which arises from lung epithelium.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c001--> The older name was oat cell carcinoma.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c002--> Of all lung cancers in the United States, 14% are small cell carcinomas.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What is the main risk factor?** Cigarette smoking is strongly linked to this tumor.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c004-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does it spread early?** The tumor grows and metastasizes rapidly.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c005--> Within the airway wall, it tends to extend beneath the lining and encircle the bronchus.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c006-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does it sit, and what does the cut surface show?** Extensive lymph node metastases accompany a mass that usually lies around the hilum.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c007--> The tumor tissue is white and soft.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c008--> Large areas of hemorrhage and necrosis are common.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c009-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the cells look like?** The small cells have scant cytoplasm and form sheets; their shape ranges from round to spindle-like.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c010--> Nucleoli are missing or barely visible, and the nuclear chromatin has a fine granular texture.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c011--> Counting 10 high-power fields, equal to a 2 mm² area, gives 60 to 70 mitoses, a very high rate.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c012--> Large necrotic areas are common.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c013--> A tumor cell is about as wide as three small resting lymphocytes, though no strict size cutoff exists.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c014--> Behavior and outcome follow the small cell part when, rarely, a non-small cell carcinoma is found in the same tumor.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c015-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- **Specimen:** a small biopsy or a cytology sample is frequently what first allows the diagnosis.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c016-->
- **Routine H&E:** look first for necrosis and frequent mitoses, then for sheets of small cells whose nuclei have fine granular chromatin and barely visible nucleoli.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c017-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** most of these tumors stain for neuroendocrine proteins such as synaptophysin, chromogranin and CD56.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c018-->
- **Ruling out other types:** a positive p40 or p63 usually points to squamous cell carcinoma, and TTF-1 with Napsin usually points to adenocarcinoma.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c019-->
- Special stains, electron microscopy and molecular tests are not described for this tumor in the source pages used.

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** Advanced disease is already present in 70% of patients at first presentation.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c020--> Paraneoplastic syndromes are a frequent feature.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c021--> Eaton–Lambert syndrome, ectopic ACTH (corticotropin) syndrome and diabetes insipidus are examples.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c022--> See [[paraneoplastic-syndromes]].

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Why does exact typing matter on a small sample?** Targeted therapy needs molecular tests, so non-small cell cancers need exact typing even on small samples.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c023--> Immunostains give the right subtype in over 95% of lung cancers, even on a small biopsy or cytology sample.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c024-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it respond to treatment?** Other lung cancers do not share the early, marked sensitivity of small cell tumors to chemotherapy.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c025--> Treatment rests mainly on chemotherapy.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c026--> Treatment follows the small cell pattern when a small cell carcinoma is mixed with non-small cell components.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c027-->

## High-yield summary

- Closely tied to **cigarette smoking**.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c038-->
- Extensive lymph node metastases plus a typically **perihilar** mass.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c039-->
- **Extensive necrosis** with small cells whose nucleoli are inconspicuous and whose chromatin is **finely granular**.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c040-->
- Neuroendocrine markers: **synaptophysin, chromogranin, CD56**.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c041-->
- Paraneoplastic syndromes: **Eaton–Lambert, ectopic ACTH, diabetes insipidus**.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c042-->
- Patients present late because the tumor spreads **rapidly**.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c043-->
- **Chemotherapy** is the mainstay, and the tumor is **very chemosensitive** early on.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c044-->
- Small cell treatment applies to a **combined** tumor.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c045-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is "small" an exact size limit?** No. No strict cutoff exists; the cells are roughly three small resting lymphocytes wide.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c046-->

**Does a non-small cell component make the tumor a non-small cell cancer?** No. The tumor is called combined small cell carcinoma and treated as small cell carcinoma, because the small cell component determines its behavior.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c047-->

**Is it as resistant to chemotherapy as other lung cancers?** No. Early on, chemotherapy works very well, which sets it apart from other lung cancers.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c048-->

**Is every aggressive neuroendocrine lung carcinoma a small cell carcinoma?** No. Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma is another high-grade neuroendocrine tumor whose 5-year survival resembles that of small cell carcinoma.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c049-->

## Mnemonics

Remember S-C-L-C: Smoking, Central (perihilar) mass, Lymph node spread, Chemotherapy sensitivity.<!--c:small-cell-lung-carcinoma-c050-->
