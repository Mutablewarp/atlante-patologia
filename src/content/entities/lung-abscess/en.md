## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a lung abscess?** In a lung abscess, pus collects in one spot and destroys the lung around it, airways and vessels included.<!--c:lung-abscess-c001--> Aspiration is the most common cause.<!--c:lung-abscess-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes a lung abscess?** More than 90% of aspiration abscesses trace back to anaerobes of the mouth and throat.<!--c:lung-abscess-c003--> Fusiform bacteria and Bacteroides are common players in these mixed infections.<!--c:lung-abscess-c004--> S. aureus and K. pneumoniae, among others, may also be found.<!--c:lung-abscess-c005-->

**Who is at risk?**
- Aspiration, and so abscess, is often favored when consciousness is depressed.<!--c:lung-abscess-c006-->
- Alcoholism heads the list of predisposing conditions.<!--c:lung-abscess-c007-->
- Anything that impairs neurologic function, such as epilepsy or a drug overdose, raises the risk.<!--c:lung-abscess-c008-->

**Which causes are not aspiration?**
- Post-pneumonic: a necrotizing pneumonia can turn into an abscess.<!--c:lung-abscess-c009-->
- Obstruction: a blocked bronchus can lead to an abscess.<!--c:lung-abscess-c010-->
- Septic emboli: infected pulmonary emboli can seed abscesses.<!--c:lung-abscess-c011-->
- Penetrating chest trauma is another cause.<!--c:lung-abscess-c012-->
- Infection can also spread from adjacent tissues.<!--c:lung-abscess-c013-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does aspiration sometimes end in an abscess?** Two conditions are needed. First, anaerobes must have multiplied in the mouth; periodontal disease and poor oral hygiene are typical settings.<!--c:lung-abscess-c014--> Second, the body must fail to expel the material: coughing or airway clearance is weak.<!--c:lung-abscess-c015-->

**Why the right lung?** The right main bronchus leaves the trachea in almost the same line, so aspirated material tends to go there.<!--c:lung-abscess-c016-->

**How does actinomycosis reach the lung?** Actinomyces israelii is a normal resident of the nose and mouth.<!--c:lung-abscess-c017--> Aspirated mouth and throat secretions can carry it into the lung, and so can spread from a liver or subdiaphragmatic actinomycotic abscess.<!--c:lung-abscess-c018--> Actinomyces looks like a fungus but is a gram-positive bacterium.<!--c:lung-abscess-c019-->

## Gross morphology

**What does a lung abscess look like?** Most are 2 to 6 cm across.<!--c:lung-abscess-c020--> Several cavities are found in 10% to 20% of abscesses; the usual background is septic emboli or a necrotizing pneumonia.<!--c:lung-abscess-c021--> Lung abscesses are more often on the right than on the left.<!--c:lung-abscess-c022--> The edge of an acute abscess blends into the neighboring lung.<!--c:lung-abscess-c023--> A young abscess has an inflamed, hemorrhagic rim.<!--c:lung-abscess-c024--> Later, fibrous tissue walls off the margin.<!--c:lung-abscess-c025-->

**What is special in actinomycosis?** The lung shows a cluster of small abscesses that communicate with one another.<!--c:lung-abscess-c026--> The yellow specks seen by eye, called sulfur granules, are clumps of club-ended filaments from the colony rim.<!--c:lung-abscess-c027-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What fills a lung abscess?** An acute abscess is packed with neutrophils.<!--c:lung-abscess-c028--> How many macrophages and how much necrotic debris are seen varies with the age of the lesion.<!--c:lung-abscess-c029-->

**What does actinomycosis show?** Branching filaments form colonies in the purulent necrotic core.<!--c:lung-abscess-c030--> Granulomatous tissue forms the rim of the abscesses.<!--c:lung-abscess-c031-->

**And Nocardia?** Chronic Nocardia infection can add granulomatous features to the abscesses it causes in the lung.<!--c:lung-abscess-c032--> Right-angle branching marks its fine, beaded filaments.<!--c:lung-abscess-c033-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look for a neutrophil-rich infiltrate with necrotic debris.<!--c:lung-abscess-c034-->
- Nocardia stands out best on a Gram stain or Gomori methenamine silver.<!--c:lung-abscess-c035-->
- Nocardia is also weakly acid fast.<!--c:lung-abscess-c036-->
- In actinomycosis, the colonies stain gram-positive and are made of branching filaments.<!--c:lung-abscess-c037-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong in actinomycosis?** Reaching the pleura, they may cause empyema or a bronchopulmonary fistula (a channel between lung and bronchus).<!--c:lung-abscess-c038--> The chest wall can be invaded as well.<!--c:lung-abscess-c039-->

## High-yield summary

- **Aspiration** is the most common cause of lung abscess.<!--c:lung-abscess-c046-->
- **Alcoholism** heads the list of predisposing conditions.<!--c:lung-abscess-c047-->
- Aspiration abscesses are mostly **polymicrobial anaerobic** infections from the oropharynx.<!--c:lung-abscess-c048-->
- Non-aspiration causes include **septic emboli** and **bronchial obstruction**.<!--c:lung-abscess-c049-->
- The **right lung** is involved more often.<!--c:lung-abscess-c050-->
- Abscesses with **several cavities** typically follow septic emboli or necrotizing pneumonia.<!--c:lung-abscess-c051-->
- **Actinomyces israelii** causes many small interconnecting abscesses with **sulfur granules**.<!--c:lung-abscess-c052-->
- Actinomycosis can reach the pleura and chest wall, with **bronchopulmonary fistula** and **empyema**.<!--c:lung-abscess-c053-->
- Gram or silver stains best show **Nocardia**, with its beaded filaments that branch at right angles.<!--c:lung-abscess-c054-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is an aspiration abscess caused by one organism?** No. It is usually a mix of anaerobic bacteria.<!--c:lung-abscess-c055-->

**Are actinomycetes fungi?** No. They look like fungi but are bacteria.<!--c:lung-abscess-c056-->

**Does Nocardia mainly affect healthy people?** No. It is an opportunist that mostly strikes people with failing immune defenses, as in lymphoma.<!--c:lung-abscess-c057-->

**Does any aspiration cause an abscess?** No. A large load of oral anaerobes and impaired cough or airway clearance are both needed.<!--c:lung-abscess-c058-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the core features?** RAIN: **R**ight lung, **A**spiration, **I**mpaired cough or clearance, **N**ecrotic neutrophil-filled cavity.<!--c:lung-abscess-c059-->
