## Definition and epidemiology

Protein quality control (proteostasis) is the network of cellular systems that folds proteins into working shape, repairs those that become damaged and removes those that are faulty or no longer needed.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c001--> It has three interlocking arms: molecular chaperones (largely heat shock proteins), the proteasome, which degrades single proteins usually after they are tagged with ubiquitin, and autophagy, in which lysosomes clear what proteasomes cannot handle.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c002--> Disease follows when these systems work too little or too much, and they are frequently impaired in acquired disorders.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

Misfolding is a constant by-product of protein synthesis: cells generate many conformations and then edit them, which costs less energy than building only the correct one.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c004--> The system must cope with:

- **Random folding errors** in proteins whose amino acid sequence is normal.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c005-->
- **Mutant proteins** that cannot reach their native shape, such as mutant CFTR or α1-antitrypsin.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c006-->
- **Acquired damage** from oxidants, heat, heavy metals or altered pH and ionic strength, which unfolds proteins and exposes hydrophobic residues normally buried inside.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c007-->
- **Proteins with time-limited jobs**, such as those that drive cell division, which must be destroyed once their task is done.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c008-->
- **Damaged organelles and pathogen-derived proteins**, which must be eliminated, the latter also for presentation to the immune system.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Ubiquitination.** Ubiquitin works as an address tag that sends many proteins to their destination, the proteasome among others; this small protein of 76 amino acids, almost unchanged through evolution, is usually bound to a lysine of the target protein, while lysines of ubiquitin itself serve to join further ubiquitins into chains.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c010--> Tagging is a relay of three enzymes: an E1 enzyme activates ubiquitin and hands it to an E2 conjugating enzyme (there are dozens of these), and an E3 ligase transfers it onto the chosen protein.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c011--> The E3 ligases (about 800 are known) supply the specificity, so they decide which proteins survive.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c012--> Further ubiquitins can be linked to one another to build a chain, and a chain of four K48-linked ubiquitins is the most efficient signal for proteasomal degradation.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c013--> Single ubiquitins and other linkage types serve other purposes, including signaling, endocytosis, DNA repair, protein trafficking and gene transcription.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c014--> Deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs, about 100 known) trim or remove ubiquitin, so the tag is reversible.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c015-->

**The proteasome.** The 26S proteasome is a barrel-shaped 20S core, whose subunits cut proteins with caspase-like, trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like activities, capped at one or both ends by 19S regulatory particles.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c016--> The 19S caps recognize the ubiquitin chain, cut it off for reuse and use ATP to open the gate to the core.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c017--> Constitutive 26S proteasomes destroy short-lived regulatory proteins, products of defective mRNAs, proteins that chaperones fail to fold, and misfolded membrane or secretory proteins sent back from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER-associated degradation, ERAD).<!--c:protein-quality-control-c018--> Inflammatory signals such as IFNγ and TNFα make cells assemble immunoproteasomes with an 11S cap, which help clear damaged proteins and probably also take part in antigen processing.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c019--> The ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS) also regulates signaling: degrading the inhibitor IκB releases NF-κB, which switches on pro-survival genes.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c020-->

**Chaperones (heat shock proteins).** Heat shock proteins (HSPs) are molecular chaperones grouped into small HSPs (for example HSP27), HSP40s (J-proteins), HSP60s, HSP70s, HSP90s and HSP100s.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c021--> Besides the HSPs that cells make all the time, there are inducible ones, which stresses such as heat or ischemia switch on through the transcription factor HSF-1.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c022--> HSP70, often helped by HSP40, waits at the ribosome for the emerging chain, and HSP90 or HSP60 can add the finishing touches to folding.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c023--> When a mature protein unfolds, HSP40, small HSPs and HSP70 recognize its exposed hydrophobic patches; the co-chaperone Hip helps HSP40 activate HSP70 by ATP hydrolysis, and Hop hands the client on to HSP90.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c024--> During stress, small HSPs keep misfolded proteins in a folding-ready state, which limits aggregation.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c025--> HSP70, possibly with HSP110, can also pull apart some aggregates and try to refold their proteins.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c026--> If refolding fails, chaperones cooperate with E3 ligases such as CHIP to ubiquitinate the protein and send it to the proteasome.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c027--> HSPs also block apoptosis: they inhibit cytochrome c release, Bax entry into mitochondria, Apaf-1 and the activation of caspases 9 and 3.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c028-->

**When misfolded proteins escape.** Exposed hydrophobic surfaces make misfolded proteins stick to each other and form aggregates.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c029--> Small soluble aggregates are the most dangerous: they can perforate the plasma membrane or mitochondrial membranes (which can trigger apoptosis) and push normal proteins to misfold.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c030--> In prion diseases the normal α-helical prion protein turns into a β-pleated sheet and the misfolded protein builds up in the brain, causing a spongiform encephalopathy; most cases are sporadic, the others are inherited (mutations in the prion protein gene) or acquired through exposure to the abnormal protein.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c071--> "Prion-like" misfolding (templating), in which a misfolded protein makes normal molecules of the same protein adopt its abnormal shape, probably explains the predictable spread of other neurodegenerative diseases from the region where they begin.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c072--> Large insoluble aggregates disturb the cytoskeleton and intracellular transport and amplify oxidant injury, yet they are less cytotoxic than the small soluble ones.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c031--> The bridge protein p62 hands ubiquitinated aggregates that are too large for the proteasome over to forming autophagosomes.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c032--> Some defective proteins are delivered to lysosomes by chaperones instead (chaperone-mediated autophagy).<!--c:protein-quality-control-c033-->

## Microscopic morphology

- When clearance fails, aggregated proteins become visible inclusions: Lewy bodies (α-synuclein) in substantia nigra neurons in Parkinson disease, neurofibrillary tangles (tau) in cortical neurons in Alzheimer disease, and Mallory bodies (intermediate filaments) in hepatocytes in alcoholic liver injury.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c034-->
- Mutant α1-antitrypsin that cannot be secreted collects as globules in the hepatocyte cytoplasm.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c035-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **Specimen:** a liver biopsy can show hepatocyte inclusions such as [[g:mallory-denk-bodies|Mallory–Denk (Mallory) bodies]].<!--c:protein-quality-control-c073--> In Alzheimer disease the neuropathological diagnosis is made at autopsy, on a standard set of brain sections, where immunohistochemistry maps how far β-amyloid has spread.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c074-->
- **H&E:** in neurons, Lewy bodies are eosinophilic and frequently ringed by a pale halo.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c075--> In hepatocytes, Mallory–Denk bodies are eosinophilic clumps, often close to the nucleus, whereas α1-antitrypsin globules are only faintly eosinophilic.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c076-->
- **Special stains:** **PAS after diastase** removes glycogen and leaves α1-antitrypsin globules stained.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c036--> In Alzheimer disease both plaques and tangles take up Congo red, and silver impregnation shows them as well.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c077-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** staining for ubiquitin picks up aggregated proteins with high sensitivity but does not tell which disease is present; antibodies against the aggregating protein itself, such as tau or α-synuclein, identify it, and the result is read together with the clinical picture.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c078-->
- **Electron microscopy:** light microscopy shows only the larger inclusions, in which the cell packs the fibrils and which may even protect it by walling them off; the early fibrils, which can be highly toxic, need the electron microscope to be seen.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c079--> In the liver, Mallory–Denk bodies turn out to be bundles of keratin intermediate filaments, and α1-antitrypsin globules are misfolded protein held in dilated cisternae of the endoplasmic reticulum.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c080-->
- **Molecular tests:** in cystic fibrosis, where the misfolded CFTR is degraded by the proteasome, the diagnosis is functional and genetic: finding CFTR mutations confirms a sweat test that shows too much chloride.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c081-->

## Clinical presentation

**Too little quality control:**

- Mutations in parkin, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, cause some inherited forms of Parkinson disease.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c037-->
- Accumulated mutant proteins also make neurons malfunction and die in further neurodegenerative disorders, for example Huntington disease and familial amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c038-->
- Cardiac myocytes may also die by apoptosis from protein build-up, whether after ischemia and reperfusion or under pressure overload.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c039-->
- In cystic fibrosis the common ΔF508 mutation yields a misfolded CFTR that is degraded by the proteasome before reaching the cell membrane, so epithelia lack a chloride channel.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c040-->
- In α1-antitrypsin deficiency the misfolded protein is retained in hepatocytes, injuring the liver, while the missing circulating antiprotease leads to emphysema.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c041-->

**Too much activity, or protection of the wrong cells:**

- Excessive UPS activity causes muscle wasting (sarcopenia) in aging and in advanced cancer.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c042-->
- The E6 protein of cancer-associated human papillomaviruses brings a ubiquitin ligase to p53 and speeds its degradation.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c043-->
- HSPs, especially HSP90, keep the mutant proteins of cancer cells from aggregating, and they also help viruses fold their proteins in infected cells.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c044-->

## Complications and prognosis

- Aggregates that turn into β-pleated sheets cannot be digested, and proteostasis suffers further because they hold on to the ubiquitin attached to them, draining the cell's free supply.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c045-->
- Insoluble aggregates trap Fe²⁺, which fuels reactive oxygen species; β-sheet aggregates also inhibit proteasomes, so they can kill the cell.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c046-->
- Misfolded β-sheet proteins can also be deposited outside cells as amyloid.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c047-->
- Proteasome inhibitors exploit the system therapeutically: in plasma cell myeloma, cells that make large amounts of secretory protein are overwhelmed by it once proteasomes are blocked.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c048-->
- Proteasome inhibition also keeps IκB bound to NF-κB and preferentially affects activated T cells, which is why it can help in graft-versus-host disease.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c049-->

## High-yield summary

- Ubiquitin tagging: **E1** activates, **E2** conjugates, **E3 ligase** chooses the target.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c055-->
- A chain of four **K48-linked** ubiquitins is the classic signal for the **26S proteasome** (**20S core** plus **19S caps**).<!--c:protein-quality-control-c056-->
- **DUBs** remove ubiquitin, so the tag is reversible.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c057-->
- **HSP70** (with **HSP40**) folds new chains; **HSP90** completes folding; **HSF-1** induces HSPs under stress.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c058-->
- **Small soluble aggregates** are more toxic than large **insoluble** inclusions.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c059-->
- Aggregates too big for the proteasome go to **autophagy** through the bridge protein **p62**.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c060-->
- Misfolded ER proteins are handled by **ERAD**: return to the cytosol, ubiquitination, proteasomal degradation.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c061-->
- **Proteasome inhibitors** are used to treat **plasma cell myeloma**.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c062-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- Ubiquitin is not only a death tag: single ubiquitins and non-K48 chains control signaling, endocytosis and DNA repair.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c063-->
- The large visible inclusions are not the most toxic species; small soluble aggregates are worse.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c064-->
- Proteasomal degradation does not always need ubiquitin: 20S proteasomes can directly degrade mildly oxidized proteins.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c065-->
- Misfolding does not always cause storage: mutant CFTR is destroyed, so cystic fibrosis is a loss-of-function disease.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c066-->
- HSPs are not always on the patient's side: they also protect cancer cells and virus-infected cells.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c067-->
- Finding Mallory–Denk bodies does not prove that alcohol is the cause: non-alcoholic steatohepatitis and Wilson disease can show them too.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c082-->

## Mnemonics

**"Activate, Conjugate, Ligate"** (original): the E-numbers go in alphabetical order of their verbs, E1 **A**ctivates, E2 **C**onjugates, E3 **L**igates.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c068-->

**Fold, Hold, Throw** (original): chaperones **fold** proteins, small HSPs **hold** them during stress, and the proteasome or autophagy **throws out** what cannot be rescued.<!--c:protein-quality-control-c069-->
