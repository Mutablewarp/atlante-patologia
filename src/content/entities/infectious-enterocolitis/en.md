## Definition and epidemiology

**What is infectious enterocolitis?** Microbes cause it, and the result is diarrheal disease of the small bowel and colon.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c001--> Bacteria cause diarrhea by invading the gut, adhering to it, or releasing toxins (toxigenic).<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c002--> Infants are most at risk, and deaths cluster where sanitation is poor.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which microbes cause it?**
- **Toxigenic bacteria:** the prototypes are *Vibrio cholerae* and toxigenic *E. coli*.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c004-->
- **Invasive bacteria:** *Shigella*, *Salmonella*, *Yersinia*, *Campylobacter* and some *E. coli* strains are the usual culprits.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c006-->
- **Viruses:** rotavirus and norovirus lead the list of viral gastroenteritis in the United States.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c007-->
- **Mycobacteria:** most cases start when patients swallow infectious sputum or eat food carrying *M. tuberculosis* or *M. bovis*.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c008-->
- **Protozoa:** *Giardia lamblia* spreads through unprotected drinking water or contact with an infected person.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c009-->
- **Opportunists:** in AIDS and other immunosuppression, nearly every class of microbe can infect the gut.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does each group cause diarrhea?**
- Toxigenic bacteria sit on the gut lining and release their toxin from there.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c014--> Little or no mucosal damage results.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c015--> The gut keeps secreting salts and water because toxins upset its ion transporters.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c016--> Poor absorption and fast transit matter less than secretion in infectious diarrhea.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c017-->
- Toxigenic bacteria work mostly in the upper gut; invasive ones settle mainly in the distal ileum and colon.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c018--> Injured mucosa may also fail to take fluid back up.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c020-->
- Rotavirus injures the cells lining the gut surface, so absorption suffers.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c022--> In norovirus infection absorption fails, with focal mucosal injury in the proximal small bowel.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c023-->
- The tubercle bacillus survives gastric acid, since its cell wall protects it.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c024--> Lymphoid tissue is abundant in the ileocecal region, where the bacillus usually lodges.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c025-->
- A falling CD4 count raises the risk of opportunistic infection.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c027-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the bowel look like?**
- Shigellosis hits the rectum and sigmoid hardest; the mucosa looks granular and bleeds, and many superficial ulcers wind across it.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c029-->
- Typhoid ulcers are scattered, mostly in the terminal ileum, where infected Peyer patches undergo necrosis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c030-->
- In *Yersinia* infection the mucosa over the Peyer patches ulcerates acutely, and the patches themselves are hyperplastic.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c032-->
- Tuberculous ulcers of the bowel differ in size and run crosswise to its long axis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c033--> A napkin-ring stricture, a localized ring-shaped narrowing, may follow scarring of healing ulcers.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c034--> Caseation and enlargement are the usual findings in mesenteric nodes.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c035-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the biopsy and the nodes show?**
- Cholera and toxigenic *E. coli* cause minimal inflammatory change.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c036--> *Salmonella*, *Shigella* and *Campylobacter* typically give a brief self-limited colitis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c037--> *Yersinia* and mycobacteria produce granulomas.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c038-->
- *Shigella* ulcers form where mucosal lymphoid follicles disintegrate, after crypt abscesses of neutrophils appear.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c039--> *Shigella* colitis lacks the crypt branching and the heavy lymphocyte-plasma cell infiltrate of ulcerative colitis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c040--> *Campylobacter* resembles *Shigella* under the microscope.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c041-->
- In early typhoid, big macrophages full of bacilli stain blue; debris and red cells lie nearby.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c042--> Regional nodes show lymphoid hyperplasia and necrosis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c043-->
- *Yersinia* granulomas in the ileum and appendix can mimic Crohn disease.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c047-->
- Few bacilli lie in the necrotizing granulomas of intestinal TB, which favor lymphoid tissue like Peyer patches yet can occur in any layer.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c048-->
- *Giardia* is usually abundant and sits on the epithelium without entering it.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c050-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at?**

**How it is studied:**
- Stool or duodenal fluid can show *Giardia* trophozoites.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c052-->
- Different bacterial infections of the gut produce different histologic reaction patterns.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c054-->
- PCR identifies *Yersinia* well; culture can be tricky.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c055-->
- The reference pages used here do not cover special stains, IHC or electron microscopy.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient complain of?**
- Toxigenic infection causes watery diarrhea.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c056--> Cholera can kill through the dehydration that the diarrhea causes.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c057-->
- Loose stools, cramps and fever mark *Yersinia* infection.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c058--> It can mimic appendicitis through mesenteric adenitis and pain in the right lower abdomen.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c059-->
- Tainted or undercooked food, or sick domestic animals, usually spread *Campylobacter*.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c061-->
- Diarrhea in infants is often due to rotavirus.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c063--> Norovirus gives vomiting and diarrhea that usually resolve quickly.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c064-->
- Many intestinal TB patients have a palpable right lower abdominal mass, and almost all have chronic pain.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c065--> Fever and a run-down, undernourished state are common in intestinal TB.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c066-->
- Giardiasis may impair absorption and cause heavy watery diarrhea with nausea and vomiting.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c067-->
- Persistent diarrhea affects the majority of people with AIDS.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c068-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the cause?**
- Patients with common variable immunodeficiency, who make little immunoglobulin and have few plasma cells in the gut wall, often get giardiasis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c069--> To exclude common variable immunodeficiency, check the plasma cells whenever a biopsy shows *Giardia*.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c070-->
- Crohn disease or ischemia can produce a stricture that mimics a tuberculous one.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c071-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?**
- Late in typhoid, and during recovery, the ileum may bleed or perforate; these are the complications to dread.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c072-->
- Nontyphoidal *Salmonella* can reach the skeleton and the meninges through the blood.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c075-->
- Intestinal TB may obstruct, perforate, fistulize or form abscesses.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c079-->

## High-yield summary

- **Toxigenic** diarrhea is watery, with bacteria on the surface and little mucosal damage.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c086-->
- **Invasive** bacteria injure the distal ileum and colon.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c087-->
- **Typhoid** ulcers lie over **Peyer patches** of the terminal ileum.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c088-->
- **Yersinia** can mimic appendicitis and Crohn disease.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c089-->
- **Shigella** colitis shows no branched crypts, unlike ulcerative colitis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c090-->
- **Rotavirus** often lies behind diarrhea in infants.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c091--> **Norovirus** hits the proximal small bowel.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c092-->
- **Intestinal TB** favors the ileocecal region and can leave a **napkin ring** stricture.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c093-->
- **Giardia** adheres without invading; look for **plasma cells**.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c094-->
- A lower **CD4 count** means more risk of **opportunistic** infection.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c095-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do toxigenic bacteria invade?** No. They stay on the surface.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c096-->

**Does *Giardia* invade the bowel wall?** No. It adheres to the epithelium, and the mucosa can look normal.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c097-->

**Is *Yersinia* infection only diarrhea?** No. It can mimic appendicitis.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c098-->

**Is a stricture always Crohn disease?** No. Tuberculosis can mimic it.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c099-->

## Mnemonics

**Toxins Top, Invaders Distal:** toxigenic bacteria stay on the upper surface; invasive ones injure the distal ileum and colon.<!--c:infectious-enterocolitis-c100-->
