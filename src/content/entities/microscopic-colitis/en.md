## Definition and epidemiology

**What is microscopic colitis?** Hence the name microscopic colitis: in collagenous and lymphocytic colitis the colon looks normal and only the microscope reveals the lesion.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c001-->
The main symptom is chronic watery diarrhea.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c002--> When weight loss or abdominal pain is also present, it is usually mild.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c003--> The frequency rises with age<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c004--> and women are affected more often than men.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c005--> Autoimmune disease is linked to it, and so are smoking and some drugs.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c006--> The cause is unknown.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c007--> Celiac disease is frequently associated with it.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c008-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the pathologist see on the biopsy?** In lymphocytic colitis, more than 20 intraepithelial lymphocytes are counted per 100 epithelial cells, and the surface epithelium is injured.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c009--> Extra chronic inflammatory cells in the lamina propria lie typically near the surface.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c010--> Unlike inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), it leaves the gland architecture intact.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c011-->
Collagenous colitis shows the same changes as lymphocytic colitis.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c012--> Its hallmark is an uneven collagen band under the epithelium, usually over 10 μm thick.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c013--> Capillaries are caught inside the thickened band.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c014--> By contrast, lymphocytic colitis has no thickened collagen layer beneath the epithelium.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c015-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How do you diagnose a colitis that looks normal?** Gross inspection shows nothing, so biopsies read under the microscope are the only route to the diagnosis.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c016-->
Biopsies taken at random along the whole colon help, since changes can be strongest on the right.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c017--> Collagenous colitis can need several biopsies, as the band varies in thickness.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c018-->

## High-yield summary

- **Microscopic colitis** means collagenous plus lymphocytic colitis, with a grossly normal colon.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c022-->
- The leading symptom is **chronic watery diarrhea**.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c023-->
- **Lymphocytic colitis** has over 20 intraepithelial lymphocytes per 100 epithelial cells.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c024-->
- In **collagenous colitis**, the collagen layer beneath the epithelium is usually thicker than 10 μm.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c025-->
- **Normal gland architecture** separates both from IBD.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c026-->
- Biopsy the colon at **random sites**: the right side may show the strongest changes.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c027-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a normal colon at endoscopy rule out microscopic colitis?** No. Gross inspection finds nothing; the abnormality appears only under the microscope.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c028-->

**Are lymphocytic and collagenous colitis the same lesion?** Not quite. Both have intraepithelial lymphocytes, but only collagenous colitis has the thickened collagen band.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c029-->

**Do NSAIDs cause microscopic colitis?** It is unclear. Many patients take NSAIDs for joint pain, so the link may be incidental.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c030-->

**Is microscopic colitis a form of IBD?** No. Unlike IBD, it leaves the gland architecture intact.<!--c:microscopic-colitis-c031-->
