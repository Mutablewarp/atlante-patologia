## Definition and epidemiology

**What is hereditary nephritis?** Genetic defects in type IV collagen cause hereditary nephritis, an inherited glomerular disease.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c001--> Alport syndrome is hereditary nephritis with inherited hearing loss.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c002--> Some patients have eye defects, usually of the lens.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c003--> COL4A5 encodes the α5 chain of type IV collagen; its mutation causes the commonest, X-linked form.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c004--> This X-linked type makes up 85% of hereditary nephritis.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c005--> Mutations of COL4A3 or COL4A4 account for recessive, non-X-linked hereditary nephritis.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c006--> Unlike the X-linked form, it affects both sexes alike.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c007-->

**How is thin basement membrane disease related?** Thin basement membrane disease arises with one mutated copy of COL4A3 or COL4A4, Alport syndrome with two.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c008-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen under the microscope?** At first, Alport glomeruli gain matrix and a few mesangial cells.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c009--> Sclerosis spreads from patchy to widespread; in advanced kidneys the tubulointerstitium is scarred, wasted and holds foam cells.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c010--> Pale spaces between layers of a split lamina densa, and a GBM of varying width, are the electron microscopy findings that settle the diagnosis.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c011-->

**And in thin basement membrane disease?** Light microscopy shows nothing remarkable in the glomeruli.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c012--> On electron microscopy the GBM is 150 to 300 nm, versus a normal 350 to 450 nm.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c013-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How do the two diseases present?** Early hematuria is the first feature in boys with X-linked Alport syndrome.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c014--> Between the second and fourth decades, proteinuria and declining kidney function usually appear.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c015--> Half of these males lose high-frequency sensorineural hearing.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c016--> Women with the X-linked form usually have a milder course than men.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c017--> In thin basement membrane disease the hematuria is often silent, found only on microscopy, and occasionally visible.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c018--> Kidney function usually holds and protein loss stays minor.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c019-->

## High-yield summary

- A **type IV collagen** defect (X-linked **COL4A5**) causes Alport syndrome, with hematuria, sensorineural **hearing loss** and lens defects.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c023-->
- **Electron microscopy** shows a split, layered lamina densa in Alport syndrome.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c024-->
- **Thin basement membrane disease** is **benign familial hematuria**, with a GBM 150 to 300 nm thick.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c025-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is thin basement membrane disease just mild Alport syndrome?** Usually not: renal failure and heavy proteinuria are generally absent.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c026-->

**Does anti-GBM serum react with the Alport GBM?** No. Anti-GBM serum does not recognize the altered membrane.<!--c:alport-and-thin-basement-membrane-disease-c027-->
