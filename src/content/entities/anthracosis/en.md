## Definition and epidemiology

**What is anthracosis, and what is coal workers' pneumoconiosis?** Anthracosis is the harmless buildup of inhaled carbon in the lung, seen as black pigment inside alveolar macrophages.<!--c:anthracosis-c001--> Coal workers' pneumoconiosis (CWP) is the lung disease of coal miners caused by breathing coal dust.<!--c:anthracosis-c002--> CWP has a simple and a complicated form, the latter also called progressive massive fibrosis.<!--c:anthracosis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What do miners inhale, and which part of the dust is dangerous?** Silica is a variable part of coal dust, alongside amorphous carbon and other mineral matter from the ground.<!--c:anthracosis-c004--> Carbon on its own does not cause fibrosis.<!--c:anthracosis-c005--> Anthracosilicosis can develop when inhaled coal particles come with silica, because silica is a potent cause of fibrosis.<!--c:anthracosis-c006--> Bituminous coal, the soft kind, has less quartz than hard anthracite.<!--c:anthracosis-c007--> Quartz exposure, and so risk, is greater underground than on the surface or in coal loading.<!--c:anthracosis-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does pure carbon stay harmless while mixed dust scars the lung?** Macrophages that ingest amorphous carbon survive, and the only result is harmless anthracosis.<!--c:anthracosis-c009--> Nodules are better called anthracosilicosis, since they form when coal dust is mixed with silica or a similar scarring dust.<!--c:anthracosis-c010--> Complicated CWP arises in lungs that already have simple CWP.<!--c:anthracosis-c011-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the lung look like?** Simple CWP leaves the lung dotted with multiple black spots, each 1 to 4 mm across.<!--c:anthracosis-c012--> Macules cannot be felt, while nodules can be felt.<!--c:anthracosis-c013--> Both lungs usually carry several Caplan nodules, mostly at the periphery, measuring 1 to 10 cm.<!--c:anthracosis-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do macules and nodules look like under the microscope?** A macule is a crowd of carbon-filled macrophages around a distal respiratory bronchiole.<!--c:anthracosis-c015--> The cells also crowd nearby alveoli and the tissue surrounding the bronchioles.<!--c:anthracosis-c016--> Loss of smooth muscle in the respiratory bronchioles probably explains focal dust emphysema, a mild dilation of these airways.<!--c:anthracosis-c017--> A nodule is dust-filled macrophages in fibrotic stroma, round or irregular, with or without a bronchiole beside it.<!--c:anthracosis-c018--> Deposits of inhaled dust sit within Caplan nodules, which otherwise resemble rheumatoid nodules under the microscope.<!--c:anthracosis-c019--> In a true rheumatoid nodule, palisading macrophages and chronic inflammation form a border around a large area of necrosis at the center.<!--c:anthracosis-c020--> A Caplan nodule differs from the true rheumatoid nodule, and it may be a hybrid of a silicotic and a rheumatoid nodule.<!--c:anthracosis-c021-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on microscopy, look for carbon-laden macrophages around the respiratory bronchioles.<!--c:anthracosis-c022-->
- The reference pages used for this card describe no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test, and no sampling method.

## Clinical presentation

**How much do the lesions affect breathing, and what else can occur?** At worst, simple CWP causes only minor loss of lung function, although it was once thought to be severely disabling.<!--c:anthracosis-c023--> Smoking is usually the explanation for severe airflow obstruction in coal miners.<!--c:anthracosis-c024--> The original cases of Caplan syndrome were coal miners with rheumatoid arthritis who had rheumatoid nodules in the lungs.<!--c:anthracosis-c025--> Other pneumoconioses, for example silicosis or asbestosis, can also be accompanied by pulmonary rheumatoid nodules, and the name is now used for that association too.<!--c:anthracosis-c026-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How are the lesions found and classified?** Small nodular densities on a chest radiograph represent the macules and nodules.<!--c:anthracosis-c027--> A lesion measuring 2.0 cm or more defines complicated CWP.<!--c:anthracosis-c028-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** Complicated CWP can seriously impair breathing.<!--c:anthracosis-c029-->

## High-yield summary

- **Anthracosis** is harmless carbon pigment in alveolar macrophages.<!--c:anthracosis-c033-->
- Black foci of 1 to 4 mm are the macules and nodules of **simple CWP**.<!--c:anthracosis-c034-->
- A **nodule** with a fibrotic stroma means silica was mixed in, so it is **anthracosilicosis**.<!--c:anthracosis-c035-->
- In a lung with simple CWP, a lesion of 2.0 cm or more marks **progressive massive fibrosis** (complicated CWP).<!--c:anthracosis-c036-->
- **Caplan syndrome** is rheumatoid lung nodules, 1 to 10 cm, in a worker with a pneumoconiosis.<!--c:anthracosis-c037-->
- In a coal miner, **smoking** is usually the cause of severe airflow obstruction.<!--c:anthracosis-c038-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does the carbon itself scar the lung?** No. Amorphous carbon is not fibrogenic; fibrosis comes with silica.<!--c:anthracosis-c039-->

**Does simple CWP cause severe disability?** No. The loss of lung function is small, even in the worst cases.<!--c:anthracosis-c040-->

**Is Caplan syndrome found only in coal miners?** No. Silicosis, asbestosis and other pneumoconioses count too, although the first cases were coal miners with rheumatoid arthritis.<!--c:anthracosis-c041-->

**Is a Caplan nodule the same as an ordinary rheumatoid nodule?** No. It differs from it and may be a blend of silicotic and rheumatoid nodules.<!--c:anthracosis-c042-->

**Is a coal-dust nodule pure coal pneumoconiosis?** No. Nodules appear when silica or a similar scarring dust is mixed into the coal dust.<!--c:anthracosis-c043-->
