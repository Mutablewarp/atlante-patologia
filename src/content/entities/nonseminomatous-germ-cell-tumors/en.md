## Definition and epidemiology

**What are non-seminomatous germ cell tumors?** In the testis, most of them are either pure embryonal carcinoma or a mixed germ cell tumor.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c001--> Several of the five types can sit together in one mixed tumor: choriocarcinoma, yolk sac tumor, embryonal carcinoma, teratoma and seminoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c002--> In adults, the testis seldom holds a pure choriocarcinoma or a pure yolk sac tumor.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c003-->

Of all testicular germ cell tumors, 55% are non-seminomatous.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c004--> Seminoma and non-seminomatous elements coexist in as many as one third of testicular germ cell tumors.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c005--> Patients are usually found slightly earlier in life than seminoma patients, although both groups peak in the third and fourth decades.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Where do these tumors come from?** They may derive from germ cell neoplasia in situ (GCNIS), from embryonal cells, or from seminoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

**How can one tumor contain so many tissues?** Embryonal carcinoma cells resemble the cells of a preimplantation embryo.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c008--> A teratoma results when these cells differentiate toward all three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c009--> The tissues may be adult-type (mature) or fetal-type (immature).<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c010--> Two extraembryonic products are also possible: yolk sac-like epithelium, and chorionic epithelium made of cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c011--> A teratocarcinoma, or malignant teratoma, still holds the embryonal cells that gave rise to its body and extraembryonic tissues.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c012--> When malignant trophoblast takes over and is the only component left, the tumor is a choriocarcinoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c013--> A yolk sac tumor is a single clone of malignant cells imitating yolk sac lining.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c014-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the cut surface show?** Solid or partly cystic masses, with no fixed size or shape.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c015--> In the solid areas, hemorrhage appears red, necrosis yellow and viable tumor white.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c016-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does each component look like?**
- **Embryonal carcinoma:** nuclei look piled on one another, because undifferentiated cells with large, dark nuclei have almost no cytoplasm to separate them.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c017-->
- Embryonal carcinoma may form gland-like spaces as well as cords and solid sheets.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c018-->
- Many embryonal carcinoma cells are dividing or dying by apoptosis.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c019-->
- **Teratoma:** mature and immature elements often sit side by side, within tissues derived from all three germ layers.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c020-->
- Retinal pigment cells, central nervous system tissue and skin are ectodermal products.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c021-->
- Bone, cartilage and muscle, both smooth and striated, come from mesoderm.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c022-->
- Salivary gland, bronchial lining and gut-type tissue are endodermal.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c023-->
- Epidermoid and dermoid cysts are teratoma subtypes with ectodermal elements only.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c024-->
- Pilosebaceous units distinguish a dermoid cyst from an epidermoid cyst; both are lined by keratinized squamous epithelium.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c025-->
- **Yolk sac tumor:** several patterns are seen, and each imitates part of the fetal yolk sac.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c026-->
- Glomeruloid **Schiller–Duval bodies** are its signature structure.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c027-->
- **Choriocarcinoma:** highly malignant cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c028-->
- Yolk sac-type and syncytiotrophoblast-type parts are often found in these tumors, which sets them apart from seminomas.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c029-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** H&E sections can look very different from case to case.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c030-->
- Embryonal carcinoma reacts with PLAP and OCT3/4, like seminoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c031-->
- It also expresses cytokeratins and CD30.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c032-->
- It does not express c-KIT (CD117), which separates it from seminoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c033-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do they present?** Most present as a testicular mass.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c034--> In some patients a metastasis is the first thing that reveals the tumor.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c035-->

**What about boys before puberty?** Yolk sac tumor accounts for the majority of testicular neoplasms in boys younger than 4.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c036--> Most of these boys are younger than 2 years.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c037--> A benign teratoma is the tumor most often found in the testis of boys aged 4 to 12.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c038-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** Diagnosing a yolk sac tumor relies on seeing Schiller–Duval bodies together with several microscopic patterns.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c039--> A tumor with any seminoma component is still managed as non-seminomatous.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c040-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How aggressive are they?** Metastasis is easier and more widespread than with seminoma, and growth is faster too.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c041--> Embryonal carcinoma gets into blood vessels and also infiltrates the testis and epididymis.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c042--> Its metastases reach the lungs and other organs as well as the abdominal lymph nodes.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c043--> Malignant change in a teratoma's somatic tissue can produce sarcoma or carcinoma (squamous or glandular).<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c044-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What else looks like it?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[seminoma]] | Cells resemble immature spermatogonia.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c045--> | Positive for OCT3/4 and c-KIT (CD117).<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c046--> |
| Embryonal carcinoma | Undifferentiated cells with crowded, overlapping nuclei.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c047--> | Positive for CD30 and cytokeratins, negative for c-KIT.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c048--> |
| Spermatocytic tumor | Benign, GCNIS-negative, and a different entity from classical seminoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c049--> | Typical seminoma markers are absent.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c050--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Embryonal carcinoma** is undifferentiated, with overlapping nuclei, and is CD30 and cytokeratin positive but c-KIT negative.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c051-->
- Pure embryonal carcinoma and **mixed germ cell tumor** account for most cases.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c052-->
- **Yolk sac tumor** is recognized by **Schiller–Duval bodies**.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c053-->
- **Choriocarcinoma** is made of cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c054-->
- **Teratoma** contains tissues of all three germ layers.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c055-->
- A **seminoma component** does not change the treatment: the tumor is handled as non-seminomatous.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c056-->
- Non-seminomatous tumors **spread faster and wider** than seminomas, and metastases can be the first sign.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c057-->
- In the first 4 years of life, the usual testicular tumor is the **yolk sac tumor**.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c058-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a testicular tumor with a seminoma component treated as a seminoma?** No. If it also has non-seminomatous elements, it is treated clinically as non-seminomatous.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c059-->

**Do embryonal carcinoma and seminoma stain the same way?** Only in part. Both react with PLAP and OCT3/4, but embryonal carcinoma is c-KIT negative.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c060-->

**Is every teratoma benign?** No. Any of its somatic elements can become malignant and give carcinoma or sarcoma.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c061-->

**Is an epidermoid or dermoid cyst of the testis a mixed tumor?** No. It is pure ectoderm, a teratoma subtype with no GCNIS, no immature tissue and no other tumor types.<!--c:nonseminomatous-germ-cell-tumors-c062-->
