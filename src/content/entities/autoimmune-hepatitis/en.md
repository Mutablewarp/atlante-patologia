## Definition and epidemiology

**What is autoimmune hepatitis?** Blood autoantibodies and high serum immunoglobulins mark this severe form of chronic hepatitis.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c001--> Women make up about 70% of patients, and onset is possible at any age.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c002--> Overlap syndrome combines autoimmune hepatitis with features of [[primary-biliary-cholangitis|PBC]] or [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|PSC]], and is still poorly defined.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What predisposes to it?** Susceptibility to type I disease is tied to the HLA-DRB1 gene.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c004--> Autoimmune diseases of the gut, thyroid and joints often coexist with type I disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c005--> Thyroiditis and type 1 diabetes are frequent companions of type II disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c006--> For type II, the genetic basis remains undefined.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which antibodies define the two types, and what do they target?** About 80% of patients have type I, which makes it the commoner type.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c008--> The serum of type I patients contains antinuclear and anti-smooth muscle antibodies.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c009--> Type II mostly affects children between 2 and 14 years of age.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c010--> The hallmark of type II is anti-LKM antibodies, which react with liver and kidney microsomes.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c011--> CYP2D6, an enzyme of the P450 family that clears drugs, is the chief antibody target in type II.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c012--> In type I, antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity probably targets mainly the asialoglycoprotein receptor of the hepatocyte membrane.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c013-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the liver look like?** No distinctive gross picture is described here. Cirrhosis can result when the disease goes untreated.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** More marked interface hepatitis and lobular damage separate the biopsy from the acute or chronic viral hepatitis it resembles.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c015--> Finding many plasma cells among the inflammatory cells is a key diagnostic pointer.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c016--> Severe cases may show confluent necrosis.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c017--> In interface hepatitis, inflammatory cells around the portal tract eat into the limiting plate in places, leaving a ragged edge.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c018--> The portal infiltrate is mononuclear and varies in density, with plasma cells; eosinophils may be added in this disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c019-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which tests help?** No special stain or molecular test is described here.
- **Serology:** antinuclear and anti-smooth muscle antibodies point to type I.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c020-->
- **Serology:** anti-LKM antibodies point to type II.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c021-->
- **Serology:** serum immunoglobulins are elevated.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c022-->
- **Biopsy on H&E:** look for interface hepatitis with many plasma cells.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c023-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** It can begin quietly, with tiredness and a dull ache under the right ribs.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c024--> Many patients, or their relatives, have an autoimmune disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c025--> Aminotransferases can exceed 1,000 IU/mL later in the disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c026--> Blood globulins are often markedly raised (hyperglobulinemia).<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c027--> Fulminant onset is rare; severe cases can bring jaundice and liver failure.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c028--> Type I usually stays asymptomatic for long; about one patient in four already has cirrhosis when first seen.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c029-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What supports the diagnosis?**
- On biopsy, many plasma cells in the infiltrate weigh strongly in favor of the diagnosis.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c030-->
- Blood tests show autoantibodies, together with high serum immunoglobulin levels.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c031-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens with and without treatment?** Without treatment, cirrhosis is a frequent outcome.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c032--> A corticosteroid combined with azathioprine or a similar immunosuppressant generally controls the disease.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c033--> Liver transplantation can be an option once cirrhosis has developed.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c034--> After liver transplantation, up to 20% of patients see the disease return.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c035--> Star-shaped portal tracts result from collagen laid down after interface hepatitis destroys periportal hepatocytes.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c036-->

## High-yield summary

- **Type I** is the commoner form; its antibodies are **antinuclear** and **anti-smooth muscle**.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c042-->
- **Type II** affects mainly children, with **anti-LKM** antibodies against **CYP2D6**.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c043-->
- The biopsy shows **interface hepatitis** with a **plasma-cell-rich** infiltrate.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c044-->
- **Azathioprine**, an immunosuppressant, is given together with a **corticosteroid**.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c045-->
- Untreated disease often ends in **cirrhosis**.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c046-->
- An **overlap syndrome** with PBC or PSC exists.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c047-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is autoimmune hepatitis only a disease of young women?** No. It can appear at any age.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c048--> Type II occurs mainly in children.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c049-->

**Does a silent course exclude advanced disease?** No. In type I, a long time without symptoms is usual, and some patients first come to attention with cirrhosis.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c050-->

**Is autoimmune hepatitis usually fulminant?** No. Fulminant presentation is rare, although severe cases can end in liver failure.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c051-->

**Does interface hepatitis prove an autoimmune cause?** No. It is a feature of chronic hepatitis in general, and what points here is the plasma-cell-rich infiltrate.<!--c:autoimmune-hepatitis-c052-->
