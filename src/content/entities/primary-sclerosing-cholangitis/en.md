## Definition and epidemiology

**What is primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)?** Inflammation and scarring of unknown origin slowly close the bile ducts, inside and outside the liver, so bile flows poorly.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c001-->
Men make up about 70% of patients.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c002-->
The mean age of patients is around 40 years.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c003-->
Roughly 14 of every 100,000 people have the disease.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c004-->
Cholangitis recurs and obstructive jaundice persists as the blockage worsens.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c005-->
The end point is secondary biliary cirrhosis.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What is PSC linked to?** The cause is unknown.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c007-->
[[ulcerative-colitis]] coexists in about two thirds of patients, which is the link to remember.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c008-->
Crohn disease of the colon is a less common partner, seen in a few cases.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c009-->
Riedel struma (a fibrosing form of thyroiditis), lymphoma and retroperitoneal fibrosis have also been reported alongside PSC.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c010-->
About a quarter of cases stand alone, with no other disease.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c011-->
HLA B8 and some other HLA types are linked to the disease, and it can run in families.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**What is thought to injure the ducts?** Both inherited and immune mechanisms are thought to play a part.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c013-->
The portal tracts hold more T cells than normal.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c016-->
Perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (P-ANCA) are frequently found.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c015-->
Serum gamma globulins are often raised (hypergammaglobulinemia).<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c014-->
Bacteria crossing a leaky colon wall may play a role, but there is no proof.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c017-->

## Gross morphology

**Which ducts are involved?** The same process can block the large ducts too, inside and outside the liver.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c018-->
The gallbladder wall shows the same inflammation.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c019-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the liver show under the microscope?** Like primary biliary cholangitis, PSC liver disease is staged in four steps.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c020-->
- **Stage 1, portal:** fibrosis in concentric onion-skin layers appears in the portal tracts, with inflammation around the ducts.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c021-->
- **Stage 2, periportal:** many bile ducts disappear (obliteration).<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c022-->
- **Stage 2, periportal:** bands of scar tissue reach from the portal tracts into the surrounding liver tissue.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c023-->
- **Stage 3, septal:** bridging fibrosis.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c024-->
- **Stage 4, cirrhosis:** secondary biliary cirrhosis.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c025-->

A scar is left in the place of a destroyed bile duct.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c026-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on contrast radiography, the intrahepatic ducts look beaded.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c027-->
- **How it is studied:** even when a portal tract is barely inflamed, onion-skin fibrosis can already ring a bile duct.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c028-->
- **How it is studied:** a trichrome stain highlights the scar that replaces a destroyed duct.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c029-->
- The reference pages describe no immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test for PSC.

## Clinical presentation

**How does PSC present?** Patients may come to attention with advanced liver disease or with nothing but abnormal cholestatic tests.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c030-->
Abscesses can follow infection.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c031-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What points to PSC?** In patients without symptoms, the first sign can be abnormal cholestatic liver tests.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c032-->
Beaded intrahepatic ducts on cholangiography reflect the tendency of the disease to be segmental.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c033-->
A normal-looking cholangiogram with a typical clinical picture defines small duct PSC.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c034-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens to patients over time?** Patients with symptoms survive a median of 8 to 9 years.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c035-->
Patients without symptoms do better.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c036-->
Up to 20% of patients develop [[cholangiocarcinoma]].<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c037-->
Liver transplantation may cure the disease.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c038-->
Even so, after a transplant the disease often returns in the new liver.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c039-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[primary-biliary-cholangitis\|Primary biliary cholangitis]] | Nearly all patients are women.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c040--> Its florid duct lesion is inflammation crowding around and damaging a bile duct in the portal tract.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c041--> | Diagnosis needs two of three criteria; an antimitochondrial antibody titer of 1:40 or higher is one of the three.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c042--> |
| [[cholangiocarcinoma]] | A tumor that arises in up to 20% of PSC patients, so it is a complication to look for.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c043--> | Not covered in the reference pages. |

## High-yield summary

- PSC goes with **ulcerative colitis** in about two thirds of patients.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c044-->
- Early PSC shows **onion-skin periductal fibrosis** in the portal tracts.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c045-->
- Cholangiography shows **beaded ducts**, reflecting the tendency of the disease to be segmental.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c046-->
- **Cholangiocarcinoma** develops in up to 20% of patients.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c047-->
- PSC is mostly a disease of **men**, unlike primary biliary cholangitis.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c048-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Are PSC and primary biliary cholangitis the same disease?** No. PSC mostly affects men, whereas nearly all patients with primary biliary cholangitis are women.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c049-->

**Does a normal cholangiogram rule out PSC?** No. A patient can have the typical picture of PSC and a normal-looking cholangiogram: this is small duct PSC.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c050-->

**Is PSC always a complication of ulcerative colitis?** No. About a quarter of cases stand alone, with no other disease.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c051-->

**Does a liver transplant end the problem?** Not always. After a transplant the disease often returns in the new liver.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c052-->

## Mnemonics

**BEADS:** **B**eaded ducts, **E**xtra- and intrahepatic ducts, **A**ssociated ulcerative colitis, **D**uct onion-skin fibrosis, **S**econdary biliary cirrhosis.<!--c:primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-c053-->
