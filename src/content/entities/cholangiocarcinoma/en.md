## Definition and epidemiology

**What is cholangiocarcinoma?** It is a carcinoma that grows from the cells lining the bile ducts.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c001-->
The intrahepatic form can start at any level of the intrahepatic biliary tree, from the finest ducts to the largest ones.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c002-->
The perihilar form sits at the hilum, where the right and left hepatic ducts meet.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c003-->
Adenocarcinoma accounts for nearly every tumor of the bile duct outside the liver, wherever along its course it arises.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c004-->

**Who gets it?** The intrahepatic form affects men and women, mostly at older ages.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c005-->
Patients are on average 60 years old at presentation.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c006-->
The tumor is especially frequent in Asian areas where infection with the liver fluke *C. sinensis* is widespread.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c007-->
Gallbladder cancer is more common than extrahepatic bile duct cancer.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c008-->
Extrahepatic bile duct tumors affect men and women about equally.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c009-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which conditions raise the risk?** Primary sclerosing cholangitis ([[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|PSC]]) is a strong predisposing condition.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c010-->
A cholangiocarcinoma is found in one in four livers removed for transplantation in PSC.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c011-->
Both Caroli disease and choledochal cysts predispose to the tumor.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c012-->
Chronic hepatitis B or C and alcohol-related cirrhosis are linked to a rising number of intrahepatic cases.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c013-->
In Asia, bile duct cancer is linked to *C. sinensis* flukes living in the bile ducts.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c014-->
Patients with extrahepatic bile duct tumors commonly have gallstones too.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c015-->
Inflammatory diseases of the colon are associated with extrahepatic bile duct tumors.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the tumor look like to the naked eye?** In the perihilar form, the tumor can spread through the wall of the duct, or form a small firm fibrous nodule that occludes the lumen.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c017-->
A rare perihilar variant is intraductal and papillary.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c018-->
Any of these can eventually form a mass.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c019-->
Extrahepatic tumors can infiltrate diffusely or grow inward, into the lumen (endophytic growth).<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the typical histology?** Small cuboidal cells line the ductules or glands that make up an intrahepatic tumor.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c021-->
Abundant fibrous tissue surrounds the tumor glands, which look fairly mature.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c022-->
If a tumor shows features of both hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma, it is called a combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c023-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on a liver biopsy, the tumor appears as glands lying in abundant fibrous tissue.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c024-->
- In a liver biopsy, a tumor rich in fibrous tissue can pass for a metastasis from the breast or pancreas.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c025-->
- The reference text used for this card does not describe a special stain, an immunohistochemical panel or a molecular test for this tumor.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Every perihilar tumor obstructs the extrahepatic bile ducts and causes symptoms.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c026-->
Obstructive jaundice is how carcinoma of the extrahepatic bile duct presents.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c027-->
Symptoms appear early in the course of the disease.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c028-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it behave?** Invasion of the portal and hepatic veins is milder than in HCC.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c029-->
Local spread follows the nerves.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c030-->
Metastases reach many parts of the body, with portal lymph nodes a favored site.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c031-->
A liver transplant rarely removes the tumor for good.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c032-->
The prognosis of extrahepatic bile duct carcinoma is poor.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c033-->
Early symptoms give it a slightly better outlook than gallbladder carcinoma.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c034-->

## High-yield summary

- Cholangiocarcinoma arises from **bile duct epithelium**; **PSC** is the strong risk factor, with about one in four PSC livers removed for transplant containing it.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c039-->
- It is divided by site into **intrahepatic**, **perihilar** (junction of the right and left hepatic ducts) and extrahepatic.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c040-->
- Histology: a **gland-forming** tumor with abundant **fibrous** tissue.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c041-->
- Perihilar tumors cause **biliary obstruction**; extrahepatic ones present with **obstructive jaundice**.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c042-->
- It spreads **along nerves** and to **portal lymph nodes**, and **transplantation** rarely cures it.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c043-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is cholangiocarcinoma a hepatocellular carcinoma?** No. It comes from biliary epithelium, although tumors with both morphologies exist.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c044-->

**Is it always inside the liver?** No. Bile duct cancer outside the liver is cholangiocarcinoma too, and it is nearly always adenocarcinoma.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c045-->

**Does a gland-forming, fibrous tumor on liver biopsy prove cholangiocarcinoma?** No. Metastatic breast or pancreatic carcinoma can look similar.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c046-->

**Can transplantation cure it?** Rarely: it seldom eradicates the tumor.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c047-->

## Mnemonics

**Three Cs to remember:** sclerosing **C**holangitis, bile duct **C**ysts and **C**lonorchis flukes.<!--c:cholangiocarcinoma-c048-->
