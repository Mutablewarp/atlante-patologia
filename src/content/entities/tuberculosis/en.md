## Definition and epidemiology

**What is tuberculosis?** Any organ can be infected, but the lungs are the main target; the disease is chronic and communicable.<!--c:tuberculosis-c001--> Its typical lesion is a granuloma with a necrotic center.<!--c:tuberculosis-c002--> Only some infected people fall ill, so infection is not the same as active disease.<!--c:tuberculosis-c003--> Disease at first exposure is primary; disease long after it is secondary.<!--c:tuberculosis-c004-->

Developed countries now see less of it, but it occurs worldwide.<!--c:tuberculosis-c005--> Immigrants from endemic areas and people with HIV are at high risk.<!--c:tuberculosis-c006--> Reactivation may explain why the elderly are most affected in the United States.<!--c:tuberculosis-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which organism causes it, and how is it caught?** *M. tuberculosis bovis* causes an occasional case; most come from the Koch bacillus, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis hominis*.<!--c:tuberculosis-c008--> It is a thin, beaded rod that grows only where oxygen is present.<!--c:tuberculosis-c009--> Acid fastness means the cell keeps carbolfuchsin despite an acid-alcohol wash, thanks to waxy wall lipids.<!--c:tuberculosis-c010--> Humans are the only host; there is no environmental reservoir.<!--c:tuberculosis-c011-->

Spread is airborne: droplet nuclei, the dried remains of expelled droplets, carry the bacilli through the air.<!--c:tuberculosis-c012--> Unpasteurized milk from infected cows transmits the bovine type.<!--c:tuberculosis-c014-->
**What favors reactivation?** Weakened immunity, as in AIDS, old age or cancer treatment, can wake dormant bacilli.<!--c:tuberculosis-c016-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why can the bacillus survive inside macrophages?** Alveolar macrophages take up the inhaled bacilli.<!--c:tuberculosis-c017--> The bacillus survives and multiplies inside the macrophage because its wall lipids stop the phagosome from fusing with lysosomes.<!--c:tuberculosis-c018--> Lymph nodes of the hilum and mediastinum can receive bacilli, ferried from the lung by infected macrophages.<!--c:tuberculosis-c019-->

**What finally contains the infection?** IFNγ from antigen-primed T cells turns macrophages into effective killers of the bacilli.<!--c:tuberculosis-c020--> Containment works when the host is immunocompetent and bacilli are few: granulomas wall them off in fibrous tissue, and activated macrophages destroy them.<!--c:tuberculosis-c022--> Caseous necrosis is the price of hypersensitivity when bacilli are numerous.<!--c:tuberculosis-c023--> Caseation points toward tuberculosis but is not specific; other agents can cause it.<!--c:tuberculosis-c024-->

**Why does progressive primary disease happen?** Infection advances when granulomas are weak or absent, as in young children and immunocompromised people.<!--c:tuberculosis-c025--> In secondary disease, cavities appear where the T-cell response to now-familiar antigens destroys tissue.<!--c:tuberculosis-c026-->

## Gross morphology

**What does primary tuberculosis look like?** The Ghon focus is a small patch of consolidated lung under the pleura, the first lesion of primary disease; the hilar nodes receive its drainage.<!--c:tuberculosis-c027--> With involved hilar or mediastinal nodes, it is a Ghon complex.<!--c:tuberculosis-c028--> A Ghon nodule ends as a scarred, calcified subpleural nodule; earlier it is a sharply outlined lump with a necrotic center.<!--c:tuberculosis-c029--> In progressive primary disease, the growing lesion liquefies into cavities.<!--c:tuberculosis-c030--> If the necrosis erodes a bronchus, disease spreads within the lung.<!--c:tuberculosis-c031--> In middle lobe syndrome, the right middle lobe collapses because enlarged nodes compress its bronchus.<!--c:tuberculosis-c032-->

**And secondary tuberculosis?** In secondary disease, small caseous foci sit in a hazy scarred patch, usually at the apex and back of the upper lobes.<!--c:tuberculosis-c033--> A focus that erodes into a bronchus and drains leaves a cavity; other foci often heal and calcify.<!--c:tuberculosis-c034--> A cavity wall has three layers: collagen outside, granulation tissue in the middle, and a gray lining facing the lumen.<!--c:tuberculosis-c035--> Caseous material fills the lumen.<!--c:tuberculosis-c036--> A healed cavity wall scars and calcifies.<!--c:tuberculosis-c037-->

**What is miliary tuberculosis?** Bacilli that spread to several organs form tiny yellow nodules like millet seeds: miliary tuberculosis.<!--c:tuberculosis-c038--> Miliary lesions favor the lungs, liver and spleen, and the meninges may be involved later.<!--c:tuberculosis-c039--> Blood-borne bacilli can also seed distant sites, such as the kidneys and bones.<!--c:tuberculosis-c085-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the typical lesion under the microscope?** The center of the granuloma is caseous: soft, cheese-like necrotic debris.<!--c:tuberculosis-c040--> Langhans giant cells and epithelioid macrophages surround the core.<!--c:tuberculosis-c041--> Loose clumps of macrophages without Langhans giant cells can replace a true granuloma when the host is immunocompromised.<!--c:tuberculosis-c044-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What is looked at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** beaded, acid-fast rods are seen when a lung lesion is smeared.<!--c:tuberculosis-c045-->
- On routine H&E, expect a caseating granuloma with some fibrosis.<!--c:tuberculosis-c046-->
- The Ziehl–Neelsen stain highlights acid-fast mycobacteria in tissue.<!--c:tuberculosis-c047-->
- Gram staining does not show the bacilli as gram-positive, since the lipid in their wall blocks the dye.<!--c:tuberculosis-c048-->
- Colonies grow slowly, visible after 3 to 6 weeks.<!--c:tuberculosis-c049-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does each form present?** Primary tuberculosis is usually symptom-free.<!--c:tuberculosis-c050--> Vague complaints such as fever and night sweats typically herald progressive primary disease.<!--c:tuberculosis-c051--> High fever and pleurisy can mark a sudden onset instead.<!--c:tuberculosis-c052--> Secondary disease usually causes cough, low-grade fever and weight loss.<!--c:tuberculosis-c053--> Hemoptysis, sometimes fatal, follows when a cavity wall erodes nearby small pulmonary arteries.<!--c:tuberculosis-c054-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What points to the diagnosis?** Secondary tuberculosis is suggested by cavities in one or both lung apices on a chest film.<!--c:tuberculosis-c056--> Caseation on biopsy points toward tuberculosis, though not conclusively.<!--c:tuberculosis-c058-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it end?** Most healthy adults contain the infection, and their Ghon complexes end as small calcified scars.<!--c:tuberculosis-c059--> Secondary disease arises when immunity falters and the few bacilli that survived for years begin multiplying again.<!--c:tuberculosis-c060-->
**What are the complications of secondary disease?**
- Empyema and pneumothorax follow a bronchopleural fistula, a breach between a cavity beneath the pleura and the pleural space.<!--c:tuberculosis-c062-->
- Swallowed material may cause intestinal tuberculosis; coughed-up material, laryngitis.<!--c:tuberculosis-c063-->
- *Aspergillus* growing in a persistent open cavity forms a fungal mass, the aspergilloma.<!--c:tuberculosis-c064-->
- Spread through the blood, mostly in secondary disease, gives miliary tuberculosis.<!--c:tuberculosis-c065-->
- Secondary [[amyloidosis]] often complicated long-standing cavitary tuberculosis in the past.<!--c:tuberculosis-c066-->

Untreated, secondary tuberculosis eventually kills, after a wasting illness.<!--c:tuberculosis-c067--> Patients take ethambutol, rifampin, pyrazinamide and isoniazid for prolonged courses.<!--c:tuberculosis-c068--> Incomplete adherence to the full regimen is the usual reason resistant strains have appeared.<!--c:tuberculosis-c069-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[caseous-necrosis]] | Tuberculosis characteristically caseates, yet other causes exist.<!--c:tuberculosis-c070--> | Acid-fast stain, culture |
| [[leprosy]] | Tuberculoid granulomas lack necrosis and only vaguely resemble tuberculous ones.<!--c:tuberculosis-c071--> | Acid-fast stain (bacilli are rare) |
| *M. avium-intracellulare* | Much slower course, though clinically and pathologically similar.<!--c:tuberculosis-c072--> | Ziehl–Neelsen stain of tissue |
| Actinomycosis | Pus-filled, communicating microabscesses with yellow "sulfur granules".<!--c:tuberculosis-c073--> | Gram stain |

## High-yield summary

- **Ghon complex** = subpleural Ghon focus plus involved hilar nodes in primary tuberculosis.<!--c:tuberculosis-c074-->
- The lesion is a **caseating granuloma**: Langhans giant cells and epithelioid macrophages ring a cheesy center.<!--c:tuberculosis-c075-->
- **Secondary (cavitary)** disease typically arises at the top and back of the upper lobes.<!--c:tuberculosis-c076-->
- **Miliary** tuberculosis is dissemination with millet-seed nodules in many organs.<!--c:tuberculosis-c077-->
- **Acid-fast** bacilli stain poorly with the Gram stain, so an acid-fast stain is used.<!--c:tuberculosis-c078-->
- **Progressive primary** disease favors young children and people with weak immunity.<!--c:tuberculosis-c079-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is infection the same as active tuberculosis?** No. Many infected people feel well; active disease means the infection is damaging tissue and causing symptoms.<!--c:tuberculosis-c080-->

**Does caseous necrosis prove tuberculosis?** No. Other agents can cause it; it only prompts suspicion.<!--c:tuberculosis-c081-->

**Is secondary tuberculosis always a new infection?** No. Usually old bacilli reactivate, but fresh exposure can also cause it.<!--c:tuberculosis-c082-->

**Does the Gram stain show the bacillus?** No. The lipid-rich wall blocks the dyes, so an acid-fast method is used.<!--c:tuberculosis-c083-->

## Mnemonics

**CAVES** for secondary disease: **C**ough, **A**pex of the upper lobe, **V**essel erosion (hemoptysis), **E**xcavation, **S**weats and weight loss.<!--c:tuberculosis-c084-->
