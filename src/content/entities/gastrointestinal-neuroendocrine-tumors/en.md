## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a gastrointestinal neuroendocrine tumor?** Today's name is well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (NET); older texts say carcinoid.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c001--> Behavior depends chiefly on where in the gut the tumor arises.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c002--> Indolent behavior is usual in the appendix and rectum, which are the first and second most frequent sites.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c003--> Despite their usually small size, ileal NETs are often the more aggressive ones.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**In which settings do gastric NETs arise?** There are three: autoimmune gastritis, Zollinger–Ellison syndrome and sporadic disease.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c005--> Sporadic gastric NETs are more often antral.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does autoimmune gastritis lead to a gastric NET?** Strong antral G-cell activity raises gastrin, driving ECL-cell growth in body and fundus.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c007--> Micronodules that detach from the gland bases form late, after ECL hyperplasia and chain-like rows of cells.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c008--> Persistently high gastrin keeps pushing the nodules to merge, until a detectable tumor results.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c009--> Zollinger–Ellison syndrome also raises gastrin, and gastric NETs can develop in that state.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c010-->

## Gross morphology

**What does a NET look like to the naked eye?** Gastric NETs of autoimmune gastritis are often multiple, seen as tiny bumps on thinned mucosa.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c011--> The mucosa over a small NET is usually unbroken; the nodule lies in the submucosa.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c012--> Larger NETs often ulcerate secondarily; they can form polyps or grow in the wall or around it.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c013--> On sectioning, a NET is white to yellow and firm.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c014--> Desmoplasia around a NET that has crossed the wall can obstruct the bowel, mainly by kinking and adhesions.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c015--> Multiplicity occurs in about 40% of ileal NETs.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c016--> Typically, appendiceal NETs are oval and found at the tip.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the cells look like?** Small round NET cells form solid nests, strands or rosettes.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c018--> Mitoses are rare, and the nuclei are very regular.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c019--> Neurosecretory-type granules sit in the abundant pink, eosinophilic cytoplasm.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c020--> In the stomach, the surface epithelium may cover a nest of unremarkable NET cells.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c021-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist measure?** Staining for the cell-cycle marker Ki-67 shows how many tumor cells are proliferating.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c022--> Beyond site, size and mitotic rate refine the prediction of behavior.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c023-->

## Clinical presentation

**What is carcinoid syndrome?** Only a small share of NET patients get carcinoid syndrome, which is triggered by substances the tumor secretes.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c024--> Extensive liver metastases are usually present when it appears.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c025--> Episodic flushing and diarrhea are classic, with diarrhea often the most distressing.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c026--> Serotonin is thought to cause the diarrhea.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c027--> Right-sided valve disease affects roughly half of these patients too.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c028--> Appendiceal NETs are usually tiny and come to light by chance in appendectomy specimens.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c029-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which test supports carcinoid syndrome?** Monoamine oxidase, found in the liver and elsewhere, converts serotonin into 5-HIAA.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c030--> Testing urine for 5-HIAA is one way to diagnose carcinoid syndrome.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c031-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What decides the outcome?** Gastric NETs linked to autoimmune gastritis seldom behave aggressively.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c032--> Those with Zollinger–Ellison syndrome behave intermediately.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c033--> Prognosis is poorer for sporadic gastric NETs.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c034--> Appendiceal NETs under 1 cm are clinically benign.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c035--> Metastasis is uncommon, about 1%, for appendiceal NETs of 1 to 2 cm.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c036--> Beyond 2 cm, aggressiveness rises with size.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c037--> Nodal deposits can far outgrow the primary tumor.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c038--> Distant metastases, especially hepatic, follow blood-borne spread.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c039-->

**Why is the right heart damaged?** Serotonin reaches the heart when liver metastases let it bypass breakdown in the liver.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c040--> The pulmonic valve narrows and the tricuspid valve leaks because fibrous plaques distort them.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c041--> Lung monoamine oxidase activity spares the left heart.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c042-->

## High-yield summary

- **Carcinoid** is the old term; use **well-differentiated NET**.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c047-->
- Gastric NET behavior depends on the setting: **autoimmune gastritis** is rarely aggressive, **sporadic** disease is worse.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c048-->
- The **appendix** is the commonest site, and tumors under 1 cm are benign.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c049-->
- A **urine 5-HIAA** test helps diagnose carcinoid syndrome.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c050-->
- Carcinoid syndrome is mostly seen with **liver metastases**; the **right heart** is affected.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c051-->
- **Ki-67** measures proliferative activity.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c052-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is "carcinoid" still the right name?** No. Use well-differentiated NET instead.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c053-->

**Do all gastric NETs behave alike?** No. Behavior depends on the setting.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c054-->

**Does every small bowel NET cause carcinoid syndrome?** No. It usually appears only once the tumor has spread widely to the liver.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c055-->

**Does carcinoid heart disease affect both sides?** No. Only the right side.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c056-->

**FDR:** Flushing, Diarrhea, Right-sided valve disease, the core of carcinoid syndrome.<!--c:gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumors-c057-->
