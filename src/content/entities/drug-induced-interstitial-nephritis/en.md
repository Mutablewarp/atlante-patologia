## Definition and epidemiology

**What is drug-induced and toxic tubulointerstitial nephritis?** It is a group of kidney injuries in which a drug or toxin damages the tubules and interstitium.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c001--> The two main drug patterns are acute hypersensitivity nephritis and chronic analgesic nephropathy.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c002--> Light chains and urate crystals injure the tubules as well.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c003--> Of the kidney diseases that myeloma causes, light-chain cast nephropathy is seen most often.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which drugs and toxins are responsible?**

- **Hypersensitivity nephritis:** Beta-lactam antibiotics are the leading antibiotic cause; NSAIDs and diuretics also often trigger it.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c005-->
- **Analgesic nephropathy** follows heavy use of mixed painkillers, with aspirin typically paired with acetaminophen or phenacetin.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c006-->
- Affected patients usually have a lifetime intake above 2 kg of analgesic.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c007-->
- Phenacetin is the agent most often behind it.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c008--> The United States is one of many countries where it is no longer allowed.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c009-->
- **Light chains:** Tubular injury and casts follow when monoclonal light chains appear in the urine.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c010-->
- **Urate:** high uric acid from gout, or from tumor lysis after chemotherapy, can injure the kidney.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c011--> Long-term use of certain diuretics, which reduce uric acid excretion, can also cause hyperuricemia.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c012--> Lead poisoning lasting years makes the proximal tubules secrete less uric acid, which causes saturnine gout.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c013-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the kidney get injured?**

- Activated T cells and eosinophils in the kidney show that hypersensitivity nephritis is a type IV, cell-mediated reaction.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c014-->
- Either the drug (or a metabolite) or a tissue component the drug changed can serve as the immune target.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c015-->
- Vessel changes that cause ischemia may be involved, as may direct toxicity; the exact mechanism is unclear.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c016-->
- Distal tubular cells secrete Tamm–Horsfall glycoprotein; in acidic urine, filtered light chains bind to it and form casts.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c017-->
- Casts block the tubules, and free light chains are toxic to the epithelium; both cause renal dysfunction.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c018-->
- The structure of the light chain decides which of AL amyloidosis, light-chain deposition disease or cast nephropathy develops.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c019-->
- Obstruction of the collecting ducts by uric acid crystals, formed in the acidic urine there, causes AKI.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c020-->
- Blood uric acid spikes in tumor lysis, since the DNA of the many dying cells is degraded into it.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c021-->
- Gout causes chronic urate nephropathy: monosodium urate crystals collect within the tubules and the surrounding interstitium.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c022-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the papillae look like?**

- Analgesic nephropathy kills whole papillae; many do not fall off but remain as formless masses.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c023-->
- Such dead papillae often calcify.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c024-->
- A necrotic papilla may fall off entirely or stay joined to the kidney along its demarcation line.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c025-->
- Acute urate nephropathy shows yellow streaks in the papillae, from precipitated uric acid.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c026-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the pathologist see in each disease?**

- **Hypersensitivity nephritis:** lymphocytes, with an occasional eosinophil, form scattered patches in the cortex.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c027-->
- About 5% to 10% of the white cells in the tissue are eosinophils.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c028-->
- Involvement is usually milder in the medulla than in the cortex.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c029-->
- Eosinophils tend to cluster within tubular lumens.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c030-->
- Tubulitis means leukocytes patchily entering the lining of proximal and distal tubules.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c031-->
- Mononuclear cells and some eosinophils infiltrate the edematous interstitium.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c032-->
- Pyelonephritis or blood-borne bacterial infection is suspected when neutrophils appear, since they are scarce here.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c033-->
- Late in the course, small granulomas may form.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c034-->
- Inflammation spares the glomeruli and the blood vessels.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c035-->
- When NSAIDs are the culprit, the biopsy may also show features of minimal-change disease.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c036-->
- **Analgesic nephropathy:** Before any other lesion appears, the capillaries under the urothelium have uniformly thickened walls.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c037-->
- Early damage stays within the papillae and inner medulla, where tubular and capillary basement membranes thicken and small foci of tissue die.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c038-->
- Little inflammation surrounds the necrosis; as it merges, it reaches the collecting ducts only after the corticomedullary junction.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c039-->
- Tubules in the overlying cortex shrink, and scarring with chronic inflammation follows.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c040-->
- **Light-chain cast nephropathy:** Many deep-pink casts with a glassy sheen lie in the lumens of distal tubules and collecting ducts.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c041-->
- Cracks and sharp corners give the casts a crystal-like look.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c042-->
- Macrophages and multinucleated giant cells may gather around the casts as a foreign-body response.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c043-->
- Nephrocalcinosis, tubular atrophy and interstitial fibrosis mark the more chronic lesions.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c044-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look for lymphocytes with eosinophils in the interstitium and for tubulitis.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c045-->
- In the casts of suspected cast nephropathy, immunostains show light chains and Tamm–Horsfall protein.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c046-->
- The source describes no special stain, electron microscopy or molecular test here.

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?**

- Patients typically develop AKI some 2 weeks into the drug course.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c047-->
- Fever and rash may accompany it.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c048-->
- Tubular dysfunction is common; renal tubular acidosis and urinary sodium wasting are examples.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c049-->
- Silent until late, analgesic nephropathy then causes anemia, high blood pressure, blood in the urine and tubular dysfunction.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c050-->
- Dead papillary tips that break off and travel down the ureter can cause renal colic.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c051-->
- Renal failure in cast nephropathy can be acute or chronic.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c052-->
- Most urinary protein is light chains, but the loss can stay below nephrotic levels.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c053-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests point to the diagnosis?**

- In this nephritis, urinary eosinophils are typical; red cells and, less often, white-cell casts may accompany them.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c054-->
- In myeloma, nephrotic-range proteinuria points away from cast nephropathy, toward AL amyloid or light-chain deposits.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c055-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Do the kidneys recover?**

- Once the drug is withdrawn, full recovery usually follows within weeks to months.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c056-->
- ESRD is a frequent end point of analgesic nephropathy, reached through progressive renal failure.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c057-->

## High-yield summary

- Hypersensitivity nephritis is a **type IV** reaction with T cells and eosinophils.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c062-->
- It usually causes AKI about **2 weeks** after the drug starts.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c063-->
- **Stopping the drug** usually leads to full recovery.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c064-->
- In analgesic nephropathy, **papillary necrosis** is an early event.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c065-->
- **Phenacetin** is the analgesic most often linked to the nephropathy.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c066-->
- Light chains form casts with **Tamm–Horsfall glycoprotein** in acidic urine.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c067-->
- **Tumor lysis** raises uric acid, which precipitates in the collecting ducts and causes AKI.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c068-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is analgesic nephropathy the same as NSAID-induced acute nephritis?** No. Analgesic nephropathy is chronic, whereas analgesics can also trigger an acute interstitial nephritis that presents as AKI.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c069-->

**Are neutrophils expected in hypersensitivity nephritis?** No. They are rare, and finding them suggests infection.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c070-->

**Does heavy proteinuria in myeloma mean cast nephropathy?** No. Nephrotic-range loss in myeloma suggests AL amyloid or light-chain deposits, not casts.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c071-->

**Are the glomeruli inflamed in hypersensitivity nephritis?** No. The glomeruli and the vessels are free of inflammation.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c072-->

## Mnemonics

**FEW:** Fever and rash, Eosinophils in the urine, Weeks (about 2) after the drug is started.<!--c:drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis-c073-->
