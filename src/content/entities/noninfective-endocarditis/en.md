## Definition and epidemiology

**What is non-infective endocarditis?** NBTE is almost always seen in cancer or another wasting disease; the sterile vegetations form on valves that look normal.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c001-->
The mitral and aortic valves are hit with the same frequency.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c002-->
Libman–Sacks endocarditis is the lupus form, with warty vegetations on the endocardium.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which diseases come with these vegetations?**
- Many cases are paraneoplastic, most often with blood cancers or pancreatic and lung adenocarcinomas.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c004-->
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation, or a debilitating disease that is not a neoplasm, can also be the setting for NBTE.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c005-->
- Libman–Sacks endocarditis belongs to SLE, a disease that often involves the heart.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do the vegetations form?** What triggers NBTE is not well established.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c007-->
Either blood that clots too readily or immune complexes being deposited may underlie it.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c008-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the vegetations look like, and where do they sit?**
- NBTE vegetations look much like those of infective endocarditis.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c009-->
- NBTE vegetations stay small.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c010-->
- Libman–Sacks vegetations measure up to 4 mm.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c011-->
- On the atrial side of the mitral leaflets, next to the annulus, is where Libman–Sacks vegetations usually sit.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c012-->
- Spread to the papillary muscles and chordae tendineae is possible.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c013-->
- The aortic valve is seldom involved in Libman–Sacks endocarditis.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the vegetation made of?** Platelet–fibrin masses with neither microorganisms nor inflammation make up NBTE vegetations.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c015-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it show up?** At autopsy, NBTE is frequently found by chance.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c016-->
In SLE, other manifestations of the disease usually overshadow the cardiac symptoms.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c017-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it recognized?** The pages used for this card do not describe laboratory or imaging criteria. The tissue clue is the absence of microorganisms and inflammation in the vegetation, as described under microscopic morphology.

## Complications and prognosis

**Is it dangerous?**
- The valves affected by NBTE are not destroyed.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c018-->
- Emboli can travel from the vegetations and infarct many organs; this is the chief hazard of NBTE, though rare.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c019-->
- After healing, Libman–Sacks lesions usually leave a clean valve: no scar, normal function.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c020-->

## Differential diagnosis

**How do I tell these vegetations apart?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[infective-endocarditis]] | Grossly alike, but infective endocarditis has organisms, inflammation and destroyed valves.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c021--> | Look for microorganisms and inflammation in the vegetation.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c022--> |
| [[rheumatic-heart-disease]] | Chronic rheumatic valves have rigid, joined leaflets; the aortic cusps typically weld at the commissures.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c023--> | Look at the leaflets and commissures for scarring and fusion, not only at a vegetation.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c024--> |
| NBTE versus Libman–Sacks | NBTE comes with cancer or wasting and strikes both valves alike; Libman–Sacks comes with SLE and favors the mitral valve.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c025--> | Clinical setting: malignancy or wasting disease versus SLE.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c026--> |

## High-yield summary

- In a cancer or wasting patient, sterile vegetations on normal-looking valves mean **NBTE**, also called marantic endocarditis.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c027-->
- Most often it comes with **blood cancers** or **adenocarcinoma**, mainly of pancreas and lung.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c028-->
- **Libman–Sacks endocarditis** is the verrucous endocarditis of SLE, mostly on the mitral valve.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c029-->
- NBTE leaves valves intact and is **sterile and free of inflammation**, unlike infective endocarditis.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c030-->
- The main risk of NBTE is **embolization**.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c031-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is NBTE an infection because it looks like infective endocarditis?** No. The vegetations are sterile and lack inflammation.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c032-->

**Does Libman–Sacks endocarditis leave a scarred, dysfunctional valve?** Usually not: it heals with no scar and the valve keeps working.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c033-->

**Does NBTE occur only with cancer?** No. It can also accompany disseminated intravascular coagulation or non-neoplastic debilitating disease.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c034-->

## Mnemonics

**MARANTIC = "wasting away".** The word comes from the Greek for wasting away: picture a wasted patient with sterile vegetations.<!--c:noninfective-endocarditis-c035-->
