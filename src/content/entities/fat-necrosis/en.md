## Definition and epidemiology

Fat necrosis is a form of necrosis confined to adipose tissue; what makes it distinctive is the triglyceride stored inside fat cells, which is broken down and turned into soap.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c001--> It has two main settings: enzymatic fat necrosis in pancreatitis and traumatic fat necrosis, typically in the breast.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

- **Acute pancreatitis (enzymatic):** the fat around the gland and in the peritoneal cavity is digested by activated pancreatic enzymes that break down lipids and proteins.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c003-->
- **Obesity** is linked to more frequent and more severe pancreatitis: once lipase is released, the abundant peripancreatic fat gives it more to act on, so fat necrosis can be more extensive.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c004-->
- **Trauma:** damaged adipocytes release their own triglyceride and lipases, so no pancreatic enzyme is needed; the breast is a common site.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c005-->
- **Surgery and radiotherapy:** in the breast, fat necrosis can also be iatrogenic, appearing after an operation or after radiotherapy.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c056-->
- **Ruptured silicone breast implant:** the surrounding tissue shows fat necrosis together with a foreign-body giant-cell reaction.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c006-->
- **Newborn:** prolonged compression of the subcutaneous fat during labor and delivery can make it ischemic; the resulting fat necrosis is an uncommon birth injury.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c007-->
- **Acinar cell carcinoma of the pancreas:** in some patients the tumor releases so much lipase into the blood that fat necrosis appears far away, in the subcutis, as part of a paraneoplastic syndrome with joint pain and eosinophilia.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

In pancreatitis the pancreas digests itself and the fat around it: enzymes that escape from damaged acini and ducts become active within the tissue, instead of in the gut lumen where they normally work.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c009--> Trypsin acts as the trigger enzyme: its main harm is indirect, through activation of the precursors of phospholipase A2 and elastase.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c010-->

Within the fat, the chain runs: membrane damage, then triglyceride release, then lipolysis, then fatty acid plus calcium, then soap.

- Membrane damage: the adipocyte is opened by enzymes that attack lipids (phospholipases) and proteins (proteases), and its triglyceride store leaks out.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c011-->
- Lipolysis: lipase cleaves that triglyceride, yielding free fatty acids.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c012-->
- Soap: the fatty acids bind calcium and drop out of solution as insoluble salts, a process called **saponification**.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c013-->

Free fatty acids also damage cells directly; this toxicity is thought to explain how lipase leakage in hyperlipidemia can set off pancreatitis.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c014-->

## Gross morphology

- Surrounding fat looks normal; the necrotic zones stand out as **chalky white**, with ragged borders.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c015-->
- In acute pancreatitis, the fat around the pancreas and in the nearby mesentery is dotted with **yellow-white foci**.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c016-->
- Time course: as calcium and magnesium soaps accumulate, foci that were soft at first become hard and chalky.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c017-->
- In the breast the lesion forms a **hard mass**, often pulling on the overlying skin.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c018-->
- In the breast, the center of a lesion can liquefy and leave a cavity full of liquefied fat: the oil (lipid) cyst.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c057-->

## Microscopic morphology

- Dead adipocytes form irregular islands; of each cell, only a remnant of its outline persists.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c019-->
- **Calcium soaps:** the edge of the dead fat is lined by blue (**basophilic**) material with no visible structure.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c020-->
- An acute inflammatory infiltrate mixes with the soaps and the adipocyte remnants.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c021-->
- **Pancreas:** acinar necrosis lies next to fat necrosis and hemorrhage; the damage comes in patches, and necrosis of the entire gland is rare.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c022-->
- **Breast, early:** bleeding and an acute inflammatory reaction surround the dead fat cells; cholesterol clefts can also be found.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c023-->
- **Breast, later:** foamy macrophages take up the lipid droplets, and so do multinucleated giant cells.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c024-->
- **Breast, late:** fibrosis and dystrophic calcification.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **Specimen:** in the breast, a needle core biopsy of the lump usually settles the cases that clinical examination and imaging leave open.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c058-->
- **H&E:** usually enough for the diagnosis; the picture changes with the age of the lesion (see Microscopic morphology).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c059--> Old bleeding leaves its mark too, as macrophages filled with hemosiderin.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c060--> The Perls (Prussian blue) stain shows that their yellow-brown granules are iron (see [[g:perls-prussian-blue]]).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c061-->
- **Fat stains:** in routine sections fat shows up only as empty spaces, because paraffin embedding washes most of it out; the lipid itself is shown on frozen sections with oil red O or Sudan red, which stain neutral fat red (see [[g:fat-stains]]).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c062-->
- **Calcium:** older lesions may calcify; von Kossa colors the calcium black and is more specific than H&E, on which the deposits look deep blue (see [[g:von-kossa-stain]] and [[dystrophic-calcification]]).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c063-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** when foamy cells raise the question of carcinoma, a keratin stain is the key: carcinoma cells are keratin-positive, whereas the macrophages of fat necrosis are keratin-negative and express CD68 instead.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c064--> The tumor to keep in mind is invasive lobular carcinoma, which can hide in the scar of an old lesion because its cells advance one at a time or in single file; a keratin stain shows them up.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c065-->
- **Silicone, on H&E:** after a leak from an implant, small refractile particles may persist in clear spaces and in nearby macrophages, although tissue processing removes most of the silicone.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c066-->

## Clinical presentation

- **Acute pancreatitis:** intense upper abdominal pain that radiates to the back, plus vomiting; in severe attacks, shock may set in within hours.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c026-->
- In the peritoneum the enzymes produce a severe chemical peritonitis, accompanied by foci of fat necrosis.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c027-->
- Soap formation uses up calcium, so **hypocalcemia** can follow; when marked, it shows as neuromuscular irritability (tetany).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c028-->
- **Breast:** a hard mass, often with skin tethering, that clinically imitates carcinoma.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c029-->
- **Newborn:** in the first or second week after birth, firm nodules or plaques appear under the skin; the skin over them may be red-violet or keep its normal color.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c030-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

In pancreatitis, injured acini spill their enzymes into the blood early, so serum lipase and amylase rise; the result supports the diagnosis together with the typical pain.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c031--> In the breast the answer comes from histology: necrotic fat with foamy macrophages and giant cells, rather than infiltrating carcinoma cells.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c032--> Imaging helps but can mislead: on mammography, an oil cyst, radiolucent because it is full of fat, is a reassuring benign sign, whereas some calcifications of fat necrosis cannot be told apart from those of carcinoma.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c067-->

## Complications and prognosis

- **Breast:** once calcified, the lesion becomes an even more convincing mimic of a tumor.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c033-->
- **Severe pancreatitis, first week:** prolonged shock puts the lungs (ARDS) and kidneys (acute renal failure) at risk.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c034-->
- **Infected necrosis:** gram-negative organisms from the bowel can colonize the dead gland, and death rates then climb sharply.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c035-->
- **Pseudocyst:** a recognized later complication of acute pancreatitis.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c036-->
- **Chronic pancreatitis:** repeated or severe attacks can heal with patchy scarring and, at times, calcification.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c037-->

## Differential diagnosis

|---|---|---|
| [[liquefactive-necrosis]] | Enzymatic digestion turns any tissue into fluid (abscess, brain infarct); there are no soaps and fat is not specifically targeted.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c038--> | Fluid-filled cavity with neutrophils |
| [[coagulative-necrosis]] | Ischemic death of solid organs with ghost outlines of parenchymal cells.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c039--> | Site: parenchyma, not fat |
| [[caseous-necrosis]] | Granular eosinophilic debris at the center of granulomas, typically tuberculosis.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c040--> | Granulomas; acid-fast stain |
| Silicone granuloma (leaking breast implant) | Leaked silicone provokes a foreign-body granulomatous reaction; severe cases form a hard mass with skin retraction, and fat necrosis may coexist.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c041--> | Implant history; clear spaces and macrophages with refractile silicone particles |
| Invasive breast carcinoma | Also a hard mass with skin tethering.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c042--> | Biopsy: malignant epithelium |

## High-yield summary

- Fat necrosis affects only **adipose tissue**; the key substrate is stored **triglyceride**.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c044-->
- Two settings: **acute pancreatitis** (enzymatic) and **trauma** (classically the breast).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c045-->
- Sequence: **phospholipases** and proteases open the cell, **lipase** frees **fatty acids**, fatty acids plus calcium form **soaps** (**saponification**).<!--c:fat-necrosis-c046-->
- Gross: **chalky white** foci; micro: blue (**basophilic**) soap deposits outlining the dead fat.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c047-->
- Severe pancreatitis can cause **hypocalcemia** because soaps consume calcium.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c048-->
- Breast fat necrosis: **hard mass** with **skin tethering** that mimics **carcinoma**; it evolves to **foamy macrophages**, fibrosis and **calcification**.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c049-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- Fat necrosis is not fatty change: steatosis is lipid stored inside living parenchymal cells, while fat necrosis is death of adipocytes.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c050-->
- Traumatic fat necrosis does not need pancreatic enzymes; the lipases come from the injured fat cells themselves.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c051-->
- The blue amorphous material is calcium soap, not nuclear debris.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c052-->
- Hypercalcemia is a cause of acute pancreatitis, whereas a fall in blood calcium is a consequence of its fat necrosis; do not mix up the two directions.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c053-->
- A negative history of trauma does not rule out breast fat necrosis, since only some patients remember an injury.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c054-->

## Mnemonics

**SOAP** (original): **S**aponification, fatty acids plus calcium; **O**rigin either enzymatic (pancreas) or traumatic (breast, where the injured fat cells supply their own lipases); **A**dipose tissue only, with chalky white foci; **P**seudotumor in the breast, most convincing once calcified.<!--c:fat-necrosis-c055-->
