## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a seminoma, and who gets it?** Seminoma is a malignant germ cell tumor of the testis made of uniform cells that resemble spermatogonia.<!--c:seminoma-c001--> Seminoma accounts for 40% of germ cell tumors and is the commonest cancer of the testis.<!--c:seminoma-c002--> Incidence is highest between 30 and 40 years.<!--c:seminoma-c003--> Dysgenetic gonads are the only setting in which it occurs before puberty.<!--c:seminoma-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What does a seminoma grow from?** It starts from germ cell neoplasia in situ (GCNIS), a preinvasive lesion that almost all invasive germ cell tumors have beside them.<!--c:seminoma-c005--> Infertility biopsies and undescended testes each reveal a small isolated focus of GCNIS in roughly 2% of cases.<!--c:seminoma-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the tumor develop and spread?** GCNIS cells look like fetal germ cells but have prominent nucleoli and are far bigger.<!--c:seminoma-c007--> OCT3/4 is found in the nucleus, and PLAP and CD117 on the surface, of GCNIS cells, the same pattern as in fetal germ cells.<!--c:seminoma-c008--> Invasion may begin only after GCNIS has stayed quiet for 5 to 10 years, as in infertile men with past cryptorchidism.<!--c:seminoma-c009--> PLAP sits on the cell membrane of seminoma cells, as it does in primordial germ cells, and the cells themselves resemble immature spermatogonia.<!--c:seminoma-c010--> Inside the testis the tumor runs along the seminiferous tubules and reaches the rete testis after it has invaded the parenchyma.<!--c:seminoma-c011--> Abdominal lymph node spread usually comes after epididymal involvement, which is itself a later event.<!--c:seminoma-c012-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the cut tumor look like?** Usually it is a nodule with a clear edge, and it feels firm and rubbery.<!--c:seminoma-c013--> Pressure from the tumor may leave the adjacent testis atrophic and fibrotic.<!--c:seminoma-c014--> Cut open, it looks lobulated and evenly pale brown to gray-yellow.<!--c:seminoma-c015--> Larger tumors may show some necrosis or hemorrhage, but this is uncommon.<!--c:seminoma-c016-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does seminoma look like on H&E?** Each polygonal cell has a vesicular nucleus in the center, and all the cells match.<!--c:seminoma-c017--> Glycogen and some lipid fill the generous cytoplasm, which therefore can look clear or pale pink.<!--c:seminoma-c018--> Fibrous septa partition the tumor cells into nests or sheets.<!--c:seminoma-c019--> Besides lymphocytes, the septal infiltrate includes plasma cells and macrophages.<!--c:seminoma-c020--> Giant-cell granulomas can also be found in the septa.<!--c:seminoma-c021--> A seminoma and an ovarian dysgerminoma look alike under the microscope (see [[ovarian-germ-cell-tumors]]).<!--c:seminoma-c022--> Antibodies to hCG are the best way to reveal syncytiotrophoblastic giant cells, which are present in 20% of tumors.<!--c:seminoma-c023--> Anaplastic seminoma is the name for the 5% of tumors with many mitoses and nuclei of varying size and shape.<!--c:seminoma-c024-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on H&E, look for sheets of clear cells with central nuclei, separated by septa rich in lymphocytes.<!--c:seminoma-c025-->
- **OCT3/4** and **CD117** (c-Kit) are the markers to rely on: seminoma cells stain with both.<!--c:seminoma-c026-->
- **PLAP** also stains the cell membrane.<!--c:seminoma-c027-->
- Antibodies to **hCG** highlight the syncytiotrophoblastic giant cells.<!--c:seminoma-c028-->

Specimen handling, electron microscopy and molecular tests are not covered by the source pages.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a seminoma present?** A scrotal mass that keeps enlarging is the usual presentation.<!--c:seminoma-c029--> Orchiectomy can still cure the tumor at the stage when it is often found.<!--c:seminoma-c030-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it confirmed?** Histology with positive OCT3/4 and CD117 supports the diagnosis.<!--c:seminoma-c031--> Even though syncytiotrophoblastic cells secrete hCG, blood hCG is usually undetectable.<!--c:seminoma-c032--> Prophylactic orchiectomy is indicated when a testicular biopsy shows GCNIS.<!--c:seminoma-c033-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Can a seminoma be cured?** Many seminomas are found while still curable: orchiectomy, alone or with abdominal lymph node removal, can cure them.<!--c:seminoma-c034--> When surgery alone does not cure it, radiotherapy steps in, because seminoma responds strongly to radiation.<!--c:seminoma-c035--> Chemotherapy can cure even widely disseminated, advanced disease.<!--c:seminoma-c036--> More than 90% of patients are now cured, whatever the histologic subtype.<!--c:seminoma-c037--> The syncytiotrophoblastic and anaplastic variants are microscopically distinct, yet clinically they are not significantly different from classical seminoma.<!--c:seminoma-c038-->

## High-yield summary

- **Seminoma** is the commonest testicular cancer (40% of germ cell tumors).<!--c:seminoma-c043-->
- It grows as **sheets of clear cells** with lymphocytes in the septa and sometimes granulomas.<!--c:seminoma-c044-->
- Markers: **OCT3/4** and **CD117**, plus PLAP.<!--c:seminoma-c045-->
- It is **highly radiosensitive**, and the cure rate is above 90%.<!--c:seminoma-c046-->
- The precursor is **GCNIS**.<!--c:seminoma-c047-->
- A mixed tumor with a seminoma part is treated as **nonseminomatous**.<!--c:seminoma-c048-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do syncytiotrophoblastic cells raise the blood hCG?** Usually not. They secrete hCG, but blood levels are usually undetectable.<!--c:seminoma-c049-->

**Does a seminoma part make a mixed germ cell tumor a seminoma for treatment?** No. These tumors are treated as nonseminomatous.<!--c:seminoma-c050-->

**Is a spermatocytic tumor a kind of seminoma?** No. It is benign, appears after age 40 and has no relation to classical seminoma, whatever its old name suggests.<!--c:seminoma-c051-->

**Is anaplastic seminoma more aggressive?** No. Clinically it behaves like classical seminoma.<!--c:seminoma-c052-->

## Mnemonics

**SPERM:** Sheets of clear cells, PLAP with OCT3/4 and CD117, lymphocytes in septa (Enclosing granulomas at times), Radiosensitive, Mostly cured.<!--c:seminoma-c053-->
