## Definition and epidemiology

**What is an aortic dissection?** Blood enters the wall of the aorta and runs lengthwise inside it, creating a second, false channel.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c001--> Branches of the aorta can be drawn in as well, but the aorta itself is the typical location, with the ascending segment affected most often.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c002--> Type A is confined to the ascending aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c003--> Type B spares the ascending aorta and involves the distal aorta only.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c004-->

As many as 1 autopsy in 400 shows a dissection.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c005--> Male patients outnumber female patients 3 to 1.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c006--> Few ages are spared, but the peak is in the sixth and seventh decades.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What weakens the aortic wall?** A past of high blood pressure is nearly universal among patients.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c008--> The condition also goes with a bicuspid aortic valve, atherosclerosis and aortic root dilation of unknown cause.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c009--> Filamin mutations and the [[marfan-syndrome|Marfan]], [[ehlers-danlos-syndromes|Ehlers–Danlos]] and Loeys–Dietz syndromes account for some cases.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c010--> The fibrillin gene, which codes for an extracellular matrix protein, carries the mutations found in Marfan syndrome, a disorder of connective tissue throughout the body.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c011--> Mutations in ACTA2 (α-actin), MYH11 (smooth muscle β-myosin) and the TGFβ receptors 1 and 2 are found in other patients.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c012--> Medial fibrosis and patchy elastin loss appear with normal aging, though only to a mild degree.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c013--> Medial necrosis of the aorta can appear in [[wilson-disease]] after treatment with penicillamine, a drug that removes copper.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the wall give way?** A weak aortic media is usually the underlying problem.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c015--> Erdheim first called this cystic medial necrosis; patchy loss of elastic and muscle fibers is the core change.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c016--> Why the media degenerates remains unclear.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c017--> Fibulin-5, which controls how elastic fibers are assembled, is expressed at lower levels in patients with thoracic dissection.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c018--> Smooth muscle cells that secrete MMP-2 and its inhibitor abnormally may also play a part.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c019--> In animals, copper deficiency in the diet blocks lysyl oxidase, a copper-dependent enzyme, so collagen is poorly cross-linked and the aorta dissects.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c020--> A lysyl oxidase inhibitor, β-aminopropionitrile, has the same effect when fed to animals.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c021--> Weak aortic connective tissue, from one shared molecular defect, may be the common thread.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c022-->

**What starts the dissection?** What first triggers the splitting of the media is debated.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c023--> A crosswise split of the intima and inner media is found in over 95% of cases.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c024--> Luminal blood pushes through the tear and splits the layers of the media.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c025--> The tear may come second: if cystic medial necrosis has already weakened the media, bleeding from the vasa vasorum can strain the intima until it splits.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c026-->

## Gross morphology

**Where is the tear, and how far does the blood travel?** Most tears sit just over the aortic annulus (1 to 2 cm), in the ascending aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c027--> The outer third of the wall is peeled away from the inner two thirds in a matter of seconds.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c028--> Arteries to the neck, kidneys, bowel and heart can be caught up as well.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c029--> A clot lies between the layers of the media in the lengthwise-opened aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c030--> A second tear farther downstream lets the blood return to the lumen in 5% to 10% of cases; the aorta then has two channels, the "double-barreled" pattern.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c031--> About as often, the false channel opens into a large artery, usually the iliac.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c032-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the media look like?** Myxoid matrix with metachromatic staining fills spaces in the media where elastic and muscle fibers have been lost in patches.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c033--> Despite the name, there is no true cyst: what looks like one is matrix pooled inside the media.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c034--> In an atherosclerotic aorta, an elastic stain places the split in the media's outer third.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c035-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** an elastic stain shows the plane of the dissection within the media.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c036-->
- Cystic medial necrosis is recognized on aldehyde fuchsin by metachromatic pools in the wall.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c037-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** The typical complaint is a tearing sensation in the front of the chest, severe and of sudden onset.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c038--> Doctors may mistake it for a heart attack (myocardial infarction).<!--c:aortic-dissection-c039--> One or more arterial pulses are often lost.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c040--> A murmur of aortic regurgitation is also frequent.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c041--> Many patients are hypertensive.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c042--> Low blood pressure ominously suggests that the aorta has ruptured.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c043--> Standard criteria identify tamponade and heart failure, which may complicate dissection.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c044-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications, and what is the outlook?** Bleeding from the false channel into the pericardium or nearby spaces often kills; its outer wall is thin.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c045--> Without drugs against hypertension or surgery, 80% of patients were dead within 2 weeks.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c046--> Of the survivors, half died within the next 3 months.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c047--> Today fewer than 20% of patients die overall, thanks to prompt surgery and blood pressure control.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c048-->

## High-yield summary

- Almost every patient has a history of **hypertension**.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c053-->
- The classic medial lesion is **cystic medial necrosis**, whose "cysts" are pools of matrix.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c054-->
- Specific associated disorders include **Marfan syndrome** (fibrillin gene), Ehlers–Danlos and Loeys–Dietz syndromes.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c055-->
- **Type A** involves the ascending aorta alone; **type B** involves only the distal aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c056-->
- A **transverse intimal tear** is present in over 95% of cases, usually in the ascending aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c057-->
- The classic presentation is **tearing chest pain**, which can mimic myocardial infarction.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c058-->
- **Hypotension** suggests rupture.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c059-->
- Death often follows blood escaping the aorta, as into the **pericardium**.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c060-->
- A **double-barreled aorta** forms in 5% to 10% of cases.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c061-->
- Overall mortality is now under 20% thanks to surgery and **blood pressure control**.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c062-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is the "cyst" of cystic medial necrosis a real cyst?** No. The spaces are pools of matrix between medial cells.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c063-->

**Is a low blood pressure reassuring?** No. A fall in blood pressure is a warning that the aorta may have ruptured.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c064-->

**Is sudden tearing chest pain always a myocardial infarction?** No. Aortic dissection can be misdiagnosed as one.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c065-->

**Does type A mean a dissection of the descending aorta?** No. Type A involves the ascending aorta alone, and type B only the distal aorta.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c066-->

**Is the outlook still as grim as before treatment existed?** No. Overall mortality is now under 20%, against 80% at 2 weeks before treatment.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c067-->

## Mnemonics

**TEAR:** Tearing chest pain, Elastic fibers lost from the media, Ascending aorta for type A, Rupture signaled by hypotension.<!--c:aortic-dissection-c068-->
