## Definition and epidemiology

**What is an aneurysm, and which shapes can it take?** In an aneurysm, a segment of artery bulges because its media is weak, from birth or acquired later.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c001--> The sac wall is the original arterial wall, stretched.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c002--> Frequency rises with age.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c003--> Up to 10% of unselected autopsies show an aortic or other arterial aneurysm.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c004-->

Etiology, configuration and site are the three bases for classifying aneurysms.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c005-->
- **Fusiform:** the dilation runs lengthwise, giving the vessel an oval outline.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c006-->
- **Saccular:** a weak spot in the media lets the wall bulge out as a pouch.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c007-->
- **Dissecting:** not a true dilation but a hematoma, with the wall layers pushed apart by blood in the media.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c008-->
- **Arteriovenous:** an artery and a vein joined directly.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c009-->

**What is an abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA)?** A dilation counts as an AAA when the diameter has increased by 50% or more.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c010--> AAAs typically appear after age 50, and no other aneurysm is as common.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c011--> Above age 80, prevalence is 6%.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c012--> Women are affected far less often than men.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c013--> Hypertension is present in roughly half of patients.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c014--> Less often, popliteal, iliac and thoracic aortic aneurysms occur.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c015-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes aortic aneurysms?** Severe [[atherosclerosis]] is the invariable backdrop of abdominal aneurysms.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c016--> Faulty matrix remodeling and inflammation are probably two of several causes of AAA.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c017--> A genetic role is suspected but poorly understood, since AAA recurs in families.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c018--> [[syphilis|Syphilis]] lost its place as the leading cause of aortic aneurysm as the infection became less common.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c019--> Bacterial endocarditis ([[infective-endocarditis]]) is the usual source when a mycotic aneurysm forms during septicemia.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c020--> A neighboring tuberculous or bacterial abscess can also produce a mycotic aneurysm.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c021--> Cerebral berry aneurysms start from an arterial wall that was faulty from birth.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c022-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does an aneurysm keep growing?** Wall stress rises as the aneurysm widens, one factor governing its growth.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c023--> Collagen I and III in media and adventitia is degraded by MMPs and cathepsins K, L and S, driving growth.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c024--> AAA formation is associated with inflammatory cytokines IL-8, MCP-1, TNF-α and IL-1β.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c025--> Remodeling is steered by chemokines and growth factors present in the aneurysm wall.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c026-->

**How does syphilis weaken the aorta?** Periarteritis and obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum in the ascending aorta are the syphilitic lesion.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c027--> Scars from the dead media patches fragment the elastic lamellae once the vasa vasorum close.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c028--> Pressure on the weakened ascending aorta or arch makes a fusiform, rupture-prone aneurysm.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c029-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does it sit and what does it look like?** Most AAAs sit in the infrarenal aorta, just above where it divides.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c030--> Saccular forms occur, but the fusiform shape is the usual one.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c031--> The iliac arteries can be involved by extension and sometimes harbor separate aneurysms too.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c032--> The celiac axis and superior mesenteric artery risk blockage at their origins when the aneurysm climbs above the level of the renal arteries.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c033--> Atherosclerosis seldom touches the ascending aorta, so an aneurysm there is most often syphilitic.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c034--> In an atherosclerotic AAA, the lining shows complicated atherosclerosis.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c035--> Fragments can embolize downstream from the variably organized mural thrombus found in most AAAs.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c036--> The syphilitic aortic intima looks like tree bark, from the scars beneath it in the media.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c037--> A berry aneurysm is a saccular bulge at a circle of Willis branch point, named for a berry on a twig.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c038-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the wall show under the microscope?** Fibrous tissue takes over where the wall was destroyed in an atherosclerotic AAA.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c040--> Only scattered islands of media remain, and atheroma penetrates the wall to different depths.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c041--> Patchy inflammation and thickening affect the adventitia.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c042--> In syphilitic aortitis, diseased vasa vasorum reach from the adventitia through the outer two thirds of the wall.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c043-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- On microscopy of an atherosclerotic AAA, look for fibrous replacement of the wall with scattered residual media.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c044-->
- In syphilitic aorta, look for macrophages, plasma cells and lymphocytes cuffing the vasa vasorum.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c045-->
- The reference pages name no special stain, IHC, electron microscopy or molecular test here.

## Clinical presentation

**How does an AAA present?** Many AAAs are silent.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c046--> Expansion often causes abdominal pain.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c047--> Sudden ischemic symptoms may follow an embolus from the mural thrombus that blocks a peripheral artery.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c048--> Most symptomatic aneurysms exceed 5-6 cm, yet an aneurysm of any size can cause symptoms.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c049--> A ruptured AAA bleeding into the retroperitoneum or chest is an emergency: shock, pain and a pulsating abdominal mass.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c050--> A mycotic aneurysm is prone to rupture and hemorrhage.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c051-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is an AAA found?** Many AAAs are incidental findings, felt as an abdominal mass or seen on scans requested for other reasons.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c053-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What makes an aortic aneurysm dangerous?** No complication is more feared than rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c054--> Even with prompt surgery, half of patients with a rupture die.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c055--> Large aneurysms are often treated before symptoms, with a prosthetic graft that replaces or bypasses the diseased segment.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c056--> Size determines how likely an AAA is to rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c057--> Rupture is rare (2%) below 4 cm.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c058--> Within five years of detection, roughly a quarter to two fifths of AAAs wider than 5 cm rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c059--> Rarely, thrombus growth alone obstructs aortic flow.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c060--> Rupture of a berry aneurysm causes [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]].<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c061-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Atherosclerotic AAA | Abdominal aorta, usually fusiform, with mural thrombus.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c062--> | Complicated atherosclerosis with fibrous replacement of the wall.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c063--> |
| Syphilitic aneurysm ([[syphilis]]) | Ascending aorta and arch, fusiform, "tree-bark" intima.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c064--> | Periarteritis and obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c065--> |
| Mycotic aneurysm ([[infective-endocarditis]]) | Arises in septicemia and tends to rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c066--> | Look for the source: bacterial endocarditis or an adjacent abscess.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c067--> |
| Berry aneurysm ([[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]]) | Saccular, at branch points of the circle of Willis.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c068--> | Presents with subarachnoid hemorrhage on rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c069--> |
| Dissecting aneurysm ([[aortic-dissection]]) | Blood splits the media into a false lumen, with abrupt "tearing" chest pain that can mimic infarction.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c070--> | Cystic medial necrosis: metachromatic pools on aldehyde fuchsin.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c071--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Atherosclerosis** underlies an abdominal aortic aneurysm, which is usually fusiform and lies below the renal arteries.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c072-->
- **Syphilitic** aneurysms involve the ascending aorta, vasa vasorum endarteritis and a tree-bark intima.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c073-->
- **Mycotic** aneurysms are infected walls, from septicemia or endocarditis, and tend to rupture.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c074-->
- **Berry** aneurysms are saccular, sit at circle of Willis branch points and cause subarachnoid hemorrhage.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c075-->
- **Rupture risk** rises with size: rare below 4 cm, 25% to 40% within 5 years above 5 cm.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c076-->
- A **dissecting** aneurysm is really a hematoma within the media.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c078-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a dissecting aneurysm just a dilated aorta?** No. It is a hematoma that splits the layers of the media.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c079-->

**Is syphilis still the main cause of aortic aneurysm?** No. It once was, but as syphilis became rarer so did syphilitic aneurysms.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c080-->

**Does "mycotic" mean fungal?** Not here. The term covers infectious aneurysms, most commonly due to bacterial endocarditis.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c082-->

**Do all AAAs cause symptoms?** No. Many are silent and are found by chance.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c083-->

## Mnemonics

**"Syphilis climbs, atheroma falls":** syphilitic aneurysms sit high in the ascending aorta, atherosclerotic ones low in the abdominal aorta.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c084-->

**"Berry on a twig":** a berry aneurysm hangs at a branch point of the circle of Willis.<!--c:aortic-aneurysm-c085-->
