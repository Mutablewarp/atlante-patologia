## Definition and epidemiology

**What is hypertensive vascular disease?** Above all in the kidney, long-standing high blood pressure damages vessels, mostly small arteries and arterioles.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c001--> Current guidelines define hypertension as a systolic pressure of 130 mm Hg or higher that persists on ambulatory monitoring.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c002--> Treatment aims to limit complications, even without symptoms.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c003--> Over 90% of patients have the essential (primary) form, with no identified cause.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c004--> Secondary hypertension results from an identifiable disease.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it?** Essential hypertension has a polygenic basis, many genes of small effect, plus environmental contributors such as salt intake and a low nephron number.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c006--> Renal or endocrine diseases can cause secondary hypertension, for example renal artery stenosis or aldosterone excess (Conn syndrome).<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c007--> In children, hypertension can result from a single-gene defect in mineralocorticoid pathways or sodium transport.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does blood pressure rise?** Blood pressure depends on cardiac output and on vessel resistance.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c009--> Different triggers lead to the same end point: peripheral resistance rises.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c010--> Once the kidney's vessels are structurally damaged, renal function falls and sodium is retained; before that, the vessel changes can be reversed.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c011-->

**What does the renin–angiotensin system do?** Angiotensin II is made in two steps: renal renin cleaves angiotensinogen into angiotensin I, and ACE converts that.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c012--> The kidney keeps sodium because angiotensin II prompts adrenal aldosterone release; it also narrows vessels and raises sympathetic drive via the brain.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c013--> Atrial natriuretic peptide pushes the other way: it makes the kidney lose more sodium in the urine.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c014--> Treatments that act on this axis, such as ACE inhibitors, work well, which shows its importance.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the kidneys look like?** A shrunken kidney with a granular surface can result from chronic hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c016-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes are seen in the vessels?** In chronic hypertension, small muscular arteries duplicate their internal elastic lamina and thicken the intima with fibromuscular tissue.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c017--> Aging produces comparable changes, so they do not prove hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c018-->

**What is hyaline arteriolosclerosis?** In hyaline arteriolosclerosis, plasma proteins and basement membrane material pile up in the arteriole wall, which turns thick and glassy under the light microscope.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c019--> It is seen in chronic hypertension and also in diabetes.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c020-->

**What is hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis?** Fibrinoid necrosis arises when fibrin and plasma proteins enter the wall of small arteries whose smooth muscle has died.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c021--> The onion-skin look comes from smooth muscle cells multiplying in rings after the injury.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c022-->

**How does this reach the kidney?** Glomerular scarring and interstitial fibrosis are common histologic findings in long-standing hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c023--> Malignant nephrosclerosis names the kidney lesions of malignant hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c024-->

## Clinical presentation

**What does it do to the patient?** Working against high pressure over time thickens the left ventricle, and heart failure and arrhythmias can follow.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c025--> It also promotes atherosclerosis and aortic dissection.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c026-->

**What is malignant hypertension?** Very few patients have it: pressure climbs quickly to at least 180 mm Hg systolic and 120 mm Hg diastolic.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c027--> Signs of organ injury include proteinuria and retinal hemorrhage.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c028-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it detected?** Pressure varies during the day, so a sustained elevation is needed for the diagnosis.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c029--> In severe hypertension, retinal arterioles look narrowed and widened in segments.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c030--> A rapid rise in pressure shows in the retina as cotton wool spots and small bleeds.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c031-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications?** Most strokes and most myocardial infarctions occur in hypertensive people.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c032--> The prothrombotic paradox: most hypertensive complications come from clots, not bleeding.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c033--> Malignant hypertension can be fatal through infarction of vital organs such as the brain and the kidney.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c034-->

## High-yield summary

- **Essential hypertension** is idiopathic, polygenic and over 90% of cases.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c039-->
- **Hyaline arteriolosclerosis** goes with chronic hypertension, and also with diabetes.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c040-->
- **Hyperplastic (onion-skin) arteriolosclerosis** with fibrinoid necrosis marks malignant hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c041-->
- The kidney becomes contracted and granular; in malignant hypertension the lesion is **malignant nephrosclerosis**.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c042-->
- Complications are mostly thrombotic: the **prothrombotic paradox**.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c043-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is arteriosclerosis of small arteries diagnostic of hypertension?** No: aging produces similar changes.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c044-->

**Is hyaline arteriolosclerosis specific to hypertension?** No: it also occurs in diabetes.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c045-->

**Does hypertension mainly cause hemorrhage?** No: infarction and stroke are mostly thrombotic.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c046-->

**What is Mönckeberg medial sclerosis?** A different lesion: it calcifies the muscular layer of large and medium arteries, chiefly in the limbs and mostly in older people.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c047-->

## Mnemonics

**Glass is slow, onion is fast.** Glassy hyaline change goes with chronic hypertension, and the onion-skin lesion with malignant hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-vascular-disease-c048-->
