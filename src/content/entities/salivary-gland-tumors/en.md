## Definition and epidemiology

**What are salivary gland tumors, and where do they arise?** Pleomorphic adenoma is the most common one.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c001--> It is benign and mixes epithelial and stromal elements.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c002--> Monomorphic adenomas, the other benign group, have no mesenchymal part, and their epithelium is regular and mostly forms glands.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c003--> Minor glands give 15% of malignant salivary tumors, the submandibular gland 10% and the parotid the remaining 75%.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c004--> About half of intraoral minor gland tumors are malignant.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c006--> Pleomorphic adenomas make up two-thirds of major gland tumors.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c007-->

<aside class="edition">This card follows the textbook's descriptive grouping. Current WHO families and grades may differ: check the latest WHO classification before relying on a grade.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who gets them, and is there a known cause?** No environmental cause is covered here. Most patients with pleomorphic adenoma are women or middle-aged.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c008--> Rarely, a pleomorphic adenoma that has been present for many years harbors a carcinoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which genetic changes define the main types?**
- In most pleomorphic adenomas, translocations at 8q12 switch on the PLAG1 gene.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c010-->
- A t(11;19) translocation, present in over 60% of mucoepidermoid carcinomas, creates a MECT1–MAML2 fusion gene.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c011--> The fusion presumably disturbs Notch signaling.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c012-->
- A t(6;9) translocation is a consistent finding in adenoid cystic carcinoma and joins MYB to NFIB.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c013-->
- The ETV6–NTRK3 fusion oncogene of secretory carcinoma results from a t(12;15) translocation.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c014--> The fusion lets cells survive and divide, because its NTRK3 kinase never switches off.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c015-->
- Warthin tumor may start from salivary tissue trapped in parotid lymph nodes, though its origin is uncertain.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the cut surface show?** A parotid pleomorphic adenoma has a sharp border and, cut open, a shiny white surface.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c017--> Pleomorphic adenoma has a fibrous capsule, yet nodules of tumor push out into the surrounding gland.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c018--> Acinic cell carcinoma usually measures under 3 cm and is a round mass walled off by a capsule, sometimes cystic.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c019--> Warthin tumor tends to be cystic, and papillae protrude into the cysts.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What pattern identifies each tumor?**
- **Pleomorphic adenoma:** the main cells are myoepithelial and sit in a cartilage- or mucus-like matrix, while ductal cells form tubules or small cysts.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c021-->
- **Warthin tumor:** lymphoid tissue rich in germinal centers surrounds the oncocyte-lined cysts and papillae of Warthin tumor.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c022-->
- **Mucoepidermoid carcinoma:** mucus-secreting cells share the tumor with squamous-type and intermediate cells.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c024--> High-grade tumors have highly irregular cells and almost no glands, whereas low-grade ones form cystic, duct-like spaces.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c025-->
- **Adenoid cystic carcinoma:** cribriform, tubular or solid growth is built from small cells with little cytoplasm.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c026--> A tumor is high grade when more than 30% of it grows as solid sheets.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c027--> The "cysts" of the cribriform pattern are not true cysts but pseudocysts holding basement membrane–like material.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c028-->
- **Acinic cell carcinoma:** acinic cell carcinoma imitates the secretory cells of normal acini and has abundant basophilic cytoplasm.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c029-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** on H&E, identify the pattern listed above.
- PAS stains the eosinophilic material inside the ducts of pleomorphic adenoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c031-->
- FISH with MAML2 break-apart probes shows gene disruption in mucoepidermoid carcinoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c032-->
- Specimen type, IHC and electron microscopy are not covered here.

## Clinical presentation

**How does each tumor present, and where?**
- **Pleomorphic adenoma:** the lump enlarges slowly without pain; to the touch it is smooth, firm and mobile.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c033--> Most arise in the superficial lobe of the parotid, which is affected nine times as often as the submandibular gland.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c034--> A lateral pharyngeal or tonsillar swelling can be a deep parotid pleomorphic adenoma that has grown into the parapharyngeal space.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c035--> Sudden growth or new pain in a pleomorphic adenoma present for years raises suspicion of carcinoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c036--> Curative surgery can require resecting the facial nerve.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c037-->
- In 15% of cases, the clearly benign parotid Warthin tumor is bilateral.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c038--> Warthin tumor is the only salivary tumor that favors men.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c039-->
- Mucoepidermoid carcinoma accounts for 10% of minor gland tumors and 5% to 10% of major gland tumors.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c040--> It mostly affects adult women, who notice a slowly enlarging, painless and firm swelling.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c041-->
- Two-thirds of adenoid cystic carcinomas arise in minor glands, where it forms 20% of tumors, against 5% in major glands.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c042--> Perineural spread explains why it is often painful.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c043-->
- Most secretory carcinomas arise in the parotid.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c045-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the type and grade established?** Histology assigns the grade: in mucoepidermoid carcinoma by the mix of cell types and spaces, in adenoid cystic carcinoma by the solid fraction.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c046--> Fine-needle aspiration, its reporting system and imaging are not covered here.

## Complications and prognosis

**Which tumors recur, and which are lethal?**
- Leaving behind capsule-protruding nodules, or implanting tumor during surgery, makes a pleomorphic adenoma recur.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c047-->
- When carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma stays inside the capsule or goes no deeper than 1.5 mm beyond it, the prognosis is excellent.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c048--> Once invasion exceeds 1.5 mm, the tumor behaves aggressively and the outlook is poor.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c049-->
- Whatever the primary site, over 90% of patients with mucoepidermoid carcinoma survive 5 years.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c050--> In high-grade tumors survival is much lower, at 20% to 40%.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c051--> Grade decides treatment: high-grade tumors need radiation plus surgery; low-grade ones, surgery.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c052-->
- Despite slow spread, adenoid cystic carcinoma is found late and is hard to excise, so the long-term outlook is poor.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c053--> About 15% of patients with a high-grade tumor survive 5 years.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c054-->
- After surgery, about 90% of patients with acinic cell carcinoma are alive at 5 years.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c055--> Local recurrence occurs in one-third of them.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c056-->

## High-yield summary

- **Pleomorphic adenoma** is the most common salivary tumor and is benign.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c063-->
- About **half of minor gland tumors** are malignant.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c064-->
- **Warthin tumor** is the one salivary tumor more common in men.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c065-->
- **MAML2** marks mucoepidermoid carcinoma, **MYB–NFIB** adenoid cystic carcinoma and **ETV6–NTRK3** secretory carcinoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c066-->
- **Perineural invasion** makes adenoid cystic carcinoma painful.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c067-->
- The **facial nerve** may have to be resected in curative surgery for pleomorphic adenoma.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c068-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does "mixed tumor" mean malignant?** No. The tumor is benign, and its neoplastic cells are epithelial.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c069-->

**Is a recurrent pleomorphic adenoma a cancer?** Usually not: a recurrence is most often tumor that grew back in place.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c070-->

**Does slow growth mean a good outcome?** Not for adenoid cystic carcinoma: it grows slowly yet has a poor long-term prognosis.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c071-->

**Can a low-grade mucoepidermoid carcinoma be ignored?** No: even a low-grade tumor can still give metastases.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c072-->

## Mnemonics

**Three tumors, three fusions:** Mucus is **MAML2**, the sieve (cribriform) **bites** with **MYB**, Secretory is **ETV6**.<!--c:salivary-gland-tumors-c073-->
