## Definition and epidemiology

**What is the most common cancer found in the liver?** Usually a secondary deposit, not a tumor that began in the liver.<!--c:liver-metastases-c001-->
The liver is involved in about one third of all metastatic cancers.<!--c:liver-metastases-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which primary tumors usually spread to the liver?** Breast, lung and gut cancers reach the liver about half the time.<!--c:liver-metastases-c003-->
Melanoma, blood and pancreatic cancers often reach the liver too.<!--c:liver-metastases-c004-->
The groups above are not exclusive, since any tumor may reach the liver.<!--c:liver-metastases-c005-->

## High-yield summary

- A malignant tumor in the liver is most often a **metastasis**.<!--c:liver-metastases-c006-->
- Liver involvement occurs in about **one third** of all metastatic cancers.<!--c:liver-metastases-c007-->
- In about half of cases, **breast, lung and gastrointestinal** cancers reach the liver.<!--c:liver-metastases-c008-->
- **Blood cancers, pancreatic cancer and melanoma** often seed the liver too.<!--c:liver-metastases-c009-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hepatocellular carcinoma the most common liver cancer?** No. A cancer found in the liver is most often a metastasis from elsewhere.<!--c:liver-metastases-c010-->

**Do only a few tumor types reach the liver?** No. Typical primaries exist, yet a liver metastasis can come from any tumor type.<!--c:liver-metastases-c011-->
