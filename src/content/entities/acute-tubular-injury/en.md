## Definition and epidemiology

**What is acute tubular injury?** It is damage to the tubular epithelial cells that makes kidney function fail suddenly, from a cause inside the kidney itself (intrarenal acute kidney injury, AKI).<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c001--> No cause of intrarenal AKI is more common than ischemia.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c002--> The older name, acute tubular necrosis (ATN), is a misnomer: ischemia usually does not kill tubular cells on a wide scale.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c003--> Recovery is possible in principle, although the failure is severe.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it, and how do the ischemic and nephrotoxic forms differ?** Low blood pressure is the usual setting of the ischemic form, in which renal perfusion falls.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c005--> Heart failure, dehydration and shock (for example from sepsis or burns) can trigger it.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c006--> In the nephrotoxic form, a chemical agent damages the tubular cells.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c007--> Examples are mercury, cisplatin, aminoglycosides and radiographic contrast.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c008--> Pigment nephropathy is tubular injury from myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis) or hemoglobin (hemolysis) reaching the urine in large amounts.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do tubular cells die, and why does filtration fall?** The high energy turnover of tubular cells means that a lack of oxygen quickly empties their ATP.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c010--> Toxins that upset oxidative pathways hurt these busy cells easily.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c011--> Another reason is that these cells absorb toxins and concentrate them.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c012--> Tubuloglomerular feedback is one reason the arterioles narrow inside the kidney, which lowers glomerular filtration pressure.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c013--> Casts of dead cells that block the tubule raise the pressure behind them, and filtration drops.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c014--> Through injured tubule walls, filtrate seeps back into the interstitium (backleak).<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c015--> A lower glomerular ultrafiltration coefficient (Kf) contributes too.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the kidneys look like?** The medulla is congested and the cortex pale; overall, the kidneys are swollen in the ischemic form.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the section show in the ischemic and in the toxic form?** In the ischemic form, glomeruli and blood vessels look normal.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c018--> The segments hurt most are the proximal tubules and, in the outer medulla, the thick ascending limb of Henle's loop.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c019--> In epithelial simplification the lining is thin and flat, has no brush border, and surrounds a wide lumen.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c020--> The urine holds "muddy" brown casts: shed tops of tubular cells, whose color comes from cytochromes.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c021--> Patches of tubular basement membrane lie bare where single dead cells, with a few live ones, were shed; necrosis is subtle.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c022--> The interstitium is often edematous.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c023--> Inflammation is essentially absent in ischemic injury and slight in the toxic form.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c024--> In toxic injury each toxin has its own most sensitive segment, usually the proximal tubule, and necrosis there is more extensive than in ischemia.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c025--> Mercury poisoning kills proximal tubular cells on a large scale; the distal and collecting tubules escape.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c026--> Casts tinted reddish brown by heme pile up when hemoglobin or myoglobin reaches the urine.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c027-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- **Urine:** the sediment shows muddy brown granular casts together with degenerating tubular cells.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c028-->
- **Routine H&E:** ischemic injury more often shows a flat tubular lining than necrosis.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c029-->
- **Electron microscopy:** proximal tubular cells have fewer infoldings of the basolateral membrane.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c030-->
- **Special stains, IHC and molecular tests:** not covered in the source pages for this card.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Serum creatinine rises rapidly.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c031--> Oliguria is the usual pattern, and the non-oliguric form is the less common one.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c032--> Uremia with hyperkalemia and metabolic acidosis occurs in many patients, even if briefly, and some need dialysis.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c033-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which urine tests point to it?** Urinalysis can separate acute tubular injury from glomerulonephritis and tubulointerstitial nephritis.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c034--> In prerenal ischemic AKI the fractional excretion of sodium is typically below 1%.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c035--> Fractional excretion of sodium above 2% marks overt tubular epithelial damage, as seen in intrarenal tubular injury.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c036-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How long does it last, and does the kidney recover?** Recovery begins when the patient passes more urine and the serum creatinine starts to fall.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c037--> Two things matter most for how long the failure lasts: what caused it and whether that cause can be reversed, though other factors play a part.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c038--> Stopping the cause as soon as injury begins may restore function in 1 to 2 weeks.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c039--> Recovery may also take months.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c040--> Regenerating tubular cells are crowded and enlarged, with large nuclei and mitoses.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c041--> The kidney regains its normal structure completely once the surviving cells have regrown.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c042-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Prerenal ischemic AKI | Tubular epithelium is structurally intact; the fall in filtration is functional and reversible.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c043--> | Fractional excretion of sodium |
| Acute tubular injury | Epithelial cells and muddy brown casts in the urine.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c044--> | Urinalysis sediment |
| [[postinfectious-glomerulonephritis\|Acute glomerulonephritis]] | Casts made of red cells, plus protein in the urine.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c045--> | Urinalysis sediment |
| [[drug-induced-interstitial-nephritis\|Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis]] | Casts made of white cells, plus pus cells in the urine.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c046--> | Urinalysis sediment |

## High-yield summary

- AKI most often has an **ischemic** origin, and ischemic tubular injury is the commonest intrarenal form.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c047-->
- **Ischemic** injury: **brown casts** and a **flat lining** without brush border; true necrosis is rare.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c048-->
- **Toxic** injury shows more necrosis, mostly in the **proximal tubule**, as in mercury poisoning.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c049-->
- **Fractional excretion of sodium** above 2% marks overt tubular damage.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c050-->
- **Recovery** begins when more urine is passed and creatinine starts to fall.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c051-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is "acute tubular necrosis" always an accurate name?** No. Overt necrosis is uncommon in typical ischemic injury, where the cells may only be flattened.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c052-->

**Is the damage spread evenly through the kidney?** No. In ischemic injury the damage is patchy (focal).<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c053-->

**Are all casts the same color?** No. Hemoglobin or myoglobin injury adds red-brown casts colored by heme, unlike the brown casts of ischemia.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c054-->

## Mnemonics

**FLAT** summarizes ischemic injury: **F**ocal, **L**ost brush border, **A**pical cytoplasm sloughed, **T**ubular casts brown.<!--c:acute-tubular-injury-c055-->
