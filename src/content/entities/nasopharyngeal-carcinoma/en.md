## Definition and epidemiology

**What is nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC)?** It is a carcinoma of the nasopharynx with two main forms: keratinizing and nonkeratinizing.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c001--> The nonkeratinizing form is either differentiated or undifferentiated.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c002--> No other cancer of the nasopharynx comes close to NPC in frequency.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c003--> Southeast Asia and some African regions see the undifferentiated nonkeratinizing form most often.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which virus is linked to NPC, and what else raises the risk?** Nonkeratinizing tumors are associated with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c005--> Around 85% of affected patients have EBV antibodies.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c006--> In undifferentiated nonkeratinizing tumors, the viral genome is found in 75% to 100% of cases.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c007--> In keratinizing tumors the viral findings vary more.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c008--> Researchers have not pinned down any environmental cause of nasopharyngeal carcinoma.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c009--> Inherited susceptibility is suspected because the HLA A2/sin type is more frequent among Chinese patients.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which genetic changes are seen?** Chromosomal deletions are frequent, and 3p, 9p and 14q stand out among them.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c011-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does the tumor arise?** It can arise in the fossa of Rosenmüller or on the lateral wall of the nasopharynx.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c012-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the two nonkeratinizing patterns look like?**
- In the differentiated pattern the cell borders are distinct, and the tumor looks stratified.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c013-->
- Undifferentiated tumor cells lie in clusters and blend into a syncytium, with poorly defined borders.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c014-->
- The nucleus of each undifferentiated cell is large and oval, and pink cytoplasm is scant.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c015-->
- Undifferentiated tumors may be heavily infiltrated by lymphoid cells.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c016-->
- The old name "lymphoepithelioma" comes from this infiltrate; it is obsolete and misleading.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c017-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the reference pages do not describe the specimen type or the sampling; only the slide findings below are supported.
- On H&E, compare the borders: sharp and layered, or blurred and fused with big oval nuclei.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c018-->
- On immunohistochemistry, both subtypes express cytokeratin.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c019-->
- They do not express hematologic or lymphoid markers.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c020-->
- EBV DNA is usually demonstrable in the tumor by in situ hybridization.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c021-->

## Clinical presentation

**Why is NPC often found late, and how does it show itself?** Because of where it sits, the tumor stays silent for a long time.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c022--> A palpable cervical node metastasis is the first finding in about half of patients.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c023--> Even then, many patients have no complaint from the nasopharynx.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c024--> Neurologic and hearing symptoms appear when the tumor invades the orbit, the cranium or the parapharyngeal space.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c025--> Invasion of the skull base involves the cranial nerves.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c026--> Middle ear symptoms come from tumors sitting in the fossa of Rosenmüller or on the lateral nasopharyngeal wall.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c027--> Obstruction of the eustachian tube is common.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c028--> Cervical nodes are often involved early, since the local lymphatic network is abundant.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c029-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What supports the diagnosis?** Serology shows EBV antibodies in most patients.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c030--> EBV DNA is usually detectable in tumor tissue by in situ hybridization.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c031--> Immunohistochemistry shows cytokeratin without lymphoid markers.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c032-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** Undifferentiated NPC is radiosensitive.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c033--> When the tumor has not left the nasopharynx, most patients are alive at 5 years or longer.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c034--> Spread to the neck nodes or beyond, or involvement of the cranial nerves, worsens survival.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c035-->

## High-yield summary

- NPC is split into keratinizing and nonkeratinizing types, and the nonkeratinizing ones are tied to **EBV**.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c047-->
- The undifferentiated form clusters in **southeast Asia** and some African regions.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c048-->
- EBV DNA in the tumor is usually shown by **in situ hybridization**.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c049-->
- Tumor cells express **cytokeratin** and no lymphoid markers.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c050-->
- A **cervical lymph node** metastasis is the first sign in about half of patients.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c051-->
- Poor survival is predicted by nodal spread or **cranial nerve** involvement.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c052-->
- Undifferentiated NPC is **radiosensitive**.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c053-->
- In **angiofibroma** of adolescent males, biopsy is contraindicated because of bleeding.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c054-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is "lymphoepithelioma" a lymphoma?** No. The tumor cells express cytokeratin and not lymphoid markers, so it is a carcinoma.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c055-->

**No throat or nasal symptoms: does that exclude NPC?** No. A neck node is the first sign in about half of patients.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c056-->

**Is EBV present in every NPC?** No. In nonkeratinizing undifferentiated tumors it is found in 75% to 100% of cases; keratinizing ones vary.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c057-->

**Should a vascular nasopharyngeal mass in an adolescent boy be biopsied?** No. If it is an angiofibroma, biopsy can cause dangerous bleeding.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c058-->

## Mnemonics

**N-E-C** helps to remember the core findings.
- **N**eck node: often the first sign.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c059-->
- **E**BV: detected in the tumor by in situ hybridization.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c060-->
- **C**ytokeratin: positive on immunohistochemistry.<!--c:nasopharyngeal-carcinoma-c061-->
