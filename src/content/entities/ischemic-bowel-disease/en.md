## Definition and epidemiology

**What is ischemic bowel disease?** Whatever reduces blood flow to the intestine can injure the bowel wall.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c001--> Damage can stay in the mucosa or involve the whole wall (transmural), and most cases are acute.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c002--> Atherosclerosis that severely narrows two or more major arteries typically underlies the rarer chronic form, with its repeated bouts.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What cuts off the blood supply?**

- Occlusive: segmental acute ischemia results when a thrombus or embolus abruptly blocks a large mesenteric artery.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c004-->
- Vasculitis is an uncommon cause, and it tends to hit small arteries.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c005-->
- Bowel trapped in a hernia, twisted (volvulus) or telescoped (intussusception) can lose arterial and venous flow.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c006-->
- Cocaine and some drugs occlude or constrict small vessels; thrombophilic states add risk.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c007-->
- Endothelial injury by bacterial toxins links E. coli O157:H7 and Shigella infection to occlusive ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c008-->
- Nonocclusive: a fall in cardiac output during shock (septic, hemorrhagic or after acute myocardial infarction).<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c010-->
- Venous: inflammation (pylephlebitis), stasis or hypercoagulability can cause mesenteric vein thrombosis.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c011-->
- The inferior vein is the site in just 5% of cases, the superior in nearly all others.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c012-->
- Chronic: mesenteric atherosclerosis is the usual cause.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c013-->
- Women, and people older than 65 years, are at higher risk of chronic ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the bowel die?**

- Occlusive: collaterals cannot compensate in time when a large artery is suddenly blocked.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c015-->
- A segment or almost all the small bowel infarcts, depending on how large the blocked vessel is.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c016-->
- Nonocclusive: vital organs such as the brain take priority for blood flow during shock.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c017-->
- Alpha-adrenergic drugs, often given in shock, divert even more blood from the gut.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c018-->
- The ischemia deepens because arterioles collapse when perfusion pressure plunges.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c019-->
- Watershed areas, where two arterial territories meet, are the colon's weak points in nonocclusive ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c020-->
- Territories of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries meet at the splenic flexure, a watershed.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c021-->
- Both the inferior mesenteric and the internal iliac arteries contribute blood to the rectosigmoid.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c022-->
- Splanchnic and systemic arteries both supply the rectum, so it is usually spared.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c023-->
- Arterial inflow falls and ischemia develops because a blocked vein lets blood and fluid pool in the segment.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c024-->
- Collaterals usually carry the flow when the superior mesenteric vein is blocked, so infarction is avoided.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c025-->
- Chronic: eating raises the gut's need for blood, and narrowed arteries cannot meet it.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c026-->

## Gross morphology

**What does infarcted bowel look like?**

- Purple discoloration throughout, with edema, marks infarcted bowel.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c027-->
- Venous occlusion may blur the usually sharp border with normal bowel.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c028-->
- Bleeding is worst in venous occlusion, and fills the infarcted mucosa and submucosa.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c029-->
- Broad patches of mucosa slough off, leaving a thin, stretched wall.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c030-->
- Gas may collect within the wall and the mesenteric veins (pneumatosis).<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c031-->
- An inflammatory exudate coats the serosa, which looks cloudy.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c032-->
- A pseudomembrane, hemorrhagic nodules or several ulcers may be seen at endoscopy.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c033-->
- Long-standing small-bowel ischemia can leave single or multiple concentric strictures.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c034-->
- Small ulcers often dot the atrophic mucosa over a stricture.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c035-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen on the slide?**

- Coagulative necrosis, with ghost cells, affects the surface epithelium.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c036-->
- The mucosa around it is edematous and hemorrhagic, with ulcers and crypt abscesses.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c037-->
- On colonic biopsy, only a few crypt bases survive among faint outlines of dead crypts.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c038-->
- The more severe the injury, the deeper the necrosis, up to full wall thickness.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c039-->
- In a chronic stricture, granulation tissue may reach the muscle layers, and the submucosa is thick and fibrotic.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c040-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

- **How it is studied:** the specimen is an endoscopic mucosal biopsy for colitis, or resected bowel or an autopsy for infarction.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c042-->
- On microscopy, look for coagulative necrosis of the surface epithelium and ghost crypts.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c043-->
- The reference pages describe no special stains, IHC, electron microscopy or molecular tests.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?**

- Abdominal pain of abrupt onset is a symptom of acute ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c044-->
- Depending on where the bleeding is, the stool may be maroon, contain bright red blood, or be a bloody diarrhea.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c045-->
- Intestinal angina is the recurrent abdominal pain of chronic ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c046-->
- Pain typically starts under half an hour after a meal and lasts some hours.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c047-->
- Intestinal angina may herald frank infarction.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c048-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it told from other colitis?**

- Clinically, infectious colitis or inflammatory bowel disease may pass for ischemic colitis.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c049-->
- The reference pages give no laboratory or imaging criteria.

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outcome?**

- Shock may follow the frequent perforation of untreated bowel.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c050-->
- Multiple organ dysfunction ends the worsening as infarction spreads.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c051-->
- A worse outcome is possible when bleeding is absent.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c052-->
- If the proximal superior mesenteric artery is blocked, almost all the small bowel dies, and removing it is not survivable.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c053-->
- Cause and extent of involvement set the outlook and the management.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c054-->
- In the colon, resting the bowel and stabilizing the circulation aim to restore blood flow.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c055-->
- More than 50% of patients with severe disease die.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c057-->
- Full recovery is possible, but a stricture may develop instead.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c059-->
- With an ischemic small-bowel stricture, stasis and bacterial overgrowth occasionally cause malabsorption; obstruction is the other risk.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c061-->

## High-yield summary

- **Nonocclusive** ischemia is a flow problem, typically low output in shock, and can reverse.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c072-->
- The colonic **watershed** areas are the **splenic flexure** and the **rectosigmoid**.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c073-->
- The **rectum** is usually spared thanks to its dual blood supply.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c074-->
- The microscopic hallmark is **coagulative necrosis with ghost crypts**.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c075-->
- **Intestinal angina** is pain within half an hour of eating, from atherosclerotic chronic ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c076-->
- Mortality in severe acute ischemia is **above 50%**.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c077-->
- **Angiodysplasia** is a colonic arteriovenous malformation that bleeds.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c078-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is the rectum a typical site of nonocclusive ischemic colitis?** No. It is usually spared because of its dual arterial supply.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c080-->

**Does a thrombosed mesenteric vein always infarct the bowel?** No. Collateral channels usually carry the flow.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c081-->

**Does the absence of bleeding make acute ischemia milder?** No. The outcome may be worse without bleeding.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c082-->

**Is angiodysplasia an infarct?** No. It is a localized arteriovenous malformation whose thin-walled dilated vessels bleed.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c083-->

**Do symptoms alone separate ischemic colitis from other colitis?** No. They can be indistinguishable clinically.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c084-->

**Is radiation damage only an acute event?** No. The chronic phase comes later, with vessel thrombosis and lasting ischemia.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c085-->

## Mnemonics

**How do I recall the colon's weak spots?** "S-R-S": Splenic flexure and Rectosigmoid are the watersheds, the rectum is Spared.<!--c:ischemic-bowel-disease-c086-->
