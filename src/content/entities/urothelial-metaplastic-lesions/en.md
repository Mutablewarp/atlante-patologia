## Definition and epidemiology
**What are benign proliferative and metaplastic urothelial lesions?** They are non-cancerous changes of the urinary tract lining. The bladder is the usual site, yet any segment of the tract can be involved.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c001-->
Flat umbrella cells face the lumen above a distinct basal layer, and the whole epithelium is only a few cells thick.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c002-->
The group covers Brunn buds and nests, cystitis cystica and glandularis, squamous metaplasia and nephrogenic metaplasia.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors
**What sets these changes off?** Long-standing inflammation is the usual setting: it comes from neurogenic bladder, stones or urinary infections and, much less often, from bladder exstrophy.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c004-->
A previous inflammatory condition cannot always be identified.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c005-->
Stones are the special association of squamous metaplasia, which otherwise follows chronic injury and inflammation.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c006-->

## Pathogenesis
**How does the lining end up changed?** The lining responds with hyperplasia, with or without metaplasia, mostly in chronically inflamed bladders.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c007-->
Cystitis glandularis is the name for urothelium that metaplasia has replaced with mucus-producing epithelium.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c008-->
The origin of nephrogenic metaplasia is unclear; in some cases kidney tubule cells passed in urine may seed it lower down.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c009-->

## Gross morphology
**What can the surgeon or pathologist see?** Cystitis cystica shows as grouped, fluid-filled cysts in the bladder; the ureter and urethra can show the same change.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c010-->
Nephrogenic metaplasia may look like a tumor, because it protrudes into the bladder.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c011-->

## Microscopic morphology
**What does each lesion look like on the slide?** Where the surface urothelium dips into the lamina propria in a bulb-like shape, the structure is a Brunn bud.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c012-->
A Brunn nest differs from a bud because its urothelial cells have lost any link to the surface and sit within the lamina propria.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c013-->
The cysts of cystitis cystica are Brunn nests that have become cystic, and their lining is ordinary urothelium.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c014-->
Nephrogenic metaplasia forms a papillary nodule that grows outward; its core is crowded tiny tubules in the lamina propria.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c015-->

## Clinical presentation
**Do these lesions cause problems?** Brunn buds are common and count as normal variants.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c016-->
A nephrogenic protrusion sometimes obstructs the ureters, and surgery is then required.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c017-->
They must not be mistaken for cancer.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c018-->

## Complications and prognosis
**Do they turn into cancer?** Urothelial bladder carcinoma is more likely to develop in these patients; cystitis glandularis adds a higher risk of adenocarcinoma.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c019-->
Still, the lesions themselves have not been shown to be preneoplastic.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c020-->

## High-yield summary
- **Brunn bud** stays connected to the surface; **Brunn nest** is detached in the lamina propria.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c024-->
- **Cystitis cystica** is Brunn nests that have turned into cysts, lined by ordinary urothelium.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c025-->
- **Cystitis glandularis** is mucus-producing metaplasia.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c026-->
- **Squamous metaplasia** answers chronic injury, with stones as the typical partner.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c027-->
- **Nephrogenic metaplasia** is small tubules that resemble renal tubules and can obstruct the ureters.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c028-->
- **Cancer risk** is higher, but the lesions are not themselves preneoplastic.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c029-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Are these lesions cancer?** No. They are benign changes that must be told apart from carcinoma.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c030-->
**Are they precancerous?** No evidence says so, even though the patients carry a higher cancer risk.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c031-->
**Is a Brunn nest the same as a Brunn bud?** No. A bud is still an invagination of the surface; a nest has lost contact with it.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c032-->

## Mnemonics
**How do I remember the four types?** "Big Cats Sleep Nightly": Brunn, Cystitis, Squamous, Nephrogenic.<!--c:urothelial-metaplastic-lesions-c033-->
