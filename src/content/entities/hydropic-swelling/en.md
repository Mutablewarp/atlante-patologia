## Definition and epidemiology

Hydropic swelling is a reversible enlargement of the cell caused by a rise in its water content.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c001--> It is the morphologic signature of acute, still-reversible cell injury.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c002--> As a general cellular process it has no epidemiology of its own; its frequency follows the diseases that produce it.

## Etiology and risk factors

The triggers are varied: chemical and biologic toxins, viral and bacterial infections, ischemia, and extremes of heat or cold.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c003--> What they share is a loss of the cell's control over cytoplasmic ion concentrations, above all sodium.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c004-->

- **Ischemia:** without oxygen the cell cannot generate ATP aerobically and falls back on inefficient anaerobic production.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c005-->
- **Toxic injury:** toxic damage to the liver can cause marked hydropic swelling of hepatocytes.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c006--> Carbon tetrachloride and an overdose of acetaminophen (paracetamol) are typical culprits: both damage mainly the centrilobular zone, where swollen hepatocytes lie beside necrotic ones.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c057-->
- **Infection:** in acute viral hepatitis many hepatocytes are swollen to varying degrees.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c007-->

## Pathogenesis

Volume control depends on three elements working together: an intact plasma membrane, the membrane sodium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase) and the ATP that powers it.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c008--> The membrane is slightly leaky to Na+, so sodium enters passively down its gradient and must be pumped out continuously at an energy cost.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c009-->

Sodium is therefore retained if ATP runs out, if the pump itself is injured, or if the membrane lets in more of it than a working pump can clear.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c010-->

Once Na+ builds up inside the cell, water follows to keep the cytoplasm isosmotic, and the cell swells.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c011--> The endoplasmic reticulum takes up most of the extra water: ion shifts probably draw it into the cisternae, which therefore dilate.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c012-->

In ischemia the sequence is amplified. ATP depletion stalls the Na+/K+ exchange; the ensuing intracellular acidosis switches on the Na+/H+ exchanger, which loads the cell with more Na+.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c013--> The sodium excess then activates the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger, and Ca2+ accumulates because the ATP-dependent calcium pump cannot clear it.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c014--> This calcium overload is the bridge from reversible swelling toward membrane damage and necrosis.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c015-->

Mitochondria may also swell in acute injury, especially ischemia, because loss of their membrane potential impairs their own volume regulation.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c016-->

## Gross morphology

In the brain the change can also be seen with the naked eye: when the brain is edematous, cytotoxic edema included, its sulci are narrowed and its gyri flattened.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c058-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Light microscopy.** The cell is enlarged and its cytoplasm looks expanded and pale.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c017--> The nucleus keeps its normal position (central in hepatocytes).<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c018--> Organelles are not lost; the same number is simply spread through a larger volume.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c019--> In toxic liver injury the change can be most severe in the centrilobular hepatocytes.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c020-->

**Electron microscopy.** Ultrastructure shows the full set of reversible changes:

- dilated, fluid-filled ER cisternae;<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c021-->
- polysomes that disaggregate and detach from the rough ER, with free ribosomes in the cytosol;<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c022-->
- swollen mitochondria with a paler matrix and less distinct cristae, typically in ischemic injury;<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c023-->
- plasma membrane blebs, which can pinch off without killing the cell;<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c024-->
- segregation of the fibrillar and granular parts of the nucleolus.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c025-->

These organelle changes depress cell function, for example protein synthesis and energy production.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c026-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **Specimen:** the change turns up in tissue taken for the disease behind it, such as a liver biopsy: after an acetaminophen overdose, the hepatocytes that survive around the centrilobular necrosis are markedly swollen.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c059-->
- **H&E:** besides the pale, enlarged cytoplasm (see Microscopic morphology), check the nucleus of every cell, because swollen and dead cells can share one field: in alcoholic hepatitis, hepatocytes swollen to different degrees lie among dead ones with karyorrhectic or pyknotic nuclei.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c060-->
- **Fat stains:** most fat is lost when tissue is embedded in paraffin, so routine sections show only empty vacuoles where it was.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c061--> Fat itself is shown on a frozen section, where lipid stains such as Sudan red color it red (see [[g:fat-stains]]).<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c062-->
- **PAS and PAS-D:** a pale cytoplasm that is PAS-positive but loses its staining after diastase digestion (PAS-D) holds glycogen, not water.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c063-->
- **Electron microscopy:** it shows the organelle changes listed under Microscopic morphology; in the heart, ischemic injury in its first, still reversible stage escapes the light microscope and shows only on electron microscopy, as slightly swollen mitochondria and an edematous sarcoplasm.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c064-->
- **Limits:** the section shows the injury, not who caused it: drug-induced liver damage reproduces the patterns of other liver diseases, so histology alone often cannot prove that a drug was to blame.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c065-->
- **IHC and molecular tests:** none is used to recognize the swelling itself.

## Clinical presentation

Hydropic swelling is not a disease but a finding within many diseases. Recognized organ settings include:

- **Brain:** cytotoxic edema is intracellular water accumulation equivalent to hydropic swelling, usually after ischemia, and it affects mainly the gray matter.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c027-->
- **Liver:** hepatocytes show variable hydropic swelling in acute viral hepatitis and in alcoholic hepatitis.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c028--> Retained bile is another cause: in obstruction of the extrahepatic bile ducts, hepatocytes that the bile has both stained and swollen show what is called feathery degeneration.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c066-->
- **Esophagus:** mild injury of the squamous epithelium in gastroesophageal reflux appears as cell swelling (hydropic change).<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c029-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

Not applicable.

## Complications and prognosis

If the injurious stress is removed in time, the cell recovers its normal structure and function.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c030--> For example, myocardium deprived of blood flow for less than 30 minutes can recover once perfusion is restored.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c031--> The mitochondrial swelling of reversible injury also resolves on recovery.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c032-->

If the insult is severe or prolonged, injury becomes irreversible and the cell dies; the exact point of no return is not known.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c033--> In dying cells the same ultrastructural changes are exaggerated and joined by calcified mitochondria, membrane discontinuities and nuclear changes.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c034--> Once membranes become leaky, the swollen cell is close to breaking apart.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c035-->

In the brain the swelling also has a mechanical cost: because the skull cannot yield, intracranial pressure rises and the cerebral blood supply suffers, and in severe edema the cerebellar tonsils can herniate, which may be fatal.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c067-->

## High-yield summary

- Hydropic swelling = **reversible** cell enlargement from **intracellular water**.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c040-->
- The primary defect is failure to expel **sodium**: membrane leak, damaged **Na+/K+-ATPase** or lack of **ATP**.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c041-->
- Water follows Na+ to keep the cell **isosmotic**.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c042-->
- Light microscopy: large cell, **pale cytoplasm**, **normally positioned nucleus**.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c043-->
- Electron microscopy: **dilated ER cisternae**, detached ribosomes, swollen mitochondria, membrane **blebs**.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c044-->
- **Ischemia** is the classic cause: no aerobic ATP, so the pump stalls.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c045-->
- In the brain, hydropic swelling is called **cytotoxic edema** and affects mainly the gray matter.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c046-->
- Special stains exclude look-alikes: **PAS** for glycogen, **oil red O** for neutral fat.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c070-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- Swelling alone does not mean the cell is dead; the nucleus is normal in hydropic swelling.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c047--> Death is signaled by nuclear changes such as pyknosis, karyorrhexis and karyolysis.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c048-->
- Membrane blebs are not proof of irreversible injury, because they can detach while the cell survives.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c049-->
- Water does not flood in first; sodium retention comes first and water follows osmotically.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c050-->
- "Hydropic" villi in molar pregnancy and a "hydropic" fetus describe edema of tissues or of the whole body, not this cellular process.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c051-->
- Cytotoxic brain edema (intracellular, gray matter) differs from vasogenic edema, which is extracellular fluid mainly in the white matter.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c052--> The blood–brain barrier stays intact in cytotoxic edema but loosens in vasogenic edema, and infarcts often combine the two.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c068-->
- A central nucleus does not rule out fat: microvesicular steatosis also keeps the nucleus in the middle of the hepatocyte, with the fat broken into tiny vacuoles, so check with a fat stain.<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c069-->

## Mnemonics

**SWELL** (original): **S**odium stays in, **W**ater follows, **E**R cisternae dilate, **L**ight pale cytoplasm with a normally placed nucleus, **L**asts only while the stress lasts (reversible).<!--c:hydropic-swelling-c053-->
