## Definition and epidemiology

**What is acute viral hepatitis?** A hepatotropic virus invades hepatocytes, and liver cells die with inflammation.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c001--> In industrialized countries, five viruses (A to E) account for nearly every case.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c002--> Hepatocyte death is the hallmark, whichever virus is responsible.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c003--> Among the causes of acute hepatitis, HAV ranks first.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which viruses cause it, and how do they spread?**
- **HAV** is a small RNA picornavirus.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c005--> Contaminated water, food or undercooked shellfish carry it (fecal–oral route).<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c006-->
- **HBV** is a DNA virus of the hepadnavirus group.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c007--> HBV is infectious only in blood, saliva and semen, so close contact is now its usual route.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c008-->
- **HCV** is an enveloped RNA flavivirus.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c009--> Infected blood spreads it, and injecting drugs with unsafe practices is a major risk.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c010-->
- **HDV** is a defective RNA virus that occurs only with HBV, either together (coinfection) or afterward (superinfection), because it cannot assemble without HBsAg.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c011-->
- **HEV** is an enteric RNA virus.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c090--> HEV spreads through contaminated water, through insufficiently cooked pork or game from infected animals, and through blood or mother-to-child transmission.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**Does the virus kill the hepatocyte directly?** No. In HAV, HBV and HCV the immune response causes the injury, since the viruses are not directly cytopathic.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c013--> In hepatitis B, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells do most of the damage by killing hepatocytes that display viral antigens.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c014--> In hepatitis C, cytotoxic T cells probably injure the liver by killing infected hepatocytes.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the liver look like?** In massive necrosis it weighs about a third of normal, as little as 500 g.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?**
- Acidophilic bodies are apoptotic hepatocytes: shrunken, intensely pink, at times with a pyknotic nucleus.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c020-->
- The cells differ in size and staining, and many are swollen (ballooned).<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c021-->
- In regenerating hepatocytes the nuclei are enlarged and the cytoplasm is bluish (basophilic).<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c022--> Lobular disarray is the loss of the neat plate pattern that these changes cause.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c023-->
- Lymphocytes ring necrotic hepatocytes and spread through the lobule.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c024--> Lymphocytes under the central vein wall give central phlebitis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c025-->
- Kupffer cells swell, storing lipofuscin and debris.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c026-->
- Dilated bile canaliculi, some plugged with bile, mark cholestatic hepatitis, the severe form of the bile stasis that is common in acute hepatitis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c027-->
- In confluent necrosis a whole zone of the lobule dies at once, whereas ordinary acute hepatitis kills cells here and there.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c030-->
- Bridging necrosis joins a portal tract to another portal tract or to a central vein, and one central vein to another.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c031--> Bands of connective tissue form because the collagen scaffold collapses after neighboring plates die.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c032-->
- Submassive necrosis destroys one or more adjoining lobules completely, a step beyond bridging.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c033-->
- Massive necrosis leaves only collagen frameworks, as almost every hepatocyte is dead.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c034--> Little inflammation accompanies the destruction, and the sinusoids hold macrophages and debris.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c035-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- **Specimen:** a liver biopsy is key to predicting whether hepatitis C will worsen.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c037-->
- **Routine H&E:** look for acidophilic bodies, ballooned hepatocytes, lobular disarray and lymphocytes.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c038-->
- **Special stain:** reticulin shows the collapse bands of bridging necrosis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c039-->
- No immunohistochemical or molecular findings are covered here.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?**
- Hepatitis A starts with anorexia, malaise and fever; jaundice may follow.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c040--> Most infections are anicteric.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c041-->
- Hepatitis A cannot turn chronic and has no carrier state.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c042--> Immunity after infection lasts for life.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c043-->
- Most adults recover from acute hepatitis B; its incubation is far longer than in A, and the illness is a bit worse.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c044--> Jaundice is absent in many infections, and in nearly all of those in children.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c045-->
- Most people with acute hepatitis C have a mild illness or none.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c046--> Most patients then develop chronic disease.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c047-->
- HDV coinfection usually resembles acute hepatitis B but can be fulminant.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c048--> HDV acquired by an HBV carrier usually aggravates the chronic hepatitis already there.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c049-->
- Hepatitis E is an icteric illness that resolves by itself, as in A, with fever, arthralgias and hepatomegaly.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c050--> Pregnancy makes it very dangerous.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c051-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests point to the virus?**
- Any jaundice in hepatitis A shows up only after aminotransferases, which rise with liver injury, begin to fall.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c053--> Detecting IgM anti-HAV in serum identifies HAV as the cause of an acute hepatitis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c054-->
- In acute hepatitis B, no serum marker appears before HBsAg.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c055--> Once HBsAg fades, anti-HBs appears; it means the patient has recovered and is immune for life.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c056-->
- Anti-HBc does not protect: it neither removes the virus nor prevents a new infection, and merely marks past infection.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c057-->
- A patient whose serum contains HBeAg is at peak infectivity; the virus is multiplying actively at that time.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c058-->
- In hepatitis C, HCV RNA appears before anti-HCV antibodies.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c059-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How can it go wrong?**
- Hepatitis A almost always ends in full recovery with no lasting damage.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c060-->
- Fulminant hepatitis is very rare in hepatitis A and somewhat more frequent in hepatitis B.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c061-->
- Fulminant hepatitis B often proves fatal: so many liver cells die that the liver fails.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c062--> Most deaths come from brain swelling, sepsis or failure of the heart and lungs.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c063-->
- The label fulminant hepatitis applies once submassive necrosis causes the liver to fail within a short time.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c064-->
- Massive necrosis (acute yellow atrophy) is rare and almost always ends in death.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c065-->
- Chronic HBV or HCV infection can lead to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c068-->

## High-yield summary

- **Acidophilic bodies**, **ballooned hepatocytes** and **lobular disarray** with lymphocytes mark acute viral hepatitis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c073-->
- **HAV** and **HEV** are enteric and self-limited.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c074-->
- **HBV** and **HCV** are blood-borne and can become chronic.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c075-->
- In hepatitis B the injury is **immune-mediated**: cytotoxic T cells, not the virus itself, destroy the hepatocytes.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c076-->
- **Massive** necrosis is the worst confluent necrosis, then **submassive**, then **bridging**.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c077-->
- **Fulminant hepatitis** means the liver fails quickly after severe necrosis.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c078-->
- **IgM anti-HAV** confirms hepatitis A.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c079-->
- **HBsAg** appears before every other serum marker of acute hepatitis B.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c080-->
- **HDV** needs **HBsAg**, so it occurs only with HBV.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c081-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hepatitis A ever chronic?** No. It has no chronic form and no carrier state.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c082-->

**Does the virus itself destroy the liver cells?** No. HAV, HBV and HCV are not directly cytopathic.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c083-->

**Are acidophilic bodies specific for viral hepatitis?** No. Many other liver diseases show them too.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c084-->

**Does anti-HBc mean protection?** No. It only tells that infection occurred earlier.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c085-->

**Is confluent necrosis proof of viral hepatitis?** No. Autoimmune hepatitis and hepatotoxic drugs can cause it too.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c086-->

**Is hepatitis E mild in everyone?** No. Pregnant women are at high risk.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c087-->

## Mnemonics

**Eat A and E, bleed B and C.** A and E are enteric; B and C are blood-borne.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c088-->

**Bridge, Block, Blanket.** Bridging links structures, submassive blocks out lobules, massive blankets nearly everything.<!--c:acute-viral-hepatitis-c089-->
