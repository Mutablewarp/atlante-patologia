## Definition and epidemiology

**What is drug-induced liver injury?** Almost any liver disease can be imitated by a reaction to a drug.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c001--> Mild cases show only raised transaminases; the worst end in acute liver failure.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c002--> In the United States, acute liver failure is most often drug related.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c003--> Predictable injury grows with dose, as with acetaminophen (paracetamol).<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c004--> Idiosyncratic injury strikes few patients whatever the dose, and they have a metabolic or genetic susceptibility.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c005-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Which histologic patterns can a drug produce?** Each drug usually causes its own typical pattern of toxicity.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c006-->

- **Zonal necrosis:** acetaminophen necrosis is centrilobular, but extreme doses involve the whole lobule.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c007--> Drug-metabolizing enzymes are probably more active centrally, which would explain the zone.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c008--> Inflammation is scant.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c009-->
- **Cholestatic pattern:** sex hormones and some antibiotics, such as sulfamethoxazole, cause pure cholestasis: bile retained in hepatocytes and canaliculi, no inflammation.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c010--> With inflammation it is called cholestatic hepatitis.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c011-->
- **Hepatitic pattern:** isoniazid and others can cause hepatitis that mimics the viral form.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c012--> Eosinophils raise suspicion of a drug cause, but no infiltrate proves it.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c013-->
- **Steatotic pattern, macrovesicular:** amiodarone gives a steatohepatitis-like picture rich in Mallory–Denk hyalins; methotrexate and corticosteroids cause macrovesicular fat.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c014-->
- **Steatotic pattern, microvesicular:** Reye syndrome badly injures mitochondria and other organelles; hepatocytes show tiny fat droplets and a central nucleus.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c015-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is drug injury diagnosed?** No test exists that can foresee or confirm drug hepatotoxicity.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c016--> Ask a patient with jaundice or raised liver enzymes about everything they take, supplements included.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c017--> Viral, alcoholic and other liver diseases have to be ruled out too.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c018--> Drug patterns overlap with non-drug diseases, so biopsy often adds little.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c019-->

## High-yield summary

- **Acetaminophen** gives dose-dependent **centrilobular necrosis**.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c024-->
- **Idiosyncratic** reactions do not depend on dose.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c025-->
- Patterns: **cholestatic**, **hepatitic**, **steatotic**.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c026-->
- **Microvesicular** fat signals severe mitochondrial injury.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c027-->
- Diagnosis rests on **drug history** and exclusion of other causes.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c028-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does an eosinophil-rich infiltrate prove a drug cause?** No. Eosinophils make it likelier, but no infiltrate is specific.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c029-->

**Is the fat in drug-induced steatosis always dangerous?** No. Macrovesicular fat alone is benign; microvesicular fat should raise concern for severe disease.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c030-->

**Does a liver biopsy confirm a drug cause?** Not usually. Its patterns overlap with non-drug diseases, so biopsy often cannot decide.<!--c:drug-induced-liver-injury-c031-->
