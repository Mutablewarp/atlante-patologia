## Definition and epidemiology

**What counts as a benign renal tumor?** The group includes papillary renal adenoma, oncocytoma, medullary fibroma, angiomyolipoma and mesoblastic nephroma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c001--> Pathologists still argue about whether an epithelial kidney tumor can ever be called benign.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c002--> Every carcinoma begins as a small lesion, so a size cutoff cannot separate adenoma from carcinoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c003--> A few epithelial tumors under 3 cm do metastasize, although most do not.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c004--> Adenoma is an acceptable label for a papillary or tubulopapillary growth that measures less than 5 mm.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c005--> At autopsy, 40% of people over 70 carry a papillary adenoma found by chance, and the frequency rises with age.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c006--> Among primary kidney tumors removed by surgery, oncocytomas make up 5% to 10%.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which of these tumors has a known association?** Angiomyolipoma is strongly linked to tuberous sclerosis.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c008--> Angiomyolipomas are found in 80% of patients with tuberous sclerosis.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c009--> Yet in most people who have an angiomyolipoma, tuberous sclerosis is absent.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c010--> Mesoblastic nephroma is present from birth, as either a neoplasm or a hamartoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which cell does each tumor come from?** Oncocytoma arises from the intercalated cells of the collecting duct.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c012--> Mitochondria fill the cells of an oncocytoma and give them their typical appearance.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c013--> Medullary fibroma is also called renomedullary interstitial cell tumor.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c014--> The cells of mesoblastic nephroma belong to the fibroblast or myofibroblast lineage.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What do they look like on the cut surface?** Lipochrome pigment gives oncocytoma its usual mahogany-brown color.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c016--> On gross inspection, angiomyolipoma can pass for renal cell carcinoma, because it is yellow with a bosselated outline.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c017--> A medullary fibroma sits mostly in the middle of a renal pyramid; it is tiny (under 0.5 cm) and pale gray.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c018--> Mesoblastic nephromas vary widely in size: the smallest are below 1 cm and the largest exceed 15 cm.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c019--> Mesoblastic nephroma borders are usually ragged, as tumor extensions reach into adjacent kidney.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the key microscopic picture of each?** Oncocytoma nuclei are round and bland, and the cytoplasm is pink and granular.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c021--> Thick-walled vessels, smooth muscle and mature fat are mixed together in an angiomyolipoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c022--> A loose stroma holds the small cells of a medullary fibroma, which range in shape from stellate to polygonal.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c023--> Mesoblastic nephroma consists of spindle cells.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c024-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which special tests help?** The reference pages used for this card describe no special stains, immunohistochemistry or molecular tests for these tumors.

## Clinical presentation

**How do they come to attention?** Up to half of adult autopsies find a medullary fibroma by chance.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c025--> Mesoblastic nephromas are usually found in infants younger than 3 months.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c026--> The reference pages give no symptoms for these tumors.

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How do you tell them from carcinoma or Wilms tumor?** A well-formed capsule, always present, and the absence of necrosis separate angiomyolipoma from renal cell carcinoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c027--> Mesoblastic nephroma needs distinction from Wilms tumor.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c028--> The reference pages give no laboratory or imaging findings.

## Complications and prognosis

**Do they spread or come back?** Oncocytomas rarely metastasize.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c029--> If strips of tumor are left in the kidney at surgery, a mesoblastic nephroma can recur.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c030-->

## High-yield summary

- **Angiomyolipoma** contains vessels with thick walls, smooth muscle and fat.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c033-->
- **Angiomyolipoma** has a capsule and no necrosis, unlike carcinoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c034-->
- **Tuberous sclerosis**: 80% of patients have angiomyolipoma.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c035-->
- **Oncocytoma**: mitochondria-rich granular cells and a mahogany-brown cut surface.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c036-->
- **Papillary adenoma**: a papillary or tubulopapillary tumor that stays below 5 mm.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c037-->
- **Mesoblastic nephroma**: residual tumor strips after surgery can cause recurrence.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c038-->
- **Medullary fibroma**: a tiny incidental autopsy finding.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c039-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do most patients with angiomyolipoma have tuberous sclerosis?** No. Most do not, even though 80% of tuberous sclerosis patients have the tumor.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c040-->

**Is a small epithelial renal tumor automatically an adenoma?** No. Carcinomas start small, so size alone cannot make the call.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c041-->

**Is oncocytoma malignant?** No. It is benign and rarely metastasizes.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c042-->

## Mnemonics

**How do I remember what is inside an angiomyolipoma?** **AML** = **A**dipose tissue, **M**uscle, **L**umen-bearing thick vessels.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c043-->

**Why is oncocytoma brown?** Mahogany mitochondria: the pigment sits in the mitochondria that fill the cells.<!--c:benign-renal-tumors-c044-->
