## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a pleomorphic adenoma?** It is a benign tumor of the salivary glands that combines epithelial cells with a stromal component.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c001--> It is the most common salivary gland tumor.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c002--> Among minor gland tumors about half are pleomorphic adenomas, and among major gland tumors the share is about two-thirds.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c003--> The parotid is the usual site, and the tumor mostly lies in its superficial lobe.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c004--> Compared with the submandibular gland, the parotid is affected about nine times as often.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c005--> Women and middle-aged people are the groups most often affected.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c006--> The older name, benign mixed tumor, reflects a tissue that looks part epithelial and part cartilage-like or mucinous.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c007--> The mixed look is misleading: the neoplastic cells are held to be epithelial.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which abnormalities are documented?** Chromosome 8 shows loss of heterozygosity, and the regions 3p21 and 12q13-15 are rearranged.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Which gene is switched on?** *PLAG1* (pleomorphic adenoma gene 1) codes for a zinc-finger protein.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c010--> Reciprocal translocations involving 8q12 turn it on in most of these tumors.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c011-->

**What changes when a carcinoma develops?** Carcinomas arising in these tumors may carry *HMGIC* and *MDM2* mutations and further 12q13-15 or 8q12 changes.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c012--> p53 and other tumor suppressors are marked for breakdown in the proteasome by the *MDM2* product, a nuclear E3 ubiquitin ligase.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c013--> *HMGIC* encodes a chromatin protein that is not a histone.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c014-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the tumor look like on the cut surface, and what surrounds it?** Cut open, a resected parotid shows a mass with a sharp border and an even white color.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c015--> As the tumor enlarges, the fibrous tissue around it is condensed into a capsule.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c016--> The capsule is incomplete and covers only part of the tumor.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c017--> A nodule pushing out of the tumor lies outside the capsule; such outgrowths are sometimes termed "podocytes".<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c018-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Which cells and which stroma make up the tumor?** The stroma is chondromyxoid, and glandular and myoepithelial cells are mixed within it.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c019--> Two cell types make up the epithelium: ductal and myoepithelial.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c020--> Clear fluid or eosinophilic material fills the tubules and small cysts that the ductal cells build.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c021--> Most of the cells are myoepithelial; they are smaller than the ductal cells and lie around them.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c022--> Myoepithelial cells often form nests or cords, with cartilage-like matrix in the gaps.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c023-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**
- **Specimen:** cut in half, the resected parotid shows the tumor.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c024-->
- **Microscopy:** under the microscope, look for ducts, myoepithelial cells and chondromyxoid stroma side by side.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c025-->
- **Special stain:** the material inside the ductal spaces is PAS-positive.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c026-->
- **Immunohistochemistry and electron microscopy:** the reference gives no findings for this tumor.
- **Molecular:** in most tumors, translocations at 8q12 are found to activate *PLAG1*.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c027-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Typically it is a painless lump that enlarges slowly and can be moved freely.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c028--> A bulge at the side of the throat or tonsil can be a deep parotid tumor that has extended into the parapharyngeal space.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c029--> To remove the tumor for good, the surgeon may have to give up the facial nerve.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c030-->

**Which change should raise suspicion of malignancy?** Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma is rare.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c031--> Rapid growth or new pain in a tumor that has been there for many years points to it.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c032-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** Under the microscope the tumor shows epithelial cells set in a stroma that is partly cartilage-like and partly mucinous.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c033-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Why does the tumor recur?** Capsule-free nodules left in place at surgery go on growing.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c034--> Scars of earlier operations can harbor recurrences, because tumor implanted at surgery grows there.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c035--> When the tumor returns, the usual explanation is local regrowth rather than malignant change.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c036--> To prevent this, the surgeon must take the tumor together with an intact capsule and a rim of normal gland of adequate width.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c037-->

**When does it become dangerous?** High-grade malignancy is the usual type among the carcinomas that develop in it.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c038--> [[mucoepidermoid-carcinoma]] and [[adenoid-cystic-carcinoma]] are examples of the nearly unlimited range of salivary cancers that can arise here.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c039--> In situ (noninvasive) carcinoma is the name for a cancer that stays within the capsule and spares the normal salivary tissue.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c040--> Minimally invasive carcinoma extends beyond the capsule by 1.5 mm at most.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c041--> These two forms have an excellent prognosis.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c042--> Tumors that grow over 1.5 mm past the capsule (widely invasive) do badly: they spread and often come back.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c043-->

## High-yield summary

- **Pleomorphic adenoma** is the most common salivary gland tumor, usually in the **parotid**.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c050-->
- Under the microscope, a **chondromyxoid stroma** holds the **epithelial and myoepithelial cells**.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c051-->
- The **incomplete capsule** leaves nodules behind, so limited excision leads to **recurrence**.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c052-->
- **8q12** translocations activate ***PLAG1*** in most tumors.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c053-->
- **Carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma** is suspected when an old tumor grows fast or hurts.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c054-->
- Prognosis of the carcinoma depends on invasion beyond the capsule: **1.5 mm** is the cut-off.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c055-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a "benign mixed tumor" made of two different neoplastic cell lines?** No. The tumor cells are thought to derive from epithelium.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c056-->

**Does a recurrence mean the tumor has become malignant?** No. Most often the tumor has simply regrown locally.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c057-->

**Is the tumor fully enclosed by its capsule, so that shelling it out is enough?** No. The capsule is incomplete, so nodules pushing out through it must be removed with the tumor, or it comes back.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c058-->

**Are all salivary gland tumors more common in women?** No. Among them, Warthin tumor alone is more common in men.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c059-->

## Mnemonics

**CAPE:** Chondromyxoid stroma, Absent capsule in places, Parotid, Epithelial plus myoepithelial cells.<!--c:pleomorphic-adenoma-c060-->
