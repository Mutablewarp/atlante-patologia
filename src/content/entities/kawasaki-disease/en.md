## Definition and epidemiology

**What is Kawasaki disease?** It is a vasculitis with necrosis of the vessel wall that starts suddenly in infants and young children; it is also called mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c001--> The coronary arteries are the vessels it mainly attacks.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers it?** Researchers have searched hard for an infectious cause, but none has been identified.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c003--> A subset of cases has been tied to infection with parvovirus B19 or with the New Haven coronavirus.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c004--> Chlamydia, Staphylococcus and Streptococcus have been linked to still other cases.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c005--> Superantigens made by viruses or bacteria could be the link between them.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**How could a microbe cause this damage?** Massive immune activation follows when a superantigen bridges MHC class II and the Vβ region of the T-cell receptor.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c007--> Too many inflammatory cytokines build up in the coronary arteries, partly through IL-1β and IL-18 activity.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c008--> Autoantibodies directed at endothelial and smooth muscle cells are found in some patients.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c009-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the heart show at autopsy?** In children who die of it, aneurysms are plainly visible on the coronary arteries.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does an affected coronary artery look like under the microscope?** In a fatal case, the internal elastic lamina of the coronary artery is broken in places, leaving wide gaps.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c011--> Where the lamina is breached, small aneurysms form and contain clot.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c012-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

## Clinical presentation

**What does the child have?**
- High fever.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c013-->
- Rash.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c014-->
- Lesions of the conjunctiva and the mouth.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c015-->
- Lymphadenitis.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c016-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it diagnosed?** The pages used for this card give no diagnostic criteria, laboratory findings or imaging findings.

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the danger?** Aneurysms of the coronary arteries, due to vasculitis, develop in 70% of patients.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c017--> Only a small fraction of these aneurysms, 1% to 2%, prove fatal.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c018--> The disease itself is usually self-limited.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c019-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[anca-associated-vasculitis\|Churg–Strauss syndrome]] | Medium and small arteries are ringed by eosinophils and necrotizing granulomas, in a young person with asthma.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c020--> | A positive P-ANCA, found in about two-thirds of patients.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c021--> |
| [[thromboangiitis-obliterans\|Buerger disease]] | Heavy smokers whose distal arm and leg arteries become blocked by inflammation.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c022--> | Vessel-wall microabscesses are the clue: neutrophils in the middle, Langhans giant cells and fibroblasts outside.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c023--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome** is the other name of the disease.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c024-->
- It is a necrotizing vasculitis of **infants and young children**.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c025-->
- **Coronary aneurysms** occur in 70% of patients.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c026-->
- Only 1% to 2% of the aneurysms are **lethal**.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c027-->
- A **superantigen** ties MHC class II to the Vβ region of the T-cell receptor.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c028-->
- The disease is usually **self-limited**.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c029-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Has an infectious agent been proven to cause it?** No. Many microbes are suspected, but none has been identified as the cause.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c030-->

**Is it a disease of the aorta, like Takayasu arteritis?** No. Its main target is the coronary arteries.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c031-->

**Do all coronary aneurysms kill?** No. Only 1% to 2% are lethal.<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c032-->

## Mnemonics

**CRAFT** helps recall the findings: Conjunctival lesions, Rash, Adenitis (lymph node), Fever, and oral lesions (the T marks the mouth).<!--c:kawasaki-disease-c033-->
