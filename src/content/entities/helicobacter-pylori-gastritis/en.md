## Definition and epidemiology

**What is Helicobacter pylori gastritis?** It is a chronic gastritis caused by *H. pylori*, a short bacillus that lives on top of the foveolar epithelium.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c001--> Worldwide, no other agent causes more gastritis or gastric cancer than *H. pylori*.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c002--> Warren and Marshall's 1984 findings finally accounted for chronic gastritis.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c003--> The infection is commonest among people of low socioeconomic status and in developing countries.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c004-->

Chronic gastritis is very common everywhere, and it goes with extra inflammatory cells in the lamina propria.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which organisms cause it?** A group of non-pylori Helicobacters, often labeled *H. heilmannii*, infects humans and mimics *H. pylori* gastritis.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c006--> Cats, dogs and pigs are common hosts, and the organisms live in their stomachs.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c007--> Compared with *H. pylori*, they are bigger and wind into several tight coils.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c008--> The treatment regimens that work against *H. pylori* work on them as well, and they too make urease.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c009--> When infection is absent, atrophic gastritis can still be favored by what a person eats, for instance smoked and salted foods.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do the bacteria cause damage if they do not invade?** Because the bacteria sit in mucus over the foveolar cells, the earliest inflammation is in the superficial lamina propria.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c011--> Foveolar cells make a neutral mucin that shelters *H. pylori*; on special stains it is PAS positive and Alcian blue negative.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c012--> Ammonia neutralizes the surrounding acid, which lets the bacteria survive; they make it from urea with the enzyme urease.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c013--> The toxins vacA and cagA injure the epithelium, provoke inflammation, or both; the bacteria never invade.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c014-->

**Which part of the stomach is involved?** Usually the antrum.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c015--> The gastritis can spread beyond the antrum to the proximal stomach if the infection lasts long or if PPIs are taken chronically.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c016--> When gland loss (atrophy) and intestinal metaplasia occur in patches of the antrum, body or fundus, the picture is called multifocal atrophic gastritis.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c017--> Cancer is believed to follow a stepwise path: inflammation, then intestinal metaplasia, then dysplasia, then adenocarcinoma.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c018-->

## Gross morphology

**Can the endoscopist recognize it?** Gastritis is harder to judge at endoscopy than esophagitis or colitis.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c019--> Sampling should be tied to landmarks, since the stomach's cardia, body and antrum differ in build and role.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** The superficial lamina propria holds an infiltrate that is mostly lymphocytes and plasma cells.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c021--> When lymphoid aggregates are seen, which happens often, Helicobacter is strongly suggested.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c022--> Pit abscesses may occur: they are collections of neutrophils in the foveolae, and such neutrophils are often present.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c023--> Such neutrophils show that the existing chronic gastritis is flaring up, so acute gastritis is the wrong label.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c024--> Lying in the mucus above the pit-lining cells are the bacteria, small bent rods.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c025--> In multifocal atrophic gastritis, glands are lost and intestinal metaplasia is present.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c026-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the specimen is an endoscopic biopsy, and landmarks record which gastric region was sampled, since regions differ.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c027-->
- On routine H&E, the bacteria can usually be found by an experienced observer.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c028-->
- When a special stain is needed, most often it is silver impregnation (Warthin–Starry).<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c029-->
- Immunostaining is also effective.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c030-->
- The reference pages give no data on electron microscopy or molecular tests.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient feel?** Patients may have no symptoms, or only non-specific dyspepsia.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c031--> Peptic ulcer disease is what long-standing Helicobacter gastritis most often brings about.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c032--> Among patients whose gastritis is antral-predominant, a duodenal ulcer develops in 5% to 10%.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c033-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it recognized?** On a gastric biopsy, a silver stain makes the bacteria stand out on the surface of the pit-lining cells, and the upper lamina propria contains lymphocytes and plasma cells.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c034-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the risks of long infection?** Of all distal gastric cancers, 70% are attributable to chronic infection, which makes cancer the most serious risk.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c035--> Gastric cancer is also frequent in countries where H. pylori gastritis is widespread.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c036--> The infection also raises the risk of a gastric lymphoma arising from mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT).<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c037--> In about 75% of patients with this lymphoma, clearing the infection alone brings lasting remission.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c038-->

**How is it treated?** Clarithromycin is given together with a PPI and with one of two antibiotics, amoxicillin or metronidazole, as triple therapy.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c039-->

## High-yield summary

- **Antral** disease is the usual pattern; chronic PPI use or long infection lets it extend to the proximal stomach.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c046-->
- The acid around the bacteria is neutralized by ammonia, the product of **urease**.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c047-->
- Damage in Helicobacter gastritis comes from the toxins **vacA** and **cagA**, since the bacteria do not invade.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c048-->
- Finding **lymphoid aggregates** strongly points toward Helicobacter.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c049-->
- **Warthin–Starry**, a silver impregnation, is the stain most often chosen for the bacteria.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c050-->
- Cancer sequence: inflammation, **intestinal metaplasia**, dysplasia, adenocarcinoma.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c051-->
- Links: **peptic ulcer**, gastric adenocarcinoma and **MALT lymphoma**.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c052-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do neutrophils mean acute gastritis?** No. They show that a long-standing chronic gastritis is flaring up; acute gastritis is a separate disease.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c053-->

**Do the bacteria invade the mucosa?** No. They stay at the surface and act through cytotoxins.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c054-->

**Is every multifocal atrophic gastritis caused by H. pylori?** No. Diet may play a part even when there is no infection, though Helicobacter is behind most cases.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c055-->

**Is autoimmune gastritis the same disease?** No. Antibodies against parietal cells and intrinsic factor attack only the body and fundus, where those cells live.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c056-->

**Is every Helicobacter of the stomach H. pylori?** No. *H. heilmannii* species also cause a similar gastritis.<!--c:helicobacter-pylori-gastritis-c057-->
