## Definition and epidemiology

Fatty change (steatosis) is the abnormal build-up of triglycerides inside the cytoplasm of parenchymal cells.<!--c:fatty-change-c001--> The liver is where it is most striking, but the heart, kidney and skeletal muscle can also store fat.<!--c:fatty-change-c002--> Hepatocytes always hold a little fat, so steatosis means an excess that is visible as cytoplasmic lipid droplets.<!--c:fatty-change-c003--> It is extremely common: virtually every chronic heavy drinker develops a fatty liver.<!--c:fatty-change-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

Every cause tips the balance between lipid arriving in the cell and lipid leaving it.<!--c:fatty-change-c005-->

- **Alcohol:** the classic cause; ethanol disturbs almost every step of hepatic lipid handling.<!--c:fatty-change-c006-->
- **Obesity and insulin resistance (NAFLD):** obesity, type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia and metabolic syndrome are risk factors for nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, and insulin resistance appears to be the strongest one.<!--c:fatty-change-c007-->
- **Diabetes in general:** more free fatty acids reach the liver.<!--c:fatty-change-c008-->
- **Drugs and toxins:** carbon tetrachloride, corticosteroids and antimetabolites such as methotrexate cause macrovesicular steatosis.<!--c:fatty-change-c009-->
- **Hepatitis C** is another recognized setting of fatty liver.<!--c:fatty-change-c010-->
- **Protein malnutrition (kwashiorkor):** the liver becomes conspicuously fatty.<!--c:fatty-change-c011-->
- **Mitochondrial failure:** acute fatty liver of pregnancy and Reye syndrome produce microvesicular steatosis.<!--c:fatty-change-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**Normal lipid traffic.** Hepatocytes take up free fatty acids (from adipose tissue and from dietary chylomicrons).<!--c:fatty-change-c013--> Inside the cell, fatty acids are either burned by mitochondrial β-oxidation or re-esterified to triglycerides in the endoplasmic reticulum.<!--c:fatty-change-c014--> New triglycerides are packaged into lipoproteins, mainly VLDL, and exported, or kept in storage.<!--c:fatty-change-c015-->

**Where the balance fails.** Fat accumulates when one or more of these steps is pushed out of balance:

- **More fuel in:** increased delivery of free fatty acids, as in diabetes or after ethanol-driven lipolysis.<!--c:fatty-change-c016-->
- **Less burning:** reduced mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation.<!--c:fatty-change-c017-->
- **Less export:** impaired release of lipoproteins.<!--c:fatty-change-c018-->

**Ethanol** acts at almost every step: it raises fatty acid synthesis and triglyceride production, slows mitochondrial oxidation and impairs lipoprotein release.<!--c:fatty-change-c019--> Most of the fat in an alcoholic fatty liver comes from the diet.<!--c:fatty-change-c020-->

**Kwashiorkor** shows the export arm in isolation: carbohydrate supplies the substrate for fat, but protein deficiency limits the apoproteins needed to carry it out of the cell.<!--c:fatty-change-c021-->

**Microvesicular steatosis** reflects severe injury to mitochondria.<!--c:fatty-change-c022--> In acute fatty liver of pregnancy, the mother's β-oxidation can be swamped by fatty acids that return across the placenta from a fetus unable to oxidize them, classically because of LCHAD deficiency.<!--c:fatty-change-c023-->

## Gross morphology

The fatty liver is enlarged and yellow, and in heavy drinkers it can weigh up to three times its normal weight.<!--c:fatty-change-c024--> Water and protein add to the weight gain alongside the fat.<!--c:fatty-change-c025-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Macrovesicular steatosis.** Droplets range from a few small ones to coalesced vacuoles that fill the whole cytoplasm.<!--c:fatty-change-c026--> When the droplets fuse into a single large one, the hepatocyte looks like a fat cell: its nucleus is squeezed flat against the cell membrane.<!--c:fatty-change-c027-->

**Microvesicular steatosis.** Countless tiny droplets fill the cell without displacing its nucleus, which stays in the middle.<!--c:fatty-change-c028--> In acute fatty liver of pregnancy the hepatocytes are also enlarged.<!--c:fatty-change-c029-->

**Ultrastructure.** In the drinker's hepatocyte the electron microscope finds oversized, sometimes giant, mitochondria and a proliferated smooth ER: changes due to ethanol itself, not to the stored fat.<!--c:fatty-change-c030-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**How it is studied:**

- **Specimen:** a needle core biopsy of the liver, which pathologists prefer to a surgical wedge for a diffuse disease; only tissue shows whether steatohepatitis has developed on top of simple fatty change, which keeps the biopsy the reference standard in NAFLD.<!--c:fatty-change-c070--> One limit is sampling error, since the liver is 50 000 to 65 000 times larger than even a good core; cores of at least 1.5 cm, or a second core, lower the risk.<!--c:fatty-change-c071-->
- **H&E:** on routine H&E sections the vacuoles look empty, because lipid is dissolved during tissue processing.<!--c:fatty-change-c031--> The report names the pattern (macrovesicular, microvesicular or mixed) and grades the share of hepatocytes with fat: NAFLD requires fat in more than 5% of them, and 5–33% is mild, 34–66% moderate, more than 66% severe.<!--c:fatty-change-c072--> In adults the fat of NAFLD usually starts around the central vein (acinar zone 3), whereas in most children it does not.<!--c:fatty-change-c073-->
- **Fat stains:** fat is shown on **frozen sections** with lipid stains such as Sudan red, which colors it red (see [[g:fat-stains]]).<!--c:fatty-change-c032--> **Oil red O** marks neutral lipid red.<!--c:fatty-change-c033--> It matters most when the droplets are tiny: in pure microvesicular steatosis, oil red O may be what proves that the finely vacuolated cytoplasm holds fat.<!--c:fatty-change-c074-->
- **PAS and PAS-D:** PAS identifies glycogen, which helps separate glycogen-laden cells from fat-laden ones.<!--c:fatty-change-c034--> Diastase digests glycogen, so material that loses its PAS staining on the diastase-treated slide (PAS-D) is glycogen.<!--c:fatty-change-c075--> The two stores can coexist, as in type I glycogen storage disease (von Gierke disease).<!--c:fatty-change-c076-->
- **Trichrome:** collagen stains blue; in steatohepatitis it usually appears first around the central venule and between single hepatocytes, in a "chicken-wire" pattern.<!--c:fatty-change-c077-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** ballooned hepatocytes, the injured cells that mark steatohepatitis, are recognized inconsistently on H&E by different pathologists; keratin 8/18 immunostaining may help to pick them out, because these cells have lost their normal keratins.<!--c:fatty-change-c078-->
- **Skeletal muscle:** when weakness or exercise intolerance suggests a lipid myopathy, the muscle biopsy is divided: a piece fixed in glutaraldehyde is kept in case electron microscopy is needed, and an unfixed piece is frozen for cryostat sections.<!--c:fatty-change-c079--> On frozen H&E the fibers are riddled with vacuoles, and oil red O turns the same vacuoles red, proof that they hold neutral fat.<!--c:fatty-change-c080-->
- **Molecular tests:** in some cases of acute fatty liver of pregnancy, DNA analysis finds the fetal defect: the baby carries an LCHAD mutation, such as Glu474Gln, that exposes it to a fatty liver and to hypoketotic hypoglycemia.<!--c:fatty-change-c081-->

## Clinical presentation

- **Liver:** uncomplicated alcoholic fatty liver is clinically quiet or nearly so, however marked the histology.<!--c:fatty-change-c035--> In nonalcoholic fatty liver disease many patients remain asymptomatic, with only moderate rises in liver enzymes.<!--c:fatty-change-c036-->
- **Acute fatty liver of pregnancy:** abdominal pain, jaundice and anorexia, almost always in the third trimester.<!--c:fatty-change-c037-->
- **Reye syndrome:** an uncommon disease of children that typically follows a viral fever (influenza, chickenpox) and combines microvesicular fat with liver failure and encephalopathy.<!--c:fatty-change-c038--> Aspirin given during that illness is the classic association, although aspirin toxicity alone is now known not to explain the syndrome.<!--c:fatty-change-c069-->
- **Heart and skeletal muscle:** in carnitine deficiency, fatty acids cannot enter mitochondria and lipid droplets pile up in muscle fibers.<!--c:fatty-change-c039--> The systemic form can also involve the heart and liver.<!--c:fatty-change-c040--> In diphtheria the toxin can disturb the heart: the myocardium may then show focal necrosis and, in its myocytes, fat droplets.<!--c:fatty-change-c082-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

Morphologically, steatosis is recognized by lipid droplets in the hepatocyte cytoplasm; on biopsy, what matters beyond the fat is whether steatohepatitis or fibrosis is present.<!--c:fatty-change-c041--> Because drugs can reproduce almost any pattern of liver injury, a full drug and supplement history is essential.<!--c:fatty-change-c042--> A trichrome stain shows any pericellular fibrosis that accompanies steatohepatitis.<!--c:fatty-change-c043-->

## Complications and prognosis

- **Fat storage is reversible**, and there is no evidence that fat itself impairs cell function.<!--c:fatty-change-c044-->
- Alcoholic fatty liver clears quickly and completely once drinking stops, and on its own it does not progress.<!--c:fatty-change-c045-->
- Alcoholic hepatitis usually develops on a fatty liver, but fat itself has not been shown to cause it.<!--c:fatty-change-c046-->
- Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease spans simple steatosis, steatohepatitis (NASH), bridging fibrosis and cirrhosis.<!--c:fatty-change-c047--> Once cirrhosis sets in the fat often disappears, so NAFLD probably explains many cases of "cryptogenic" cirrhosis.<!--c:fatty-change-c048-->
- Large droplets are harmless in themselves, so what counts is the disease behind them; small droplets point to mitochondrial failure and may come with life-threatening liver disease.<!--c:fatty-change-c049-->
- The fatty liver of kwashiorkor reverts to normal and does not lead to chronic liver disease.<!--c:fatty-change-c050-->

## High-yield summary

- Steatosis = **triglycerides** stored in the cytoplasm of parenchymal cells, mainly **hepatocytes**.<!--c:fatty-change-c056-->
- Three failure points: **more fatty acids in**, **less β-oxidation**, **less VLDL export**.<!--c:fatty-change-c057-->
- **Macrovesicular**: one large vacuole, nucleus pushed to the **periphery**; alcohol, metabolic syndrome, steroids, methotrexate.<!--c:fatty-change-c058-->
- **Microvesicular**: many small vacuoles, **central nucleus**, marker of **mitochondrial** injury; acute fatty liver of pregnancy, Reye syndrome.<!--c:fatty-change-c059-->
- Empty vacuoles on H&E; fat proven by **oil red O** or **Sudan** on **frozen sections**.<!--c:fatty-change-c060-->
- Fat storage is **reversible**; stop the cause and the liver clears.<!--c:fatty-change-c061-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

- **Steatosis is not fat necrosis.** Steatosis is fat inside living cells; fat necrosis is death of adipose tissue with soap formation.<!--c:fatty-change-c062-->
- Smaller droplets do not mean milder disease: microvesicular steatosis is the dangerous pattern.<!--c:fatty-change-c063-->
- Stored fat is not the culprit in alcoholic liver disease: ethanol causes the cell injury, and fat has not been shown to cause alcoholic hepatitis.<!--c:fatty-change-c064-->
- A cirrhotic liver without fat does not exclude NAFLD as the cause, because steatosis often vanishes at that stage.<!--c:fatty-change-c065-->
- A central nucleus does not rule out fat: microvesicular steatosis keeps the nucleus central, like hydropic swelling.<!--c:fatty-change-c066-->

## Mnemonics

**The three Fs of a fatty cell** (original): **F**uel floods in (more fatty acids), **F**ire goes out (β-oxidation fails), **F**reight gets stuck (no apoproteins or VLDL to ship it out).<!--c:fatty-change-c067-->

**Micro-M** (original): **M**icrovesicular = **M**itochondria hit, nucleus in the **M**iddle, potentially **M**ortal.<!--c:fatty-change-c068-->
