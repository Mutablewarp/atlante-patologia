## Definition and epidemiology

**What is this disease?** It is fatty liver disease that is not caused by alcohol, and its liver injury looks much like that of alcoholic liver disease.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c001-->
At the mild end of the spectrum is fat alone; at the severe end are bridging fibrosis and cirrhosis, and hepatitis may or may not be present.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c002-->
Fat plus hepatitis is called NASH (nonalcoholic steatohepatitis).<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c003-->
In the liver, the metabolic syndrome shows up as this disease.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c004-->

<aside class="edition">The reference text used for this card calls the disease NAFLD.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c005--></aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who is at risk?** Type 2 diabetes and obesity raise the risk, as do abnormal lipids and the metabolic syndrome.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c006-->
Insulin resistance seems to weigh most.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c007-->
Cirrhosis develops in up to one fifth of the people with NASH, who make up about half of those with severe obesity plus diabetes.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**What goes with insulin resistance in the liver?** Mitochondria oxidize more fatty acids, and oxidative stress rises, lipid peroxidation included.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c009-->
Alcoholic hepatitis may act through some of the same mechanisms.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c010-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Inflammation sits in both the lobules and the portal tracts, next to fat-laden hepatocytes.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c011-->
Scarring and Mallory–Denk hyaline accompany the loss of some hepatocytes.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c012-->
Fibrosis is common around the central veins, a pattern shared with alcoholic disease.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c013-->
Steatosis alone means fat without hepatitis; steatohepatitis adds inflammation on top of the fat.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c014-->
Once cirrhosis forms, the fat often disappears.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c015-->

## Clinical presentation

**Does the patient notice anything?** Often not: many patients have no symptoms, and progression toward cirrhosis tends to be insidious.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c016-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What do the blood tests show?** Liver enzymes in the serum show just a moderate elevation.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c017-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Where can it lead, and can it be treated?** It may end in cirrhosis, and many cirrhoses labeled cryptogenic probably stem from it.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c018-->
Weight loss, also when achieved surgically, usually improves it.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c019-->
The reference states that no definitive treatment was available.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c020-->

## High-yield summary

- The name **NAFLD** marks a mimic of **alcoholic liver disease** in which alcohol is not the cause.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c024-->
- **Steatosis** is fat alone; **steatohepatitis (NASH)** adds hepatitis.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c025-->
- **Insulin resistance** is the strongest risk factor, within the **metabolic syndrome**.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c026-->
- The histology overlaps with **alcoholic liver disease**.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c027-->
- **Cryptogenic cirrhosis** is probably caused by NAFLD in many cases, since fat often disappears in cirrhosis.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c028-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does "nonalcoholic" mean the fat is harmless?** No. Fat plus hepatitis can progress to bridging fibrosis and cirrhosis.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c029-->

**Can the pathologist separate it from alcoholic liver disease on the slide?** Not easily: the two share many histologic features.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c030-->

**Does a cirrhotic liver without fat exclude it?** No. Steatosis often vanishes by the time cirrhosis is established.<!--c:nonalcoholic-fatty-liver-disease-c031-->
