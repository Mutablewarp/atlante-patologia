## Definition and epidemiology

**What is eosinophilic esophagitis?** This esophageal disease is immune-mediated and often affects people with allergies or asthma.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c001--> Males are affected more often, and onset is possible at any age.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers it, and who is at risk?** Foods and environmental allergens are probably important triggers, acting through allergy.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c003--> Many patients, or their close relatives, have atopy, an inherited tendency to allergic disease.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c004-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the damage arise?** The mechanism is not completely understood.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c005-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the endoscopist see?** Trachealization or felinization is the name given to concentric rings of mucosa.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c007--> Besides rings, endoscopy may show lengthwise furrows and narrowed segments.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c008--> In some patients the esophagus looks normal.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c009-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Samples from several heights of the esophagus are needed, because the lesions are distributed unevenly.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c010--> Fluid accumulates between the epithelial cells, and both the basal layer and the papillae are overgrown.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c011--> Counting eosinophils inside the epithelium, the key threshold is 15 or more per high-power field.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c012--> Near the surface they form layers, and clusters of them make small microabscesses.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c013--> Their granules are visibly released.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c014-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the specimen is an endoscopic biopsy, taken at multiple levels.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c015-->
- Eosinophils inside the epithelium are counted per high-power field.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c016-->
- The reference text names no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test for this disease.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Food getting stuck, or trouble with solids, is the usual adult complaint.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c017--> Young children may be brought in because of feeding difficulty or poor growth.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c018--> Some patients blame specific foods for food catching as they swallow.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c019--> Many patients are first suspected when the usual reflux therapy does not help.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c020--> The blood eosinophil count is sometimes mildly high.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c021-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** The clinician and the pathologist must interpret the findings together.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c022--> A PPI trial of some weeks, or pH monitoring, can be used to exclude reflux.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c023--> The pathologist reviews biopsies taken at different heights.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c024-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Can it be treated?** Many patients go into remission when the culprit food is removed from their diet.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c025--> Swallowed corticosteroids are one drug option, along with other immune-modulating drugs.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c026--> The reference text gives no long-term outcome data.

## Differential diagnosis

**What else causes esophagitis with a similar look or biopsy?**

## High-yield summary

- **Atopy**, an allergic tendency, is common in patients and their families.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c034-->
- The diagnostic threshold is **at least 15 intraepithelial eosinophils per high-power field**.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c035-->
- Endoscopy: **concentric rings** (trachealization) and **vertical furrows**.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c036-->
- Versus reflux: eosinophils sit **proximal or mid** and **do not respond to antireflux therapy**.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c037-->
- **Food impaction** or **dysphagia for solids** is the classic adult complaint.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c038-->
- Treatment: **food elimination** or **swallowed corticosteroids**.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c039-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a normal endoscopy exclude it?** No. Some patients have a normal-looking esophagus, so biopsies are still needed.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c040-->

**Do eosinophils in the biopsy prove it?** No. Reflux can also raise eosinophils; the difference is that they are usually distal and subside with antireflux therapy.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c041-->

**Is it a disease of adults only?** No. It can occur at any age, and children may show feeding difficulties or failure to thrive.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c042-->

**Are white plaques always Candida?** No. Small white plaques or exudates can occur in eosinophilic esophagitis.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c043-->

## Mnemonics

**FEED:** Furrows and rings, Eosinophils (15 or more per high-power field), Elimination diet, Dysphagia.<!--c:eosinophilic-esophagitis-c044-->
