## Definition and epidemiology

**What are acute gastritis and reactive gastropathy?** Acute gastritis is a short episode of injury to the stomach lining, occurring when damaging agents briefly overpower the mucosal defenses.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c001--> Irritants, usually NSAIDs, make the gastric mucosa overgrow in reactive gastropathy.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c002--> Reactive gastropathy occurs in as many as 40% of people who take NSAIDs chronically.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What injures the gastric mucosa?**
- The lining is protected by tight junctions, mucus and blood flow.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c004-->
- Ischemia from cocaine, low blood pressure or low blood volume can injure the mucosa.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c005-->
- NSAIDs block production of prostaglandins, which support the mucus layer.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c006-->
- Alcohol, acids and alkalis injure the mucosa on direct contact.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c007-->
- Heavy drinking, radiation and bile reflux also cause reactive gastropathy.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the mucosa break down?** Gastric ulcers may develop in the course of acute gastritis, which reflects an imbalance between defenses and injury that is relatively brief.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c009--> In reactive gastropathy, foveolar cells proliferate in response to the injurious agents.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c010-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the stomach look like?**
- Acute gastritis may produce gastric ulcers that are multiple and, typically, shallow.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c011-->
- The gastric body, whose mucosa produces acid, is where they are found.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c012-->
- Acute gastritis is hemorrhagic in most cases.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c013-->
- The body of the pictured stomach has shallow erosions or ulcers, one of them bleeding.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the pathologist see on H&E?**
- Erosive acute gastritis can show prominent fibrosis and inflammation.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c015-->
- Irregular, corkscrew-shaped pits are what foveolar hyperplasia leaves behind.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c016-->
- The lamina propria may also contain proliferating smooth muscle.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c017-->
- Inflammation is rare in reactive gastropathy; it is seen mainly around an ulcer or erosion.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c018-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

Not applicable.

## Clinical presentation

**How dangerous is it?** Acute gastritis can threaten life.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c019--> Patients at risk may be given preventive treatment.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c020-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Does endoscopy settle the diagnosis?** For esophagitis and colitis endoscopy is more accurate than it is for gastritis.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c021-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How is reactive gastropathy treated?** The best management is to remove the cause.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c022-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Acute hemorrhagic or erosive gastritis | Usually hemorrhagic; the erosive type shows prominent fibrosis and inflammation.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c023--> | Biopsy histology |
| Reactive gastropathy | Foveolar hyperplasia with little inflammation.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c024--> | Biopsy histology |
| Chronic gastritis | Inflammatory cells are more numerous in the lamina propria.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c025--> | Biopsy histology |

## High-yield summary

- **NSAIDs** are the most common cause of reactive gastropathy.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c029-->
- **Alcohol**, radiation and **bile reflux** are other causes of it.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c030-->
- **Foveolar hyperplasia** gives corkscrew pits.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c031-->
- Inflammation stays minimal in **reactive gastropathy**, except around an erosion or ulcer.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c032-->
- The ulcers of acute gastritis are shallow and multiple, and the gastritis is usually **hemorrhagic**.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c033-->
- Reactive gastropathy is treated by **removing its cause**.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c034-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is reactive gastropathy an inflammatory gastritis?** No. The mucosa responds by hyperplasia, and inflammation is uncommon except with an ulcer or erosion.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c035-->

**Are NSAIDs the only cause?** No. Excess alcohol, radiation and bile reflux can cause it too.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c036-->

**Is acute gastritis always mild?** No. It can be life-threatening.<!--c:acute-gastritis-and-reactive-gastropathy-c037-->
