## Definition and epidemiology

**What are sinonasal tumors?** They arise in the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses and range from benign papillomas to aggressive cancers and lymphomas.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c001-->
Squamous papilloma is the commonest benign nasal cavity tumor.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c002-->
Schneiderian papillomas grow from the sinonasal lining.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c004-->
Fungiform, oncocytic and inverted papillomas are the three types.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c005-->
Together they are under 5% of sinonasal tumors.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c006-->
Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), with or without keratin, makes up most malignant tumors.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c007-->
A third of the cancers start in the nasal cavity; over half start in the maxillary sinus.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which exposures cause sinonasal cancer?** Aromatic hydrocarbons, chromium and nickel are chemicals that can cause it.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c009-->
Work in leather and textiles, with cutting oils, or in furniture woodworking raises risk, yet no single chemical has been pinned down.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c010-->
Men over 50 are affected far more often, reflecting this occupational exposure.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c011-->
The significance of HPV 6 and 11, found in inverted papillomas, is uncertain.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c012-->
EBV is linked to NK/T-cell lymphoma.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c013-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these tumors damage the nose?** Olfactory neuroblastoma derives from the neural crest.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c014-->
An inverted papilloma can erode bone though it is benign.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c015-->
Tissue next to an NK/T lymphoma dies of ischemia because tumor cells invade small and medium vessels and often occlude them.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**Where does each tumor sit?** Inverted papilloma can extend into the sinuses from its usual site, the lateral nasal wall.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c018-->
The olfactory mucosa of the superior turbinate, cribriform plate and upper septum gives rise to olfactory neuroblastoma.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c019-->
NK/T lymphoma ulcerates the nasal lining late, after it has swollen and stiffened.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c021-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does each tumor look like on H&E?** Inverted papilloma gets its name because the surface lining folds down into the tissue beneath it.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c023-->
Olfactory neuroblastoma cells have scant cytoplasm and round nuclei, giving a small round cell tumor.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c026-->
Rosettes in this tumor are of two kinds: Flexner–Wintersteiner true rosettes and Homer Wright pseudorosettes.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c027-->
The lymphoid infiltrate of NK/T lymphoma clusters around vessels, is polymorphic and causes necrosis.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c028-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** in a small round cell tumor of the upper nose, H&E shows cells in a pink fibrillary background, sometimes forming rosettes.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c029-->
- Cytokeratin and EMA are absent in olfactory neuroblastoma, whereas synaptophysin and NSE are expressed.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c030-->
- <aside class="edition">Since 2017, the WHO has graded olfactory neuroblastoma in four tiers.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c033--></aside>

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** Facial deformity may result when a maxillary sinus tumor grows into surrounding tissue.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c034-->
In nickel workers, SCC usually starts in the middle turbinate.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c036-->
The latency after nickel exposure is 2 to over 30 years.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c037-->
Adenocarcinoma, mainly in the maxillary and ethmoid sinuses, is what most other occupational exposures produce.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c038-->
NK/T lymphoma often starts quietly and looks like an ordinary nasal or sinus inflammation.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c042-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which imaging helps?** The tumor starts at the cribriform plate; with contrast on T1 MRI it is bright and can fill the nose.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c045-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How do these tumors behave?** Squamous and glandular sinonasal carcinomas usually spread by local invasion of nearby structures, not by metastasis.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c047-->
Patients with these carcinomas usually live only a few years.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c048-->
An inverted papilloma may come back unless the surgeon removes more than the visible lesion.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c049-->
Malignant change to SCC occurs in about 5% of them.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c050-->
Olfactory neuroblastoma often seeds lymph nodes, near and far, along lymphatic channels.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c051-->
Its outcome usually follows tumor grade.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c053-->
Five-year survival is about 85% after complete craniofacial resection plus chemotherapy or radiation.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c054-->
NK/T lymphoma usually kills by bleeding from eroded vessels or by infection, aspiration pneumonia included.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c056-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Olfactory neuroblastoma | Upper nasal mass of small round cells with rosettes.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c058--> | Synaptophysin positive, cytokeratin negative.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c059--> |
| Sinonasal SCC | Keratinizing or nonkeratinizing, often maxillary.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c060--> | Biopsy histology |
| NK/T-cell lymphoma | Angiocentric necrotizing infiltrate, EBV-linked.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c061--> | Biopsy histology |
| [[anca-associated-vasculitis\|Granulomatosis with polyangiitis]] | No well-formed granulomas; vasculitis with giant cells and microabscesses instead.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c064--> | Serum ANCA and proteinase 3.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c080--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Inverted papilloma** becomes SCC in about 5% of cases.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c066-->
- **Nickel** leads to SCC of the middle turbinate.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c067-->
- **Adenocarcinoma** of the maxillary and ethmoid sinuses follows most other occupational exposures.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c068-->
- **Olfactory neuroblastoma** is neural crest, **synaptophysin** positive, cytokeratin negative.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c069-->
- **Homer Wright** and Flexner–Wintersteiner rosettes mark it.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c070-->
- **NK/T-cell lymphoma** is angiocentric, EBV-linked and ischemic.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c071-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is an inverted papilloma harmless?** No. About 5% turn into SCC.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c074-->

**Do all sinonasal cancers spare lymph nodes?** No. Olfactory neuroblastoma often spreads to lymph nodes along lymphatic channels.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c076-->

**Is the old "lethal midline granuloma" a granulomatous disease?** No. It is a malignant lymphoma.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c077-->

**Is olfactory neuroblastoma a carcinoma?** No. It is a neural crest tumor without cytokeratin.<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c078-->

## Mnemonics

**Nickel in the nose, squamous it goes; other trades, adeno it grows.**<!--c:sinonasal-tumors-c079-->
