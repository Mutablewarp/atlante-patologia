## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a Wilms tumor?** It is a malignant embryonal kidney tumor built from blastema, stroma and epithelium.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c001--> It is the most common solid abdominal tumor of childhood.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c002--> Peak age is 1 to 3 years, and 98% of cases appear before the tenth birthday.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which syndromes and genes raise the risk?** Sporadic, unilateral disease accounts for 90% of cases.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c004--> Three congenital syndromes account for another 5%; they bring tumors early, often in both kidneys.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c005-->

- **WAGR syndrome** (Wilms tumor, Aniridia, Genitourinary anomalies, intellectual disability): deletion of 11p13 takes out PAX6, the aniridia gene, and WT1, the Wilms tumor gene.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c006-->
- **Denys–Drash syndrome:** glomerular mesangial sclerosis plus disordered sexual development.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c007-->
- **Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome:** macroglossia, enlarged organs and overgrowth that can be limited to one side.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c008-->

Even without a syndrome, familial tumors (about 6%) start early and are bilateral.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c009--> Gonadoblastoma, not Wilms tumor, is the tumor of Frasier syndrome, even though WT1 is mutated there too.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the tumor arise?** WT1 curbs tumors by setting which other genes are transcribed.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c011--> As in retinoblastoma, WAGR tumors need two hits: a germline WT1 deletion, then loss of the second allele.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c012--> Denys–Drash syndrome is more severe than WAGR, possibly because its WT1 mutations are dominant negative.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c013--> Only 10% to 20% of sporadic tumors carry WT1 mutations, so other genes contribute.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c014--> The Beckwith–Wiedemann locus at 11p15.5 shows loss of heterozygosity or imprinting in about 70% of tumors.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c015--> In sporadic tumors the lost allele is always the maternal one.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c016--> More IGF-II, a paternally expressed gene of this region, may favor tumor growth.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c017--> WTX may curb Wnt signaling by helping destroy β-catenin; CTNNB1 gain-of-function mutations make it persist.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c018--> Nephrogenic rests, leftover immature blastema, occur in all syndromic cases and may be clonal forerunners.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c019-->

## Gross morphology

**What does it look like on the cut surface?** A single large mass is typical; only a thin shell of cortex and capsule surrounds its pale tan, bulging cut face.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c020-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the triphasic pattern?** It looks like developing kidney: metanephric blastema plus immature stroma and epithelium.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c021--> Some tumors have only two components or one; most have all three in varying proportions.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c022-->

- **Blastema:** nests and trabeculae of small oval cells with little cytoplasm.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c023-->
- **Epithelium:** small tubules, a few mimicking immature glomeruli.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c024-->
- **Stroma:** mainly spindle-shaped cells that have not matured.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c025--> Heterologous elements most often take the form of skeletal muscle.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c026-->
- **Anaplasia:** atypical mitoses and large, hyperchromatic nuclei.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c027-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- On routine H&E, look for the three components and for anaplastic nuclei.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c028-->
- WT1 mutations are present in every tumor associated with WAGR.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c029-->
- The reference pages describe no special stains, IHC or EM.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a child with Wilms tumor present?** The abdominal mass is usually what draws attention.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c030--> Hypertension and hematuria may be present.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c031--> Pain, bowel obstruction or traumatic rupture can also occur.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c032--> Familial cases are bilateral in 20%, sporadic ones in only 5%.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c033--> Inheritance in familial cases is usually autosomal dominant.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c034-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it found?** An abdominal mass in a child usually leads to the diagnosis.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c035--> The reference pages name no specific laboratory or imaging test.

## Complications and prognosis

**What predicts the outcome?** Under 2 years of age, outcome tends to be better.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c036--> Spread beyond the renal capsule at surgery worsens the prognosis.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c037--> Worse outcomes go with anaplasia, commoner in older patients.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c038--> About 90% of patients survive long term with resection plus chemotherapy and radiation.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c039-->

## High-yield summary

- **Triphasic** tumor: **blastema**, **stroma** and **epithelium**.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c042-->
- **WAGR** is an **11p13** deletion that removes **WT1** and **PAX6**.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c043-->
- **Denys–Drash** carries a dominant-negative **WT1** mutation.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c044-->
- **Beckwith–Wiedemann** shares the **11p15.5** locus, where sporadic tumors lose the maternal allele.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c045-->
- **Nephrogenic rests** are possible precursor lesions.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c046-->
- **Anaplasia** and spread beyond the **renal capsule** predict a worse outcome.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c047-->
- Peak age is **1 to 3 years**.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c048-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does every Wilms tumor show all three components?** No. Most do, but some have only two or one.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c049-->

**Do all Wilms tumors carry a WT1 mutation?** No. Only 10% to 20% of sporadic tumors do.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c050-->

**Does a WT1 mutation always cause Wilms tumor?** No. In Frasier syndrome it leads to gonadoblastoma.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c051-->

**Is a young age a bad sign?** No. Under 2 years the outlook tends to be better, while anaplasia is commoner in older patients.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c052-->

## Mnemonics

**BSE:** **B**lastema, **S**troma, **E**pithelium, the three parts to look for.<!--c:wilms-tumor-c053-->
