## Definition and epidemiology

**What is chronic bronchitis?** It is diagnosed from the history: for two or more years the patient has coughed up sputum, with no other cause found.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c001--> Together with emphysema, it makes up chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c002--> Airway narrowing is the mechanism of obstruction in chronic bronchitis.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it?** Cigarette smoke is the leading cause: about 90% of patients smoke.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c005--> Smoking has a much larger effect than pollution, though living in a polluted area or breathing toxic industrial fumes at work also raises the risk.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c007--> Infection may help start the disease and speed its progression, and patients also suffer respiratory infections more often and more severely.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does smoke damage the bronchi?** The exact mechanism is still unclear.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c008--> Rodent bronchi develop squamous metaplasia when exposed to proteases, a change antiprotease pretreatment prevents; instilled acid, SO2 and smoke have the same effect.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c009--> Autonomic stimulation may contribute, since adrenergic and cholinergic agonists also induce metaplasia in rodents.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c010--> Mucus cells multiply and swell and make up a larger share than serous cells, so the glands grow.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c011-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the airway look like?** Both the proximal and distal airways can hold too much mucus.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c012--> Several glands open into one widened duct, and each such duct shows as a small pit on the mucosal surface.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c013--> The lumen is narrowed because the wall is thickened by larger glands and by edema.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes on the slide?** Both the submucosal glands and their individual acini are enlarged, so the bronchus holds more mucus-secreting tissue; this is the central lesion.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c015--> To quantify the glands, divide their thickness by the thickness of the full bronchial wall; the result is the Reid index.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c016--> In chronic bronchitis the Reid index is above 0.5, whereas values up to 0.4 are normal.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c017--> The surface epithelium has more goblet cells.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c018--> Smoke injures the lining and squamous metaplasia follows, probably apart from the other lesions.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c019--> Hyperreactive airways may be explained by more smooth muscle in the wall.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c020--> Scattered lymphocytes and a thickened basement membrane are also drawn in the wall.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c021-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E sections of bronchi, look for enlarged mucous glands, more goblet cells and squamous metaplasia.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c022-->
- Special stains, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy and molecular tests: the reference pages give no role for them.

## Clinical presentation

**What does the patient report?** Years of coughing up sputum.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c024--> Cough and sputum persist all year; early on they peaked in winter.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c025--> Later the patient becomes cyanotic and breathless on exertion, and cor pulmonale may develop.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c026--> "Blue bloater" names the patient whose cor pulmonale brings edema on top of cyanosis.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c027--> It is hard to tell how much of one patient's disease comes from chronic bronchitis and how much from emphysema, since the two frequently occur together.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c023-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it diagnosed?** Mild disease can have normal histology and the changes grade into one another, so the diagnosis rests on the clinical history.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c028--> On spirometry, patients with COPD have a lower forced expiratory volume.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c029-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong?** Advanced disease can tip into acute respiratory failure, with oxygen falling and carbon dioxide rising; infection, thromboembolism, left ventricular failure or heavy air pollution may trigger it.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c030--> Haemophilus influenzae and S. pneumoniae are the bacteria that most often infect the lungs of these patients, whose retained mucus predisposes them.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c031--> Patients must quit smoking; bronchodilators are used, chest infections get early antibiotics, and airway drainage is sometimes added.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c032-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What else causes chronic airflow obstruction?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[emphysema]] | It destroys the walls of the airspaces past the terminal bronchioles, leaving them enlarged but not scarred, and elastic recoil is lost.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c033--> | Lung morphology |
| [[asthma]] | Airway narrowing is shared with chronic bronchitis, but chronic bronchitis is defined by chronic productive cough.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c034--> | Clinical history |
| [[bronchiectasis]] | Some classifications place it among the obstructive diseases.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c035--> | Clinical history and imaging |

## High-yield summary

- **Chronic bronchitis** is a clinical diagnosis: at least two years of productive cough that nothing else explains.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c036-->
- **Cigarette smoking** is behind about 90% of cases.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c037-->
- **Mucous gland hypertrophy** raises the **Reid index** above 0.5; normal is 0.4 or less.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c038-->
- **Goblet cell hyperplasia** and **squamous metaplasia** are part of the picture.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c039-->
- Chronic bronchitis and **emphysema** together form **COPD** and often coexist.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c040-->
- In the **blue bloater**, cor pulmonale adds edema to cyanosis.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c041-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is chronic bronchitis a histologic diagnosis?** No. It is defined clinically, and mild cases may have normal histology.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c042-->

**Is the Reid index a measure of airspace size?** No. It measures the thickness of the mucous glands against the bronchial wall.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c043-->

**Do chronic bronchitis and emphysema block airflow the same way?** No. Chronic bronchitis narrows the airways; emphysema removes elastic recoil.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c044-->

## Mnemonics

**How to remember the core lesion?** COUGH: Chronic productive cough, Overgrown mucous glands (Reid index above 0.5), Under cigarette smoke, Goblet cells increased, Hypoxemia and cyanosis late.<!--c:chronic-bronchitis-c045-->
