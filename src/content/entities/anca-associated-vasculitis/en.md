## Definition and epidemiology

**Which diseases make up ANCA-associated vasculitis?** Three small-vessel vasculitides are tied to antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA): granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA).<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c001--> The upper airways, lungs and kidneys are the usual targets.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c002--> In the kidney, all three injure glomeruli and the small arteries feeding them.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c008-->

<aside class="edition">GPA was formerly called Wegener granulomatosis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c003--> EGPA was formerly called Churg–Strauss syndrome.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c004--></aside>

GPA mostly affects men, in the fifth and sixth decades of life.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c005--> MPA shows no sex preference and may occur at any age.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c006--> EGPA is a disease of young people with asthma.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers these diseases?** The cause of GPA is unknown, and so is the cause of EGPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c009--> GPA improves with immunosuppressants, so it is probably immune-mediated.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c010--> Autoantibodies such as ANCA and rheumatoid factor point to an autoimmune mechanism in EGPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c011--> GPA may involve bacteria: sulfa drugs curb its relapses.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do ANCAs damage vessels?** ANCAs mostly recognize myeloperoxidase (MPO) and proteinase 3 (PR3) in neutrophils and monocytes.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c013--> The two main immunofluorescence patterns are cytoplasmic (C-ANCA) and perinuclear (P-ANCA).<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c014--> ANCA-activated neutrophils stick to endothelium and kill it with granule contents and oxygen radicals.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c015--> The inflammation grows because these neutrophils also switch on kinins and the alternative complement pathway.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c016--> ANCA lesions show hardly any deposited antibody or complement, the pauci-immune pattern described in MPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c017-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lesions look like to the naked eye?** Most GPA lung specimens contain several nodules in both lungs.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c018--> Cut nodules often have a central cavity and a brownish or bloody surface.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c019-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Three lesions occur together in GPA: vasculitis, granulomas and tissue necrosis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c020--> Necrosis is either small neutrophil abscesses or a broad map-like area.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c021--> Macrophages rim areas of necrosis, and granulomas contain multinucleated giant cells.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c022--> The inflamed vessel can be a capillary, a vein or an artery.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c023--> Neutrophils often invade alveolar capillaries (capillaritis).<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c024--> Blood in the alveoli, recent or longstanding, is frequent.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c025--> In MPA, lung biopsy reveals capillaritis with neutrophils and alveolar bleeding.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c026--> In EGPA, eosinophils crowd around vessels in many organs, together with necrotizing granulomas.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c027--> Dead eosinophils pile up in the center of an EGPA necrotic focus, staining it pink.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c028--> Glomeruli in ANCA disease undergo necrosis and develop crescents.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c029--> Segments without necrosis can look almost normal, with a few neutrophils or slightly more endocapillary cells.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c030--> In GPA, crescents are a late glomerular change, preceded by focal necrosis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c031-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on H&E, GPA is recognized by vasculitis, granulomatous inflammation and necrosis together.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c032-->
- **Elastic stain:** it highlights arteries whose elastic laminae the inflammation has erased.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c033-->
- **Silver stain (kidney):** crescents sit inside Bowman space, and glomerular basement membranes are interrupted in places.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c034-->
- **Immune deposits (kidney):** ANCA glomerulonephritis has almost no immunoglobulin or complement in the glomeruli.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c035-->
- **Serology:** blood tests for ANCA point toward GPA or a similar vasculitis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c036-->
- Electron microscopy and molecular tests: the source for this card does not cover them.

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** In GPA, the eye, kidney and lung are affected less often than the head and neck.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c037--> In the head and neck, GPA can cause ear infection with hearing loss, and sinus and airway disease.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c038--> Lung disease in GPA can cause cough with hemoptysis, or pleuritis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c039--> Glomerular disease can end in renal failure, and hematuria with proteinuria is common.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c040--> GPA also causes fever and weight loss and can reach joints, skin and nerves.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c041--> Almost all MPA patients have glomerulonephritis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c042--> MPA is among the frequent causes of pulmonary-renal syndrome.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c043--> MPA may also involve the upper airways, the skin and the musculoskeletal system.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c044--> Nephritic signs and a fast decline in kidney function usually mark ANCA glomerulonephritis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c045--> Goodpasture syndrome causes pulmonary-renal syndrome far less often than ANCA vasculitis with lung involvement does.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c046-->

**What are the three phases of EGPA?**
- **Prodrome:** airway allergy (asthma, rhinitis) and/or eosinophil excess in blood or tissues.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c047-->
- **Vasculitic phase:** peripheral neuropathy or skin vasculitis shows that vessels outside the lung are inflamed.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c048-->
- **Postvasculitic phase:** heart disease such as pericarditis or heart failure is common, and asthma can persist.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c049-->

Compared with GPA, EGPA usually causes less severe kidney and sinus disease.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c050-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the diagnosis?** PR3-ANCA with a C-ANCA pattern is typical of GPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c051--> MPO-ANCA with a P-ANCA pattern is typical of MPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c052--> In the vasculitic phase of EGPA, P-ANCA is usually found.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c053--> A GPA chest radiograph can show a solitary nodule, but multiple nodules are more frequent.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c054--> On kidney biopsy, anti-GBM and immune-complex disease show deposits; ANCA disease has hardly any.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c055--> EGPA is diagnosed from vasculitis, eosinophilia and asthma together.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c056-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications and the outlook?** Sudden diffuse lung bleeding in GPA threatens life, causing respiratory failure and often kidney failure.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c057--> After remission, relapse remains a risk.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c058--> ANCA glomerulonephritis can recur in a kidney transplant.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c059-->

<aside class="edition">These figures come from an older edition. Corticosteroids plus cyclophosphamide control GPA in most patients.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c060--> Nearly 90% now survive five years.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c061--> Severe EGPA may need cyclophosphamide, but corticosteroids help most patients.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c062--> Of treated EGPA patients, 60% survive five years.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c063--> With immunosuppression, under 20% of ANCA glomerulonephritis patients reach end-stage renal disease within 5 years, against over 80% untreated.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c064--></aside>

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[tuberculosis]] | The cavitating lung nodules of GPA resemble tuberculous lesions.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c065--> | Histology of the lung lesion |
| [[polyarteritis-nodosa]] | EGPA can mimic it when vessels develop aneurysms with fibrinoid necrosis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c066--> | Asthma and eosinophilia point to EGPA |
| [[crescentic-glomerulonephritis]] | Anti-GBM and immune-complex forms show immune deposits; ANCA disease shows little or none.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c067--> | Deposit pattern on kidney biopsy |
| Other eosinophilic diseases | Drug-induced vasculitis and parasitic or fungal infection must be excluded in suspected EGPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c068--> | Exclusion of these causes |
| Necrotizing sarcoid granulomatosis | It usually stays within the lung and is not a systemic vasculitis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c069--> | Extent of disease |

## High-yield summary

- **GPA** is a necrotizing granulomatous vasculitis that hits the airways and the kidneys.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c070-->
- Nearly every **MPA** patient has glomerulonephritis alongside a **pauci-immune** small-vessel vasculitis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c071-->
- **EGPA** is the vasculitis that comes with eosinophilia and asthma.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c072-->
- **PR3 with C-ANCA** fits GPA; **MPO with P-ANCA** fits MPA and EGPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c073-->
- The kidney lesion is **crescentic glomerulonephritis** with segmental necrosis and little or no immune deposit.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c074-->
- **Naming:** GPA was Wegener granulomatosis; EGPA was Churg–Strauss syndrome.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c075-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does "pauci-immune" mean that the disease is not immune-mediated?** No. ANCAs themselves are thought to cause the disease.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c076--> In pauci-immune lesions like MPA and ANCA glomerulonephritis, deposits of antibody and complement are scanty or absent.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c077-->

**Is Goodpasture syndrome the main cause of lung-kidney disease?** No. Lung-involving ANCA vasculitis is far more common.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c078-->

**Are cavitating lung nodules always tuberculosis?** No. GPA produces similar lesions and must be distinguished from tuberculosis.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c079-->

**Is GPA only a lung disease?** No. It often spreads to the eyes and skin as well.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c080-->

## Mnemonics

- **Cats Purr Gently:** C-ANCA, PR3, GPA.<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c081-->
- **Pumas Mostly Meow:** P-ANCA, MPO, MPA (and EGPA).<!--c:anca-associated-vasculitis-c082-->
