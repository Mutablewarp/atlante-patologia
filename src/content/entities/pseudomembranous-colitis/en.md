## Definition and epidemiology

**What is pseudomembranous colitis?** Pseudomembranous colitis is any colitis in which exudate forms plaques on the lining of the colon.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c001--> *Clostridioides difficile* (*C. difficile*) causes most cases.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c002--> Typically, a course of antibiotics comes first.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who gets it, and why?**
- *C. difficile* infection is chiefly a complication of antibiotic therapy.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c004-->
- Nearly any antibiotic can set it off, but some are riskier than others.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c005-->
- Time spent in hospital adds a second major risk.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c006-->
- About 1% to 5% of adults carry the bacterium.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c007-->
- Carrier status develops in about 30% of hospitalized patients.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c008-->
- Among elderly inpatients, up to nearly 70% may be carriers.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c009-->
- People with inflammatory bowel disease or weak immunity are also at higher risk.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c010-->
- Transmission is fecal-oral, and the ingested organism may be a spore or a vegetative cell.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does an antibiotic lead to colitis?** Antibiotic exposure removes the normal gut flora, and with it the protection of the colon.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c012--> Because *C. difficile* withstands antibiotics better than the flora, it takes over and makes toxins A and B.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c013--> Toxin A is the inflammatory toxin: it mobilizes mediators of inflammation.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c014--> Toxin B damages cells directly.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c015--> Tissue damage comes from the toxins; the bacterium itself does not invade.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c016--> The lesion is believed to start as necrosis of the surface epithelium.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c017-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the colon look like?** Small-bowel disease is occasional; whole-colon disease is frequent.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c018--> The hallmark is plaques stuck to the mucosa: yellow, standing above the surface, none larger than 2 cm.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c019--> No ulcers are present; the mucosa between the plaques is only congested and edematous.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c020--> Big pseudomembranes form where plaques merge in severe disease.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c021-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is a pseudomembrane made of?** Neutrophils and fibrin are among its components.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c022--> Necrotic epithelial debris and mucin complete it.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c023--> The crypts swell with mucin and neutrophils and lose their normal organization.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c024--> Mild disease may show only scattered injury to the surface lining, with no true pseudomembrane.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- Specimen: the toxins of *C. difficile* are looked for in stool.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c026-->
- Under the microscope, look for the pseudomembrane and for crypts distended by mucin and neutrophils.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c027-->

## Clinical presentation

**What do patients complain of?** Watery diarrhea of modest severity is almost the rule when *C. difficile* follows antibiotics.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c028--> Colitis is not the usual outcome.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c029--> Severe diarrhea in pseudomembranous colitis comes with fever and cramps, and the white cell count rises.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c030--> Blood may be present in the diarrhea.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c031-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it diagnosed?** Most diagnoses come from detecting toxins in stool, using molecular tests, ELISA or a cytotoxin assay.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c032-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong, and how is it treated?** Fulminant colitis develops in some patients.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c033--> Death, toxic megacolon and colonic perforation are possible consequences.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c034--> Supportive fluids and electrolytes accompany metronidazole or vancomycin.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c035--> Promptly stopping the offending antibiotic is part of treatment.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c036--> Fulminant colitis may require colectomy.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c037--> One in five patients has a recurrence.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c038--> When the infection keeps coming back, a fecal transplant puts healthy flora back in the gut.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c039--> Lowering the incidence depends heavily on preventing transmission in hospitals.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c040-->

## High-yield summary

- **Antibiotics** and **hospitalization** are the main risk factors.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c044-->
- **Toxin A** calls in inflammatory mediators.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c045-->
- **Toxin B** harms cells directly.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c046-->
- Gross hallmark: yellowish **plaques** standing above the mucosa.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c047-->
- Diagnosis: **toxins in stool**.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c048-->
- Treatment: stop the inciting antibiotic and give **metronidazole or vancomycin**.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c049-->
- Complications: **perforation** and **toxic megacolon**.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c050-->
- Recurrence is common; multiple recurrences call for a **fecal transplant**.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c051-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does *C. difficile* invade the colon wall?** No. Toxins A and B mediate the damage; the organism is not invasive.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c052-->

**Does every *C. difficile* infection cause pseudomembranous colitis?** No. Usually the infection stops at watery diarrhea, without colitis.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c053-->

**Are pseudomembranes specific to *C. difficile*?** No. Enteric infections, notably verotoxin-producing *E. coli*, can produce them, and so can ischemic colitis.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c054-->

**Do only a few antibiotics trigger the disease?** No. Nearly all antibiotics are implicated, and the risk is higher for some.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c055-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the chain of events?** ABCD: Antibiotics wipe out the flora, Bacterial toxins do the harm, Colon plaques form, Diarrhea follows.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c056--> For the toxins: A Alerts the inflammation, B Breaks the cells.<!--c:pseudomembranous-colitis-c057-->
