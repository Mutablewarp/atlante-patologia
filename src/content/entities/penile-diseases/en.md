## Definition and epidemiology

**What is penile pathology?** Penile disease covers developmental anomalies, inflammatory lesions, Peyronie fibrosis and squamous tumors.<!--c:penile-diseases-c001--> In hypospadias the urethral meatus sits too far back, on the ventral surface.<!--c:penile-diseases-c002--> Epispadias is the dorsal counterpart: the urethra opens on the top of the penis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c003--> In phimosis a narrow foreskin opening prevents retraction over the glans.<!--c:penile-diseases-c004--> Uncircumcised men account for most penile cancers, which start in squamous mucosa and skin.<!--c:penile-diseases-c005-->

<aside class="edition">Penile precancer and carcinoma terms change between editions. This card follows the 8th-edition reference; check the current classification.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes these lesions?** The urethral folds do not fully close in hypospadias.<!--c:penile-diseases-c006--> Uncircumcised men can acquire phimosis after foreskin trauma or repeated infections.<!--c:penile-diseases-c007--> In diabetes or weak immunity fungi may be involved, yet bacteria cause most cases.<!--c:penile-diseases-c008-->

Sexually transmitted agents:
- Genital herpes: HSV-2, less often HSV-1.<!--c:penile-diseases-c009-->
- Primary syphilis: *Treponema pallidum*.<!--c:penile-diseases-c010-->
- Chancroid: *Haemophilus ducreyi*.<!--c:penile-diseases-c011-->
- Lymphogranuloma venereum: *Chlamydia trachomatis*.<!--c:penile-diseases-c012-->
- Condyloma acuminatum: HPV 6, less often 11.<!--c:penile-diseases-c013-->

Smegma is a suspected contributor to penile cancer, but no single cause has been established.<!--c:penile-diseases-c014--> Some penile cancers are probably linked to HPV 16 and 18.<!--c:penile-diseases-c015--> Smoking raises the risk.<!--c:penile-diseases-c016-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these lesions arise?** In Peyronie disease, the septum of the corpus cavernosum loses patches of muscle to collagen.<!--c:penile-diseases-c017--> In paraphimosis, venous blood cannot drain from the glans because a narrow foreskin, forced back, strangles it.<!--c:penile-diseases-c018--> Only a minority of Bowen disease and erythroplasia lesions become invasive.<!--c:penile-diseases-c021--> About half of invasive penile cancers contain HPV DNA.<!--c:penile-diseases-c022-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lesions look like?**
- Hypospadias: the opening typically sits at the corona or on the undersurface of the glans.<!--c:penile-diseases-c023-->
- Balanitis xerotica obliterans: the involved glans looks white and feels hard.<!--c:penile-diseases-c024-->
- Bulky condylomas look like cauliflowers and can be mistaken for verrucous carcinoma; smaller ones are flat shaft warts.<!--c:penile-diseases-c025-->
- Peyronie disease: the overlying skin looks normal, but a poorly delimited firm zone can be felt in the shaft.<!--c:penile-diseases-c026-->
- Bowen disease: single shaft plaque, red or gray-white, with sharp edges.<!--c:penile-diseases-c027-->
- Erythroplasia of Queyrat: red plaques, single or multiple, on glans and foreskin; they gleam and feel soft.<!--c:penile-diseases-c028-->
- Invasive carcinoma usually involves the glans or foreskin, often as an ulcer or an exophytic papillary tumor.<!--c:penile-diseases-c030-->
- Verrucous carcinoma: a condyloma-like exophytic growth.<!--c:penile-diseases-c031-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the microscope show?**
- Condyloma: papillomatosis, acanthosis, hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c032-->
- Carcinoma in situ: the dermis is intact, while atypical keratinocytes occupy the whole thickness of the epidermis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c035-->
- Invasive squamous carcinomas are typically well differentiated, with keratin formed in foci.<!--c:penile-diseases-c037-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

**How it is studied:**
- On histology, absence of invasion into the dermis defines carcinoma in situ.<!--c:penile-diseases-c038-->
- In bowenoid papulosis, HPV 16 is the type most often found.<!--c:penile-diseases-c040-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?**
- Peyronie disease: erections are painful and the penis bends.<!--c:penile-diseases-c041-->
- A penile herpes lesion goes from a cluster of vesicles to an ulcer, then a crust.<!--c:penile-diseases-c042-->
- Primary syphilis: palpable groin nodes may accompany a chancre, a single soft ulcer.<!--c:penile-diseases-c043-->
- Chancroid ulcers are shallow, and the groin nodes that go with them hurt and discharge pus.<!--c:penile-diseases-c044-->
- When first seen, most penile squamous carcinomas have not left the penis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c046-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings guide diagnosis?** Enlarged inguinal nodes do not prove metastasis; many are only reactive.<!--c:penile-diseases-c047-->
## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** Circumcision cures phimosis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c049--> Clinical stage is the main predictor of survival in penile cancer; histologic grade matters less.<!--c:penile-diseases-c050--> Superficially invasive cancer has a good survival.<!--c:penile-diseases-c051--> Inguinal node metastases lower it considerably.<!--c:penile-diseases-c052--> Amputation is usually needed.<!--c:penile-diseases-c053--> Verrucous carcinoma rarely metastasizes.<!--c:penile-diseases-c054--> Bowenoid papulosis almost always regresses.<!--c:penile-diseases-c055--> Most Peyronie disease is mild.<!--c:penile-diseases-c056-->

## Differential diagnosis

**How do the look-alikes differ?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[human-papillomavirus-infection\|Condyloma acuminatum]] | Wart-like and non-invasive.<!--c:penile-diseases-c057--> | Histology |
| Verrucous carcinoma | Invades locally despite cytologically benign cells and a condyloma-like look.<!--c:penile-diseases-c058--> | Histology |
| [[carcinoma-in-situ\|Bowen disease]] | A single plaque on the shaft, with atypical keratinocytes through the whole epidermis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c059--> | Histology |
| Bowenoid papulosis | Several papules in young, sexually active men.<!--c:penile-diseases-c060--> | HPV detection |
| [[syphilis\|Primary syphilis]] | A single soft ulcer, not grouped vesicles.<!--c:penile-diseases-c061--> | Identify the agent |

## High-yield summary

- **Hypospadias** is ventral and **epispadias** dorsal.<!--c:penile-diseases-c063-->
- **Bowen disease** and **erythroplasia of Queyrat** are carcinoma in situ of the penis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c065-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does HPV on the penis always mean cancer?** No. Condyloma is benign, and bowenoid papulosis regresses.<!--c:penile-diseases-c066-->

**Are enlarged groin nodes always metastases?** No. Many are only reactive.<!--c:penile-diseases-c067-->

**Is verrucous carcinoma just a large condyloma?** No. It invades locally.<!--c:penile-diseases-c068-->

**Is Peyronie disease an infection?** No. It is an idiopathic fibrosis.<!--c:penile-diseases-c069-->
