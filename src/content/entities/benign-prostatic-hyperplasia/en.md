## Definition and epidemiology
**What is benign prostatic hyperplasia?** BPH (nodular hyperplasia of the prostate) is a non-cancerous enlargement of the gland: both its glands and its stroma grow.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c001--> The patient notices it as obstructed urine outflow.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c002--> Asia has the lowest frequency; Western Europe and the United States have the highest.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c003--> Among American men, it is more frequent in Black than in white men.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c004--> Men in their sixties are the most often symptomatic.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c005--> Autopsy finds it far more often than symptoms do.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c006--> Beyond age 80, three men in four (75%) show some hyperplasia.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c007--> BPH in a man under 40 is rare.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors
**What does the prostate need in order to grow?** Androgens. Without testosterone the prostate neither develops nor keeps its secretory function.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c009--> 5α-reductase is the enzyme that makes dihydrotestosterone (DHT), the active form of the androgen.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c010--> Prepubertal boys and castrated men do not get BPH.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c011--> If castration happens before puberty, age-related BPH never develops.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c012-->

## Pathogenesis
**How do androgens drive the overgrowth?** Glandular and stromal cells both proliferate because DHT binds to nuclear receptors in them.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c013--> The event that starts BPH has not been identified.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c014--> A man with BPH can end up with a smaller prostate if he takes finasteride or dutasteride, which inhibit 5α-reductase.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c015--> Giving testosterone does not produce hyperplasia.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c016--> Blood testosterone drops with aging whether or not a man has BPH.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c017--> In BPH, blood testosterone relative to DHT can be low, while serum DHT stays the same as in other men.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c018-->

## Gross morphology
**Where does it start, and what does the cut prostate show?** The first nodules appear in the transitional zone, in the submucosa around the proximal urethra.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c019--> The urethra sits in the middle and the normal gland around it; both are squeezed as the nodules grow.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c020--> A thin strip of normal prostate under the capsule is all that remains once BPH is advanced.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c021--> A fibrous pseudocapsule wraps each of the many nodules, and the urethra is left as a narrow slit.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c022--> The name "surgical capsule" is given to the fibrotic rim of compressed peripheral gland.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c023--> Hemorrhage or infarction can affect part of a larger nodule.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c024--> Small stones can also be present.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c025-->

## Microscopic morphology
**What is inside the nodules?** The growth involves glandular epithelium and a stroma of smooth muscle and fibroblasts, and their proportions vary.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c026--> Acini of unequal size lie in disorder within the stroma, the usual fibromyoadenomatous pattern.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c027--> Papillary hyperplasia is frequent, and the acinar lining is a basal layer capped by tall columnar cells.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c028--> Corpora amylacea, pink-staining concretions built in layers, lie inside acini, often beside chronic inflammatory cells.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c029--> Expanding nodules compress the glands of the uninvolved periphery, and these are frequently atrophic.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c030--> BPH commonly comes with nonspecific prostatitis: chronic inflammatory cells pack the glands and their surroundings, often with acute cells and focal gland loss.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c031--> One case in five (20%) has focal infarcts of different ages.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c032--> Ducts next to an infarct typically develop squamous metaplasia in their lining.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c033-->

## Clinical presentation
**Why does the patient have trouble urinating?** Bladder outlet obstruction, caused by compression of the prostatic urethra, is the origin of the symptoms.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c035--> Patients typically say that they urinate more often and that the stream has lost force.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c036-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**What does the examiner find?** On rectal examination the prostate feels bigger than normal, and its firm surface is studded with nodules.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c037--> Laboratory and imaging criteria are not covered by the source pages used for this card.

## Complications and prognosis
**What happens if the obstruction goes on?** Residual urine increases, and the bladder wall hypertrophies as the bladder dilates.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c038--> Acute and chronic pyelonephritis are among the complications.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c039--> Long-standing severe obstruction raises pressure upstream, dilating ureters and kidneys (hydroureter, hydronephrosis); renal failure and death may follow.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c040--> Drugs that block 5α-reductase, or surgery, are treatment options.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c041--> In some patients α1-blockers are added; they loosen prostatic and bladder-neck muscle, so urine flows better.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c042--> Cryotherapy, radiofrequency ablation and transurethral resection are the interventional options.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c043-->

## High-yield summary
- **BPH** is a **nodular** overgrowth of glands and stroma that obstructs urine outflow.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c046-->
- The nodules start in the **transitional zone** around the proximal urethra.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c047-->
- **DHT**, made by **5α-reductase**, drives the growth, and **finasteride** or **dutasteride** shrink the gland.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c048-->
- BPH is absent before puberty and in **castrated** men.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c049-->
- A typical nodule is **fibromyoadenomatous**, with a **two-layered** epithelium.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c050-->
- Severe, prolonged obstruction leads to **hydronephrosis** and renal failure.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c051-->
- Hyperplasia is **periurethral**, whereas carcinoma usually arises in the **peripheral** glands.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c052-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Is BPH the stage before prostate cancer?** No. Carcinoma usually starts in the peripheral glands, whereas BPH is hyperplasia of the part around the urethra.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c053-->

**Does BPH start in the peripheral part of the gland?** No. Hyperplastic nodules start around the urethra, where they compress and distort it.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c054-->

**Do men with BPH have high testosterone or high DHT?** No. Testosterone in the blood falls with age, and serum DHT is the same as in men without BPH.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c055-->

**Would testosterone treatment cause BPH?** No. Testosterone given from outside does not induce hyperplasia.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c056-->

**Does the absence of symptoms mean the prostate is normal?** No. Autopsies find BPH in many more men than ever develop prostatism.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c057-->

**How can I remember the main points?** FLOW: **F**ibromyoadenomatous nodules, **L**umen of the urethra squeezed, **O**utlet obstruction, **W**eak stream.<!--c:benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-c058-->
