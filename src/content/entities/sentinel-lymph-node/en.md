## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a sentinel lymph node?** Of all the nodes that drain a tumor's region, it is the one that receives lymph first, so metastases are most likely to lodge there.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c001--> Surgeons biopsy this single node to learn whether the regional nodes are involved.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c002--> A macrometastasis is evident on clinical examination; a micrometastasis shows up only when sentinel or elective nodes are removed and studied.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c003--> Outside nodal staging, the term micrometastasis means a small group of tumor cells that has settled in a distant organ.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c004-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do micrometastases matter if most tumor cells never form metastases?** Metastasis is inefficient: most circulating tumor cells never produce a clinically relevant secondary tumor.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c005--> By the usual view, only a cancer stem cell can start a lasting metastasis.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c006--> Proliferation is just one outcome for a newly arrived cell focus; the others are death, lasting dormancy, or dormancy followed by reactivation.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c007--> A dormant deposit is still active: a little division and a little cell death go on, and they cancel out.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c008--> Dormant deposits must escape CD8+ T cells and may show fewer ligands that switch on NK cells.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c009--> Inflammation can wake dormant deposits.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c010--> Dormant micrometastases and cancer stem cells largely escape adjuvant therapy, which targets hidden deposits after resection but favors fast-dividing cells.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c011-->

## Microscopic morphology

**How are nodal deposits sorted, and how are small ones found?** Routine slides can miss epithelial cells in a node, which cytokeratin staining can reveal.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c012--> In breast cancer, sentinel nodes studied closely now yield more micrometastases and isolated tumor cells (ITCs).<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c013--> In the reference chapter on the breast, a micrometastasis measures between 0.02 cm and 0.2 cm, or contains more than 200 cells.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c014--> It names ITCs next to micrometastases when it describes sentinel node findings.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c015-->

<aside class="edition">Size cut-offs for nodal deposits change with staging edition. These numbers come from one textbook; check the current edition before using them.</aside>

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

- **How it is studied:** the sentinel node is identified during surgery and sent for pathologic evaluation.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c016-->
- Immunohistochemistry on nodes targets cytokeratin, a marker of epithelial cells.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c017-->
- Breast sentinel nodes seldom need deeper levels or more immunostains, experts generally agree.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c018-->

## Clinical presentation

**When is a sentinel node biopsy used?** Resected axillary nodes must be examined by a pathologist, because in breast cancer their status is a key prognostic indicator.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c019--> Sentinel biopsy lowers the risk of lymphedema and nerve damage that comes with axillary dissection.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c020--> Melanoma staging now routinely relies on the sentinel node to tell whether the regional nodes hold tumor.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c021--> In melanoma, sentinel biopsy is usually considered once thickness passes 1 mm; from 0.8 mm it is also considered when there is ulceration or a high dermal mitotic rate.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c022-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the sentinel node found, and what does the result change?** The surgeon finds the sentinel node by tracing lymph flow with an injected dye plus a radioactive isotope.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c023--> A negative breast sentinel node means the axilla can safely be left undissected.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c024--> Deposit size and the count of involved nodes together set the melanoma N category.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c025-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Does a positive sentinel node change the outlook?** Compared with women whose nodes are negative, those with only micrometastases or ITCs in breast cancer fare little differently.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c026--> Ten-year survival in melanoma falls by 26% when the nodes are involved, as against clinically localized disease.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c027--> Outcome also tracks the number of positive nodes: with four or more, 10-year survival is 47%; with two or three, 68%; with one, 75%.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c028-->

## High-yield summary

- The **sentinel node** receives lymph from the tumor region first, which makes it the likeliest site of metastasis.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c032-->
- A **negative sentinel node** in breast cancer lets the surgeon avoid axillary dissection.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c033-->
- **Micrometastases and ITCs** in breast sentinel nodes change prognosis only slightly.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c034-->
- Sentinel biopsy is weighed for melanomas over **1 mm** thick or with risk features.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c035-->
- **Dormant micrometastases** mostly escape adjuvant therapy.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c036-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a negative sentinel node prove the whole axilla is free of tumor?** No. Only the node most likely to be involved has been cleared, and that is the basis for sparing the axilla.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c037-->

**Does finding ITCs in a breast sentinel node call for more sectioning?** Not usually: extra levels and stains seldom pay off.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c038-->

**Is a dormant micrometastasis an inactive cell?** No. Its cells still divide and die at low rates that offset each other.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c039-->

## Mnemonics

**FIRST:** Follow dye and isotope, Isolate the first node, Report it, Spare the axilla if it is negative, Think of staging.<!--c:sentinel-lymph-node-c040-->
