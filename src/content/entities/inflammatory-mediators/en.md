## Definition and epidemiology
**Which chemical signals run acute inflammation, and where do they come from?** Inflammatory mediators are the chemical signals that dilate vessels, make them leaky and call inflammatory cells to an injured site.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c001--> The plasma cascades (complement, kinin, coagulation) work only after a protease cuts their proteins.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c002--> Cells supply the rest, in two ways. A mast cell keeps histamine ready in its granules.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c003--> Prostaglandins and leukotrienes, by contrast, are made from scratch only after a stimulus.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c004-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**Which findings point to a faulty mediator system?** Absence of C1 inhibitor goes with hereditary angioedema.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c005--> Lacking C1q, C1r, C1s or C4, all early components, strongly predisposes to systemic lupus erythematosus.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c006--> Neisseria infection is the risk when the terminal components C6, C7 and C8 are lacking.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c007-->

## High-yield summary
- **Histamine**, released by mast cells, dilates arterioles and also opens gaps between venular endothelial cells, hence the early leakage.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c010-->
- Platelet aggregation releases **serotonin**, whose effects resemble those of histamine.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c011-->
- Arachidonic acid goes two ways: **5-lipoxygenase** gives leukotrienes, **cyclooxygenase** gives prostaglandins and thromboxane.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c012-->
- Neutrophils are attracted by **LTB4**, and so are monocytes and macrophages.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c013-->
- Asthma depends heavily on **LTC4, LTD4 and LTE4**, which contract smooth muscle and increase vascular permeability.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c014-->
- The endogenous pyrogens, the cytokines that cause fever, are **IL-1, IL-6 and TNFα**.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c015-->
- Neutrophils are the usual targets of **CXC chemokines**, macrophages and lymphocytes of **CC chemokines**; exceptions exist.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c016-->
- Vascular permeability rises under **C3a and C5a**, the anaphylatoxins.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c017-->
- **The membrane attack complex** (C5b-9) lyses target cells.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c018-->
- **Bradykinin** is released when kallikrein cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen; the kallikrein itself comes from activated **factor XII**.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c019-->
- Endothelium uses **nitric oxide** to relax the smooth muscle of vessels.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c020-->
- **Nitric oxide** also helps activated macrophages kill microbes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c021-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Is coagulation separate from the inflammatory cascades?** No. One shared first step, activation of factor XII (Hageman factor), connects the coagulation, kinin and complement systems.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c022-->

**Do aspirin and other NSAIDs block every arachidonic acid product?** No. Leukotrienes and lipoxins are still made: these drugs act upstream, on cyclooxygenase, so only prostanoid synthesis falls.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c023-->

**Are all arachidonic acid products pro-inflammatory?** No. Lipoxins are anti-inflammatory.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c024-->

**Does complement only lyse bacteria?** No. Its fragment C3b coats microbes so that phagocytes take them up.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c025--> Its fragment C5a attracts leukocytes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c026-->
