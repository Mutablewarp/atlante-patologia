## Definition and epidemiology
**Which chemical signals run acute inflammation, and where do they come from?** Inflammatory mediators are the chemical signals that dilate vessels, make them leaky and call inflammatory cells to an injured site.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c001--> Plasma supplies three cascades (complement, kinins and coagulation); their proteins are inactive until a protease cuts them.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c002--> Cells supply the rest: some mediators sit ready-made in granules, such as histamine in mast cells.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c003--> Others are built on demand after a stimulus, such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c004-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**Which findings point to a faulty mediator system?** Absence of C1 inhibitor goes with hereditary angioedema.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c005--> People who lack an early complement component (C1q, C1r, C1s or C4) are much more likely to develop systemic lupus erythematosus.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c006--> Missing terminal components (C6, C7, C8) leave a person open to Neisseria infection.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c007-->

## High-yield summary
- In the first phase of inflammation, venules leak because **histamine** pulls their endothelial cells apart; the same mediator dilates arterioles and is released by mast cells.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c010-->
- **Serotonin** acts much like histamine and is released when platelets aggregate.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c011-->
- **Cyclooxygenase** turns arachidonic acid into prostaglandins and thromboxane; **5-lipoxygenase** turns it into leukotrienes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c012-->
- **LTB4** attracts neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c013-->
- **LTC4, LTD4 and LTE4** contract smooth muscle and raise permeability, which makes them central in asthma.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c014-->
- **IL-1, IL-6 and TNFα** are the endogenous pyrogens.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c015-->
- **CXC chemokines** act mainly on neutrophils; **CC chemokines** act mainly on macrophages and lymphocytes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c016-->
- **C3a and C5a** are anaphylatoxins that raise vascular permeability.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c017-->
- **The membrane attack complex** (C5b-9) lyses target cells.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c018-->
- **Factor XII** activates kallikrein, which cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen to release **bradykinin**.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c019-->
- **Nitric oxide** from endothelium relaxes vascular smooth muscle.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c020-->
- **Nitric oxide** also helps activated macrophages kill microbes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c021-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Is coagulation separate from the inflammatory cascades?** No. Activation of factor XII (Hageman factor) links the coagulation, kinin and complement systems.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c022-->

**Do aspirin and other NSAIDs block every arachidonic acid product?** No. They block cyclooxygenase, so prostanoid synthesis falls, but leukotrienes and lipoxins are not blocked.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c023-->

**Are all arachidonic acid products pro-inflammatory?** No. Lipoxins are anti-inflammatory.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c024-->

**Does complement only lyse bacteria?** No. Its fragment C3b coats microbes so that phagocytes take them up.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c025--> Its fragment C5a attracts leukocytes.<!--c:inflammatory-mediators-c026-->
