## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a gastric polyp?** At endoscopy or autopsy, it is any bump that visibly rises from the stomach mucosa.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c001--> Most gastric polyps are not true neoplasms, which sets the stomach apart from the colon.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c002--> The three types to know are fundic gland, hyperplastic and adenomatous polyps.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c003--> Today the fundic gland type is the commonest.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers each type?**
- The original description of fundic gland polyps came from patients with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).<!--c:gastric-polyps-c005-->
- Fundic gland polyps in people taking PPIs are usually single or few, unlike the carpet seen in FAP.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c006-->
- Chronic gastritis or reactive gastropathy is the background of hyperplastic polyps.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c007-->
- Intestinal metaplasia in the surrounding mucosa is the usual setting of intestinal-type adenomas.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these lesions form?** The pathway disturbed in fundic gland polyps is Wnt/β-catenin, whether the polyp is sporadic or syndromic.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c009--> A hyperplastic polyp is a patch of mucosa that overgrows after injury.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c010--> **Ménétrier disease, a related thickening of the folds: what drives it?** Too much TGFα, an EGFR activator, makes the foveolar cells proliferate massively.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c011-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the endoscopist see?** Fundic gland polyps are confined to the body and fundus.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c012--> FAP patients have very many fundic gland polyps in the proximal stomach.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c013--> Hyperplastic polyps may be single or multiple.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c014--> Outside FAP, an adenoma is typically a solitary lesion.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c015--> The antrum escapes in Ménétrier disease, while the folds of the body and fundus are markedly thickened.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c016-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen under the microscope?**
- Fundic gland polyp: cystic glands that still hold acid-secreting, enzyme-producing and mucus-producing cells.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c017-->
- Hyperplastic polyp: an inflamed lamina propria with overgrown foveolar epithelium that may form small cysts.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c018-->
- Eroded surfaces in hyperplastic polyps can bring reactive atypia.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c019-->
- Adenoma: whether foveolar or intestinal in type, it is dysplastic by definition.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c020-->
- Intestinal-type adenoma: pencil-shaped, dark nuclei, like those of an intestinal adenoma.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c021-->
- Foveolar-type adenoma occurs in FAP, though intestinal metaplasia plays no part.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c022-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Do special stains or molecular tests help?** The source used here describes no special stain or immunostain for these polyps. The molecular finding is a change in the Wnt/β-catenin pathway in fundic gland polyps.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c023-->

## Clinical presentation

**When are there many polyps?** Besides FAP, several syndromes give multiple gastric polyps: Cowden, Cronkhite–Canada, Peutz–Jeghers and familial juvenile polyposis.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c024--> **What about Ménétrier disease?** Peripheral edema marks its severe cases, and abdominal pain is the usual complaint in a disease that begins insidiously.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c025--> Infection-related Ménétrier disease can be treated with antibacterial or antiviral drugs; other options are octreotide, cetuximab and gastrectomy.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c026-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is a syndromic polyp recognized?** Syndromic polyps have no distinctive look; the usual mistaken identity is a large hyperplastic polyp.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c027--> To classify such a polyp, look for the underlying syndrome, not at the polyp's appearance.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c028--> In Ménétrier disease, blood tests show low albumin from protein lost through the mucosa.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c029-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Are they dangerous?** PPI-associated polyps seem harmless.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c030--> Focal dysplasia is a rare finding in the fundic gland polyps of FAP.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c031--> Gastric adenoma can be linked to cancer, though less firmly than the colonic adenoma.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c032--> Gastric carcinoma is more likely to develop in Ménétrier disease.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c033-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Fundic gland polyp | Cystic glands with parietal and chief cells.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c034--> | Histology |
| Hyperplastic polyp | Foveolar hyperplasia with inflamed lamina propria.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c035--> | Histology |
| Gastric adenoma | Dysplasia present by definition.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c036--> | Histology |
| Ménétrier disease | Thick folds from foveolar hyperplasia, not discrete polyps.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c037--> | Histology and albumin |
| Zollinger–Ellison syndrome | Thick, bumpy folds; a gastrin-secreting tumor drives the parietal cells to multiply.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c038--> | Histology and clinical syndrome |
| [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis\|Polyposis syndromes]] | Plain polyps, easily mistaken for an oversized hyperplastic one.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c039--> | Syndrome features |

## High-yield summary

- Most gastric polyps are **not true neoplasms**.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c040-->
- **Fundic gland polyps** are the most common, tied to **PPIs**.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c041-->
- **Hyperplastic polyps** follow chronic gastritis or injury.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c042-->
- **Adenomas** are dysplastic; the intestinal type usually arises on metaplasia.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c043-->
- **Polyposis syndromes** with gastric polyps: Cowden, Cronkhite–Canada, Peutz–Jeghers, juvenile polyposis, FAP.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c044-->
- **Ménétrier disease** causes hypoalbuminemia through mucosal protein loss.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c045-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Are gastric polyps like colonic polyps?** No: in the stomach most are not neoplasms.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c046-->

**Is a hyperplastic polyp a neoplasm?** No: it is a reactive response, and the colonic polyps that share its name are unrelated.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c047-->

**Do Ménétrier disease and Zollinger–Ellison syndrome thicken the folds the same way?** No: the first is foveolar and the second parietal cell hyperplasia.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c048-->

**Does a gastric adenoma become carcinoma like a colonic one?** Not so closely.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c049-->

## Mnemonics

**FHA: Fundus-PPI, Hurt mucosa, Atypia.** Fundic gland polyps go with PPIs, hyperplastic polyps with injured mucosa, adenomas with dysplasia.<!--c:gastric-polyps-c050-->
