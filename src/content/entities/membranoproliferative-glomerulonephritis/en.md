## Definition and epidemiology

**What is MPGN?** It is not one disease but a glomerular reaction that many conditions can trigger: extra cells, thick capillary walls.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c001-->
Two broad groups exist: immune complex–mediated MPGN and C3 glomerulopathy.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c002-->
<aside class="edition">Dense deposit disease used to be called type II MPGN, so older sources classify differently.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c003--></aside>
Older children and young adults are hit hardest, but any age can be affected.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c004-->
Countries with much chronic infection see far more immune complex MPGN.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What lies behind it?** In the primary immune complex form, the antigen is usually unknown.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c006-->
The antigen may come from an infection or an autoimmune condition.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c007-->
Persistent infections, such as bacterial endocarditis or hepatitis C, can cause a secondary form.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c008-->
Cryoglobulinemia and cancer are other secondary causes.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c009-->
Rarely, a monoclonal immunoglobulin is the culprit.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c010-->
Uncontrolled alternative complement activity, from a gene defect or an autoantibody, underlies C3 glomerulopathy.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**Immune complexes or complement: what is deposited?** MPGN deposits are either immune complexes or complement alone, lodged in the mesangium and capillary walls.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c012-->
Complexes keep lodging in glomeruli, fed by a silent infection with circulating antigen, so the glomerulus remodels.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c013-->
MPGN that resolves once its associated disease is gone was probably caused by that disease.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c014-->
C3 glomerulopathy arises when the proteins that restrain the alternative pathway, such as factor H and factor I, are absent or fail.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c015-->
Prolonged C3 activation results when C3 nephritic factor, an IgG autoantibody present in some patients, preserves the alternative-pathway convertase.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c016-->
The defect lies in the recipient, so C3 glomerulopathy often comes back in a transplanted kidney.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c017-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the glomeruli look like?** Monocytes and macrophages infiltrate the enlarged glomeruli, where mesangial cells multiply markedly.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c018-->
The old name lobular glomerulonephritis referred to this accentuated lobulation.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c019-->
On silver stain, thickened capillary walls show basement membrane split into layers: double contours.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c020-->
The second contour is new basement membrane laid between endothelium and the mesangial cytoplasm creeping beneath it.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c021-->
Many C3 glomerulopathy cases show no MPGN pattern, though some resemble the immune complex form.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c022-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** in immune complex MPGN, capillary loops and mesangium stain granularly for immunoglobulin plus complement.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c023-->
- In C3 glomerulopathy it shows complement alone.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c024-->
- EM shows complement or immune complexes as dense deposits under endothelium and mesangium.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c025-->
- Subepithelial dense deposits can also occur, in differing amounts.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c026-->
- Dense material within the thickened GBM and mesangial matrix marks dense deposit disease on EM.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c027-->
- Arteriolar walls and peritubular capillary basement membranes also contain dense areas.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c028-->
- C3 glomerulonephritis lacks intramembranous dense deposits.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c029-->
- Dense deposit disease shows little or no antibody and linear C3 along the capillary walls.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c030-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Immune complex MPGN can look nephrotic, nephritic or both.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c031-->
C3 levels are often low.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c032-->
C3 glomerulopathy is rare and mostly affects children.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c033-->
C3 glomerulopathy: urine shows protein and blood, plus hypertension and renal impairment.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c034-->
Low C3 with normal C4 is common.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c035-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings sort the cases?** Low C3 occurs in both groups, so immunofluorescence decides.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c036-->
Secondary immune complex MPGN has a findable cause, for example a chronic infection or cryoglobulinemia.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c037-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it evolve?** Immune complex MPGN tends to linger and worsen slowly.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c038-->
About half of patients reach end-stage renal disease after 10 years.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c039-->
In C3 glomerulopathy, about 40% do so within 10 years.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c040-->

## High-yield summary

- **MPGN** is a pattern with two groups: **immune complex–mediated** and **C3 glomerulopathy**.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c048-->
- The **double contour** is a second basement membrane layer, built with mesangial cytoplasm, under the endothelium.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c049-->
- **Dense deposits** lie under the endothelium and in the mesangium; dense deposit disease adds an intramembranous ribbon.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c050-->
- Immunofluorescence: immunoglobulin plus complement, or **C3 alone**.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c051-->
- **C3 nephritic factor** is an autoantibody that keeps C3 switched on for longer.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c052-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is MPGN a single disease?** No. It is a pattern of injury with many causes.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c053-->

**Does an MPGN pattern prove immune complexes?** No. C3 glomerulopathy can look alike by light microscopy.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c054-->

**Is dense deposit disease an immune complex disease?** No. It is complement-driven, with C3 staining and little or no antibody.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c055-->

**Does low C3 mean MPGN?** No. Postinfectious and lupus glomerulonephritis also give nephritis with low complement.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c056-->

## Mnemonics

**TWIN:** **T**wo membrane layers (double contour), **W**hich deposit (immunoglobulin plus C3, or C3 alone), **I**ndolent infection, **N**ephrotic or nephritic.<!--c:membranoproliferative-glomerulonephritis-c057-->
