## Definition and epidemiology

**What is malignant mesothelioma?** It is a tumor that arises from mesothelial cells.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c001--> Other sites are the tunica vaginalis of the testis, the pericardium and the peritoneum, but the pleura is involved most often.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c002--> About one quarter arise in the peritoneum.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c003--> Each year, the United States sees about 2,000 new cases.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c004-->

<aside class="edition">This card follows one textbook edition. Mesothelioma classification has changed between editions, so check a current classification before using subtype names, grades or stages in a report or an exam.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it?** Asbestos exposure lies behind most cases.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c005--> Patients in South Africa, Great Britain and the United States report asbestos exposure in 80% of cases.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c006--> Typically the tumor takes a long time to appear, with an average latency of 30 to 40 years.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c007--> Asbestos is also tied to most peritoneal tumors, just as it is to the pleural ones.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c008--> Unlike the pleural tumor, a peritoneal one in a woman seldom comes with asbestos exposure.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c009--> Some peritoneal tumors held up to 2 million asbestos fibers per wet gram.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c010--> See [[asbestos-related-disease]].

## Pathogenesis

**How does asbestos lead to the tumor?** This card does not yet cover the mechanism, nor the molecular changes such as BAP1 and MTAP loss, because its sources do not describe them.

## Gross morphology

**What does the tumor do to the lung?** The "pleural rind" is a sheet of tumor that runs through the fissures and interlobar septa and often compresses the lung it surrounds.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c011--> Tumor usually enters only the outer rim of the lung that touches it.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c012--> The tumor tends not to involve the lymph nodes.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c013--> In the abdomen, the tumor spreads widely over the serosa of the pelvic and abdominal organs, and the peritoneum itself becomes thicker.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c014-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Which patterns does it show?** In the classic, biphasic form, one tumor contains epithelial areas and sarcomatous areas side by side.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c015--> The epithelial (epithelioid) part can be mistaken for adenocarcinoma, because it builds glands and tubules.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c016--> Under the microscope the sarcomatous part looks like fibrosarcoma, being made of spindle cells arranged in sheets.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c017--> A tumor may contain just the epithelial or just the sarcomatous component.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c018--> Of the single-component forms, the sarcomatous one is rarer.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c019--> Adenocarcinoma can be hard to separate from a tumor made only of epithelial cells.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c020--> The sarcomatoid type is rare in the peritoneum, and the growth pattern ranges from tubulopapillary to solid.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c021--> In the epithelial variant, the cells have plenty of cytoplasm and a cuboidal or polygonal shape.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c022-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

- **How it is studied:** the sources of this card cover immunohistochemistry, Alcian blue and electron microscopy, listed below. They do not cover specimen type or sampling.
- **Cytokeratins:** staining is no help in separating the two tumors, since both are positive.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c023-->
- **Mesothelioma markers:** Mesothelioma usually stains for D2-40 (podoplanin), WT-1 and calretinin; adenocarcinoma usually does not.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c024-->
- **Adenocarcinoma markers:** Adenocarcinoma usually expresses CEA, Ber-EP4, B72.3, Leu-M1 or claudin-4, but not always; mesothelioma expresses none.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c025-->
- **Mucin:** its absence supports mesothelioma.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c026-->
- **Alcian blue:** staining is positive, which reflects hyaluronic acid.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c027-->
- **Electron microscopy:** long, slender microvilli.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c028-->
- **Peritoneal tumors:** HBME-1, CK5/6, calretinin and thrombomodulin mark the tumor as a mesothelioma.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c029-->
- **BAP1 and MTAP:** not covered by the sources of this card yet.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** On average, patients are 60 years old.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c030--> Pleural fluid or a pleural mass often brings patients in, along with chest pain, malaise and weight loss.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c031--> The pleural tumor stays mostly in the chest, where it presses on and invades key structures.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c032--> Metastases can reach the adrenals, liver, bones and peritoneum, or stay inside the chest, in lung parenchyma and mediastinal nodes.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c033--> Peritoneal disease typically affects middle-aged or postmenopausal patients; ascites and weight loss are among its vague symptoms.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c034-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is it diagnosed?** Distinguishing mesothelioma from adenocarcinoma depends on immunohistochemistry.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c035--> Because treatment and survival differ, a serous adenocarcinoma, including one metastatic from the ovary, must not be mistaken for a peritoneal mesothelioma.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c036--> Imaging and effusion cytology are not covered here; see [[pleural-effusion-and-pleural-fluid-cytology]].

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** The outlook is poor, and treatment is largely ineffective.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c037--> Only a few patients are still alive 18 months after diagnosis.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c038--> In its diffuse peritoneal form, mesothelioma is described as invariably fatal.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c039--> There is no effective therapy for this form.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c040-->

## High-yield summary

- **Asbestos** is the usual cause, after a latency of **30 to 40 years**.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c047-->
- The **pleural rind** is tumor that encases the lung, running along the fissures.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c048-->
- **Biphasic** means epithelial plus sarcomatous areas in one tumor.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c049-->
- **Calretinin, WT-1 and D2-40** are positive in mesothelioma; **CEA, Ber-EP4, B72.3, Leu-M1 and claudin-4** point to adenocarcinoma.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c050-->
- Prognosis is **poor**: few patients live beyond **18 months**.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c051-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a positive cytokeratin prove carcinoma and exclude mesothelioma?** No. Both tumor types stain for cytokeratin, so a positive result does not exclude mesothelioma.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c052-->

**Is mesothelioma only a pleural tumor?** No. It can also arise in the tunica vaginalis, the pericardium and the peritoneum, although the pleura is the commonest site.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c053-->

**Do all patients report asbestos exposure?** No. In the countries listed above, 80% do.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c054--> Among women with a peritoneal tumor, few have been exposed.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c055-->

**Is the pleural rind deep invasion of the lung?** No. The tumor surrounds the lung, but generally enters only its edge.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c056-->

**Is every mesothelioma malignant?** No. Well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma is benign.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c057-->

## Mnemonics

**How do I keep the IHC panel straight?** Mesothelioma is a "CWD" tumor: Calretinin, WT-1, D2-40 positive.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c058--> Adenocarcinoma is "CLiBB": CEA, Leu-M1, B72.3, Ber-EP4, plus claudin-4.<!--c:malignant-mesothelioma-c059-->
