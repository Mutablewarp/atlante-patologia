## Definition and epidemiology

**What are benign liver tumors and tumor-like lesions?** Hepatocellular adenoma, a benign liver tumor, arises mainly in women.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c001--> The group also includes focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH), hemangioma, cysts and rarer tumors such as angiomyolipoma.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c002--> No tumor of the liver is more common than hemangioma.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c003--> Up to 7% of livers examined at autopsy contain one.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c004-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers these lesions?** Adenomas were once rare; oral contraceptive use raised their frequency.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c005--> Lower-dose pills mean fewer adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c006--> Men on anabolic steroids occasionally develop adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c007--> FNH is not associated with oral contraceptives.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c008--> The cause of nodular regenerative hyperplasia is unknown; it has been linked to hormonal drugs and to several other diseases, including chronic immune disease.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why do these lesions form?** Abnormal enlarged vessels in its septa point to a local vascular origin of FNH.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c010--> FNH is not a neoplasm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c011--> Mesenchymal hamartoma comes from faulty liver mesenchyme development.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c012--> Angiomyolipoma belongs to the PEC-omas, tumors of perivascular cells that usually go with tuberous sclerosis.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c013-->

## Gross morphology

**How does each lesion look when cut?** An adenoma is usually solitary, paler than the liver around it, and ringed by a capsule.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c014--> It can reach 40 cm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c015--> Some cases also have smaller satellite adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c016--> Adenomatosis means more than 10 adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c017--> Fibrous bands fan out from a scar at FNH's center.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c018--> FNH sometimes protrudes from the liver surface and reaches 15 cm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c019--> Most hemangiomas are single and smaller than 5 cm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c020--> Von Meyenburg complexes are usually multiple, none above 1 cm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c021-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the cells and architecture show?** An adenoma lacks lobules, portal tracts and central venules, though its cells look normal.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c022--> Small arteries inside it hint at adenoma.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c023--> Cytoplasm is eosinophilic, or clear and vacuolated from glycogen or fat.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c024--> Four subtypes exist:

- **HNF1A-inactivated:** 35% of adenomas, with macrovesicular steatosis.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c025-->
- **Inflammatory:** 45%, with lymphocytic inflammation and sinusoidal dilatation.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c026-->
- **Beta-catenin-activated:** 10% of adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c027-->
- **Unclassified:** under 10%, with no specific features.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c028-->

Inflammatory cells and twisted bile ductules sit in the septa around FNH nodules.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c029--> Cavernous spaces filled with blood make up the hemangioma.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c030--> Simple cysts have a cuboidal to columnar lining.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c031--> Von Meyenburg complexes: fibrous stroma surrounds abnormal cystic bile ducts.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c032--> Congenital hepatic fibrosis: widened, scarred portal tracts crowded with well-formed ductules.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c033-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which tests separate the adenoma subtypes?** Two subtypes are defined by molecular change: HNF1A inactivation or beta-catenin activation.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c034--> The source gives no stain or marker panel.

## Clinical presentation

**How do these lesions present?** Most hemangiomas are small and cause no symptoms.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c035--> Abdominal symptoms or even peritoneal bleeding can follow with larger ones.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c036--> Bleeds into the peritoneum are a surgical emergency, and they strike roughly one adenoma patient in three.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c037--> FNH can appear in men and women of any age.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c038--> It rarely bleeds.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c039--> Nodular regenerative hyperplasia goes with portal hypertension.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c040--> Congenital hepatic fibrosis mostly affects the young; its chief problem is portal hypertension with bleeding varices.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c041--> Infantile hemangioendothelioma mostly affects infants, usually girls.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c042--> Large ones can end in heart or liver failure.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c043-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Do these lesions regress or turn malignant?** Stopping oral contraceptives can make an adenoma vanish, large ones included.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c044--> Malignant transformation is a greater risk with beta-catenin-activated adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c045--> Nodular regenerative hyperplasia is not a tumor and not a precursor.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c046--> Infantile hemangioendothelioma commonly regresses on its own.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c047--> Complete excision cures mesenchymal hamartoma.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c048-->

## Differential diagnosis

**Which lesions get confused?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatocellular adenoma | Encapsulated pale mass of normal-looking hepatocytes, with no portal tracts.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c049--> | Histology |
| Focal nodular hyperplasia | Septa and nodules give a cirrhosis-like look, see [[cirrhosis]].<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c050--> | Histology |
| Nodular regenerative hyperplasia | Small nodules, no scarring, plates two or three cells deep.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c051--> | Histology |
| Congenital hepatic fibrosis | Fibrosis without regenerative nodules, unlike cirrhosis.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c052--> | Histology |
| Cavernous hemangioma | Blood-filled spaces like hemangiomas elsewhere, see [[vascular-tumors]].<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c053--> | Histology |

## High-yield summary

- **Oral contraceptives** drive adenomas, and stopping them can shrink the tumor.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c054-->
- A **beta-catenin-activated** adenoma has an increased malignant risk.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c055-->
- **FNH** has a central scar and is not a neoplasm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c056-->
- **Hemangioma** is the most common benign liver tumor.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c057-->
- **Nodular regenerative hyperplasia** causes portal hypertension without fibrosis.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c058-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is FNH a pill-induced tumor?** No. It is not associated with oral contraceptives.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c059--> It is not a neoplasm.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c060-->

**Is a benign adenoma harmless?** No. It can bleed into the peritoneum.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c061--> The beta-catenin subtype carries a malignant risk, see [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]].<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c062-->

**Is every fibrous, nodular liver cirrhosis?** No. Congenital hepatic fibrosis has fibrosis but no regenerative nodules.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c063-->

**Is an inflammatory adenoma a telangiectatic FNH?** Older texts called it that, but it is now grouped with the adenomas.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c064-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the adenoma subtypes?** HIBU: HNF1A-inactivated, Inflammatory, Beta-catenin-activated, Unclassified.<!--c:benign-liver-tumors-c065-->
