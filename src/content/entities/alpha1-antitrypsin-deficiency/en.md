## Definition and epidemiology

**What is alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency?** It is an autosomal recessive disease.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c001--> The liver is the main producer of the missing protein, α1-antitrypsin (α1-AT), a protease inhibitor.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c002--> Some patients have both lung and liver disease, others only liver disease; the lung form was described first.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c003-->

No other inherited liver disease is as frequent, and no other inherited disease sends as many patients to liver transplantation.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c004--> About 1 in 2,000 newborns is affected.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c005--> Of these, only 10% to 15% develop liver disease.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c006--> Roughly 1 in 100 COPD cases traces back to the inherited deficiency.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c007--> Young patients with severe emphysema are the group in which it is far more frequent.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which gene and which alleles are involved?** The disease arises from mutations in the gene SERPINA1, which is also named Pi (protease inhibitor).<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c009--> A person's two SERPINA1 alleles are codominant; the combination sets the quantity and the variant of α1-AT.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c010--> Most people are MM, because M, the allele that gives normal production, is the most frequent.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c011--> Z homozygotes (PiZZ) are likely to be deficient, because Z, and to a lesser degree S, yields less α1-AT.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c012--> Among people of Scandinavian origin PiZ is more frequent; it is the most serious of the abnormal alleles.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c013-->

**Does smoking matter?** Yes. The methionine residues of α1-AT are oxidized by tobacco smoke, which lowers its activity.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the Z protein harm the liver and the lung?** PiZ is found in 95% of cases, and in this variant lysine replaces glutamate at position 342.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c015--> Misfolding of the mutant protein traps it in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) of the hepatocyte.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c016--> The cell is damaged as the protein collects in insoluble aggregates that it has no way to export.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c017--> PiZZ people have only 15% to 20% of the normal blood α1-AT, since their liver secretes the abnormal protein poorly.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c018-->

In the lung, α1-AT restrains neutrophil elastase, which attacks elastin and the rest of the alveolar wall.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c019--> Emphysema appears when elastin breakdown outruns its inhibition, upsetting the normal balance with elastin synthesis.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c020--> Lacking inhibitor, elastase digests the elastin of the distal airway walls.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c021-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lung and the liver look like?** Panacinar emphysema, with destruction of the entire acinus, is the typical lung lesion.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c022--> Septa disappear progressively from the acinar center outward, until only a sparse supporting scaffold remains.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c023--> When a smoker's lung shows both types, the upper zones carry the centrilobular change and the lower zones the panacinar one.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c024--> Cirrhosis appears early in children with neonatal hepatitis: micronodular by age 2 to 3 years, with a possible later change to macronodular.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c025-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen on the slide?** Hepatocyte cytoplasm holds droplets that are PAS-positive and only faintly eosinophilic.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c026--> Each droplet is mutant protein packed into dilated ER cisternae, with no recognizable structure.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c027--> In this cirrhotic liver, many hepatocytes hold PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules of misfolded α1-AT.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c028--> Cirrhosis is the end point of the chronic hepatitis with which the liver disease often begins.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c029--> Next to normal lung at equal power, this lung has lost most alveolar walls, leaving big irregular spaces.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c030-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, the clue is a set of faintly eosinophilic droplets within hepatocyte cytoplasm.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c031-->
- PAS after diastase: the stain no longer marks glycogen, so the globules that still stain are misfolded α1-AT.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c032-->
- Genotype: PiZZ carries the risk of cirrhosis and emphysema, whereas PiMM, the most common genotype, is the normal one.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c033-->
- Specimen type, sampling, immunohistochemistry and electron microscopy are not covered by the sources used for this card.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Some patients never develop any liver dysfunction, whereas in others neonatal hepatitis kills quickly, so the liver course is very variable.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c034--> In the newborn period, conjugated hyperbilirubinemia with cholestatic jaundice affects 10% of ZZ infants.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c035--> Among cases of neonatal conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, 30% are due to this deficiency.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c036--> Within 6 months, most of these infants have recovered.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c037--> Permanent liver disease remains in a minority, 10% to 20%.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c038--> In some patients the first complaint is a symptom of cirrhosis, arriving in early adulthood after years without symptoms.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c039-->

**And the lung?** Emphysema recognized before age 40 usually points to the genetic PiZ deficiency.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c040--> PiZZ people who do not smoke first develop emphysema between ages 45 and 50.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c041--> In PiZZ smokers, onset comes 5 to 10 years earlier.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c042--> Most PiZZ people who avoid smoking stay free of emphysema.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c043--> Breathlessness on exertion lasts for years, while cough, if present, is mild and dry.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c044-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to the diagnosis?** In the liver, PAS-positive globules that resist diastase are the histologic signature.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c045--> A very low blood α1-AT level fits the PiZZ genotype.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c046--> Imaging and a stepwise work-up are not covered by the sources used for this card.

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications and the outlook?** Liver failure and other complications of cirrhosis kill most affected children before age 10.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c047--> Liver transplantation cures the liver disease.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c048--> Hepatocellular carcinoma is frequent in the cirrhotic liver of these patients.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c049-->

## High-yield summary

- **PiZZ** people secrete the Z protein poorly, so their blood α1-AT is low.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c053-->
- In the liver, **PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules** are misfolded α1-AT retained in hepatocytes.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c054-->
- In the lung, the typical lesion is **panacinar emphysema**.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c055-->
- Liver disease runs from chronic hepatitis to **cirrhosis**, with a high risk of carcinoma.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c056-->
- The deficiency causes 30% of **neonatal conjugated hyperbilirubinemia**.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c057-->
- **Smoking** brings emphysema forward by 5 to 10 years in PiZZ people.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c058-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does every PiZZ person get liver disease?** No. Only 10% to 15% of affected people do.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c059--> Most nonsmoking PiZZ people also stay free of emphysema.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c060-->

**Are the PAS-positive globules glycogen?** No. Diastase removes glycogen and the globules remain: they are misfolded α1-AT.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c061-->

**Is liver damage caused by a lack of α1-AT, as in the lung?** No. In the liver the harm comes from mutant protein accumulating inside hepatocytes.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c062--> In the lung the harm comes from too little circulating inhibitor.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c063-->

**Is the emphysema of this disease centrilobular, like the smoker's?** Not typically: the usual pattern is panacinar.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c064-->

## Mnemonics

- **ZZ: Zipped in the liver, Zero in the lung.** The Z protein stays trapped in hepatocytes and little inhibitor reaches the lung.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c065-->
- **Diastase eats sugar, not protein.** Diastase digests glycogen but leaves the globules of misfolded protein.<!--c:alpha1-antitrypsin-deficiency-c066-->
