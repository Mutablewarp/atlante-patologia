## Definition and epidemiology

**What is rheumatic heart disease?** After a streptococcal infection, children can develop rheumatic fever, an inflammation that may reach heart, joints and brain.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c001--> Rheumatic heart disease is its cardiac side: acute pancarditis first, then permanently deformed valves.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c002--> After age 30 rheumatic fever is rare; the usual patients are children aged 5 to 15.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c003--> In the United States it has become uncommon and rarely fatal.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c004--> Young people still die of its cardiac effects in parts of Africa and Asia, among other regions.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers it?** Almost always the infection is pharyngitis caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes* (group A β-hemolytic streptococcus).<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c006--> Why only a small minority of infected people develop the disease is unknown.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c007--> It needs an abnormal immune response in a genetically susceptible host infected by a rheumatogenic strain.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c008--> The attack rate can reach 3% in some streptococcal pharyngitis outbreaks.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does it injure the heart?** Autoimmunity through molecular mimicry is the most likely mechanism.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c010--> Antibodies and T cells raised against streptococcal antigens also react with the heart muscle membrane and with valve glycoproteins.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c011--> Myosin and other cardiac proteins contain regions structurally similar to a coiled domain of streptococcal M protein.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c012--> Antibodies from patients recognize both myocardium and M protein.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c013--> Antibodies against collagen can arise because the PARF domain of some M proteins binds type IV collagen.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c014--> Abnormal cytokine production may result when streptococcal antigens resemble HLA class II molecules.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What do heart and valves look like?** In severe early disease the heart tends to be dilated.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c016--> In the acute stage, the leaflets are inflamed and edematous.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c017--> Higher closing pressure makes the left-sided valves the worst injured.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c018--> The verrucae of verrucous endocarditis are tiny fibrin nodules that settle where endothelium is lost along leaflet closure lines.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c019--> Bread-and-butter pericarditis is a shaggy fibrin exudate on both pericardial surfaces.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c020-->

Scarring during healing stiffens the leaflets and makes them thicker and smaller.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c021--> Healed verrucae scar into bridges joining adjacent leaflets, chiefly at the commissures.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c022--> Mitral involvement is the most frequent and the most severe.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c023--> In chronic mitral valvulitis the commissures and chordae often fuse, and the leaflets become thick and calcified.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c024--> Seen from the ventricle, a severely stenotic orifice is a rigid, narrow slit, the "fish mouth".<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c025--> A MacCallum plaque, a rough, wrinkled patch of atrial endocardium, results from the regurgitant jet hitting the posterior left atrial wall.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c026--> The aortic valve ranks second: in older patients, cusps often calcify on top of fibrous thickening and commissural fusion.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c027--> Mitral and aortic lesions nearly always coexist with tricuspid deformity from repeated attacks.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c028--> Pulmonic involvement is rare.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c029-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the microscopic hallmark?** Early myocarditis has no special pattern; lymphocytes and macrophages make up most of the infiltrate.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c030--> Collagen undergoes fibrinoid degeneration.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c031--> Several weeks after symptoms begin, rheumatic myocarditis gains its hallmark granuloma, the Aschoff body.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c032--> In its early form, inflammatory cells gather around a patch of swollen, pink-staining collagen next to a vessel.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c033--> Later it is a granuloma: giant cells sit among the inflammatory cells of an outer rim, around a fibrinoid center.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c034--> It ends as a nodular scar.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c035--> Anitschkow cells are macrophages; thanks to a central chromatin band, their nuclei look like caterpillars lengthwise and owl eyes in cross-section.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c036--> A multinucleated Anitschkow cell is an Aschoff giant cell.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c037-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What is examined?**
- **How it is studied:** surgically excised chronic rheumatic mitral valves can be inspected grossly.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c038-->
- In the myocardial interstitium, look for Aschoff bodies with Anitschkow cells and Aschoff giant cells.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c039-->
- Blood serology: rising antistreptolysin O or anti-DNase B titers document a recent group A streptococcal infection.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c040-->
- The reference pages describe no special stain, IHC or molecular test.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Two to three weeks after the streptococcal infection, symptoms appear.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c041--> By then, throat swabs usually grow nothing.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c042--> Carditis, a major criterion, shows as heart failure, pericarditis, cardiomegaly or murmurs.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c043--> Polyarthritis and chorea, the joint and brain features, are also major criteria.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c044--> Erythema marginatum and subcutaneous nodules, the skin findings, are major criteria too.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c045--> Fever, arthralgia, raised inflammatory markers, ECG changes and a past attack count as minor features.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c046-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** No specific test exists for rheumatic fever.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c047--> The Jones criteria require two major criteria, or one major and two minor.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c048--> Proof of a recent streptococcal infection makes the diagnosis likelier.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c049-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens afterward?** Mortality in acute carditis is low.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c050--> Heart failure from myocarditis causes most deaths.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c051--> Most acute manifestations resolve in under 3 months.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c052--> Myocardium and pericardium usually heal without permanent damage.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c053--> The valves do not: months or years after even one attack, they may be found badly scarred.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c054--> Valve damage worsens progressively with recurrent attacks.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c055--> Recurrences are linked to streptococcal types new to the patient.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c056--> Soon after an attack, up to 65% may relapse; after 10 years, only 5%.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c057--> Antibiotics given early for strep throat protect against a first attack; against later attacks they protect less often.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c058--> Symptoms of the acute illness are managed with corticosteroids and salicylates, as nothing specific treats it.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c059--> Right ventricular hypertrophy is the late result of mitral stenosis, which first enlarges the left atrium and then congests the lungs.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c060-->
- Scarred valves attract bacteria, so bacteremia, for example during dental procedures, can cause infective endocarditis.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c061-->
- Thrombi on the wall of an atrium or ventricle occur in 40% of patients with rheumatic valvulopathy.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c062-->
- These thrombi can embolize and cause infarcts in various organs.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c063-->
- Rarely, a big stalked thrombus from the left atrial appendage plugs the mitral orifice, acting as a ball valve.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c064-->
- Heart failure complicates both mitral and aortic disease.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c065-->
- Fibrinous pericarditis is often followed by adhesive pericarditis, which rarely constricts.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c066-->

## High-yield summary

- **Group A streptococcal pharyngitis** precedes the illness by 2 to 3 weeks; **molecular mimicry** is the likely mechanism of injury.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c071-->
- Acute disease is a **pancarditis**: endocardium, myocardium and pericardium.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c072-->
- The **Aschoff body**, with **Anitschkow cells**, is the characteristic myocardial lesion.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c073-->
- Chronic disease is **mitral stenosis** with a "fish mouth" orifice, then aortic disease.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c074-->
- Diagnosis rests on the **Jones criteria**, since no specific test exists.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c075-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is it an infection of the heart?** No. It is an immune reaction that starts 2 to 3 weeks after the infection, when throat cultures are usually negative.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c076-->

**Is every layer scarred?** No. Myocardium and pericardium usually heal; the valves keep the damage.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c077-->

**Is the Aschoff body there from day one?** No. Symptoms precede it by several weeks; the earliest phase is a nonspecific myocarditis.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c078-->

## Mnemonics

**MAT-P:** Mitral, Aortic, Tricuspid, Pulmonic, the order in which rheumatic disease damages the valves.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c079-->

**PEM:** Pericardium, Endocardium, Myocardium: acute rheumatic carditis hits all three layers.<!--c:rheumatic-heart-disease-c080-->
