## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a pulmonary carcinoid tumor?** The neuroendocrine cells that normally sit in the bronchial lining are thought to give rise to lung carcinoids, which are divided into a typical and an atypical subtype.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c001--> In the United States, 2% to 3% of primary lung cancers are carcinoids.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Does smoking cause carcinoid tumors?** No: they are not related to cigarette smoking.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c003--> This card lists no other risk factor.

## Pathogenesis

**What do the tumor cells do, and what are tumorlets?** Most carcinoid tumors are endocrinologically silent.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c004--> A tumorlet is a small neuroendocrine nodule under 0.5 cm.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c005--> Tumorlets are usually chance findings without clinical meaning, and they can arise alongside interstitial fibrosis or small airway disease.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c006-->

## Gross morphology

**Where in the lung do they sit, and what do they look like?** A subpleural site is found in about one-third of cases, a central site in another third, and the mid-lung in the rest.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c007--> A central carcinoid usually lies largely inside the bronchus and bulges into its lumen like a polyp.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c008--> Size varies from 0.5 to 10 cm, and the mean diameter is 3.0 cm.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c009-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does a carcinoid look like, and what makes it atypical?** The nuclei have fine granular chromatin and the cytoplasm is pink and granular; the cells look alike and grow in organoid patterns.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c010--> A carcinoid can show rosettes or trabeculae among its architectural patterns.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c011--> When the pathologist counts 2 to 10 mitoses in 2 mm², the tumor is atypical, not typical.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c012--> Finding necrosis also moves a carcinoid into the atypical group.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c013-->

<aside class="edition">The grading cut-offs above come from one textbook edition, and the classification of lung neuroendocrine tumors has changed between editions. Check the current WHO classification before quoting a cut-off.</aside>

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** under the microscope, count mitoses and look for necrosis to separate atypical from typical carcinoid.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c014-->
- The tumor cells contain neuropeptides that are easy to demonstrate.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c015-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does a carcinoid present?** About half of patients have no symptoms at diagnosis, which fits the slow growth of the tumor.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c016--> A chance finding on a chest radiograph is a common way to discover it.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c017--> When symptoms occur, the most common ones are shortness of breath, coughing up blood, or pneumonia beyond a narrowed bronchus.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c018--> A central tumor can obstruct the bronchus, so the lung beyond it becomes inflamed.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c019--> Cushing syndrome from ACTH made by the tumor itself is one example of the hormone disorder that a small subset of patients develops.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c020--> Liver metastases are usually present when carcinoid syndrome occurs, which is the case in 1% of tumors.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c021--> Diagnosis averages age 55, though any age can be affected.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c023--> Among lung tumors of childhood, bronchial carcinoid is the most common.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c024-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis reached?** Imaging often first shows a mass on a chest radiograph.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c025--> The tissue diagnosis names the subtype by mitotic count and necrosis.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c026-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Which carcinoid does worse?** The atypical form behaves worse than the typical form.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c027--> About 15% of typical carcinoids have spread to regional nodes.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c028--> For atypical carcinoids the figure is 50%.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c029--> After surgery, 90% of patients with a typical carcinoid are alive at five years.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c030--> For an atypical carcinoid the figure is 60%.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c031-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Typical carcinoid | Lacks the mitotic and necrosis criteria of the atypical form.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c032--> | Mitotic count, necrosis |
| Atypical carcinoid | Necrosis, or a mitotic count of 2 to 10 in 2 mm².<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c033--> | Mitotic count, necrosis |
| Tumorlet | Neuroendocrine nodule under 0.5 cm, usually incidental.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c034--> | Nodule size |

## High-yield summary

- Bronchial neuroendocrine cells are thought to give rise to the two subtypes, **typical** and **atypical** carcinoid.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c035-->
- A carcinoid is **atypical** if it shows **necrosis** or a mitotic count of 2 to 10 in 2 mm².<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c036-->
- A **central** tumor grows into the bronchus and can cause **postobstructive** pneumonia.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c037-->
- Carcinoids are **not related to smoking**.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c038-->
- **Carcinoid syndrome** occurs in 1% of cases and usually goes with liver metastases; most carcinoids are **endocrinologically silent**.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c039-->
- Five-year survival after surgery is **90%** for typical and **60%** for atypical carcinoid.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c040-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a lung carcinoid caused by smoking?** No. It is not related to cigarette smoking.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c041-->

**Does every carcinoid cause carcinoid syndrome?** No. Most are endocrinologically silent, and the syndrome occurs in about 1%.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c042-->

**Is atypical carcinoid defined by mitoses alone?** No. Necrosis alone is enough as well.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c043-->

## Mnemonics

**MoN rule:** **M**itoses 2 to 10 per 2 mm² or **N**ecrosis means "**N**ot typical", so atypical.<!--c:lung-carcinoid-tumors-c044-->
