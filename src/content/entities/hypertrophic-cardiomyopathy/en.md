## Definition and epidemiology

**What is hypertrophic cardiomyopathy?** It is a heart muscle disease in which the left ventricle thickens although the workload on the heart does not account for it.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c001--> Pathogenic mutations are rarer than clinically recognized unexplained hypertrophy, which affects about 1 in 500 people.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it?** A single mutated gene, dominant in effect, lies behind most cases, and that gene encodes a protein of the sarcomere.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c003--> Eight genes account for most cases: MYH7, MYBPC3, TNNT2, TNNI3, TPM1, MYL2, MYL3 and ACTC1.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c004--> MYH7 and MYBPC3 together explain about half of all cases.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c005--> Unlike dilated cardiomyopathy, HCM rarely involves TTN mutations.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c006--> Glucose metabolism and clearance are affected by variants in PRKAB2, LAMP2 and GLA, non-sarcomere genes that are also linked to HCM.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c007--> People with clinically silent hypertrophy may carry sarcomere variants that, alone or together, apparently thicken the ventricle short of full-blown HCM.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c008-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a sarcomere mutation thicken the wall?** In a given gene, a loss-of-function mutation leads to dilated cardiomyopathy, whereas HCM mutations tend to gain function and make the muscle hypercontractile.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c009--> Contraction is stronger and relaxation poorer when the mutation sits in the head of myosin.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c010--> In TNNT2 variants that cause HCM, actin-activated myosin ATPase becomes more sensitive to calcium.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c011--> A reduced creatine phosphate to ATP ratio is one sign that myocardial energetics are disturbed.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c012--> A gene active only in myocytes can still change the surrounding tissue: myocyte-derived TGFβ and extra calcium signaling make fibroblasts and smooth muscle cells proliferate and the matrix expand.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c013--> More than 90% of MYH7 carriers are affected by their second decade, so penetrance is high.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c014--> Onset in MYBPC3 carriers can be delayed to the 50s or 60s.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the heart look like?** Heart size differs among genetic forms, but enlargement is a constant finding.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c016--> A thick wall surrounds a narrowed left ventricular cavity, slit-like in places.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c017--> Space in the lumen is taken by the papillary muscles and trabeculae, which stand out.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c018--> A septum to free wall thickness ratio above 1.5, meaning asymmetric septal thickening, is found in more than half of cases.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c019--> Rarer genetic forms thicken only the apex or the papillary muscles.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c020--> A thick septum may block aortic outflow by bulging into the outflow tract; this happens early in systole.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c021--> Where the anterior mitral leaflet strikes the septum in systole, an obstructed outflow tract typically shows a fibrous endocardial plaque.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c022--> Enlargement often affects both atria.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c023-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the key finding on the slide?** Myofiber disarray is the key histologic finding of HCM, and it is greatest in the septum.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c024--> Enlarged myocytes lie at oblique or right angles to their neighbors, not side by side, and their myofibrils are disordered too.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c025--> In HCM disarray is always extensive; it can also occur in other settings, such as infants with congenital heart defects.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c026--> The interstitium contains more cells, and its matrix is expanded.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c027--> Some small intramural coronary arteries show thickened, cell-rich walls.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c028-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and what is tested?**
- **How it is studied:** on histologic sections, look for myofiber disarray (most marked in the septum), extra interstitial cells and thick-walled small arteries.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c029-->
- **Molecular testing:** a disease-causing variant in these eight genes is found in about 60% of probands, more often with a family history or early onset.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c030-->

The pages used here do not cover specimen type, sampling, special stains, immunohistochemistry or electron microscopy.

## Clinical presentation

**Does it always cause symptoms?** No. The diagnosis often comes from screening the family of an affected member, and many patients have few symptoms, if any.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c031--> Onset in the third to fifth decades is frequent, but any age is possible.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c032--> MYBPC3 mutations account mainly for cases first detected in the elderly.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c033--> Syncope, angina and dyspnea can disable some patients.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c034--> Local ischemia from thickened small coronary arteries and faulty myocardial energy handling, plus dynamic outflow obstruction, explains the symptoms.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c035--> Contraction tends to be vigorous, or hyperdynamic.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c036--> End-diastolic pressure is raised because the remodeled ventricle is stiff and relaxes poorly.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c037--> The atria enlarge partly because of mitral regurgitation, which is common in HCM, particularly with outflow obstruction.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c038--> Atrial fibrillation, which mitral regurgitation makes more likely, worsens symptoms because the stiff ventricle fills poorly.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c039-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What must be excluded before calling it HCM?** HCM is a diagnosis of exclusion: hypertrophy from hypertension, a narrowed aortic valve or intense athletic conditioning has to be ruled out first.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c040--> The pages used here give no imaging or laboratory criteria.

## Complications and prognosis

**How dangerous is it?** Roughly 1% of patients die each year.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c041--> Sudden death accounts for roughly 6 in 10 deaths.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c042--> Even patients without symptoms can die suddenly.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c043--> At autopsy, about half of sudden deaths in young athletes prove to be unrecognized HCM.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c044--> Certain TNNT2 variants are especially dangerous; no other molecular marker has proved a useful predictor.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c045--> Heart failure from impaired systolic function can eventually develop, though the course is often stable for years.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c046--> About 10% of patients come to resemble dilated cardiomyopathy.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c047-->

**How is it treated?** Beta blockers and calcium channel blockers may help the ventricle relax; as negative inotropes they also weaken contraction and lessen outflow obstruction.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c048--> Neither partial septal excision nor alcohol-induced septal infarction removes the risk of sudden death, even though both can relieve obstructive symptoms.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c049-->

## High-yield summary

- Unexplained **left ventricular hypertrophy** is the hallmark.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c054-->
- Most cases are dominant **sarcomere gene** mutations, mainly MYH7 and MYBPC3.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c055-->
- The septum is often thicker than the free wall (**asymmetric septal hypertrophy**), and it can cause **outflow obstruction**.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c056-->
- The key microscopic finding is **myocyte disarray**, most marked in the septum.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c057-->
- Roughly 1 patient in 100 dies each year, and **sudden death** can occur even without symptoms.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c058-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is any thick left ventricle HCM?** No. Athletic training, aortic stenosis and hypertension must all be excluded first.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c059-->

**Is the septum always the thick part?** No. Asymmetric septal thickening occurs in more than half of cases, and some forms spare it.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c060-->

**Is disarray proof of HCM?** Not by itself. In HCM the disarray is extensive; in infants with congenital heart defects it can look similar.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c061-->

**Does relieving the obstruction remove the sudden death risk?** No. The risk remains after myectomy or septal ablation.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c062-->

**Are patients always symptomatic?** No. Many have few symptoms yet can still die suddenly.<!--c:hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-c063-->
