## Definition and epidemiology

**What is diffuse alveolar damage (DAD)?** Whatever the trigger, sudden injury to the alveolar lining and capillary endothelium can give one recurring picture: DAD.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c001--> Healthy-looking lungs can fail fast; severe DAD is called acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c002--> Both lungs look opaque on radiographs; they are stiff and blood oxygen is low.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What triggers DAD?** Many insults end in the same pattern, so DAD is a final common pathway.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c004--> All cause acute damage to alveolar epithelial and endothelial cells.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c005-->
- Shock of any origin, from blood loss, trauma or sepsis, often precedes ARDS.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c006-->
- Infections, including viral ones, can trigger it.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c007-->
- Acidic gastric contents burn the alveolar lining cells chemically.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c008-->
- Epithelium bears most of the damage from toxic gas or near-drowning.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c009-->
- Among cytotoxic drugs, bleomycin is best known; busulfan is another.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c010-->
- The herbicide paraquat can cause DAD after ingestion.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c011-->
- High-dose oxygen and radiation are thought to injure the lung through oxygen radicals.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c012-->

**Can the cause be read from the slide?** Histology seldom names the trigger, unless an infectious agent is seen.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c013--> DAD of unknown cause is called acute interstitial pneumonia (AIP).<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the alveolar wall fail?** The interstitium fills with protein-rich fluid leaking from injured capillaries.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c015--> Hyaline membranes are fibrin-rich protein deposits on injured walls, formed once loss of type I cells lets fluid flood the alveoli.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c016--> Type II pneumocytes multiply to replace the lining, guided by intact basement membranes.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c017-->
- Capillary endothelium may be injured by radicals and enzymes from neutrophils, which complement activation is thought to trap in the lung.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c018-->
- Neutrophils are not essential: ARDS occurs in severely neutropenic patients.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c019-->
- Fluid and protein pour into the alveoli once toxic gas or near-drowning opens the epithelial seal.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c020-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lungs look like?** In fatal DAD they hold almost no air and are heavy with edema.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c021-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the exudative phase show?** The exudative phase, within a week of the insult, has edema and hyaline membranes.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c022--> Plasma protein and debris from shed epithelial cells make up the pink, glassy hyaline membranes.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c023--> Seen from day two, they are most conspicuous after four to five days.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c024--> Macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells form the interstitial inflammation.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c025--> Small pulmonary vessels may hold fibrin thrombi.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c026-->

**And the organizing phase?** Fibroblasts multiplying in the alveolar walls mark it, starting about a week after injury.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c027--> No new hyaline membranes form, inflammation continues, and cuboidal type II pneumocytes cover the denuded septa.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c028--> TGF-β and PDGF, probably released by macrophages clearing membrane debris, drive fibroblast growth.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c029--> In chemotherapy-induced DAD, type II cells often have atypical, darkly stained nuclei.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c030-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Do special studies help?** Electron microscopy shows degenerating endothelial and type I cells, the earliest injury.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c031-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does ARDS start?** Rapid, labored breathing begins after a symptom-free interval of a few hours.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c032--> Blood gases show low pCO2 with hypoxemia.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c033--> Later, cyanosis appears.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c034--> Extra oxygen alone fails, so mechanical ventilation is needed.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c035-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What do imaging and exclusion add?** Radiographs show diffuse bilateral infiltrates.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c036--> AIP can be diagnosed only after drug toxicity, collagen vascular disease and infection are ruled out.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c037-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How does it end?** ARDS mortality is above 50%.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c038--> Full recovery restores normal lung function: the exudate is resorbed and the epithelium regrown.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c039--> Mild cases clear the loose fibrosis.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c040--> Severe DAD can end as honeycomb lung, a scarred lung riddled with cysts.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c041--> Scarring can impair breathing after severe ARDS, and some survivors develop pulmonary hypertension.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c042--> Chemotherapy-induced DAD can keep progressing after the drug is stopped.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c043-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Organizing pneumonia pattern | Loose fibroblastic polyps plug distal airways and alveoli, yet lung architecture is preserved.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c044--> | Histology |
| Alveolar proteinosis | Intact, barely inflamed interstitium; alveoli full of granular, surfactant-rich material.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c045--> | Electron microscopy shows tubular myelin.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c046--> |
| [[neonatal-respiratory-distress-syndrome\|Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome]] | Usually severely premature newborns with an immature surfactant system.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c047--> | Clinical setting |
| Diffuse pulmonary hemorrhage | Neutrophils infiltrate capillary walls; acute cases show many intra-alveolar red cells.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c048--> | Immunofluorescence pattern |

## High-yield summary

- **ARDS** is the clinical form of **DAD**, acute alveolar epithelial and endothelial injury.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c049-->
- The **exudative phase** shows edema and **hyaline membranes**.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c050-->
- The **organizing phase** starts about a week in, with **fibroblast** proliferation.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c051-->
- Causes: **sepsis**, **shock**, **aspiration**, toxins, drugs, viruses.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c052-->
- Idiopathic DAD is **acute interstitial pneumonia**.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c053-->
- Severe disease can end in **honeycomb lung**.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c054-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does DAD have one specific cause?** No. Many insults lead to it, and the slide rarely shows which.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c055-->

**Are neutrophils required?** No. ARDS develops in neutropenic patients.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c056-->

**Is the alveolar framework destroyed?** No. Basement membranes survive and guide type II cells.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c057-->

**Is more oxygen enough?** No. It does not restore oxygenation alone.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c058-->

## Mnemonics

**LEAK, LINE, SCAR:** fluid and membranes leak, type II cells line the septa, fibroblasts scar the wall.<!--c:diffuse-alveolar-damage-c059-->
