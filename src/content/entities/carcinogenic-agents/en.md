## Definition and epidemiology
**Which agents cause cancer?**
- **Chemical:** the lung cancer epidemic stems mainly from cigarettes and other burnt organic matter.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c001-->
- Epoxides that bind proteins and nucleic acids form when P450 enzymes act on polycyclic hydrocarbons, many in tobacco smoke.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c002-->
- *Aspergillus flavus* can contaminate peanuts and grains with aflatoxin B1.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c003-->
- Aflatoxin B1 is a very potent liver carcinogen; its epoxide metabolite binds DNA.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c004-->
- **Physical:** pyrimidine dimers are the typical ultraviolet lesion; no other carcinogen causes them.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c005-->
- Asbestos causes malignant mesothelioma of pleura and peritoneum.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c007-->
- **Viral:** HPV is linked to carcinomas of the cervix, anus and vulva.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c009-->
- EBV is linked to Burkitt lymphoma and nasopharyngeal carcinoma.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c010-->
- Chronic HBV and HCV infection is linked to hepatocellular carcinoma.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c011-->
- Adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma stems from HTLV-1, the only retrovirus with a known human cancer.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c012-->
- HHV8 is linked to Kaposi sarcoma.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c013-->
- Merkel cell carcinoma, a skin tumor, is tied to Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV).<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c014-->
- **Bacterial:** *Helicobacter pylori* is a gastric carcinogen.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c015-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**How is carcinogenicity shown?** Exposure studies struggle with hard dose estimates and long latency.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c019-->
Carcinogenicity tracks mutagenicity, so assays such as the Ames test screen chemicals.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c020-->
A mutagen is usually, but not always, a carcinogen.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c021-->
No agent is a proven human carcinogen until independent studies confirm it.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c022-->

## Differential diagnosis
| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical carcinogen | Different chemicals hit different tissues, often one organ only.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c023--> | Mutagenicity assay |
| Ultraviolet light | In pale-skinned people, skin cancers arise mainly on sun-exposed areas.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c024--> | Exposure history |
| Asbestos | Mesothelioma follows a long latent period.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c025--> | Exposure history |
| Oncogenic DNA virus | Oncogenic DNA viruses neutralize Rb and p53 by binding them with their own proteins.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c026--> | Viral testing |
| *H. pylori* | CagA pathways converge on NF-κB, fueling inflammation, tumor growth and invasion.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c027--> | Tissue testing |

## High-yield summary
- **Procarcinogens**, which enzymes must first activate, make up most organic carcinogens.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c028-->
- **Pyrimidine dimers** are specific to UV; **nucleotide excision repair** removes them.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c029-->
- **Smoking plus asbestos** raises lung cancer risk far above either alone.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c030-->
- Only one retrovirus, **HTLV-1**, has a known role in human cancer.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c031-->
- Kaposi sarcoma seems to need **HHV8**, but the virus alone is not enough.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c032-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Does HHV8 or MCPyV infection mean cancer?** No. Most infected people never develop the cancer.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c033-->

**Is HCV proven directly oncogenic?** No. Direct oncogenicity is unproven; liver cancer usually arises in cirrhosis two or more decades after infection.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c034-->

**Are all chemical carcinogens inert until activated?** No. Some, like nitrogen mustard, act directly.<!--c:carcinogenic-agents-c035-->
