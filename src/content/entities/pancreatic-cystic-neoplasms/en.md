## Definition and epidemiology

**Which tumors form the pancreatic cystic neoplasms, and which of them can become cancer?** Four types matter: serous cystic neoplasm, mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN), intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) and solid pseudopapillary neoplasm (SPN).<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c001--> Serous cystic neoplasms are almost always benign.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c002--> Invasive carcinoma is sometimes found together with an IPMN.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c003--> It can also accompany an MCN at times.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c004--> SPN is a very low-grade malignancy.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c005-->

- Women develop serous cystic neoplasms three times as often as men; the tumor usually arises in adults, typically in the body or tail.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c006-->
- Diagnosis of an IPMN usually comes late in adult life.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c007-->
- The body and tail are the favored sites of MCN, and nearly all patients are middle-aged women.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c008-->
- Nearly every SPN patient is an adolescent girl or a young woman.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c009-->

<aside class="edition">Classification and grading of these tumors change between editions of the reference classification. Check the edition used by your course before quoting a grade.</aside>

## Etiology and risk factors

**What raises the risk of these tumors?** Von Hippel–Lindau syndrome makes a serous cystic neoplasm more likely.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**What goes wrong at the molecular level?** In von Hippel–Lindau syndrome the loss of VHL leaves growth factors undegraded.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c011--> This syndrome can give rise to many tumors, including cystic lesions of the pancreas and kidney.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c012--> Many IPMNs have a molecular profile like PanIN and ductal adenocarcinoma.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c013--> Some IPMNs do not look like PanIN, probably because they develop through other molecular pathways.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c014--> MCNs share genetic alterations with PanIN and with invasive ductal adenocarcinoma.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c015--> The gene for β-catenin, CTNNB1, is mutated in most SPNs.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What do these lesions look like to the naked eye?**
- The diameter of a serous cystadenoma can be anywhere between 1 and 25 cm.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c017-->
- Tiny calcifications sometimes sit inside the star-shaped central scar that serous cystadenomas often show.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c018-->
- Mucus fills the pancreatic ducts of an IPMN, which are widened to more than 5 mm.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c019-->
- The pancreatic head is the usual site of an IPMN.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c020-->
- The disease can be confined to one spot, scattered over several, or spread along the whole duct.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c021-->
- Some IPMNs are limited to the small peripheral (branch) ducts, whereas others sit in the main duct.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c022-->
- An MCN is a cystic mass with one or several chambers.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c023-->
- It may reach 10 cm.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c024-->
- It has no connection with the ducts of the pancreas.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c025-->
- An SPN is solid and well circumscribed.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c026-->
- Blood and necrotic debris fill the large cystic areas that often develop inside it.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c027-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the lining look like under the microscope?**
- Clear, glycogen-filled cells form one layer around each serous cyst, and tough fibrous tissue surrounds the cysts.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c028-->
- In an IPMN the tumor cells in the duct make mucin and form many papillae that protrude inward.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c029-->
- <aside class="edition">In this reference edition, dysplasia in IPMN can be low-grade or high-grade, categorized as it is for PanIN.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c030--> Check the current classification before quoting a grade.</aside>
- Under the mucin-producing lining of an MCN lies a cell-rich stroma that resembles ovarian stroma.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c031-->
- The epithelium of an MCN may be more or less atypical, as in IPMN.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c032-->
- Uniform SPN cells form pseudopapillae and also lie in loose sheets.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c033-->
- The pseudopapillae have vascular cores.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c034-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E the lining and the stroma separate the types: glycogen-rich clear cells (serous), mucinous epithelium over ovarian-type stroma (MCN), mucinous papillae inside a dilated duct (IPMN), uniform cells with pseudopapillae (SPN).<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c035-->
- In most SPNs the β-catenin protein is abnormally located in the nucleus.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c036-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do patients present?** Many patients with a serous cystic neoplasm have no symptoms; when present, they are vague and come from local mass effect.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c037--> An IPMN comes to attention either as an incidental finding or in a patient who has chronic pancreatitis.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c038--> An SPN can be mistaken clinically for other malignant pancreatic tumors.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c039-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How are these lesions recognized and managed?** A "sunburst" pattern on imaging points to a serous neoplasm: it comes from the stellate central scar, which sometimes contains microcalcifications.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c040--> On imaging, an IPMN limited to the branch ducts may be mistaken for a cystic lesion.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c041--> Surgeons often remove an IPMN that is big or looks unusual on scans, since an invasive cancer may be hiding in it.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c042--> Symptoms, or a clinical worry that the lesion could be malignant, are the occasional reasons to resect a serous tumor.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c043-->

## Complications and prognosis

**Which of them can turn into cancer, and how do patients fare?**
- Up to a third of IPMNs harbor a focus of invasive adenocarcinoma.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c044-->
- Patients whose MCN is noninvasive do very well after complete resection.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c045-->
- Complete surgical removal cures nearly all SPNs, which grow slowly.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c046-->
- About 10% of SPNs metastasize, usually to the liver.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c047-->
- Survival of many years is usual even after metastases.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c048-->

## High-yield summary

- **Serous** cystic neoplasms are almost always **benign**.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c059-->
- A **sunburst** central scar on imaging points to a serous cystic neoplasm.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c060-->
- **von Hippel–Lindau** syndrome raises the risk of serous cystic neoplasm.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c061-->
- **MCN** is defined by **ovarian-type stroma** under a mucin-secreting epithelium.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c062-->
- MCN occurs almost only in **middle-aged women**, in the body and tail.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c063-->
- **IPMN** involves the **main** duct or the **branch** ducts.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c064-->
- Up to **one-third** of IPMNs contain **invasive adenocarcinoma**.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c065-->
- **SPN** is a very low-grade malignancy of **young women**.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c066-->
- Most **SPN**s carry a **CTNNB1** mutation and nuclear **β-catenin**.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c067-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Are all pancreatic cystic neoplasms precancerous?** No. Serous cystic neoplasms are almost always benign.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c068--> IPMN and MCN, instead, can occur together with an invasive carcinoma.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c069-->

**Does a mucinous cystic neoplasm connect to the pancreatic duct?** No. It has no link with the ducts.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c070--> Duct involvement is the hallmark of IPMN: the ducts widen and the lining is mucin-producing tumor epithelium.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c071-->

**Is an IPMN always in the main duct?** No. Some are confined to branch ducts, where imaging makes them resemble ordinary cysts.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c072-->

**Is a solid pseudopapillary neoplasm benign?** Not strictly: it is a very low-grade malignancy.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c073--> About one case in ten metastasizes, usually to the liver.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c074-->

## Mnemonics

**Serous = Safe; Mucinous and IPMN = Might turn malignant; SPN = Slow, in Sisters (young women).** The risk ladder: serous almost always benign, IPMN and MCN may be linked to invasive carcinoma, SPN a slow low-grade malignancy of young women.<!--c:pancreatic-cystic-neoplasms-c075-->
