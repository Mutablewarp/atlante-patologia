## Definition and epidemiology

**Which congenital heart defects cause cyanosis or obstruct flow?** Most cyanotic congenital heart disease arises from a shunt running right to left; tetralogy of Fallot and tricuspid atresia are examples.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c001--> Among infants who die of a cyanotic defect in the first year, transposition of the great arteries is the cause in most.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c002--> The obstructive lesions are coarctation of the aorta, pulmonary stenosis and congenital aortic stenosis.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes these defects?** Persistent truncus arteriosus, tetralogy and transposition all trace back to abnormal development of the spiral septum, which normally divides the common arterial trunk.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c004--> Male sex and maternal diabetes both raise the frequency of transposition.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c005--> Some people with Down or DiGeorge syndrome have tetralogy.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c006--> Alagille syndrome, mostly linked to JAG1 mutations, includes heart disease, most often tetralogy, and NOTCH1 mutations occur in some tetralogy families.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c007--> Hypoplastic left heart has a complex, partly genetic background.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c008--> Some patients with trisomy 13, 18 or 21, or a 22q11 deletion, have double-outlet right ventricle.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c009--> Turner syndrome and bicuspid aortic valve accompany coarctation, which is also more frequent in men.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c010--> Williams syndrome, which deletes part of 7q11 including the elastin gene, goes with peripheral pulmonary stenosis and supravalvular aortic stenosis.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these defects disturb the circulation?** Two independent parallel circuits, systemic and pulmonary, form when the aorta is connected to the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery to the left, as in transposition.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c012--> Nearly all infants with transposition have an ASD, often with a VSD or PDA, and this mixing keeps them alive.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c013--> Tight pulmonary stenosis makes right ventricular output exit through the VSD into the aorta, desaturating arterial blood.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c014--> In tricuspid atresia, blood crosses from right atrium to left through a patent foramen ovale.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c015--> In total anomalous drainage the pulmonary veins never reach the left atrium directly, so an ASD or patent foramen ovale keeps the infant alive.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c016--> The aorta receives blood only from the pulmonary trunk through a PDA in hypoplastic left heart, since the right ventricle supplies the entire output.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c017--> Normally the posterior aortic shelf disappears after birth; when it persists beside the ductus, the common juxtaductal coarctation results.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c018--> In infantile preductal coarctation the isthmus fails to widen in late gestation and is still narrow at birth.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c019-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the heart look like?** Four findings define tetralogy: a VSD, pulmonary stenosis, right ventricular hypertrophy, and an aorta that overrides the defect from a rightward position.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c020--> The hypertrophied heart is boot-shaped.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c021--> Persistent truncus is one vessel sitting over a VSD; the aorta, pulmonary arteries and coronary arteries all arise from it.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c022--> Tricuspid atresia means no tricuspid valve, usually with a VSD.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c023--> The left-sided valves are small or atretic in hypoplastic left heart, and the ascending aorta and left ventricle are underdeveloped too.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c024--> D-transposition has the aorta arising from the right ventricle, and all types place it in front of the pulmonary artery.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c025--> At the ductus, just past the origin of the left subclavian artery, the aorta is focally narrowed in nearly all coarctations.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c026--> Poststenotic dilation of the pulmonary artery can develop over years; in isolated stenosis the fused cusps form a funnel.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c027--> Subvalvular aortic stenosis is an obstruction of the outflow tract just under the aortic valve, made of fibrous tissue or muscle.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c028-->

## Microscopic morphology

**Is histology useful here?** Little; these are mainly anatomic diagnoses. A dissecting aneurysm that ruptures is a risk in coarctation, since the aortic media degenerates.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c029--> Thickening and calcification develop in a bicuspid aortic valve as the years pass.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c030-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do these children present?** Squatting eases breathlessness in children with tetralogy, who also grow slowly, are cyanotic and tire with dyspnea on exertion.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c032--> The helpful PDA of tetralogy can close on its own, and cyanosis then deepens.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c033--> Cyanosis comes late in truncus arteriosus, with pulmonary vascular disease; before that, heavy lung flow causes heart failure and repeated respiratory infections.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c034--> In tricuspid atresia with a small VSD the murmur is loud, and cyanosis is worse because too little blood reaches the lungs.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c035--> Symptoms of hypoplastic left heart begin only when pulmonary vascular resistance falls; before that, a newborn may look well.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c036--> Severe hypoxemia and heart failure follow total anomalous pulmonary venous drainage.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c037--> Nosebleeds and headaches can occur with the hypertension proximal to a coarctation, which also leads to left ventricular hypertrophy.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c038--> Low pressure below the narrowing makes the legs cold and pale.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c039--> Clubbing and cyanosis appear when severe pulmonary stenosis combines with a patent foramen ovale, because blood shunts right to left.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c040--> Symptoms of bicuspid aortic stenosis usually wait until adulthood, and many children have none.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c041--> In severe aortic obstruction, ventricular arrhythmias can cause sudden death, and exertional dyspnea and angina start early.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c042-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings point to coarctation?** Blood pressure that differs between the arms and legs is the classic sign of coarctation.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c043--> Rib notching on a chest film comes from intercostal arteries that enlarge under high pressure to bypass the obstruction.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c044-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What does surgery change?** Total surgical correction of tetralogy gives an excellent long-term outcome, whereas without treatment the outlook is dismal.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c045--> An arterial-switch operation early in life can correct transposition.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c046--> Almost every infant with hypoplastic left heart dies within the first month unless surgery or transplantation intervenes.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c047--> Repairing truncus arteriosus is effective when done early, before significant pulmonary vascular change.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c048--> Tricuspid atresia is managed by staged palliation.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c049--> Heart failure and aortic dissection are complications of coarctation.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c050--> Resection of the narrowed segment is the surgical treatment of coarctation; balloon dilation has been done too.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c051-->

**Which lesions are easily confused?**

| Taussig–Bing malformation | Behaving like transposition with a VSD, this is a double-outlet right ventricle whose VSD lies under an overriding pulmonary artery.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c053--> | Origin of each great vessel |

## High-yield summary

- **Tetralogy of Fallot** is a VSD with an overriding aorta, plus pulmonary stenosis and right ventricular hypertrophy.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c056-->
- **Transposition** survival depends on an ASD, VSD or PDA that joins its two parallel circuits.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c057-->
- **Coarctation**: **rib notching** on the chest film and a pressure gap between arms and legs.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c058-->
- **Hypoplastic left heart** depends on a PDA for systemic flow.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c059-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a PDA always harmful here?** No. In tetralogy it is helpful, because the lungs would otherwise get too little blood.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c060-->

**Is corrected transposition symptomatic?** No, when it is the only malformation.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-cyanotic-and-obstructive-c061-->
