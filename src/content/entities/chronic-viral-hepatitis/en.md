## Definition and epidemiology

**What is chronic viral hepatitis?** A hepatitis virus keeps the liver inflamed and necrotic for over six months.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c001--> HBV and HCV are the viruses that mainly cause it.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c002--> Hepatitis A never becomes chronic.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c003--> Chronic HBV infection is common in Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa but uncommon in the US and Western Europe.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c004--> Most people awaiting a liver transplant have HCV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c005--> A recombinant vaccine protects very well against HBV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which viruses cause it, and how are they caught?** HBV is a DNA virus, whereas HCV and HDV are RNA viruses.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c007--> Anyone with HDV also has HBV, since HDV cannot assemble without HBsAg; it comes with HBV (coinfection) or after it (superinfection).<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c008--> Carrier mothers, unsafe injections and intimate contact spread HBV, and only blood, saliva and semen hold infectious virus.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c009--> HCV now spreads mainly through infected blood, chiefly unsafe drug injection, since donor screening ended transfusion cases.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c010--> Infection at birth usually persists, whereas most adults clear HBV: the younger the patient, the likelier chronicity.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c011--> In chronic HBV, progression is likelier with a precore promoter mutation, genotype C or F, high viremia, older age or male sex.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c012--> In chronic HCV, HIV or HBV coinfection, alcohol misuse, old age and male sex raise the risk of cirrhosis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c013-->

## Pathogenesis

**Does the virus itself kill the hepatocytes?** No: neither virus kills cells directly, and many carriers, with a large HBV load for years, show no liver injury.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c014--> Most HBV hepatocyte destruction is by CD8+ cytotoxic T cells recognizing viral antigens on the infected cell.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c015--> HCV probably harms the liver when cytotoxic T cells attack infected cells.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c016--> Even strong antiviral drugs cannot clear HBV, because its cccDNA remains in the nucleus.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c017--> HCV mutates constantly, dodging antibodies; weak cellular immunity may add to persistence.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c018-->

**How does chronic hepatitis B evolve?** It passes through three phases, and patients can move forward or back.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c019-->
- Immune tolerant: aminotransferases are normal and inflammation is slight despite a positive HBeAg and very high viral DNA.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c020-->
- Immune active: enzymes rise with hepatocyte death and viremia; cirrhosis and carcinoma often start here.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c021-->
- Inactive: very low risk, normal enzymes, low viral DNA, anti-HBe present and HBeAg absent, but reversion to the active phase is possible.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c022-->

**What do HDV and immune complexes add?** Catching HDV on top of chronic HBV usually worsens the existing hepatitis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c023--> Circulating HBsAg–anti-HBs complexes can injure organs outside the liver.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c024-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Biopsies range from a few inflamed portal tracts to cirrhosis following extensive death of hepatocytes and scarring.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c025-->
- Portal tracts hold macrophages, plasma cells and lymphocytes; ductule proliferation is a generic reaction.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c026-->
- In **interface hepatitis**, the portal-lobular boundary turns jagged where inflammation eats away the limiting plate.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c027-->
- The lobule shows focal necrosis and inflammation, with swollen Kupffer cells and a few acidophilic bodies.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c028-->
- **Ground-glass hepatocytes** in chronic HBV have smooth ER packed with HBsAg, making each cell big, with hazy pink cytoplasm.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c029-->
- Portal **lymphoid aggregates**, sometimes true follicles with reactive centers, are frequent in HCV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c030-->
- Cirrhosis can end a sequence that starts when interface hepatitis destroys periportal cells: collagen fills in, tracts turn star-shaped and scars join them.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c031-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** liver biopsy, useful in HCV because normal aminotransferases do not exclude significant fibrosis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c032-->
- On routine H&E, look for portal infiltrates, interface hepatitis, ground-glass cells (HBV) and lymphoid follicles (HCV).<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c033-->
- Reticulin staining best shows the connective-tissue bands left where bridging necrosis has collapsed the stroma.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c034-->
- Blood tests add HBV DNA level, which separates the HBV phases, and HCV RNA, which shows circulating virus.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c035-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Acute HCV is usually mild or silent, so it rarely reaches a doctor.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c036--> Inactive HBV carriers have no symptoms.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c037--> Most patients with chronic hepatitis C have mild disease for ten years or more.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c038--> Glomerulonephritis, cryoglobulinemia, polyarteritis and a serum sickness–like illness can complicate chronic HBV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c039--> Chronic HCV is also linked to B-cell lymphoma, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis, mononeuritis multiplex, sicca syndrome and mixed cryoglobulinemia with vasculitis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c040--> Fulminant, often lethal hepatitis can follow HDV coinfection, mostly in people who inject drugs.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c041-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which results define the infection and its stage?**
- HBsAg that persists without anti-HBs marks chronic HBV rather than recovery.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c042-->
- Anti-HBs marks recovery and lifelong immunity.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c043-->
- Anti-HBc shows only that HBV was met before; it is not protective, and the virus can still be present.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c044-->
- Serum HBeAg marks the most infectious stage, with intense viral replication.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c045-->
- HCV RNA appears in blood within weeks of infection.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c046-->
- Anti-HCV appears later and persists, but it does not clear the infection.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c047-->
- Rising aminotransferases show liver injury.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c048-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the outcomes?** Most acute HCV infections become chronic, and after decades only a minority reach cirrhosis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c049--> Chronic HBV raises the risk of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c050--> Carcinoma risk is raised even in the immune tolerant phase, since HBV DNA integrates into the host genome.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c051--> In HCV cirrhosis, up to 5% of patients develop hepatocellular carcinoma each year.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c052--> Coexisting HBV, hemochromatosis, α1-antitrypsin deficiency or alcohol-related liver damage makes HCV liver disease worse.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c053--> Undetectable HCV RNA (sustained virologic response) markedly lowers carcinoma risk.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c054--> Current HCV antiviral combinations cure almost all treated patients.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c055-->

## High-yield summary

- **HBV** and **HCV** are the main causes of chronic viral hepatitis; **HDV** needs HBsAg, and **HAV** never becomes chronic.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c060-->
- Injury is immune-mediated: **CD8+ T cells** kill infected hepatocytes.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c061-->
- **Ground-glass hepatocytes** reflect HBsAg stored in smooth endoplasmic reticulum (HBV).<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c062-->
- **Lymphoid aggregates** in portal tracts point to HCV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c063-->
- **Interface hepatitis** destroys the limiting plate and drives periportal fibrosis toward cirrhosis.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c064-->
- Persistent **HBsAg** without anti-HBs means chronic HBV; **HCV RNA** shows active HCV infection.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c065-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does HBV kill hepatocytes directly?** No. The immune response does most of the damage.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c066-->

**Can hepatitis A become chronic?** No. Hepatitis A leaves no carriers, and recovery brings lifelong immunity.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c067-->

**Can HDV cause hepatitis on its own?** No. It needs HBsAg, so it is always found with HBV.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c068-->

**Are ground-glass cells typical of hepatitis C?** No. They mark chronic hepatitis B.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c069--> Portal lymphoid follicles are especially associated with hepatitis C.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c070-->

**Do normal aminotransferases rule out serious disease?** No. Patients with HCV can have significant fibrosis, even cirrhosis, with normal enzymes.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c071-->

## Mnemonics

**B is Blurry, C is Clustered, D is Dependent.** B has blurry ground-glass cells, C has clustered lymphoid follicles, and D depends on the HBsAg coat of B.<!--c:chronic-viral-hepatitis-c072-->
