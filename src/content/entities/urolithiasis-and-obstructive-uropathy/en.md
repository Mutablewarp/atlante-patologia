## Definition and epidemiology

**What are urinary stones, and what is obstructive uropathy?** Location names the stone: nephrolithiasis in the renal collecting system, urolithiasis elsewhere in the urinary tract.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c001--> Men form them more often than women.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c002--> Hydronephrosis, a swollen collecting system, can follow any structural or functional block to urine drainage: this is obstructive uropathy.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Why do stones form, and what blocks the ureter?** Infection and metabolic disease change what stones are made of, and so does geography.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c004--> In uric acid and cystine stones, the urinary excretion of the main component is raised.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c005--> Calcium stones often come with urinary calcium excess but normal blood calcium.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c006--> With high uric acid, calcium salts deposit on urate crystals, so mixed stones are common.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c007-->
- Stones, clots and ureteral tumors are examples of causes that block the ureter from inside.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c008-->
- Pregnancy and endometriosis can compress the ureter from outside.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c009-->
- Local invasion or retroperitoneal nodal spread from gut and gynecologic cancers is the usual tumor route to ureteral compression.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c010-->
- Prostate, bladder or urethral disease can obstruct the ureters from below, as in a neurogenic bladder.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c011-->
- Congenital ureteropelvic junction obstruction often comes with other urinary tract anomalies (see [[congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract]]).<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c012-->
- Congenital megaureter has no peristalsis and is wide and winding throughout.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c013-->
- Ormond disease, the idiopathic form of retroperitoneal fibrosis, only rarely obstructs the ureter.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c014-->
- Surgery, radiation or drugs can lead to secondary retroperitoneal fibrosis.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c015-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do infection, blockage and inflammation connect?** Alkaline urine from urea-splitting bacteria such as Proteus promotes struvite and apatite stones.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c016--> Pyelonephritis is more likely in a kidney that is already hydronephrotic (see [[pyelonephritis]]).<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c017--> Ureteritis (ureteral inflammation) follows descending infection or reflux from below.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c018--> It often accompanies obstruction.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c019-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the stones and the obstructed kidney look like?**
- Calcium stones, the largest group, hold oxalate, phosphate or both; oxalate ones are hard, sometimes dark.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c020-->
- Phosphate stones are less hard and lighter than oxalate ones.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c021-->
- Infection stones range from hard to soft and crumbly.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c022-->
- A stone casting the pelvis and calyces is a staghorn calculus.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c023-->
- A urate stone is yellow, with a smooth surface.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c024-->
- A cystine stone is pure cystine, though calcium phosphate may coat it.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c025-->
- Size runs from gravel to stones expanding the whole pelvis.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c026-->

When both sides are obstructed, the whole collecting system balloons and the kidney tissue can waste badly.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c027-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes appear as hydronephrosis develops?** In early hydronephrosis, the collecting ducts are the first structures to widen visibly.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c028--> Next, the convoluted tubules dilate.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c029--> In late disease the proximal tubules disappear after becoming widely dilated.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c030--> Glomeruli are usually spared.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c031--> The parenchyma wastes away when pelvic and calyceal dilation persists.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c032-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Is any special test needed?** IgG4-positive plasma cells infiltrate the fibrotic lesions of IgG4-related retroperitoneal fibrosis.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c033-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do stones and obstruction present?** A stone may be well tolerated.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c034--> Renal colic, severe flank pain, begins when a stone passes into the ureter.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c035--> Mucosal erosion by stones can produce hematuria.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c036--> Postrenal acute renal failure results when both sides are acutely obstructed.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c037--> Obstruction of one side often causes no symptoms.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c038--> Infection stones often bring urosepsis, perinephric abscess and persistent infection.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c039-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which stone is invisible on a plain radiograph?** Radiolucency separates pure uric acid stones from calcium stones.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c040--> Uric acid stones usually occur without hyperuricemia or gout.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c041--> Hereditary cystinuria is the only setting for cystine stones.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c042-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What happens if the blockage stays?** Early recognition matters: many acute causes are reversible.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c043--> An unrelieved obstructed kidney atrophies.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c044--> Chronic renal failure follows when both sides stay obstructed.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c045--> Distal causes such as prostatic disease affect both kidneys; proximal ones, one.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c046--> Lithotripsy and endoscopic removal now work for large stones, which once called for open surgery.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c047--> Corticosteroids may help in IgG4-related retroperitoneal fibrosis.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c048-->

## High-yield summary

- Stone types: **calcium**, **infection (struvite)**, **uric acid**, **cystine**.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c053-->
- **Proteus** alkalinizes urine, favoring struvite and apatite and a **staghorn** cast.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c054-->
- Pure **uric acid** stones are **radiolucent**.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c055-->
- Early **hydronephrosis** dilates collecting ducts first; **glomeruli** are spared.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c056-->
- **Bilateral** acute obstruction causes acute renal failure.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c057-->
- **Ureteritis** arises from descending or reflux infection and often accompanies obstruction.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c058-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do all stones show on radiographs?** No. Pure uric acid stones are radiolucent.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c059-->

**Does a stone always hurt?** No. Stones may be well tolerated, and unilateral obstruction is often silent.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c060-->

**Is hydronephrosis always irreversible?** No. Many acute obstructions are reversible.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c061-->

**Does every uric acid stone mean gout?** No. Most patients have neither gout nor hyperuricemia.<!--c:urolithiasis-and-obstructive-uropathy-c062-->
