## Definition and epidemiology

**What are epididymitis and orchitis?** Most epididymitis is bacterial; the epididymis becomes inflamed, either suddenly or over a long course.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c001--> Orchitis means an inflamed testis, again acute or chronic.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c002--> It can occur together with epididymal disease (epididymo-orchitis) or by itself.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which organisms cause it, and does age matter?**
- When a young man gets acute bacterial epididymitis, the cause is typically gonorrhea or sexually acquired Chlamydia.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c004-->
- The commoner cause in older men is E. coli that spreads from a urinary tract infection.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c005-->
- Most bacterial orchitis is Gram-negative and usually comes with epididymitis; a preceding urinary infection is common.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c006-->
- Orchitis complicates mumps in 20% of affected men.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c007-->
- Patients with established pulmonary and renal tuberculosis are the usual ones to get tuberculous epididymitis, which is now uncommon.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c008-->
- Syphilis can also cause orchitis.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c009-->
- Autoimmune disease can cause orchitis too.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c010-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do germs and immune reactions reach the testis?**
- Ascending infection is the usual origin of epididymo-orchitis.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c011-->
- Orchitis without epididymitis is usually bloodborne.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c012-->
- A spermatic granuloma forms when sperm escape from their normal pathways and set off a strong inflammatory reaction.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c013-->
- Trauma that ruptures the epididymal ducts may be one contributing factor.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c014-->
- Type IV, cell-mediated hypersensitivity is the proposed mechanism of granulomatous orchitis of unknown cause.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c015-->

## Gross morphology

**What can be felt or seen?**
- Caseating granulomas run together in tuberculous epididymitis, giving a palpably enlarged epididymis and a beaded vas deferens.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c016-->
- Bacterial orchitis can also lead to pus and fibrosis around the testis, or to an abscess within it.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c017-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the slide show in each form?**
- Neutrophils are abundant inside the ducts in recent bacterial epididymitis, which looks like acute suppurative inflammation.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c018-->
- In long-standing epididymitis, macrophages join an infiltrate of lymphocytes and plasma cells.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c019-->
- Spermatic granuloma shows macrophages that have taken up sperm among many sperm fragments and mixed inflammatory cells.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c020-->
- Mumps (viral) orchitis interrupts spermatogenesis; mononuclear cells infiltrate the interstitium and in places enter tubule lumens.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c021-->
- Syphilitic orchitis takes one of two forms: gummas, or perivascular interstitial inflammation that includes plasma cells.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c022-->
- In granulomatous orchitis the granulomas do not caseate, and none contains organisms or sperm remnants.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c023-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** under the microscope, check whether granulomas caseate and whether sperm remnants or organisms are present; this separates the granulomatous forms.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c024-->

The reference pages name no special stain, immunostain or molecular test for these diseases.

## Clinical presentation

**What brings the patient to the doctor?**
- Epididymitis presents with a tender, painful scrotum, with or without fever.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c025-->
- One testis is most often the one affected in mumps orchitis, which brings pain and swelling.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c026-->
- Slow hardening or sudden painful swelling of a testis in midlife suggests granulomatous orchitis.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c027-->
- Weeks or months of scrotal pain and swelling are common in spermatic granuloma.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c028-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the type decided?** The granulomas of idiopathic granulomatous orchitis do not caseate, and no organism or sperm debris explains them.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c029-->

The reference pages give no laboratory or imaging criteria.

## Complications and prognosis

**What can be lost?**
- A frequent route to acquired infertility in men is gonorrheal epididymitis.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c030-->
- The infected ducts eventually become blocked by fibrosis when chronic epididymitis persists.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c031-->
- A spermatic granuloma can end in infertility: the inflammation scars the surrounding tissue and obstructs the ducts.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c032-->
- Mumps orchitis destroys seminiferous epithelium.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c033-->
- The number of seminiferous tubules destroyed in granulomatous orchitis varies.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c034-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Tuberculous epididymitis ([[tuberculosis]]) | Caseating granulomas and an enlarged epididymis, usually with lung and kidney disease.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c035--> | Histology |
| Granulomatous orchitis | Granulomas that do not caseate, with no organisms or sperm remnants.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c036--> | Histology |
| Syphilitic orchitis ([[syphilis]]) | Perivascular inflammation that includes plasma cells, or gummas.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c037--> | Histology |
| Spermatic granuloma | Sperm fragments and sperm-laden macrophages in the epididymis.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c038--> | Histology |
| Bacterial epididymitis | Neutrophils in the ducts; sexually acquired organisms or E. coli.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c039--> | Histology |
| Testicular malakoplakia | Looks like malakoplakia in other organs; E. coli infection is the typical association.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c040--> | Histology |

## High-yield summary

- **Young men:** gonorrhea or Chlamydia; **older men:** E. coli from the urinary tract.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c041-->
- **Tuberculous epididymitis** gives a beaded vas and caseating granulomas.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c042-->
- **Mumps orchitis** has become uncommon with vaccination; when it occurs, it is usually one-sided.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c043-->
- **Granulomatous orchitis** has noncaseating granulomas, no organisms, and a type IV mechanism.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c044-->
- **Gonorrheal epididymitis** can cause infertility.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c045-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is epididymitis always sexually transmitted?** No. In older men, the commoner cause is E. coli spreading up from the urinary tract.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c046-->

**Do granulomas in the testis mean tuberculosis?** No. Granulomatous orchitis of unknown cause has noncaseating granulomas and no organisms.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c047-->

**Is mumps orchitis still common?** No, vaccination has made it uncommon.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c048-->

## Mnemonics

**Young = G and C, old = coli:** **G**onorrhea and **C**hlamydia in young men, *E. **coli*** in older men.<!--c:epididymitis-and-orchitis-c049-->
