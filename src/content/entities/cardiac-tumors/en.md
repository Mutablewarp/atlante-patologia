## Definition and epidemiology

**What are cardiac tumors, and which one is the most common?** Primary tumors of the heart are rare, and most of them are benign.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c001--> A tumor found in the heart is far more likely to be a metastasis than a primary tumor, by a factor of about 100.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c002--> Among primary tumors, the myxoma is the most frequent.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c003--> It makes up roughly 50% to 80% of them.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c004--> In children, the most frequent primary tumor is the rhabdomyoma.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c005--> Rare primary tumors also include sarcomas and benign lesions of vessels, nerves and fibrous tissue, and lipomas with lipomatous hypertrophy of the interatrial septum.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c006-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Are cardiac tumors inherited?** Most myxomas are sporadic.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c007--> About 5% to 10% occur in Carney complex, an autosomal dominant syndrome (see [[inherited-cancer-syndromes]]).<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c008--> Carney complex adds skin pigmentation, hormone overproduction and myxomas outside the heart.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c009--> PRKAR1A is a tumor suppressor gene for a regulatory part of protein kinase A; inactivating germline mutations of it are linked to the syndrome.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c010--> Mutations in TSC1 or TSC2 underlie the familial form of tuberous sclerosis.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c011--> Between a third and a half of rhabdomyomas occur together with tuberous sclerosis.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c012-->

**Which cancers spread to the heart?** The cancers seen most often with cardiac spread are the common ones: lung, breast and gastrointestinal.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c013--> Most patients with these cancers never develop heart deposits.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c014--> Lymphoma and leukemia can involve the heart too.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c015--> Melanoma spreads to the heart more readily than any other cancer ([[cutaneous-melanoma]]).<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c016-->

## Pathogenesis

**Where does a myxoma come from?** The origin is not settled, but myxoma probably arises from endocardial multipotent mesenchymal stem cells, notably at the fossa ovalis.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c017-->

**Are rhabdomyoma and fibroelastoma true neoplasms?** Whether rhabdomyoma is a true neoplasm is unsettled; it may be a hamartoma.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c018--> A papillary fibroelastoma is a hamartoma, not a true neoplasm.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c019-->

**What do TSC1 and TSC2 do?** TSC1 and TSC2 make hamartin and tuberin, tumor suppressors that steer heart muscle cell growth and maturation before and after birth.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c020-->

## Gross morphology

**Where do the tumors sit, and what do they look like?**
- Roughly three in four myxomas start in the left atrium; the rest turn up in other chambers or on a valve.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c021-->
- A myxoma hangs from a short stalk, measures 5 to 6 cm and looks shiny and jelly-like.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c022-->
- The mass may move enough to block the mitral valve opening.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c023-->
- Rhabdomyomas range from 1 mm to several centimeters and are pale myocardial nodules.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c024-->
- Rhabdomyoma nodules fill both ventricles and are nearly always multiple; a third also reach the atria.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c025-->
- In half of rhabdomyomas, a bulge into a chamber ends up blocking a lumen or valve opening.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c026-->
- A papillary fibroelastoma is a valve growth of up to 3 to 4 cm whose fronds look like a sea anemone.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c027-->
- A melanoma metastasis in the myocardium is heavily pigmented.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c028-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the cells and the stroma look like?**
- A myxoma is made of stellate cells, alone or in small clusters, lying in a loose stroma full of proteoglycans.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c029-->
- Rhabdomyoma cells have small nuclei in the middle and a wide, pale cytoplasm full of glycogen.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c030-->
- In the spider cell, thin threads of cytoplasm with sarcomeres run out from the center to the cell edge, like legs.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c031-->
- Papillary fibroelastoma: looser connective tissue wraps the center of each frond, which is a dense axis of collagen and elastic fibers.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c032-->
- The frond is covered by endothelium continuous with that of the valve.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c033-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** under the microscope, look for stellate cells in myxoid stroma (myxoma), spider cells with glycogen-rich cytoplasm (rhabdomyoma) or collagen-elastic cores under endothelium (fibroelastoma).<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c034-->
- Immunohistochemistry: myxoma cells produce α-smooth muscle actin and carry markers of two lineages, primitive cardiac myocytes and endothelial precursors.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c035-->
- The reference text used for this card describes no special stain, electron microscopy or molecular test for diagnosis, so none is listed.

## Clinical presentation

**How do cardiac tumors cause trouble?**
- Mitral valve dysfunction is clinically evident in more than half of patients whose myxoma sits in the left atrium.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c036-->
- A myxoma may break into fragments that embolize, although it does not spread like an ordinary cancer.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c037-->
- A papillary fibroelastoma is usually silent.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c038-->
- It can shed fragments that embolize to distant organs.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c039-->
- Myocardial ischemia can follow when it blocks the opening of a coronary artery.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c040-->
- Myocardial metastases can mimic [[restrictive-cardiomyopathy]] clinically, most of all when fibrosis is extensive.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c041-->
- Metastatic damage to atrioventricular conduction can show on the ECG as AV block or bundle branch block.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c042-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests point to the diagnosis?** The reference text used for this card gives no imaging or laboratory criteria for cardiac tumors. The diagnosis rests on the tumor morphology described above.

## Complications and prognosis

**Can these tumors be cured?** Surgical removal of a myxoma is usually curative.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c043--> Tumor emboli that reach the brain cause fatal stroke in some patients with a left-heart myxoma.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c044--> Successful excision has been achieved in a few cardiac rhabdomyomas.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c045-->

## Differential diagnosis

**How do I tell these lesions apart?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Myxoma | Stalked polyp, usually in the left atrium, with stellate cells in a myxoid stroma.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c046--> | H&E histology |
| Rhabdomyoma | Pale nodules, often multiple, in a child, with spider cells.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c047--> | H&E histology |
| Papillary fibroelastoma | Valve fronds with a collagen and elastic core under endothelium.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c048--> | H&E histology |
| Metastasis, e.g. [[cutaneous-melanoma]] | Melanoma deposits in the myocardium are heavily pigmented.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c049--> | H&E histology |
| [[restrictive-cardiomyopathy]] | Myocardial metastases with extensive fibrosis can mimic it.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c050--> | Histology of the myocardium |

## High-yield summary

- **Primary cardiac tumors** are rare and mostly benign.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c051-->
- **Myxoma** is the most common primary cardiac tumor, usually in the **left atrium**.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c052-->
- **Carney complex** (**PRKAR1A**) accounts for a minority of myxomas.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c053-->
- **Rhabdomyoma** is the most common cardiac tumor of childhood.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c054-->
- Rhabdomyoma is linked to **tuberous sclerosis** (**TSC1/TSC2**).<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c055-->
- The **spider cell** is the hallmark of rhabdomyoma.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c056-->
- **Papillary fibroelastoma** is a valve hamartoma that can embolize or block a coronary opening.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c057-->
- **Melanoma** spreads to the heart more readily than any other cancer.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c058-->
- Left-heart **myxoma emboli** can cause fatal stroke.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c059-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a tumor in the heart usually primary?** No. Metastases are far more common than primary tumors.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c060-->

**Does a myxoma metastasize?** Not like an ordinary cancer, but fragments can embolize.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c061-->

**Are rhabdomyoma and fibroelastoma proven true neoplasms?** No. Fibroelastoma is a hamartoma, and rhabdomyoma may be one too.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c062-->

**Does every rhabdomyoma mean tuberous sclerosis?** No. Only about a third to a half are associated with it.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c063-->

**Is the commonest primary cardiac tumor malignant?** No. The myxoma is benign.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c064-->

**Which cancer is most likely to seed the heart?** Melanoma, even though lung, breast and gastrointestinal carcinomas are seen more often in affected patients.<!--c:cardiac-tumors-c065-->
