## Definition and epidemiology

**What is idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and how does it relate to UIP?** When a biopsy shows the pattern called usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) and no cause can be found, the clinical diagnosis is idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c001--> Each year 6 to 14 people per 100,000 develop UIP, which makes it a frequent form of interstitial pneumonia.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c002--> Onset averages between 50 and 60 years of age, and men are slightly more often affected.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it?** No cause of IPF is known; infection, the immune system and inherited susceptibility are all suspected.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c004--> A viral trigger is suspected because a flu-like illness came before the disease in some patients.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c005--> IPF runs in some families, and similar lung disease occurs in neurofibromatosis and Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome, so genes matter.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c006--> Fewer than one third of cases are explained by mutations in TERT, surfactant protein C or MUC5B, although these are common in families with IPF.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c007--> Systemic sclerosis, lupus and rheumatoid arthritis are among the autoimmune diseases that accompany UIP histology, in about 20% of cases.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c008--> Autoantibodies such as antinuclear antibodies and rheumatoid factor are common, yet the triggering antigen remains unknown.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c009-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the fibrosis arise?** Neutrophils are thought to arrive in response to cytokines from activated alveolar macrophages.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c010--> Interstitial fibrosis is the end point of a series of events set off when these neutrophils damage the alveolar walls.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c011--> Alveolar walls collapse and cystic spaces form wherever dense scarring has reshaped the lung architecture.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c012-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the lungs look like?** Scars run along the septa and under the pleura, mostly in the lower lobes, and the lungs are small.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c013--> The pleura looks bumpy, as in a cirrhotic liver, because the septal scars retract.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c014--> Dense scars and honeycomb cysts sit in different areas, so the fibrosis is often patchy.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c015--> Extensive cystic change may be described as honeycomb lung.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c016-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is the histologic hallmark?** Normal lung can sit right beside fibrotic areas; this patchy interstitial fibrosis is the hallmark.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c017--> Fibrosis concentrated just under the pleura is called subpleural; it also follows the interlobular septa.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c018--> Temporal heterogeneity means that the fibrotic lesions are of different ages.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c019--> Dense collagen may lie next to a fibroblastic focus, a zone of loose fibroblastic tissue.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c020--> Lined by airway-type or cuboidal cells, the cysts hold mucus plus inflammatory cells.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c021--> Dilated airspaces are invaded by growing bronchiolar epithelium.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c022--> The chronic inflammation seen in the interstitium is mild to moderate.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c023--> Mainly when UIP accompanies rheumatoid arthritis or another connective tissue disease, lymphocyte clusters with occasional germinal centers can be seen.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c024--> Pulmonary hypertension may accompany vessel changes: a fibrotic intima and a thickened media.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** the specimen is a lung biopsy, but surgical biopsy is not needed when CT is typical.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c026-->
- On histology, expect fibroblastic foci and microscopic honeycombing within patchy subpleural fibrosis.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c027-->
- Yellow dense collagen and a green fibroblastic focus are told apart on an elastin stain.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c028-->
- Molecular: TERT, surfactant protein C and MUC5B mutations are described in familial IPF.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c029-->

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** Typically over 1 to 3 years, a dry cough slowly appears along with breathlessness on effort.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c030--> Lung function testing shows a restrictive pattern.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c031--> Finger clubbing is common, mostly late.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c032--> Listening over the lung bases, the doctor classically hears fine "Velcro" rales, which are late inspiratory crackles.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c033--> Late in the disease cor pulmonale can occur, along with cyanosis and rapid breathing at rest.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c034-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What does CT show?** The typical CT shows a rim of net-like opacities under the pleura with honeycombing, strongest in the posterior lower lobes, and airways pulled open by scar (traction bronchiectasis).<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c035--> Surgical lung biopsy can be skipped in about half of patients, because their CT is distinctive.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c036-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook, and what helps?** Mean survival is only 4 to 6 years, so the prognosis is poor.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c037--> Lung transplantation is generally the only hope of survival; drug treatment consists of steroids, sometimes with cyclophosphamide.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c038--> The FDA has approved the antifibrotic drugs pirfenidone and nintedanib to slow progression.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c039-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP) | The lung is involved evenly throughout, and fibroblastic foci and honeycomb cysts are hard to find or missing.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c040--> In idiopathic NSIP, 80% of patients are alive at 5 years, which is better than in IPF.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c041--> | Distribution of fibrosis on histology: diffuse in NSIP, patchy in UIP |
| Desquamative interstitial pneumonia (DIP) | Smokers are mostly affected, and interstitial fibrosis is minimal.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c042--> | Lung biopsy |
| [[hypersensitivity-pneumonitis\|Chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis]] | It can itself produce a UIP pattern.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c043--> | Search for the exposure |
| [[sarcoidosis]] | Multiple noncaseating granulomas are the typical finding.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c044--> | Transbronchial lung biopsy |

## High-yield summary

- A **UIP** biopsy with no identifiable cause amounts to the clinical diagnosis of **IPF**.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c045-->
- Normal lung sits beside scar in **patchy subpleural fibrosis**, the hallmark.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c046-->
- Scars of different ages (**temporal heterogeneity**) and **fibroblastic foci** are typical.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c047-->
- Advanced disease shows **honeycombing**, mostly in the lower lobes.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c048-->
- **NSIP** is the contrasting pattern: diffuse and uniform.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c049-->
- Mean survival is 4 to 6 years; **pirfenidone** and **nintedanib** slow progression.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c050-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a UIP pattern prove IPF?** No. UIP is only a pattern, and several diseases can produce it, for example asbestosis or drug injury; IPF is diagnosed only when no cause is found.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c051-->

**Is the fibrosis of IPF uniform?** No. It is patchy, with normal lung beside scar, unlike the diffuse, uniform change of NSIP.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c052-->

**Does every patient with suspected IPF need a surgical lung biopsy?** No. When CT shows the typical findings, surgical biopsy is not required.<!--c:idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis-c053-->
