## Definition and epidemiology

**What are acute bronchitis and bronchiolitis?** Acute illness, and the damage it leaves behind, accounts for most bronchial and bronchiolar disease.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c001--> The line between airway and lung-tissue infection is a classification choice, not a sharp boundary.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c002--> Bronchiolitis is infection of the small peripheral airways, which many organisms that infect the larger airways also reach.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which infections injure the airways?**
- Adenovirus, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and measles are typical agents of bronchiolitis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c004-->
- Disease is worse where children are undernourished or the virus is unfamiliar.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c005-->
- Influenza is a classic cause of tracheobronchitis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c006-->
- Whooping cough is caused by the bacterium *Bordetella pertussis*, which commonly infects the airways.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c007-->
- Flare-ups of [[chronic-bronchitis]] have been tied to infection by *Haemophilus influenzae* and *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c008-->
- In the lung, *Candida albicans* usually just colonizes the airway surface, sometimes ulcerating it.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c009-->
- Neutropenia (for example after leukemia chemotherapy) or an indwelling catheter predisposes to invasive Candida.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c010-->

**Which irritants injure the airways?**
- Outdoors, the main gaseous irritants of the airways are SO2 and the oxidants ozone and nitrogen oxides.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c011-->
- Car exhaust exposed to sunlight is the origin of the oxidants.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c012-->
- Burning sulfur-containing fossil fuels is the main source of SO2.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c013-->
- NO2 exposure at work arises in welding, plating, and in cleaning or blasting metal.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c014-->
- Silo-filler disease is lung injury from inhaling high concentrations of NO2 released by decaying stored grain.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c015-->
- NO2 sinks, forming a layer just over the grain.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c016-->
- After an industrial leak, inhaled chlorine or ammonia injures mucosa throughout the bronchi and bronchioles.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c017-->
- Patients with asthma or pre-existing lung disease feel worse when air pollution rises ([[air-pollution]]).<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c018-->

**What causes constrictive bronchiolitis?**
- It may follow lung transplantation (chronic rejection, [[transplant-rejection]]) or bone marrow transplantation ([[graft-versus-host-disease]]); some cases are idiopathic.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c019-->
- Rheumatoid arthritis is the leading connective-tissue cause, and infection, mainly viral, can precede it.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c020-->
- Penicillamine, and inhalation of phosgene, ammonia or SO2, can also bring it on.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c021-->
- The best-known cases are popcorn butter-flavoring workers exposed to diacetyl and 2,3-pentanedione.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c022-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these agents damage the bronchioles?**
- Fibrosis is how the lung heals after the widespread bronchiolitis that adenovirus causes.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c023-->
- The result can be constrictive bronchiolitis, with loose fibrous tissue filling or blocking bronchioles.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c024-->
- Like adenovirus, measles can close bronchioles, and bronchiectasis may follow.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c025-->
- Loss of surfactant from the bronchiolar lining is one proposed reason RSV can overinflate the lung with no visible blockage.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c026-->
- Airway inflammation accompanies the heightened reactivity that ozone produces.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c027-->
- After chlorine or ammonia injury, bronchiectasis arises from secondary inflammation, as bronchioles close and bronchi are damaged directly.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c028-->
- In constrictive bronchiolitis, scar replaces the initial inflammation and the lumen shrinks, possibly to nothing.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c029-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the airway look like at autopsy?** In fatal influenza the bronchial surface turns bright red from mucosal inflammation and congestion.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c030-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What is seen under the microscope?**
- Chronic inflammatory cells crowd the bronchiolar wall in adenoviral bronchiolitis and also invade the tissue beside it.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c031-->
- The epithelium is disorganized in RSV bronchiolitis, and inflammation surrounds the bronchioles.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c032-->
- Fatal pertussis shows severe inflammation of bronchi and bronchioles.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c033-->
- **Aside, constrictive bronchiolitis:** scar tissue sandwiched below the epithelium and above the smooth muscle narrows the inflamed bronchiolar lumen.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c034-->
- Neighboring airways may show bronchiolectasis and mucus plugs.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c035-->
- Away from the scarred bronchioles, the lung tissue usually looks normal.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c036-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E of constrictive bronchiolitis, look for fibrosis beneath the epithelium that narrows the lumen.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c037-->
- An elastic stain may make scarred bronchioles easier to spot.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c038-->
- Their patchy distribution makes the lesions easy to miss.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c039-->
- The pages give no specimen type, IHC, electron microscopy or molecular tests.

## Clinical presentation

**What symptoms do patients have?**
- Dyspnea and cyanosis appear only in extreme cases of airway infection; the usual symptoms are cough and chest tightness.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c040-->
- Babies and children develop severe illness more often than adults.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c041-->
- RSV often sweeps through nurseries and can be acquired in hospital by children, only rarely by adults.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c042-->
- The whoop closing each long coughing fit, plus fever, marks whooping cough.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c043-->
- Cough and dyspnea can start as late as 30 hours after NO2 inhalation.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c044-->
- Severe airflow obstruction in constrictive bronchiolitis can produce dyspnea and wheezing.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c045-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests help?** In constrictive bronchiolitis, air trapped beyond the obliterated bronchioles can overinflate the lung on chest radiograph or CT, although these studies may also be normal.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c046--> The pages name no laboratory test.

## Complications and prognosis

**How do these diseases end?**
- Most infants and children recover, even from severe symptomatic infection.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c047-->
- Most RSV infections resolve on their own, though a rare case is fatal.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c048-->
- The worst after-effects among these airway infections come from adenovirus.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c049-->
- Bronchiectasis was a frequent outcome of whooping cough before vaccination.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c050-->
- Measles bronchiolitis is still serious where few people have met the virus, though vaccination has made it rare in rich countries.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c051-->
- A minority of silo-filler cases progress to bronchiolitis obliterans and fatal respiratory failure; most recover.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c052-->
- In most patients constrictive bronchiolitis worsens steadily and relentlessly.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c053-->
- No treatment works for constrictive bronchiolitis, steroids included, though many patients get them.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c054-->

## High-yield summary

- **Adenovirus, RSV and measles** are the classic agents of **bronchiolitis**.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c062-->
- **Adenovirus** heals by fibrosis and can leave **constrictive bronchiolitis**.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c063-->
- **Obliterative bronchiolitis** is a synonym of constrictive bronchiolitis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c064-->
- **Elastic stains** may make scarred bronchioles easier to spot.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c065-->
- **Silo-filler disease** is caused by **NO2** from decaying grain.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c066-->
- **Influenza** gives a fiery-red tracheobronchitis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c067-->
- **Whooping cough** is recognized by coughing fits that end in a **whoop**.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c068-->
- **Chlorine and ammonia** injury can end in **bronchiectasis**.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c069-->
- **No therapy** is effective in constrictive bronchiolitis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c070-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is bronchiolitis only a disease of infants?** No. Severe cases are commoner in infants and children, but RSV can also infect adults, rarely.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c071-->

**Does a normal chest radiograph or CT exclude constrictive bronchiolitis?** No. Imaging may be normal.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c072-->

**Do airway infections heal without a trace?** Not always. Adenovirus and measles can obliterate bronchioles, and measles can also leave bronchiectasis.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c073-->

**Do steroids cure constrictive bronchiolitis?** No. Many patients receive them, yet no therapy is effective.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c074-->

**Is bronchocentric granulomatosis a disease of its own?** No. It is a histologic pattern seen in several settings.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c075-->

**Is bronchiolitis obliterans a different disease from constrictive bronchiolitis?** No. The two names describe the same lesion.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c076-->

## Mnemonics

**ARM the bronchioles:** Adenovirus, RSV, Measles, the three classic bronchiolitis agents.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c077-->

**Heavy NO2 hugs the grain:** NO2 sinks to just over the grain, where the worker breathes it.<!--c:acute-bronchitis-and-bronchiolitis-c078-->
