## Definition and epidemiology

**What is restrictive cardiomyopathy, and what goes wrong?** Restrictive cardiomyopathy (RCM) covers diseases that stiffen the ventricles: filling suffers, while wall thickness and contraction stay normal.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c001--> It is the rarest cardiomyopathy.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c002--> In children it accounts for 2% to 5% of cardiomyopathies.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c003--> Heart failure drives the high mortality.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c004--> The core defect is diastolic: poor relaxation and filling, ejection preserved.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which diseases cause it?** Diastole fails in primary (idiopathic) RCM because mutations or other defects change how the myocytes behave mechanically.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c006--> RCM, [[hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy|HCM]] and [[dilated-cardiomyopathy|DCM]] share the sarcomeric genes MYH7 and TNNI3, the ones most often mutated in RCM.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c007--> In secondary RCM the stiffness comes from other parts of the heart.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c008--> Deposits between the myocytes, of amyloid or of metastatic carcinoma, are one cause.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c009--> In endomyocardial disease, scarring of the endocardium stiffens and thickens it.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c010--> When myocytes fill with iron, as in [[hereditary-hemochromatosis|hemochromatosis]], the picture is restrictive.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c011--> Desmin-related cardiomyopathy does the same when misfolded desmin clumps inside myocytes.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c012--> Diastole also suffers in cardiac [[sarcoidosis]].<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c013--> Pompe disease, a [[glycogen-storage-diseases|glycogen storage disease]], gives a restrictive cardiomyopathy.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c014--> Two types account for nearly all cardiac [[amyloidosis]]: immunoglobulin light chain (AL) and transthyretin (ATTR).<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c015--> ATTR comes from wild-type transthyretin or from variants of the TTR gene.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c016-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the ventricle become stiff?** Sarcomeric variants in many primary RCM patients presumably alter diastole, not systole.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c017--> The contractile apparatus is spared in secondary RCM; other myocardial components are the problem.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c018--> A stiff ventricle fills poorly and relies on preload; the atria swell and venous blood backs up.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c019--> AL amyloidosis is acquired, not inherited: a plasma cell clone makes the light chains.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c020--> Light chains circulating free are also toxic to myocytes, through reactive oxygen species generated after lysosomal damage.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c021--> Desmin aggregates presumably impair myocardial relaxation.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c022--> Endomyocardial disease has three stages.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c023-->
- Necrotic: eosinophils infiltrate the inner myocardium and myocytes die.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c024-->
- Thrombotic: injured endocardium becomes a surface for mural thrombi, which can embolize.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c025-->
- Fibrotic: compliance falls and diastole fails as the endocardium thickens with fibrosis.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c026-->

Toxic eosinophil granule contents may injure the heart in both endomyocardial fibrosis and Löffler endocarditis, probably one process.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c027-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the heart look like?** Thick, firm walls and no ventricular dilation characterize the enlarged amyloid heart.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c028--> Pale, thickened endocardium bridges the left ventricular apex and the posterior mitral leaflet in endomyocardial disease.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c029--> The innermost third to half of the wall thickness is fibrotic.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c030--> Mural thrombi may be present.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c031--> Thick ventricular walls and dilatation are the gross findings in hemochromatosis.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c032-->

**Aside: left ventricular noncompaction (LVNC).** Mainly at the apex, deep clefts and coarse trabeculae invade what should be compact wall.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c033--> Isolated LVNC exists, and it also accompanies some congenital heart diseases.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c034-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What shows up on the slide?** Little in the myocardium of primary RCM accounts for the stiffness.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c035--> The interstitium and the perivascular spaces hold the heaviest amyloid load.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c036--> Small intramural arteries often have amyloid in their walls as well.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c037--> In desmin-related disease, myocytes contain aggregates of misfolded desmin.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c038--> Elastic fibers are scanty in the fibrotic endocardium of late endomyocardial disease.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c039--> In hemochromatosis, iron lies inside myocytes.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c040--> In Pompe disease, stored glycogen vacuolates the myocytes.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c041-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** in primary RCM biopsy may reveal nothing, or at most mild interstitial fibrosis.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c042-->
- Congo red stains amyloid deposits between myocytes red.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c043-->
- Those deposits are green and birefringent when viewed with polarized light.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c044-->
- Mass spectrometry of a heart biopsy separates AL from ATTR, which look alike on histology.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c045-->

Sampling, iron stains and electron microscopy are not in the reference pages.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Cardiac amyloidosis usually presents between ages 60 and 80.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c046--> Pumping fails late in cardiac amyloidosis; before that, worsening filling makes it look like RCM.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c047--> ATTR may lie behind some cases of heart failure with a preserved ejection fraction.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c048--> At autopsy, elderly people often have wild-type ATTR, which is usually clinically silent.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c049--> It can still cause restrictive physiology.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c050--> Heart block and arrhythmias can follow amyloid infiltration of the conduction system.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c051--> Endomyocardial fibrosis is common in equatorial Africa; elsewhere in the tropics and subtropics only sporadic cases appear.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c052--> It leads to heart failure and arrhythmias.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c053--> Hypereosinophilia is the setting of Löffler endocarditis, a disease of temperate regions.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c054--> Most patients are men; a rash is common.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c055--> Desmin-related disease can also weaken skeletal muscle and disturb breathing and digestion.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c056--> Cardiac failure usually kills infants with Pompe disease, whose hearts are greatly enlarged.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c057--> Cardiac hemochromatosis impairs both systolic and diastolic function.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c058--> LVNC may lead to pump failure; rhythm problems range up to complete heart block.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c059-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests point to the diagnosis?** In symptomatic amyloid heart disease, echo shows a near-normal early ejection fraction despite concentric left ventricular thickening.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c060--> Noncompliant ventricular walls commonly lead to enlarged atria.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c061--> A typical ECG finding is low QRS voltage.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c062--> Echo may find LVNC in people without symptoms.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c063-->

## Complications and prognosis

**How do patients fare?** A worse outlook is typical when RCM and HCM features coexist.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c064--> Cardiac amyloid: better outlook lately, but poor once symptomatic.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c065--> AL treatment targets the plasma cell clone behind the disease.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c066--> Two newer ATTR approaches exist: drugs that stabilize the transthyretin molecule, and gene silencing so that the liver produces less of it.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c067--> Löffler endocarditis typically ends in heart failure and death.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c068--> Corticosteroids may improve survival.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c069--> Scarred mitral and tricuspid valves can leak in the chronic stage, which also dilates the atria.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c070-->

## High-yield summary

- RCM is **diastolic failure**: the ventricle is stiff, while contraction and wall thickness are essentially normal.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c076-->
- **Primary (idiopathic)** RCM often involves sarcomeric genes such as MYH7 and TNNI3, with few structural changes.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c077-->
- **Secondary** causes: amyloid, endomyocardial disease, iron overload, desmin aggregates, sarcoidosis and Pompe disease.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c078-->
- **Congo red** with green birefringence marks amyloid; **mass spectrometry** separates AL from ATTR.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c079-->
- **Low-voltage QRS** with thick ventricular walls on echo suggests cardiac amyloid.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c080-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a normal biopsy exclude primary RCM?** No. Primary RCM leaves the myocardium almost unchanged although diastole is abnormal.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c081-->

**Is RCM a disease of weak contraction?** No. Systole is normal or near normal; the defect is in filling.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c082-->

**Can hemodynamics alone separate RCM from constrictive pericarditis?** No. They may be hard to tell apart.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c083-->

**Do vacuolated myocytes mean a primary cardiomyopathy?** No. They point to an underlying storage disease.<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c084-->

## Mnemonics

**SHEADS** stiffen the ventricle: Sarcoidosis, Hemochromatosis, Endomyocardial disease, Amyloid, Desmin, Storage disease (Pompe).<!--c:restrictive-cardiomyopathy-c085-->
