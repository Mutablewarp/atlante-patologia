## Definition and epidemiology

**What is a left-to-right shunt heart defect?** Congenital heart disease means a heart or great-vessel malformation present at birth.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c001--> Higher pressure on the left side first sends blood through an abnormal opening from left to right.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c002--> The lesions to know are PDA, ASD and VSD; the AV canal combines an atrial and a ventricular septal defect.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c003--> VSD ranks first among congenital heart lesions; ASD and PDA are also common.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c004--> Cyanosis appears late in this group, once the shunt has turned right to left (Eisenmenger complex).<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes these defects?** Heredity is critical, yet in most patients no cause is identified.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c006--> Among environmental causes, rubella in the mother early in pregnancy is well documented.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c007--> Some ASDs are part of genetic syndromes, but for most the cause is not known.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c010--> PDA is common in premature infants and after maternal rubella early in pregnancy.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c011--> Patients with [[down-syndrome|Down syndrome]] often have a persistent common AV canal.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c012-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do the septa and ductus form?** Endocardial cushions give rise to the AV valves, while growing atrial and ventricular septa split the one-chamber fetal heart into right and left sides.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c013--> The commonest VSD is an incomplete membranous septum, which should descend to meet the muscular septum rising from the apex.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c014--> A hole, the ostium secundum, opens in the septum primum before it reaches the endocardial cushions and shuts the ostium primum.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c015--> In the fetus, blood from the placenta passes via the foramen ovale out of the right atrium and into the left, a gap the later septum secundum leaves open.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c016--> Most pulmonary-trunk blood reaches the aorta via the fetal ductus.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c018--> The ligamentum arteriosum is the fibrous remnant of the ductus, which narrows once oxygen levels rise after birth.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c019--> Spontaneous closure is rare when a full-term infant has PDA, since the ductus endothelium and media are abnormal.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c020-->

**Why does the shunt later reverse?** The pressure advantage of the left side drives blood through the defect toward the right.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c021--> Pulmonary vascular resistance climbs because long-standing excess shunted flow thickens the pulmonary arteries.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c022--> Once right ventricular pressure exceeds left ventricular pressure, the shunt turns right to left: Eisenmenger complex.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c023--> After reversal the right ventricle is overloaded, hypertrophies and fails, and cyanosis appears only at this late stage.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c024-->

## Gross morphology

**What does each defect look like?** Muscular-septum VSDs are often multiple; membranous ones are small, perimembranous ones larger, and absence of the muscular septum leaves a single ventricle.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c025--> The usual VSD lies behind the septal tricuspid leaflet, high in the septum below the pulmonary outflow tract (infracristal); the bundle of His runs just under it.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c026--> A true deficiency in the middle of the septum, ostium secundum is the most frequent ASD.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c028--> A pulmonary artery wider than the aorta can accompany a large secundum shunt, which also dilates and thickens the right atrium and ventricle.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c029--> In an ostium primum ASD the anterior mitral and tricuspid septal leaflets are usually cleft, and the defect abuts the endocardial cushions.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c030--> A complete AV canal is one shared AV valve with atrial and ventricular septal defects, from failed fusion of the AV endocardial cushions.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c031--> A sinus venosus ASD sits high by the caval inflow, and the right pulmonary veins usually empty into the wrong place: the right atrium or the superior vena cava.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c033--> A patent foramen ovale is an incompletely sealed septum primum flap that a probe can pass, not a true deficiency.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c035--> In PDA a ductus from pulmonary artery to descending aorta stays open, with a very variable lumen.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c036-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What changes are seen under the microscope?** The pulmonary arteries thicken when a shunt has raised pulmonary blood flow for a long time.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c038-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which genes matter?**
- **How it is studied:** the reference pages describe no specimens, stains or tests for these defects.
- Some familial and some sporadic ASDs are associated with NKX2.5 mutations.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c039-->

## Clinical presentation

**How do these defects present?** A small VSD can seal itself as the child grows, when tricuspid leaflets stick to its edge or neighboring muscle thickens; it seldom matters functionally.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c043--> In infants with a large VSD, the shunt commonly brings heart failure and a dilated left ventricle.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c044--> At the site of a VSD, infective endocarditis can develop.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c045--> Some young children with an ASD tire easily or get short of breath on exertion, but most have no symptoms.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c047--> Pulmonary vascular changes can flip an ASD shunt to right-to-left in adults, with cyanosis and clubbing.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c048--> A patent foramen ovale can open a right-to-left route when right atrial pressure climbs; paradoxical emboli crossing it can infarct the brain, heart and other systemic organs.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c050--> Infective endarteritis can start on a small PDA, even though the shunt barely affects the heart.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c051--> A large PDA can lead to left ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure: the left heart must raise its output to make up for blood lost into the pulmonary artery.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c052-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests confirm the diagnosis?** The reference pages give no laboratory or imaging findings.

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outcome and treatment?** The ductus of most premature infants closes by itself, and so do some small defects.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c054--> Infancy is the usual time for surgical repair of a large VSD.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c055--> A symptomatic ASD needs surgery or a catheter-delivered closure device.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c056--> Indomethacin, a prostaglandin synthesis inhibitor, makes a PDA contract and close; the alternatives are surgery or a catheter procedure.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c057--> Newborns with another heart defect that needs the ductus to survive are given prostaglandin E2 to keep it open.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c058-->

## High-yield summary

- **VSD** is the commonest congenital heart lesion, and its commonest form comes from an incomplete **membranous septum**.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c065-->
- **Ostium secundum** is the most frequent ASD, and it is a true septal deficiency.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c066-->
- A **persistent common AV canal**, a combined atrial and ventricular septal defect, is frequent in **Down syndrome**.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c067-->
- **PDA** is common in premature infants; **indomethacin** closes it, and **prostaglandin E2** keeps it open when a newborn depends on it.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c068-->
- **Eisenmenger complex** is the late reversal of a left-to-right shunt, driven by rising pulmonary vascular resistance.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c069-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a patent foramen ovale the same as an ASD?** No. A patent foramen ovale is a flap valve that did not seal, whereas an ostium secundum ASD is a genuine septal deficiency.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c072-->

**Does a left-to-right shunt make the child blue from the start?** No. Cyanosis comes late, once the shunt has reversed.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c073-->

**Is Eisenmenger complex a separate heart defect?** No. It is the reversal of an earlier left-to-right shunt.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c074-->

## Mnemonics

**PRESS:** Pulmonary Resistance rises, right pressure Exceeds Systemic pressure, and the Shunt reverses.<!--c:congenital-heart-disease-left-to-right-shunts-c077-->
