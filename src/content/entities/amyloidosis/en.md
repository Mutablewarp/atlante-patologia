## Definition and epidemiology

**What is amyloidosis?** It is a group of protein-misfolding diseases in which insoluble fibrils collect outside cells in tissues and organs.<!--c:amyloidosis-c001-->

Different types look alike on H&E and Congo red, so the diagnosis needs the misfolded protein to be identified.<!--c:amyloidosis-c003-->
Each type is now named after the protein that makes up its fibrils; in AL these are immunoglobulin light chains.<!--c:amyloidosis-c004-->
In localized forms the deposit forms where the precursor is made; in systemic forms it forms in organs far from the producing cells.<!--c:amyloidosis-c005-->
AA is the only type seen in children.<!--c:amyloidosis-c006-->
Aβ2M amyloidosis develops in people who have been on dialysis for many years.<!--c:amyloidosis-c007-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which protein builds each type?**

- **AL:** monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains, usually from a plasma cell dyscrasia, alone or with myeloma.<!--c:amyloidosis-c008-->
- **AA:** serum amyloid A, an acute-phase protein made in the liver.<!--c:amyloidosis-c009-->
- AA develops after years of inflammation, most often in rheumatic disease or a lasting infection such as tuberculosis.<!--c:amyloidosis-c010-->
- **ATTR:** transthyretin; inherited variants are autosomal dominant.<!--c:amyloidosis-c011-->
- Normal, unmutated transthyretin can itself aggregate in the elderly, and the heart is the organ chiefly hit.<!--c:amyloidosis-c012-->
- **Aβ2M:** β2-microglobulin, in long-term hemodialysis.<!--c:amyloidosis-c013-->
- **Localized forms:** amyloid β protein in Alzheimer disease, islet amyloid polypeptide in the diabetic pancreas, calcitonin in medullary thyroid carcinoma.<!--c:amyloidosis-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a soluble protein turn into amyloid?** Studies suggest that a partly unfolded precursor assembles into small oligomers and then into larger fibrils.<!--c:amyloidosis-c016-->

## Gross morphology

**What does amyloid look like to the naked eye?** In the heart the walls are bulky and stiff with a rubbery consistency, and the organ is enlarged but its cavities are not dilated.<!--c:amyloidosis-c023-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does amyloid look like on a slide?** On H&E it is an amorphous eosinophilic material.<!--c:amyloidosis-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**

**How it is studied:**

- **Specimen:** a tissue biopsy showing amyloid fibrils is required; aspirating abdominal fat is the gentlest way to obtain tissue.<!--c:amyloidosis-c028-->
- **Congo red:** amyloid stains red and shows apple-green birefringence under polarized light.<!--c:amyloidosis-c030-->
- **Immunohistochemistry:** it is required to tell the types apart.<!--c:amyloidosis-c031-->
- **Mass spectrometry:** it separates AL from ATTR in heart biopsies, where the two look alike.<!--c:amyloidosis-c033-->
- **Molecular tests:** isoelectric focusing detects variant transthyretin, and an abnormal result leads to sequencing of the TTR gene to find the exact mutation.<!--c:amyloidosis-c034-->

## Clinical presentation

**Which organs are hit, and how?**

- **AL** usually targets the kidneys and heart; nearly every other organ can be hit as well, but the brain is spared.<!--c:amyloidosis-c035-->
- Kidney: nephrotic syndrome with worsening renal function.<!--c:amyloidosis-c036-->
- Heart: stiff ventricles (restrictive cardiomyopathy) and quickly worsening heart failure.<!--c:amyloidosis-c037-->
- Macroglossia is a hallmark of AL, though only a minority of patients show it.<!--c:amyloidosis-c039-->
- **AA** shows mainly as proteinuria or renal insufficiency; cardiomyopathy is rare.<!--c:amyloidosis-c040-->
- **Familial ATTR** can hardly be separated from AL by clinical features alone.<!--c:amyloidosis-c041-->
- **Aβ2M** shows up as bone and joint disease, and carpal tunnel syndrome is usually the earliest symptom.<!--c:amyloidosis-c044-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which tests point to the type?** Serum or urine tests show a clonal immunoglobulin or free light chains in the large majority of AL patients.<!--c:amyloidosis-c045-->

A monoclonal protein in the blood does not by itself establish amyloidosis, because many older people have MGUS.<!--c:amyloidosis-c046-->
If neither a plasma cell disorder nor AA disease explains the amyloid, an inherited form must be ruled out.<!--c:amyloidosis-c048-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outlook?** Untreated AL has a median survival of about 1 to 2 years, and about 6 months when the heart is involved and its function is impaired.<!--c:amyloidosis-c050-->

AL treatment targets the plasma cells that make the light chain.<!--c:amyloidosis-c051-->
ATTR is treated with liver transplantation or drugs that stabilize the transthyretin tetramer.<!--c:amyloidosis-c052-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetic glomerulosclerosis | Its nodules can mimic those of amyloid, but amyloid is PAS negative and Congo red positive.<!--c:amyloidosis-c054--> | PAS and Congo red |
| Monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease | Similar nodules that do not stain with Congo red.<!--c:amyloidosis-c055--> | Congo red; immunofluorescence |
| [[hypertensive-heart-disease\|Hypertensive heart disease]] | Hard to separate from cardiac amyloid without an endomyocardial biopsy.<!--c:amyloidosis-c056--> | Endomyocardial biopsy |

## High-yield summary

- **Congo red** with **apple-green birefringence** identifies amyloid but not its type.<!--c:amyloidosis-c058-->
- The type guides treatment, so it must be found by **immunohistochemistry** or **mass spectrometry**.<!--c:amyloidosis-c059-->
- **AL** is light chains, **AA** is serum amyloid A, **ATTR** is transthyretin, **Aβ2M** is dialysis.<!--c:amyloidosis-c060-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does a Congo red-positive deposit tell you the type?** No. All types look alike on H&E and Congo red, so the protein must be identified.<!--c:amyloidosis-c063-->

**Does a monoclonal protein in the blood prove AL?** No. Many older people have MGUS, so a monoclonal protein alone proves nothing; yet when amyloid is confirmed on biopsy, the protein points strongly to AL.<!--c:amyloidosis-c064-->

## Mnemonics

**LATD:** "Lazy Amyloid Takes Dialysis" stands for Light chains (AL), Acute-phase SAA (AA), Transthyretin (ATTR), Dialysis β2-microglobulin (Aβ2M).<!--c:amyloidosis-c066-->
