## Definition and epidemiology

**What is Crohn disease?** A chronic immune-mediated bowel disease whose inflammation crosses the whole wall and is patchy.<!--c:crohn-disease-c001--> The distal small intestine is the usual site, and the right colon is often involved too.<!--c:crohn-disease-c002--> It can arise anywhere in the digestive tract and also reach tissues outside it.<!--c:crohn-disease-c003--> With [[ulcerative-colitis]] it forms inflammatory bowel disease (IBD); a case fitting neither is called indeterminate colitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c004-->

Developed countries have more cases; worldwide, incidence is 0.7 to 14.6 per 100,000.<!--c:crohn-disease-c005--> A Western lifestyle probably explains the steep rise.<!--c:crohn-disease-c006--> Onset peaks in adolescents and young adults, with a smaller peak in people in their 50s and 60s.<!--c:crohn-disease-c007--> People of European origin are affected most, Ashkenazi Jews especially.<!--c:crohn-disease-c008--> Adults show a slight female excess; children, a male one.<!--c:crohn-disease-c009-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes it, and who is at risk?** The cause is not fully understood.<!--c:crohn-disease-c010--> Leading theory: genes predispose, a weak mucosal barrier and altered gut flora then let an overactive immune response injure the bowel.<!--c:crohn-disease-c011--> Variants in NOD2 and autophagy genes point to poor handling of intracellular bacteria.<!--c:crohn-disease-c012--> Genes coding for epithelial adhesion proteins may weaken the barrier.<!--c:crohn-disease-c013--> Smokers have higher risk and more severe disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**Why does the gut stay inflamed?** Interferon-γ, TNF and interleukin-12 mediate a TH1-type T-cell response.<!--c:crohn-disease-c015--> In ulcerative colitis it is TH2-dominant.<!--c:crohn-disease-c016--> The mucosa reacts excessively to commensal gut bacteria, so damage becomes chronic.<!--c:crohn-disease-c017-->

**How does a fissure become a fistula?** Small cracks in the mucosa often start above lymphoid tissue.<!--c:crohn-disease-c018--> Fissures past the submucosa may cross the whole wall.<!--c:crohn-disease-c019--> A fistula can then follow this tract.<!--c:crohn-disease-c020-->

## Gross morphology

**What does the diseased bowel look like?** Inflammation usually spans the whole wall (transmural).<!--c:crohn-disease-c021--> Skip lesions: normal-looking bowel interrupts the inflamed areas.<!--c:crohn-disease-c022--> By site: colon alone 20%, small intestine alone 30%, ileum plus cecum about half.<!--c:crohn-disease-c023--> Colitis is the common pattern in older patients, ileocecal disease in younger ones.<!--c:crohn-disease-c024--> The wall and the mesentery beside it thicken and swell with fluid.<!--c:crohn-disease-c025--> In transmural disease, mesenteric fat wraps around the bowel, mainly the small intestine; the sign may be absent.<!--c:crohn-disease-c026--> Linear ulcers cut the swollen mucosa into cobblestone-like islands.<!--c:crohn-disease-c027--> Fissures are the late lesion; early ulcers are aphthous or serpiginous.<!--c:crohn-disease-c028--> Thickening and fibrosis narrow the lumen and often obstruct the small bowel.<!--c:crohn-disease-c029--> Fistulas may join adherent bowel loops.<!--c:crohn-disease-c030--> Fistulas can also open into pelvic organs or onto the skin.<!--c:crohn-disease-c031--> Most fistulas are dead ends that turn into abscesses in the abdomen or behind it.<!--c:crohn-disease-c032-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the biopsy show?** Early on, aphthous ulcers and edema appear, and inflammation may stay in mucosa and submucosa.<!--c:crohn-disease-c033--> Macrophages, eosinophils, plasma cells and lymphocytes form the infiltrate.<!--c:crohn-disease-c034--> Pyloric and Paneth cell metaplasia and distorted glands mark chronic disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c035--> Late disease brings scarring, hyalinized vessels and deep fissures.<!--c:crohn-disease-c036-->

**Which finding points to Crohn disease?** Granulomas do not caseate; most lie in the submucosa.<!--c:crohn-disease-c037--> Multinucleated giant cells may sit among the epithelioid clusters, whose lymphocyte rim recalls [[sarcoidosis]].<!--c:crohn-disease-c038--> Central necrosis is very rare.<!--c:crohn-disease-c039--> Granulomas occur in under half of cases, so a biopsy without them does not exclude Crohn disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c040--> Crypt abscesses are not specific: ulcerative colitis has them too.<!--c:crohn-disease-c041-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**
- **How it is studied:** in a mucosal biopsy, a tiny noncaseating granuloma may sit flanked by normal crypts.<!--c:crohn-disease-c042-->
- In the colon, transmural inflammation, submucosal fibrosis and fissures are typical of Crohn disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c043-->
- To catch dysplasia, most patients with long-standing IBD need regular colonoscopy with systematic biopsies.<!--c:crohn-disease-c044-->
- Chromoendoscopy and confocal laser endomicroscopy may help biopsies find more dysplastic lesions.<!--c:crohn-disease-c045-->
- Dysplasia, low or high grade: immature epithelium and oversized, atypical nuclei.<!--c:crohn-disease-c046-->
- High-grade dysplasia on biopsy strongly indicates colectomy.<!--c:crohn-disease-c047-->
- Special stains, IHC, electron microscopy, molecular tests: no role in the reference pages.

## Clinical presentation

**How does a patient present?** Fever recurs often; abdominal pain and diarrhea are typical, and the stool may carry blood or mucus.<!--c:crohn-disease-c048--> Sudden ileocecal disease can mimic appendicitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c049--> Diffuse small-bowel disease may cause marked malabsorption and malnutrition.<!--c:crohn-disease-c050--> Ileal disease also disrupts bile salt recycling, adding lipid malabsorption.<!--c:crohn-disease-c051--> Colonic involvement brings diarrhea and at times bleeding.<!--c:crohn-disease-c052--> A few patients present with recurrent anorectal fistulas.<!--c:crohn-disease-c053--> Childhood onset may slow growth.<!--c:crohn-disease-c054-->

**Is it only a bowel disease?** No. Extraintestinal manifestations are many, more often than in ulcerative colitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c055--> They include uveitis, arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum, aphthous stomatitis and sclerosing cholangitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c056--> Gallstones, kidney stones, anemia and bone loss also occur.<!--c:crohn-disease-c057-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis reached?** Crohn colitis is told from ulcerative colitis by where the disease sits and how it looks under the microscope.<!--c:crohn-disease-c058--> Distinct granulomas make it likely.<!--c:crohn-disease-c059--> The rectum is often uninvolved in Crohn colitis, whose lesions are patchy.<!--c:crohn-disease-c060--> Some specimens contain granulomas, and inflammation spans the full wall.<!--c:crohn-disease-c061--> Surgeons need the diagnosis: Crohn disease tends to relapse, making an ileoanal pouch a poor choice.<!--c:crohn-disease-c062--> Cancer risk and drug treatment also differ.<!--c:crohn-disease-c063-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications, and what is the outlook?** Obstruction and fistulas are among the most debilitating complications.<!--c:crohn-disease-c064--> The bowel sometimes perforates.<!--c:crohn-disease-c065--> Small bowel cancer risk is at least tripled.<!--c:crohn-disease-c066--> Wider colonic disease, sclerosing cholangitis or a family history raises colorectal cancer risk further.<!--c:crohn-disease-c067--> In Crohn colitis the risk is elevated beyond 10 years of disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c068--> There is no known cure.<!--c:crohn-disease-c069--> Corticosteroids, azathioprine and anti-TNF antibodies can suppress inflammation but make opportunistic infection more likely.<!--c:crohn-disease-c070--> Some patients need fistula abscesses drained and obstructed or severely diseased bowel resected.<!--c:crohn-disease-c071--> Repeated resections can cause short-bowel syndrome, and disease often returns just before the anastomosis or stoma.<!--c:crohn-disease-c072-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What can look like Crohn disease?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| [[ulcerative-colitis]] | No granulomas, fissures or skip lesions; superficial, diffuse, usually worse distally.<!--c:crohn-disease-c073--> | Colon distribution and histology |
| NSAID injury | Diaphragm-like strictures and single ulcers, usually proximal in the small bowel.<!--c:crohn-disease-c074--> Low doses of NSAIDs may still erode the distal ileum, confusing biopsy reading.<!--c:crohn-disease-c075--> | Drug history, biopsy |
| [[infectious-enterocolitis]] | Infection and radiation-related strictures mimic it but are specifically treatable.<!--c:crohn-disease-c076--> | Exclude the infectious agent |
| Indeterminate colitis | About 10% of cases cannot be definitively assigned, mostly in fulminant colitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c077--> | None definitive |

## High-yield summary

- **Transmural** inflammation and **skip lesions** define the disease.<!--c:crohn-disease-c078-->
- The **distal ileum** and right colon are the favorite sites.<!--c:crohn-disease-c079-->
- **Noncaseating granulomas** are typical but present in fewer than half of cases.<!--c:crohn-disease-c080-->
- **Fissures, fistulas** and **obstruction** are the major complications.<!--c:crohn-disease-c081-->
- **Cobblestone** mucosa and **fat wrapping** are the classic gross signs.<!--c:crohn-disease-c082-->
- **Extraintestinal** disease is commoner than in ulcerative colitis.<!--c:crohn-disease-c083-->
- **Smoking** raises risk, and the T-cell response is **TH1**.<!--c:crohn-disease-c084-->
- **Small bowel** and colorectal cancer risks are raised.<!--c:crohn-disease-c085-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Must granulomas be present?** No. Fewer than half of cases have them.<!--c:crohn-disease-c086-->

**Is it limited to the colon?** No. The distal small intestine is the main site, but any digestive segment can be affected.<!--c:crohn-disease-c087-->

**Is the inflammation continuous, as in ulcerative colitis?** No. Healthy-looking bowel lies between the inflamed segments.<!--c:crohn-disease-c088-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the main features?** CROHN: **C**obblestone mucosa, **R**ight lower quadrant (ileocecal) disease, **O**bstruction, **H**oles (fissures and fistulas), **N**oncaseating granulomas.<!--c:crohn-disease-c089-->
