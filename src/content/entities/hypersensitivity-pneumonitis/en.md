## Definition and epidemiology

**What is hypersensitivity pneumonitis?** The lung reacts to inhaled antigens, and the disorder is also called extrinsic allergic alveolitis.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c001--> The inflammation lies in the interstitium and may be acute or chronic.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c002--> Because most of the antigens are met at work, the disease names usually point to the workplace exposure.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which inhaled antigens cause it?**

- **Farmer's lung** comes from moldy hay: the patient breathes in thermophilic actinomycetes, such as *Micropolyspora faeni*.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c004-->
- **Bagassosis** is caused by moldy sugar cane carrying *Thermoactinomyces sacchari*.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c005-->
- Years of breathing bird proteins, droppings included, can cause **bird fancier's lung** in those who keep birds.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c006-->
- **Maple bark–stripper's disease** follows the handling of maple bark colonized by the fungus *Cryptostroma corticale*.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c007-->
- Suberosis (moldy cork), mushroom worker's disease (moldy compost) and inhaled pituitary snuff are further examples.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c008-->
- Fungi growing in stagnant water, for example in pool or air-conditioning systems, can also trigger the disease.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c009-->

In chronic disease especially, the culprit antigen frequently remains unidentified.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c010--> Most people with serum precipitins stay healthy, so host genes probably decide who becomes ill.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does the lung react to the antigen?** Experts still debate the relative weight of type III (immune complex) and type IV (T-cell) hypersensitivity, but both take part.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c012--> Serum IgG that precipitates the triggering agent is found in most patients.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c013--> Neutrophils flood the tissue in the acute phase, once complement is activated by antigen meeting antibodies already in the alveoli.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c014--> Granulomas and an interstitial lymphocytic infiltrate appear when exposure continues, because alveolar macrophages ingest the antigen without being able to digest it.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c015--> The acute lesion is found in respiratory bronchioles and alveoli, where neutrophils predominate.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c016--> The chronic lesion behaves like delayed hypersensitivity, with granulomas and mononuclear cells.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c017-->

## Gross morphology

Not applicable.

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the lung biopsy show in chronic disease?** Chronic disease centers on the small airways, where a cellular interstitial pneumonia develops (bronchiolocentric pattern).<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c018--> The density of the infiltrate varies from heavy to subtle; its cells are macrophages, plasma cells and lymphocytes.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c019--> Eosinophils are uncommon.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c020--> Around two-thirds of cases contain granulomas, which are loosely formed and lack caseation.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c021--> Organizing pneumonia, with fibrous organization inside the airspace, is found about as often.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c022--> The end stage can mimic usual interstitial pneumonia, because the inflammation has subsided and only pulmonary fibrosis is left.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c023--> When chronic disease is fully developed, the biopsy alone nearly establishes the diagnosis.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c024--> Subtle biopsies can stay inconclusive despite clinical history and chest imaging.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which test?**

- **How it is studied:**
  - Specimen: a lung biopsy, taken only in chronic cases.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c026-->
  - Routine H&E: look for organizing pneumonia, loosely formed granulomas and the infiltrate around bronchioles.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c027-->
  - Most of the excess T lymphocytes recovered from bronchoalveolar fluid are CD8-positive cytotoxic cells.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c028-->

The reference pages describe no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test for this disease.

## Clinical presentation

**How does it present?** Exposure pattern decides the form: how often and how strongly the antigen is met determines acute, subacute or chronic disease.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c029--> Farmer's lung is the prototype.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c030--> A farmhand abruptly becomes breathless, coughs and runs a mild fever, 4 to 6 hours after working in a hay barn.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c031--> Re-exposure brings the symptoms back after they have subsided within 24 to 48 hours.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c032--> Over time the disease becomes chronic.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c033--> Chronic disease is harder to recognize clinically: breathlessness creeps in slowly and cor pulmonale may be present.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c034-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis confirmed?** The diagnosis is confirmed with the help of skin tests and by finding precipitating antibodies in serum.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c035--> Clinical evaluation usually establishes the diagnosis of acute cases.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c036--> Lung function tests are restrictive: the lung is stiff, gas transfer falls and blood oxygen is low.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c037--> Chronic disease may add airway obstruction as a clinical problem.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c038-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What is the outcome, and how is it treated?** The only effective treatment is to end contact with the triggering antigen.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c039--> Acute forms and some chronic patients may respond to steroid therapy.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c040--> Late disease ends in pulmonary fibrosis.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c041-->

**What else can look like hypersensitivity pneumonitis?**

| Berylliosis | Chronic berylliosis can mimic sarcoidosis, its noncaseating granulomas hugging bronchi and vessels, septa and pleura.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c043--> Sensitization to beryllium can be shown by a lymphocyte proliferation test.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c044--> | Beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test |

## High-yield summary

- Most **inhaled antigens** that cause **hypersensitivity pneumonitis** (extrinsic allergic alveolitis) are met at work.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c046-->
- The mechanism is **type III plus type IV** hypersensitivity.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c047-->
- On biopsy of chronic disease, **poorly formed noncaseating granulomas** accompany a **bronchiolocentric** cellular interstitial pneumonia.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c048-->
- **Farmer's lung** is the prototype, with symptoms a few hours after exposure to moldy hay.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c049-->
- **Usual interstitial pneumonia** can be mimicked by the **fibrosis** of the end stage.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c050-->
- **Avoiding the trigger** altogether is the only fully effective measure.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c051-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is hypersensitivity pneumonitis a type I (IgE) allergy?** No. It is built from type III (immune complex) and type IV (T-cell) reactions.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c052-->

**Does a positive serum precipitin prove the disease?** No. Most precipitin-positive people stay healthy.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c053-->

**Does acute disease need a lung biopsy?** Usually not. Clinical findings are generally enough in acute disease; biopsy is kept for chronic cases.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c054-->

**Are the granulomas well formed and caseating?** No. They are poorly formed and noncaseating.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c055-->

**Is the infiltrate rich in eosinophils?** No. Eosinophils are uncommon.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c056-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the chronic picture?** BAG: **B**ronchiolocentric infiltrate, **A**ntigen inhaled, **G**ranulomas poorly formed.<!--c:hypersensitivity-pneumonitis-c057-->
