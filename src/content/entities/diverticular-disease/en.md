## Definition and epidemiology

**What is diverticular disease?** Diverticulitis is the inflammatory complication of diverticulosis, and together the two make up diverticular disease.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c001-->
The muscular coat of the colon is breached by the inner layers in diverticulosis, an acquired change rather than a congenital one.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c002-->
Frequent in the West, it is uncommon in the developing world, such as Asia and Africa.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Why do some populations get diverticulosis more often?** Diet and lifestyle are thought to matter greatly, because the disease is so unevenly distributed around the world.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c004-->
Meat and refined carbohydrates in the diet raise risk, while vegetarian or high-fiber diets lower it.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c005-->
In the small bowel, diverticulosis appears at an unusually early age mainly in people with inherited connective tissue disorders, such as Marfan and Ehlers–Danlos syndromes.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c006-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a diverticulum form, and how does it become inflamed?**
- By the fiber theory, a low-residue Western diet keeps the colon contracting, so the pressure in it rises.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c007-->
- Mucosa and submucosa may herniate when the pressure stays high for long.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c008-->
- Where nutrient vessels enter the colon wall, clefts of connective tissue break the circular muscle; these weak points must be present as well as raised pressure.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c009-->
- Older people resist pressure less well, because their connective tissue loses elasticity.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c010-->
- Diverticulitis is thought to begin when stool lodged in a diverticulum blocks and irritates it.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c011-->
- Eventually, an inflamed diverticulum can burst.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c012-->
- A chronic irritable bowel–like syndrome may follow the acute episode, with abnormal motility and dysbiosis among its causes.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c013-->

## Gross morphology

**What do colonic diverticula look like?**
- Colonic diverticula are false ones (pseudodiverticula), since the muscle layers are crossed by only two layers, mucosa and submucosa.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c014-->
- By contrast, a pouch containing the full thickness of the bowel wall is a true diverticulum.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c015-->
- Almost all cases (95%) involve the sigmoid colon.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c016-->
- The cecum, like every other part of the colon, can be involved.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c017-->
- A colon may hold only a few diverticula or hundreds.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c018-->
- Each pouch is shaped like a flask, with its mouth on the lumen and its body pushing out through the muscle.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c019-->
- Pouches measure up to 1 cm.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c020-->
- Each pouch opens into the lumen through a neck, which may be short or long, narrow or wide.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c021-->
- Often the colon bearing the diverticula has a thickened muscle coat.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c022-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the wall of a diverticulum show?** A diverticulum's wall holds epithelium and submucosa, being a continuation of the surface mucosa.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c023-->
A break in the muscle lets mucosa, with its muscularis mucosae, and submucosa bulge out of the wall.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c024-->
The wall of the diverticulum is where the inflammation of diverticulitis sits.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c025-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** on a routine histologic section, look for a gap in the muscle layer through which mucosa and submucosa have been pushed.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c026-->
- The source pages used for this card describe no special stain, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy or molecular test, and no sampling protocol.

## Clinical presentation

**What symptoms do diverticulosis and diverticulitis cause?**
- Most people with diverticulosis have no symptoms.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c027-->
- When symptoms occur, they take the form of episodic colicky abdominal pain.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c028-->
- Patients may have constipation, diarrhea, or at times both in alternation.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c029-->
- Flatulence is common.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c030-->
- In older adults, diverticula are one source of heavy lower gastrointestinal bleeding that comes abruptly and without pain.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c031-->
- Up to 5% of people with diverticulosis bleed.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c032-->
- Long-standing blood loss can cause anemia.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c033-->
- Diverticulitis eventually complicates diverticulosis in 10% to 20% of cases.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c034-->
- Fever and persistent lower abdominal pain mark acute diverticulitis.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c035-->
- These symptoms usually appear after perforation.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c036-->
- Many patients see their bowel habit change, diarrhea giving way to constipation.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c037-->
- A palpable mass is often found in the left lower quadrant, where most patients are tender.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c038-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Which findings support acute diverticulitis?** Leukocytosis is the rule.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c039-->
The source pages used for this card do not cover imaging or endoscopy.

## Complications and prognosis

**What can go wrong, and how is it treated?**
- Fecal bacteria can escape into the tissue around a diverticulum when its inflamed wall perforates.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c040-->
- Usually, the fat around the colon and the appendices epiploicae confine the resulting abscess.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c041-->
- Generalized peritonitis is an infrequent result of perforation.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c042-->
- Obstruction is possible when the fibrosis that follows repeated attacks narrows the bowel lumen.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c043-->
- A fistula can link the colon to a nearby organ, such as the bladder or vagina.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c044-->
- Pylephlebitis and liver abscess can also occur.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c045-->
- Most acute episodes settle with antibiotics and supportive care.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c046-->
- Surgery is eventually needed by about 20% of patients.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c047-->
- To prevent new attacks, options include a high-fiber diet, mesalamine and periodic courses of antibiotics.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c048-->

## High-yield summary

- Colonic diverticula are **pseudodiverticula**, made of mucosa and submucosa alone.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c051-->
- The **sigmoid colon** is the site in 95% of cases.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c052-->
- Age-weakened connective tissue at vessel entry sites lets the wall give way under the pressure raised by a **low-fiber diet**.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c053-->
- Most patients with diverticulosis are **asymptomatic**.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c054-->
- Diverticular **bleeding** is sudden and painless.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c055-->
- **Diverticulitis** can cause abscess, perforation, fistula and obstruction.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c056-->
- Acute diverticulitis means lower abdominal pain, **fever** and **leukocytosis**, with tenderness in the left lower quadrant.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c057-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a colonic diverticulum a true diverticulum?** No. Only mucosa and submucosa herniate, so it is a pseudodiverticulum.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c058-->

**Is diverticular bleeding a sign of diverticulitis?** No. Bleeding belongs to diverticulosis, and it is sudden and painless.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c059--> Diverticulitis instead brings persistent pain and fever.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c060-->

**Does everyone with diverticulosis develop diverticulitis?** No. Only 10% to 20% do.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c061-->

## Mnemonics

- **MuSS-out:** Mucosa and Submucosa slip out through the muscle, so the pouch is false.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c062-->
- **FAPO:** the complications of diverticulitis are Fistula, Abscess, Perforation and Obstruction.<!--c:diverticular-disease-c063-->
