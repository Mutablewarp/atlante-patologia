## Definition and epidemiology

**How common are metastases among lung malignancies?** Tumors that spread to the lung from another site are more frequent there than any other malignancy.<!--c:lung-metastases-c001-->
How often does a fatal cancer reach the lung? At autopsy, about a third of the people who died of cancer have lung metastases.<!--c:lung-metastases-c002-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Which primary tumors send metastases to the lung?** Lymphangitic spread usually begins in the breast or in gut or pancreatic tumors.<!--c:lung-metastases-c003-->
Pancreas and stomach are the usual primaries behind the uncommon mucinous metastasis with lepidic growth.<!--c:lung-metastases-c004-->

## Pathogenesis

**How does a tumor spread in lymphangitic carcinoma?** In lymphangitic carcinoma, lung lymphatics carry the metastasis over a wide area.<!--c:lung-metastases-c005-->
The result is a tumor cuff around veins and bronchovascular tree.<!--c:lung-metastases-c006-->

## Gross morphology

**What do lung metastases look like on the cut lung?** A lung with metastases usually contains several sharply demarcated nodules.<!--c:lung-metastases-c007-->
The name "cannon ball" metastases refers to large metastatic nodules visible on imaging.<!--c:lung-metastases-c008-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does a metastasis look like under the microscope?** In most cases a metastasis looks like the tumor that produced it.<!--c:lung-metastases-c009-->
Mucinous tumors typify the rare metastasis that grows in a lepidic pattern.<!--c:lung-metastases-c010-->

## Clinical presentation

**What symptoms does lymphangitic carcinoma cause?** Patients have cough and shortness of breath.<!--c:lung-metastases-c011-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**What does the chest radiograph show?** Lymphangitic carcinoma gives the film a reticulonodular appearance throughout the lungs.<!--c:lung-metastases-c012-->
A chest film with many big metastatic nodules is said to show a "cannon ball" pattern.<!--c:lung-metastases-c013-->

## Differential diagnosis

**What else can look like nodules or sheaths in the lung?**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Metastatic nodules ([[invasion-and-metastasis]]) | Several sharply demarcated nodules, usually similar in appearance to the primary tumor.<!--c:lung-metastases-c014--> | Histology compared with the primary tumor |
| Lymphangitic carcinoma | Spreads along lymphatics around the bronchovascular tree and veins; chest film is diffusely reticulonodular.<!--c:lung-metastases-c015--> | Chest radiograph and histology |
| Lymphomatoid granulomatosis | Angioinvasive lymphoid infiltrate, often with central necrosis, forms nodules in the lung.<!--c:lung-metastases-c016--> | Histology |

## High-yield summary

- No other malignancy of the lung is as frequent as **metastasis**.<!--c:lung-metastases-c017-->
- The usual picture is **multiple circumscribed nodules**, and big ones are called **cannon ball** metastases.<!--c:lung-metastases-c018-->
- **Lymphangitic carcinoma** usually arises from breast, pancreas or gut.<!--c:lung-metastases-c019-->
- A **mucinous lepidic** metastasis suggests a pancreatic or gastric primary.<!--c:lung-metastases-c020-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Is a lung metastasis usually a single nodule?** No. Several well-demarcated nodules are the usual finding.<!--c:lung-metastases-c021-->

**Is "cannon ball" a histologic type?** No. It only describes how big metastatic nodules look on imaging.<!--c:lung-metastases-c022-->

**Does lymphangitic carcinoma always form discrete nodules?** No. It can wrap the bronchovascular tree and veins via the lymphatics.<!--c:lung-metastases-c023-->

## Mnemonics

**How can I remember the four usual primaries of lymphangitic carcinoma?** Think "BiCePS": Breast, Colon, Pancreas, Stomach.<!--c:lung-metastases-c024-->
