## Definition and epidemiology

**What are congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract?** They are malformations of the kidneys, ureters and bladder that arise in development.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c001--> Between 2% and 3% of people have a malformation of the renal pelvis or ureter.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c002--> Only occasionally does obstruction or urinary tract infection arise; most are silent.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c003--> In renal agenesis, no renal tissue forms at all.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c004--> A hypoplastic kidney is small, with normal histology but too few nephrons.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c005--> In renal ectopia, a normal kidney sits in the wrong place.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c006--> In horseshoe kidney, the two kidneys are joined into one midline mass.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c007--> In renal dysplasia, immature tubules lie in primitive mesenchyme.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c008-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**What causes these malformations?** Several genetic or acquired factors can disturb metanephric differentiation, producing dysplasia.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c009--> In some familial dysplasias, the ureteric bud and metanephric blastema fail to induce each other properly.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c010--> Multicystic dysplasia is inherited less often than aplastic or diffuse cystic dysplasia; Meckel–Gruber syndrome, with other organs malformed, is the example.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c011--> If the ureteric bud sprouts from a misplaced embryonic point, the ureter is ectopic.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c012--> Duplicated ureters arise when the embryo forms more than one ureteric bud.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c013--> Vesicoureteral reflux is often familial.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c014-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do the anomalies arise?** A kidney that never ascended during fetal life stays low, most often in the pelvis.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c015--> Obstruction before birth is thought to cause dysplasia, since many dysplastic kidneys coexist with obstructive anomalies like ureteropelvic junction obstruction or posterior urethral valves.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c016--> Probably, muscle or fibrous tissue is laid down abnormally at the junction in ureteropelvic junction obstruction.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c017-->

**Why does Potter sequence develop?** When the fetus makes too little urine, amniotic fluid volume falls (oligohydramnios).<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c018--> Without fluid cushioning, the uterus bows the legs and flattens the face.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c019--> Lung growth suffers twice: the uterus squeezes the chest, and the fluid no longer supplies maturation signals.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c020-->

**Why does the bladder open in exstrophy?** Muscle normally replaces the anterior cloacal membrane; in exstrophy it does not.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c021--> Bladder and abdominal wall end up open together, because the fragile membrane that persisted tears.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c022-->

**Why does urine flow back in reflux?** Muscle of the bladder wall closes a ureter that tunnels slantwise through it for a good distance, so urine cannot flow back.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c023--> Voiding forces urine back up a ureter that enters squarely and has a short tunnel in the wall.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c024-->

## Gross morphology

**What do the organs look like?** In bilateral agenesis the kidney remnants may be nothing but mesenchyme.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c025--> A hypoplastic kidney has fewer lobes and usually under half normal weight.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c026--> A horseshoe kidney is usually fused at the lower poles.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c027--> Multicystic dysplasia gives a lump of differently sized cysts, not a kidney-shaped organ.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c028--> The kidney of diffuse cystic dysplasia keeps its shape, and its cysts are fairly even in size.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c029--> The naked eye can miss an aplastic dysplastic kidney, which is tiny and deformed.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c030--> Duplication usually gives a second full drainage line: two pelves, two ureters and two bladder openings.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c031--> The exstrophic bladder is open to the outside, with the posterior wall exposed, like a cup.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c032-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What do the tissues show?** A hypoplastic kidney has normal histology.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c033--> In oligomeganephronia, the few glomeruli are enlarged.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c034--> Dysplastic ducts and tubules are immature, and their cells are cuboidal or columnar.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c035--> Cartilage or smooth muscle can be embedded in the immature mesenchyme around them.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c036--> Some tubules are cystic, and rudimentary glomeruli may be present.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c037--> The exposed bladder mucosa of exstrophy undergoes squamous or glandular metaplasia.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c038-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**What does the pathologist look at, and with which stain?**
- **How it is studied:** removal alone suffices for a unilateral multicystic kidney, so the pathologist gets a nephrectomy.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c039-->
- In dysplasia, look for cartilage in mesenchyme that rings immature tubules.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c040-->
- The pages used describe no special stains, IHC, electron microscopy or molecular tests.

## Clinical presentation

**How do they present?** Potter sequence accompanies bilateral renal agenesis, and most affected infants are stillborn.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c042--> The remaining kidney hypertrophies enough to keep renal function normal, so one absent kidney is harmless alone.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c043--> Early in life, a flank swelling is usually what reveals multicystic dysplasia.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c044--> When a newborn has an abdominal mass, the commonest cause is one-sided multicystic dysplasia.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c045--> Ureters pinched as they pass over the fusion bridge of a horseshoe kidney make obstruction and pyelonephritis likelier.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c046--> In infants and children with hydronephrosis, ureteropelvic junction obstruction is the leading cause.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c047--> Agenesis of a ureter always comes with agenesis of the same kidney.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c048--> About 1 birth in 50,000 is affected by bladder exstrophy.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c049--> Some boys with exstrophy also have epispadias.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c050--> Young girls are affected by reflux more often than boys.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c051--> Three cases of reflux in four cause no symptoms.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c052-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**How is the diagnosis made?** Before a small kidney is called hypoplastic, atrophy and scarring have to be excluded.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c053--> The pages used describe no imaging or laboratory work-up.

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the complications?** In Potter sequence, the hypoplastic lungs are the greatest threat to life.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c055--> Respiratory failure, not renal failure, may kill, because dialysis is possible even in newborns.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c056--> If both ureters and both kidneys are absent, the infant cannot survive after birth.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c057--> Overwork of the remaining nephrons can lead to progressive secondary focal segmental glomerulosclerosis when one kidney is absent and its partner is hypoplastic.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c058--> Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis is also a risk in oligomeganephronia.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c059--> Reflux can lead to reflux pyelonephritis.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c061--> Bladder cancer risk stays raised for decades in the metaplastic lining, even once exstrophy is repaired.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c062-->

## High-yield summary

- **Potter sequence** is oligohydramnios from low fetal urine, with small lungs and a deformed face and legs.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c065-->
- **Pulmonary hypoplasia** is the lethal component.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c066-->
- **Bilateral agenesis** is usually stillborn.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c067-->
- **Unilateral agenesis** is tolerated through hypertrophy of the other kidney.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c068-->
- **Horseshoe kidney** is fused at the lower poles and prone to obstruction and pyelonephritis.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c069-->
- **Dysplasia**: cartilage may sit in the mesenchyme around immature tubules.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c070-->
- **Multicystic dysplasia** is the commonest newborn abdominal mass, treated by nephrectomy.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c071-->
- **Ureteral duplication** is the commonest urinary tract malformation and is mostly silent.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c072-->
- **Vesicoureteral reflux** comes from a short intravesical ureter and affects girls more.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c073-->
- **Bladder exstrophy** lacks the anterior bladder wall and keeps a raised bladder cancer risk after repair.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c074-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Does unilateral renal agenesis cause renal failure?** Not usually: the other kidney hypertrophies enough to keep normal function.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c075-->

**Is renal failure the usual cause of death in Potter sequence?** No. Pulmonary hypoplasia may be the cause of death instead.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c076-->

**Is every small kidney hypoplastic?** No. Atrophy and scarring also give small kidneys.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c077-->

**Does repair of exstrophy remove the cancer risk?** No. The metaplastic mucosa stays at risk.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c078-->

**Is reflux usually symptomatic?** No. Most cases cause no symptoms.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c079-->

**Does a duplicated ureter need treatment?** Usually not: most variants are clinically unimportant.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c080-->

## Mnemonics

**No urine, no fluid, no lungs:** low fetal urine, little amniotic fluid, hypoplastic lungs.<!--c:congenital-anomalies-kidney-urinary-tract-c081-->
