## Definition and epidemiology

**What are infectious and chemical esophagitis?** They are injuries of the esophageal lining caused by microbes, swallowed corrosives or pills, systemic disease, or medical treatment.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c001--> Candida is the most common esophageal infection.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c002--> Candida in a patient with no known predisposing factor is unusual.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c003-->

## Etiology and risk factors

**Who is at risk?** Transplant, chemotherapy, or HIV/AIDS immunosuppression opens the way to Candida.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c004--> Inhaled or swallowed corticosteroids, antibiotics, acid-suppressing drugs, and diabetes also predispose to Candida.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c005--> HSV-1 esophagitis occurs most often in recipients of marrow or solid-organ transplants.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c006--> It can also occur, rarely, in healthy people.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c007--> In AIDS or transplant patients with very weak immunity, CMV in the esophagus usually signals infection elsewhere in the body too.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c008--> In adults, corrosive injury usually follows a suicide attempt; in children, accidental poisoning.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c009--> Cleaning products that contain lye or sulfuric or hydrochloric acid can injure the esophagus.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c010--> Pill esophagitis results from a medication contacting the mucosa.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c011-->

## Pathogenesis

**How do these agents damage the mucosa?** Alkali liquefies every layer of the wall (liquefactive necrosis), turning membrane fats to soap.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c012--> Strong acid coagulates tissue instantly into an eschar, which protects the layers beneath.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c014--> Medication usually harms the squamous lining through direct chemical contact, capsules above all.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c016--> HSV mostly infects epithelium; CMV mostly infects the vessel-lining (endothelial) and supporting (stromal) cells.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c018--> Dermolytic epidermolysis bullosa affects all squamous-lined organs, the esophagus among them.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c020--> No scarring follows the blisters of bullous pemphigoid, which sit under the epithelium in skin and esophagus.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c021--> Esophagitis is also linked to erythema multiforme, Behçet syndrome, dermatitis herpetiformis, and pemphigus vulgaris.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c022--> Esophageal lesions are one manifestation of graft-versus-host disease after bone marrow transplantation.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c023--> Radiotherapy delivered from outside the body for a chest cancer can inflame the esophagus.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c024-->
## Gross morphology

**What lesions are visible?** Candida produces white mucosal plaques.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c026--> Herpes esophagitis can start as plaques, small erosions or blisters.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c027--> Later the typical lesion is an ulcer with crisp, well-defined borders.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c028--> CMV commonly causes mucosal ulcers too.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c029-->

## Microscopic morphology

**What does the microscope show?** Neutrophils in the squamous epithelium are typical of Candida esophagitis; deep immunosuppression can erase them.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c030--> Yeasts and pseudohyphae sit in necrotic squamous debris.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c031--> Either Cowdry A inclusions (pink with a pale halo) or Cowdry B inclusions (ground-glass nuclei) mark herpes-infected cells at the ulcer edge.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c032--> CMV cells swell and show one large owl's-eye nuclear inclusion plus smaller cytoplasmic inclusions.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c034--> HSV shows intranuclear inclusions only.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c035-->

## Special stains, IHC and molecular findings

**Which stain shows what?**
- **How it is studied:** on routine H&E, look for neutrophils within the squamous epithelium when Candida is suspected.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c036-->
- Silver or PAS stains show yeasts and pseudohyphae best.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c037-->

IHC and molecular tests: not covered here.

## Clinical presentation

**What do patients complain of?** Candida esophagitis usually presents with difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) and severe pain on swallowing.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c040--> Herpetic esophagitis causes odynophagia.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c041--> In dermolytic epidermolysis bullosa, swallowing is often painful and hard, and blisters break down into ulcers that ooze.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c042--> Graft-versus-host disease affecting the esophagus may cause difficulty swallowing, painful swallowing, or acid reflux.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c043-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging

**Where should the biopsy look?** HSV is found at the ulcer edge.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c038--> CMV is usually found at the ulcer base.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c039-->

## Complications and prognosis

**What are the long-term consequences?** Squamous cell carcinoma is a much likelier late problem, for life, in survivors of caustic ingestion.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c044--> Liquid alkali almost always damages the esophagus severely.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c045--> Granular alkali causes severe complications in fewer than 25% of patients.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c046--> Severe esophageal injury affects half of patients after concentrated sulfuric or hydrochloric acid is swallowed.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c047--> Stricture, when it occurs in dermolytic epidermolysis bullosa, is usually in the upper esophagus.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c048--> Irradiation can cause stricture.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c049-->

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Discriminating features | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Candida esophagitis ([[opportunistic-fungal-infections]]) | White plaques with neutrophils in the epithelium.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c050--> | Silver or PAS stain.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c051--> |
| Herpetic esophagitis ([[herpesvirus-infections]]) | Sharply demarcated ulcers; inclusions in epithelial cells.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c052--> | Inclusions at the ulcer edge.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c053--> |
| CMV esophagitis | Ulcers; infected endothelial and stromal cells.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c054--> | Owl's eye inclusion, usually at the ulcer base.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c055--> |
| [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]] | Usually proximal or mid esophagus; antireflux therapy does not help.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c056--> | Increased intraepithelial eosinophils on biopsy.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c057--> |

## High-yield summary

- **Candida** gives **white plaques**; silver or PAS shows yeasts and pseudohyphae.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c058-->
- **HSV** gives **ulcers with inclusions** at the edge, **Cowdry A or B**.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c059-->
- **CMV** is usually at the **ulcer base**, with **owl's eye** inclusions, in endothelial and stromal cells.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c060-->
- **Alkali** causes **liquefactive** necrosis; **acid** causes **coagulative** necrosis.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c061-->
- **Caustic** ingestion leaves survivors with a permanent excess risk of **squamous cell carcinoma**.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c062-->
- **GVHD**, **irradiation**, and **epidermolysis bullosa** are non-infectious causes.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c063-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps

**Do CMV and HSV form the same inclusions?** No. HSV inclusions are only intranuclear; CMV cells also carry cytoplasmic ones.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c064-->

**Does an acid burn always go deeper than an alkali burn?** No. The eschar from strong acid limits injury.<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c065-->
## Mnemonics

**Herpes at the Hem, Cytomegalovirus at the Core.**<!--c:infectious-and-chemical-esophagitis-c068-->
