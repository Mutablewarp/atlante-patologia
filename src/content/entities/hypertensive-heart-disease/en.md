## Definition and epidemiology
**What is hypertensive heart disease?** It is the damage that long-standing high blood pressure does to the heart.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c001--> The heart first thickens to cope with the extra load, and with time it fails.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c002-->

**How is hypertension defined?** For the World Health Organization, hypertension means a pressure that stays above 90 mm Hg diastolic or above 140 mm Hg systolic, or both.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c003--> In the 2017 American guidelines, a diastolic pressure of 80 to 89 or a systolic pressure of 130 to 139 marks stage 1.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c004--> Stage 2 starts at 140 systolic or 90 diastolic.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c005-->

## Etiology and risk factors
**What puts the heart under strain?** High systemic vascular resistance raises the wall stress the left ventricle must overcome.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c006--> Coronary atherosclerosis tends to be more severe in people with hypertension.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c007-->

## Pathogenesis
**How does the heart respond to the extra load?** Concentric left ventricular hypertrophy is the heart's answer to the added work.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c008--> More muscle brings wall stress back toward normal.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c009--> Beyond a certain point, more hypertrophy no longer helps.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c010--> One proposed reason: oxygen has to diffuse farther into a thicker fiber, so its core is undersupplied.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c011--> The left ventricle is also stiffened by some fibrous tissue that hypertrophy lays down between the myocytes.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c012-->

## Gross morphology
**What does the heart look like?** The cavity stays the same size while the left ventricular wall and septum thicken evenly.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c013--> Hypertensive hearts weigh more than 350 g in women and more than 375 g in men.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c014--> The right ventricle keeps a normal size.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c015-->

## Microscopic morphology
**What changes in the myocytes?** The hypertrophic myocytes are thicker than normal ones.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c016--> Their nuclei are large and boxcar-like.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c017--> The nuclei also stain darker.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c018-->

Not applicable.

## Clinical presentation
**Which functional problem is the most common?** Hypertension most often impairs diastolic function, and this alone can lead to heart failure.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c019--> A stiff, overworked ventricle with narrowed coronaries may fail or become ischemic, even infarct.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c020--> Without treatment, the usual course ends in heart failure with eccentric hypertrophy.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c021-->

## Diagnosis, key lab and imaging
**What is the criterion?** The diagnosis requires that hypertension is the only cause found for the enlarged heart.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c022--> The source used for this card gives no laboratory or imaging criteria.

## Complications and prognosis
**What kills patients with untreated hypertension?** Heart failure is the main cause of death.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c023--> Other common fatal events:
- Intracerebral hemorrhage.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c024-->
- Rupture of a cerebral berry aneurysm.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c025-->
- Myocardial infarction on coronary atherosclerosis.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c026-->
- Aortic dissection.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c027-->

Renal failure from nephrosclerosis is a further risk when hypertension is severe.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c028-->

## High-yield summary
- **Concentric** left ventricular hypertrophy with no chamber dilation.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c031-->
- **Heart weight** above 350 g in women and 375 g in men.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c032-->
- **Diastolic dysfunction** is hypertension's most frequent functional problem.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c033-->
- **Boxcar nuclei** in thick myocytes.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c034-->
- **Heart failure** is the main cause of death if untreated.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c035-->

## Common misconceptions and exam traps
**Is the left ventricle dilated in hypertensive heart disease at first?** No. The wall is thick and the chamber is not enlarged.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c036-->

**Can any big heart in a hypertensive patient be called hypertensive heart disease?** No. No other cause of enlargement may be found.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c037-->

**Is systolic failure the usual problem?** No. Hypertension more often causes diastolic than systolic dysfunction.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c038-->

## Mnemonics
**How can I remember the key points?** **WALLS**: Weight up, Atherosclerosis worse, Left ventricle concentric, Limit then eccentric, Stiff in diastole.<!--c:hypertensive-heart-disease-c039-->
